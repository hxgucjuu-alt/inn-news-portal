  # El Niño Threatens Colombia's Coffee Production, Putting 540,000 Families' Livelihoods at Risk
  
  > Colombia, the world's third-largest coffee producer, faces the threat of El Niño, with production potentially declining significantly, impacting the livelihoods of over 540,000 families dependent on coffee.
  
  Colombia is a key supplier in the global coffee market, particularly renowned for its washed Arabica coffee beans, and is the world's largest supplier of this type of coffee bean. According to reports from FTV News, approximately 540,000 families in Colombia depend on coffee cultivation and its related industry chain for their livelihoods. The coffee industry is not only a vital pillar of the country's economy but also the primary source of income for many rural households. However, this industry, upon which many rely, is currently facing severe climate challenges, and the impact of the El Niño phenomenon may be more profound than the commonly perceived threat of strong earthquakes, directly and significantly affecting coffee production.
  
  The report points out that El Niño typically causes abnormally high sea surface temperatures in the Pacific, which in turn alters rainfall patterns and temperature distributions globally. For Colombia, El Niño often signifies drought or abnormal rainfall, both of which are extreme weather patterns that adversely affect the growth cycle, flowering, fruiting, and quality of coffee cherries. Coffee trees are highly sensitive to climatic conditions, especially during the flowering and fruiting stages. If they encounter abnormal high temperatures or insufficient moisture, it directly impacts the development of coffee cherries, leading to lower yields and even affecting the flavor and quality of the beans, subsequently impacting Colombia's competitiveness in the international coffee market and its export earnings.
  
  The FTV News report emphasizes that while strong earthquakes may cause immediate damage to infrastructure and production areas, the climate anomalies brought by El Niño could have more long-term and widespread destructive power. The report cites analyses indicating that the threat of El Niño to the coffee industry is not limited to a single region but could affect multiple major coffee-growing areas across Colombia, covering a broader scope and lasting longer. This implies that even without severe geological disasters, Colombia's coffee industry could still face significant production reductions due to climatic factors, which undoubtedly poses a major challenge to the national economy that relies on coffee exports for foreign exchange.
  
  Currently, assessments and predictions regarding the specific extent of El Niño's impact on Colombian coffee production remain uncertain. The FTV News report primarily focuses on the risk of production decline that El Niño may cause but does not provide specific percentage forecasts for production reduction, nor does it clearly identify the specific provinces or production areas most severely affected. Additionally, the report does not mention whether the Colombian government or coffee industry associations have initiated any response measures, such as subsidy programs, irrigation system improvements, or variety enhancement, and this information awaits further confirmation. Evaluations regarding the timeline and severity of the impact may also differ among various sources, requiring continued observation of subsequently released official data and industry reports.
  
  The potential reduction in Colombia's coffee production will not be limited to within its borders. As a major global coffee supply source, fluctuations in Colombia's output will directly affect international coffee futures prices, potentially leading to higher prices in the global coffee market, thereby impacting global consumers and downstream players in the coffee industry chain, such as roasters and retailers. Domestically, the 540,000 families dependent on coffee for their livelihoods, mostly smallholder farmers, have relatively fragile adaptive capacity to climate change. If production drops sharply, their incomes will decrease significantly, potentially exacerbating poverty and socio-economic pressures in rural areas, and could even trigger population migration or shifts in industrial structure.
  
  There are differing views in the market regarding the threat of El Niño. On one hand, some analysts believe that advancements in modern agricultural technology and climate prediction capabilities help farmers take preventive measures in advance, mitigating some losses. On the other hand, some argue that Colombian coffee-growing regions are mostly on hillsides with insufficient irrigation infrastructure, and smallholder farmers generally lack the capital and technical know-how to adopt advanced adaptation strategies, thus their resilience to extreme weather remains limited. Therefore, until official data on specific production reductions and actual damage assessments are released, it is not appropriate to over-infer that El Niño will inevitably lead to catastrophic, widespread production declines. A careful evaluation of various possibilities and variables is still necessary.
  
  Future observation should focus on production forecast reports issued by Colombia's official meteorological agencies and the National Federation of Coffee Growers (Federación Nacional de Cafeteros), as well as market analyses from the International Coffee Organization (ICO). Additionally, the actual intensity and duration of the El Niño event will be key factors determining the extent of its impact. At the same time, attention should be paid to whether the Colombian government introduces relevant industry relief or adaptation policies, as well as the subsequent trends in international coffee prices. These indicators will help more accurately assess the true impact of this climate event on Colombia's coffee industry and the global market.

# 聖嬰現象衝擊哥倫比亞咖啡產量 54萬家庭生計拉警報

> 哥倫比亞作為全球第三大咖啡生產國，面臨聖嬰現象威脅，產量恐顯著下滑，影響逾54萬戶依賴咖啡維生的家庭生計。

哥倫比亞是全球咖啡市場的重要供應國，尤其以水洗阿拉比卡（Arabica）咖啡豆聞名，更是全球第一大此類咖啡豆供應國。根據民視新聞網的報導，哥倫比亞約有54萬戶家庭依賴咖啡種植與相關產業鏈維生，咖啡產業不僅是該國經濟的重要支柱，更是許多農村地區家庭的主要收入來源。然而，這項賴以維生的產業目前正面臨嚴峻的氣候挑戰，聖嬰現象（El Niño）的影響可能比一般認知的強震威脅更為深遠，對咖啡產量造成直接且顯著的衝擊。
報導中指出，聖嬰現象通常會導致太平洋海溫異常升高，進而改變全球各地的降雨模式與氣溫分佈。對哥倫比亞而言，聖嬰現象往往意味著乾旱或異常降雨，這兩種極端天氣型態對咖啡樹的生長週期、開花結果及果實品質均有不利影響。咖啡樹對氣候條件相當敏感，特別是在開花期與結果期，若遭遇異常高溫或水分不足，將直接影響咖啡櫻桃的發育，導致產量下降，甚至影響咖啡豆的風味與品質，進而波及哥倫比亞咖啡在國際市場上的競爭力與出口收益。
民視新聞網的報導強調，強震雖然可能對基礎設施與產區造成即時破壞，但聖嬰現象帶來的氣候異常可能更具長期且廣泛的殺傷力。報導引述相關分析指出，聖嬰現象對咖啡產業的威脅並非僅限於單一區域，而是可能影響哥倫比亞多個主要咖啡種植區，涵蓋範圍更廣，持續時間也更長。這意味著，即使沒有發生劇烈的地質災害，哥倫比亞的咖啡產業仍可能因氣候因素而面臨產量大幅縮減的困境，這對倚賴咖啡出口賺取外匯的國家經濟而言，無疑是一項重大挑戰。
目前，關於聖嬰現象對哥倫比亞咖啡產量的具體影響程度，各方的評估與預測仍存在不確定性。民視新聞網的報導主要聚焦於聖嬰現象可能導致的產量下滑風險，但並未提供具體的減產百分比預測數字，也未明確指出受影響最嚴重的特定省份或產區。此外，報導中亦未提及哥倫比亞政府或咖啡產業協會是否已啟動任何應對措施，例如補助計畫、灌溉系統改善或品種改良等，這些資訊仍有待進一步確認。不同來源之間對於影響時程與嚴重程度的評估也可能存在差異，需持續觀察後續發布的官方資料與產業報告。
哥倫比亞咖啡產業的潛在減產，其影響將不僅限於該國境內。作為全球重要的咖啡供應來源，哥倫比亞的產量波動將直接影響國際咖啡期貨價格，可能導致全球咖啡市場價格上漲，進而影響全球消費者與咖啡產業鏈下游的烘焙商、零售商。對於哥倫比亞國內而言，54萬戶依賴咖啡維生的家庭，多數為小農戶，他們對氣候變遷的調適能力相對脆弱，一旦產量銳減，收入將大幅減少，可能加劇農村地區的貧窮問題與社會經濟壓力，甚至可能引發人口遷移或產業結構的轉變。
針對聖嬰現象的威脅，市場上存在不同觀點。一方面，有分析認為現代農業技術與氣候預測能力的提升，有助於農民提前做好防範措施，減輕部分損失。另一方面，也有觀點指出，哥倫比亞咖啡產區多為山坡地，灌溉設施不足，且小農戶普遍缺乏資金與技術匯入先進的調適策略，因此對於極端天氣的抵抗力依然有限。因此，在官方具體減產資料與實際受災情況出爐前，不宜過度推斷聖嬰現象將必然導致災難性的全面減產，仍需審慎評估各種可能性與變數。
後續觀察的重點應放在哥倫比亞官方氣象機構與咖啡種植者聯合會（Federación Nacional de Cafeteros）發布的產量預測報告，以及國際咖啡組織（ICO）的市場分析。此外，聖嬰現象的實際發展強度與持續時間，將是決定影響程度的關鍵因素。同時，也需關注哥倫比亞政府是否推出相關的產業紓困或調適政策，以及國際咖啡價格的後續走勢。這些指標將有助於更準確地評估此次氣候事件對哥倫比亞咖啡產業及全球市場的真實衝擊。
