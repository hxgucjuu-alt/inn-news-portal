  # U.S. Launches 301 Investigation: Taiwan Faces Global Minimum 15% Tariff and Trade Structure Challenges
  
  > The Office of the United States Trade Representative has initiated a 301 investigation into 14 economies, with a 15% tariff recommended for Taiwan. The Federation of Industries (CNFI) noted that while this rate is the lowest globally, it indicates that U.S. scrutiny of Taiwan's trade structure has deepened, and risks regarding future supply chain policies remain.
  
  On November 26, 2026, the Office of the United States Trade Representative (USTR) officially announced the results of a 301 investigation into "structural manufacturing overcapacity" across 14 global economies. The background of this investigation stems from the U.S.'s reassessment of global supply chain restructuring and manufacturing competitiveness, aiming to adjust the international trade order through tariff measures. As a global hub for semiconductors and high-tech manufacturing, Taiwan was proposed for a maximum 15% tariff in this investigation. According to current preliminary plans, the tariff calculation method adopts a model that does not overlap with Most-Favored-Nation (MFN) status or other existing tariff programs, aiming to precisely target specific areas of structural overcapacity rather than imposing comprehensive levies.
  
  This move has not only drawn high attention from the domestic industry but is also seen as a landmark event signaling that Taiwan-U.S. trade negotiations have entered a new phase.
  
  According to cross-referenced sources, the Chinese National Federation of Industries (CNFI) issued a statement on the 26th regarding the investigation results. The CNFI confirmed that the 15% rate proposed by the U.S. is the lowest among the 14 economies investigated. This fact reflects the results of long-term trade negotiations between Taiwan and the U.S., which have played a buffering and protective role to a certain extent, preventing Taiwan from being included in a list subject to higher punitive tariffs.
  
  However, the CNFI also emphasized that this does not mean Taiwan can lower its guard, as the U.S. investigative logic has shifted from simple trade balance to a deep review of "structural manufacturing capacity." This implies that future trade risks have expanded from simple tax rate issues to more complex categories of supply chain policy and industrial structure adjustment.
  
  Information regarding this event remains inconsistent across various interpretations and U.S. implementation details. Although the CNFI confirmed the relative advantage of the 15% rate, there is no definitive conclusion yet on whether the U.S. will further conduct "precision strikes" against specific technology supply chains, or the specific impact of this investigation on subsequent Taiwan-U.S. supply chain cooperation agreements. Some market analyses and international observer institutions point out that this U.S. action is closely related to political maneuvering within the global economic system concerning pharmaceutical companies, energy, and high-tech supply chains.
  
  Although Taiwan currently receives relatively lenient treatment in terms of tariffs, there remains high uncertainty regarding whether the U.S. will use "structural overcapacity" as a pretext to demand that Taiwanese companies conduct more localized production or technology transfers within the United States in the future.
  
  The immediate impact of this policy change on Taiwan will be felt most by the high-tech manufacturing and export-oriented industries. If the U.S. continues to deepen its scrutiny of manufacturing structures, Taiwanese companies will face higher compliance costs and risks of policy volatility. Against the backdrop of increasingly intertwined Eurasian logistics and energy issues, the global trade system is undergoing a structural shift from "efficiency-first" to "security and resilience-first." For Taiwan, how to maintain existing trade advantages while addressing U.S. concerns over overcapacity will become a core challenge for future policy formulation and corporate operations.
  
  Furthermore, this investigation may indirectly affect Taiwan's bargaining chips in trade negotiations with other Eurasian economies, as U.S. 301 investigations often carry indicative significance and may trigger follow-up scrutiny from other trade partners.
  
  Although a 15% tariff rate is relatively low in international comparisons, all parties should avoid viewing this as a "final verdict" on U.S. trade policy toward Taiwan. The CNFI and relevant industry experts remind that U.S. 301 investigation procedures are usually accompanied by long-term negotiations and reviews; this is the beginning of a tough battle, not the end. Over-interpreting this tariff rate as a "safe passage" risks ignoring potential non-tariff trade barriers the U.S. may adopt later, such as requirements for supply chain transparency, mandatory environmental and labor standards, and further restrictions on the export of critical technologies. Therefore, the industry and government departments should remain highly vigilant and not focus solely on a single tax figure, but rather broaden their perspective to the political and economic layout of the global supply chain.
  
  Subsequent observation should focus on the details of U.S. policy implementation, particularly whether the criteria for defining "structural overcapacity" will become more stringent over time. In addition, how the Taiwan government communicates with the U.S. to demonstrate the rationality of Taiwan's industrial structure, and whether trade friction can be reduced through closer industrial cooperation agreements, will be key points to watch. Meanwhile, policies regarding multinational pharmaceutical companies in the global economic system, energy supply chain security, and changes in infrastructure construction in the Eurasian region may all have ripple effects from this 301 investigation. Future updates from the Office of the United States Trade Representative and further impact assessment reports from relevant Taiwanese trade associations should be continuously tracked.

# 美國發動301調查：臺灣面臨全球最低15%關稅與貿易結構挑戰

> 美國貿易代表署針對14個經濟體啟動301調查，臺灣被建議課徵15%關稅。工總指出此稅率雖屬全球最低階別，但顯示美方對臺貿易結構審查已深化，未來供應鏈政策風險仍存。


美國貿易代表署（USTR）於2026年11月26日正式公佈針對全球14個經濟體進行「製造業結構性產能過剩」的301調查結果。此項調查背景源於美國對全球供應鏈重組及製造業競爭力的重新評估，旨在透過關稅手段調整國際貿易秩序。臺灣作為全球半導體與高科技製造重鎮，在此次調查中被美方提出最高15%的建議關稅措施。根據目前的初步規劃，該關稅計算方式採取不與最惠國待遇（MFN）及其他既有關稅專案重複疊加的模式，旨在精準針對特定結構性產能過剩領域，而非全面性加徵。
此舉不僅引發國內產業界高度關注，亦被視為臺美貿易協商進入新階段的指標性事件。
根據多方來源交叉比對，中華民國全國工業總會（工總）於26日針對此調查結果發表宣告。工總確認，美方提出的15%稅率在本次被調查的14個經濟體中，屬於全球最低的級別。此一事實反映出臺美雙方過去長期累積的貿易協商成果，在一定程度上發揮了緩衝與保障作用，使臺灣未被列入更高關稅的懲罰性名單。
然而，工總亦強調，這並不代表臺灣可以掉以輕心，因為美方的調查邏輯已從單純的貿易平衡，轉向對「製造業結構性產能」的深層審查，這意味著未來的貿易風險已從單純的稅率問題，擴充套件至更為複雜的供應鏈政策與產業結構調整範疇。
目前關於此事件的資訊，在各界解讀與美方執行細節上仍存在差異。雖然工總確認了15%稅率的相對優勢，但對於美方是否會進一步針對特定科技產業鏈進行「精準打擊」，以及此項調查對後續臺美供應鏈合作協議的具體影響，目前尚無確切定論。部分市場分析與國際觀察機構指出，美國此次行動與全球經濟體系中對藥廠、能源及高科技供應鏈的政治角力密切相關。
雖然臺灣目前在關稅層面獲得相對寬容的對待，但市場對於美國未來是否會以「結構性產能過剩」為由，要求臺灣企業在美國境內進行更多在地化生產或技術移轉，仍存在高度不確定性。
此項政策變動對臺灣的具體影響，首當其衝的是高科技製造業與出口導向產業。若美方持續深化對製造業結構的審查，臺灣企業將面臨更高的合規成本與政策變動風險。在歐亞物流與能源議題日益緊密的背景下，全球貿易體系正經歷從「效率優先」轉向「安全與韌性優先」的結構性轉變。對於臺灣而言，如何在維持既有貿易優勢的同時，因應美方對於產能過剩的質疑，將成為未來政策制定與企業營運的核心挑戰。
此外，這項調查也可能間接影響臺灣與其他歐亞經濟體的貿易談判籌碼，因為美國的301調查往往具備指標性意義，可能引發其他貿易夥伴跟進審查。
儘管15%的關稅稅率在國際比較中相對較低，但各界應避免將此視為美方對臺貿易政策的「定論」。工總與相關產業專家提醒，美國的301調查程式通常伴隨著長期的談判與審查，這是一場硬仗的開始，而非終點。過度解讀此關稅稅率為「安全過關」恐忽略了美方後續可能採取的非關稅貿易壁壘，例如對供應鏈透明度的要求、環境與勞工標準的強制規範，以及對關鍵技術出口的進一步限制。因此，產業界與政府部門應保持高度警覺，不應僅關注單一稅率數字，而需將視野擴大至全球供應鏈的政治經濟佈局。
後續觀察重點應聚焦於美方後續的政策執行細節，特別是針對「結構性產能過剩」的認定標準是否會隨時間推移而變得更加嚴苛。此外，臺灣政府如何與美方進行後續溝通，以證明臺灣產業結構的合理性，以及是否能透過更緊密的產業合作協議來降低貿易摩擦，將是觀察重點。同時，全球經濟體系中的跨國藥廠政策、能源供應鏈安全，以及歐亞地區基礎設施建設的變動，皆可能與此次301調查產生連鎖反應。後續應持續追蹤美國貿易代表署的公開檔案更新，以及臺灣相關公協會對於產業衝擊的進一步評估報告。
