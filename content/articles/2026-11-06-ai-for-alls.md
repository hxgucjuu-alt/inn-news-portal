  # South Korea Promotes "AI for All" Policy: Conglomerates Including SK Telecom and KT Lead National AI Development
  
  > The South Korean Ministry of Science and ICT has announced the launch of the "AI for All" initiative. Led by three major corporations—SK Telecom, KT, and Kakao—the program aims to drive national AI infrastructure and service accessibility through cross-industry collaboration, thereby strengthening the nation's technological competitiveness in East Asia.
  
  The South Korean government has recently proposed a clear strategy for national digital transformation, with the Ministry of Science and ICT officially launching the "AI for All" artificial intelligence development program. The background of this policy stems from the South Korean government's plan to deeply integrate AI technology into national life and industrial structures to address global technological competition. According to official plans, the program aims to establish a highly competitive AI ecosystem through government guidance and private sector execution.
  
  This move is not only a key deployment for South Korea to enhance its technological autonomy within the East Asian region, but it is also seen as a core driving force for accelerating the country's digital economic transformation in the post-pandemic era, demonstrating the South Korean government's determination to pursue technological leadership and widespread social application in the field of artificial intelligence.
  
  According to cross-referenced sources, the South Korean government officially selected three major conglomerates in late August to execute the program. These three groups are led by South Korea's two major telecommunications operators, SK Telecom and KT, and the communication software giant Kakao. These companies will be responsible for managing and developing relevant AI services to ensure that the technology can be effectively implemented in public and commercial sectors. This collaborative model shows that the South Korean government intends to leverage existing telecommunications and network infrastructure advantages, utilizing the technical capabilities of large enterprises to rapidly expand the scale of AI applications.
  
  Media reports from various sources have consistently confirmed that the core goal of this program is to break down technical barriers, making artificial intelligence services more accessible to the general public, and thereby enhancing South Korea's influence in the global technology supply chain.
  
  Although the general direction of the policy has been set, information from various media sources regarding the specific execution details and long-term budget allocation for the "AI for All" program remains inconsistent. Some reports focus on the division of responsibilities among the three major companies, but the government has yet to release a complete and unified technical white paper regarding specific subsidy amounts, the formulation of technical standards, and how to ensure data privacy and information security. Furthermore, market observers and related reports hold differing views on whether this program will crowd out existing small and medium-sized enterprises or startups in South Korea.
  
  It remains to be seen whether these three major conglomerates will face challenges such as incompatible technical standards or market monopolies while integrating their respective ecosystems; these uncertainties could affect the final effectiveness of the program.
  
  This policy will have a profound impact on the technology industry chain in the East Asian region. First, by strengthening its AI infrastructure, South Korea will further intensify competition in the digital economy with neighboring countries such as Japan and China. For the industry, the AI development model led by telecom giants will help accelerate the integration of 5G and AI technologies, creating massive market demand for related hardware devices and software services. Additionally, the South Korean government's policy orientation may prompt other countries in the region to follow suit, driving competition and cooperation in AI policies among nations.
  
  If South Korea can successfully establish a standardized "AI for All" service model, it is highly likely that this model will be exported to Southeast Asia or other emerging markets in the future, thereby reshaping East Asia's influence in international digital governance.
  
  However, academia and the industry have also raised different perspectives, warning that the immediate effectiveness of this program should not be over-interpreted. First, the development of artificial intelligence technology is extremely dependent on top-tier talent and computing resources; whether the integration by large telecom operators and internet giants alone is sufficient to cope with intense international technological competition remains uncertain. Second, although the policy goal emphasizes "AI for All," if it lacks a sound legal framework and ethical standards, the issues of social equity and the digital divide that may arise during the popularization of artificial intelligence will become challenges that the government must face.
  
  Furthermore, fluctuations in international geopolitics, particularly issues regarding technology export controls and supply chain security, are also limiting factors that a technology powerhouse like South Korea, which is highly dependent on international markets, must carefully address.
  
  Subsequent observations will focus on the specific technical roadmaps to be announced by the three major conglomerates within the next quarter. The market will closely monitor how the South Korean government allocates resources to balance the interests between large enterprises and the startup ecosystem, and will examine its specific actions in handling data privacy and artificial intelligence ethics. Additionally, whether the program aligns with international technology standards during implementation or adopts a closed national technology path will be key to determining whether this South Korean policy can achieve long-term advantages in the international market.
  
  Various sectors will continue to monitor the progress of SK Telecom, KT, and Kakao in technical integration, as well as the actual penetration rate and public acceptance of these services in the South Korean domestic market; these indicators will become the core basis for evaluating the success of the "AI for All" program.

# 南韓推動「AI for All」政策：SK電訊與KT等財團主導國家級人工智慧發展

> 南韓科學技術情報通訊部宣佈啟動「AI for All」計畫，由SK電訊、KT及Kakao三大企業領軍，透過跨產業合作推動國家人工智慧基礎建設與服務普及化，強化東亞科技競爭力。


南韓政府近期針對國家數位轉型提出明確戰略，由科學技術情報通訊部（Ministry of Science and ICT）正式啟動「AI for All」人工智慧發展計畫。此政策背景源於南韓政府為應對全球科技競爭，計畫將人工智慧技術深度整合至國民生活與產業結構中。根據官方規劃，該計畫旨在透過政府引導、民間企業執行的方式，建立具備高度競爭力的AI生態系統。
此舉不僅是南韓在東亞區域內提升科技自主權的關鍵部署，亦被視為該國在後疫情時代加速數位經濟轉型的核心驅動力，展現出南韓政府在人工智慧領域追求技術領先與社會廣泛應用的決心。
根據多方來源交叉比對，南韓政府已於八月下旬正式選定三個主要財團負責執行該計畫。這三個財團分別由南韓兩大電信運營商SK電訊（SK Telecom）與KT，以及通訊軟體龍頭Kakao主導。這些企業將負責管理並開發相關的AI服務，確保技術能有效落地於公共與商業領域。此項合作模式顯示出南韓政府傾向利用既有的電信與網路基礎設施優勢，透過大型企業的技術能量，快速擴充套件AI應用規模。
各方媒體報導一致確認，此計畫的核心目標在於打破技術門檻，讓人工智慧服務能更普及地惠及一般大眾，並藉此提升南韓在全球科技供應鏈中的話語權。
儘管政策大方向已定，但關於「AI for All」計畫的具體執行細節與長期預算分配，目前各媒體來源提供的資訊仍存在差異。部分報導聚焦於三大企業在計畫中的分工權責，但對於政府投入的具體補貼金額、技術標準的制定細節，以及如何保障資料隱私與資訊安全等議題，官方尚未釋出完整且統一的技術白皮書。此外，針對這項計畫是否會對南韓既有的中小企業或新創公司造成排擠效應，目前市場觀察家與相關報導亦持有不同看法。
尚待確認之處在於，這三大財團在整合各自既有生態系時，是否會面臨技術標準不相容或市場壟斷的挑戰，這些不確定因素皆可能影響計畫的最終成效。
此項政策對東亞區域的科技產業鏈將產生深遠影響。首先，南韓透過強化人工智慧基礎設施，將進一步加劇與周邊國家（如日本與中國）在數位經濟領域的競爭。對於產業而言，由電信巨頭領軍的AI發展模式，將有助於加速5G與AI技術的融合，為相關硬體裝置與軟體服務創造龐大市場需求。此外，南韓政府的政策導向亦可能引發區域內其他國家效仿，推動各國政府在AI政策上的競合關係。
若南韓能成功建立一套標準化的「AI for All」服務模式，未來極可能將此模式輸出至東南亞或其他新興市場，進而重塑東亞在國際數位治理中的影響力。
然而，學界與產業界亦提出不同觀點，提醒外界不應過度解讀此計畫的即時成效。首先，人工智慧技術的發展極度依賴頂尖人才與算力資源，單靠大型電信商與網路巨頭的整合是否足以應對國際間的激烈技術競爭，仍存在變數。其次，政策目標雖強調「AI for All」，但若缺乏完善的法律框架與倫理規範，人工智慧在普及過程中可能引發的社會公平與數位落差問題，將成為政府必須面對的挑戰。
此外，國際地緣政治的波動，特別是科技出口管制與供應鏈安全問題，亦是南韓這類高度依賴國際市場的科技強國必須謹慎應對的限制因素。
後續觀察重點將聚焦於三大財團在未來一季內所公佈的具體技術路線圖。市場將密切關注南韓政府如何分配資源，以平衡大型企業與新創生態系之間的利益，並檢視其在處理資料隱私與人工智慧倫理方面的具體作為。此外，該計畫在實施過程中是否會與國際科技標準接軌，或是採取封閉式的國家技術路徑，將是判斷南韓此項政策能否在國際市場取得長期優勢的關鍵。
各界將持續關注SK電訊、KT與Kakao在技術整合上的進展，以及這些服務在南韓國內市場的實際滲透率與公眾接受度，這些指標將成為評估「AI for All」計畫成功與否的核心依據。
