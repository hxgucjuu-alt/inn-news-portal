  # The Global Energy Transition Challenge: AI Data Center Power Bottlenecks and Central Asia’s Geopolitical Energy Landscape
  
  > As the world moves toward net-zero carbon emissions, the expansion of the AI industry faces the challenge of power shortages. Meanwhile, Central Asia, as a critical energy region, is becoming a strategic focus for international diplomacy and climate cooperation, with nations seeking to balance economic development with green transition.
  
  Under the dual pressures of current global climate change and digital transformation, energy supply has become a core issue in international economics and geopolitics. According to World Bank President Ajay Banga at a forum during New York Climate Week in September 2026, emerging markets face severe power supply constraints while promoting the widespread adoption of AI technology. At the same time, Taiwan has actively showcased its strengths in green technology, renewable energy, and climate resilience through international exhibitions and diplomatic channels, calling for participation in the United Nations Framework Convention on Climate Change (UNFCCC) system to play a substantive role in global climate governance.
  
  Central Asia, as a hub of Silk Road civilization, also plays a key role in energy diplomacy and regional stability. German Foreign Minister Annalena Baerbock has previously signaled a deepening of regional cooperation through visits to Kazakhstan and Uzbekistan.
  
  Multiple sources confirm that the stability of energy infrastructure is the key to determining a nation's industrial competitiveness. World Bank President Ajay Banga clearly pointed out that AI data centers require massive computing power and extremely high power stability, which poses a significant barrier to entry for emerging markets with weaker power supply infrastructure. Echoing this, the technical scope showcased at the 2026 "Taiwan International Net-Zero Sustainability Expo," which includes green electricity trading, carbon management, and micro-hydropower solutions, reflects the urgent international demand for enhancing energy resilience.
  
  Furthermore, Central Asian countries such as Kazakhstan, Uzbekistan, and Tajikistan not only demonstrate their geopolitical importance through tourism and cultural exchange, but their energy policies and cross-border cooperation also continue to receive international attention, especially in the context of the global energy transition, where Central Asia's energy resources and infrastructure construction are becoming indicators of regional stability and development.
  
  However, various sources hold different perspectives on the priorities and challenges of energy development. The World Bank emphasizes the "large-scale construction limitations" of data centers in power-deficient regions, arguing that this does not align with the current power supply conditions of many countries. In contrast, Taiwan's industrial and diplomatic discourse emphasizes the potential for "technology transfer" and "green technology," attempting to assist the international community in overcoming the challenges brought by climate change through the export of renewable energy technologies.
  
  Moreover, the political stability and infrastructure construction progress in Central Asia are presented in complex ways across different reports. From the strategic considerations of diplomatic visits to the display of cultural landmarks in tourism, it is evident that the region is viewed internationally as both a potential partner for energy cooperation and a territory facing the dual challenges of geopolitics and infrastructure development.
  
  For Central Asian countries and emerging markets, the contradiction between AI industry development and power supply will directly affect their digitalization processes. Industry analysis indicates that if base-load power and green electricity supply issues cannot be resolved, emerging markets risk being marginalized in the AI race. On the other hand, by participating in international net-zero sustainability exhibitions and diplomatic initiatives, Taiwan is attempting to establish standards in green finance, circular economy, and inspection and certification. This is not only an industrial layout but also a strategic means of participating in global climate governance through technology exports. For Central Asia, how to leverage its energy advantages and secure technology and investment through international diplomacy will be key to its economic transformation over the next decade.
  
  It is worth noting that although green technology is seen as a solution, there are still limitations to the perspectives of various parties. Some experts warn that over-interpreting the universality of a single technology (such as micro-hydropower) may overlook the systemic difficulties of large-scale power grid construction. Additionally, geopolitical risks—such as potential conflicts and energy transport security in the Middle East or Central Asia—are variables that cannot be ignored. For instance, recent tanker attacks serve as a reminder of the vulnerability of the global energy supply chain in extreme environments. Therefore, when evaluating energy transition policies, it is essential to distinguish between "technical feasibility" and "geopolitical reality," avoiding the mistake of viewing a single industrial vision as a panacea for all energy bottlenecks.
  
  Future observation should focus on: first, the balancing strategies of emerging markets between AI power demand and traditional grid improvements; second, the evolution of the role of Central Asian countries in the international energy supply chain, particularly their specific progress in energy transition cooperation with major Eurasian powers; and third, the actual implementation of Taiwan's green industry technologies in international markets, as well as further developments in its participation in the global climate governance system. These indicators will determine the specific path and effectiveness of the global energy transition in the coming years.

# 全球能源轉型挑戰：AI資料中心電力瓶頸與中亞地緣能源佈局

> 隨著全球邁向淨零碳排，AI產業擴張面臨電力匱乏挑戰。與此同時，中亞地區作為能源關鍵地帶，正成為國際外交與氣候合作的戰略焦點，各國正尋求平衡經濟發展與綠色轉型。


在當前全球氣候變遷與數位轉型的雙重壓力下，能源供給已成為國際經濟與地緣政治的核心議題。根據世界銀行總裁班加（Ajay Banga）於2026年9月紐約氣候週的論壇指出，新興市場在推動AI技術普及時，面臨嚴峻的電力供給限制。與此同時，臺灣透過國際展覽與外交管道，積極展現其在綠色技術、再生能源及氣候韌性方面的實力，並呼籲參與聯合國氣候變化綱要公約（UNFCCC）體系，以期在全球氣候治理中發揮實質作用。
中亞地區作為絲路文明的樞紐，亦在能源外交與區域穩定中扮演關鍵角色，德國外交部長貝爾博克曾透過出訪哈薩克與烏茲別克，釋放深化區域合作的政治訊號。
多方來源共同確認，能源基礎設施的穩定性是決定國家產業競爭力的關鍵。世銀總裁班加明確指出，AI資料中心需要龐大的算力與極高的電力穩定度，這對於供電基礎設施較為薄弱的新興市場而言，構成了巨大的落地門檻。與此呼應的是，2026年「臺灣國際淨零永續展」所展出的技術範疇，涵蓋了綠電交易、碳管理及微型水力發電等解決方案，顯示國際間對於提升能源韌性的迫切需求。
此外，中亞國家如哈薩克、烏茲別克及塔吉克，不僅在旅遊與文化交流中展現其地緣重要性，其能源政策與跨境合作亦持續受到國際社會關注，特別是在全球能源轉型背景下，中亞的能源資源與基礎設施建設正成為區域穩定與發展的指標。
然而，各類來源對於能源發展的優先順序與挑戰存在不同視角。世銀強調的是資料中心在電力匱乏地區的「大規模興建限制」，認為這並不符合許多國家眼前的供電條件。相對地，臺灣的產業與外交論述則強調「技術轉移」與「綠色科技」的潛力，試圖透過輸出再生能源技術，協助國際社會克服氣候變遷帶來的挑戰。
此外，中亞地區的政治穩定性與基礎設施建設進度，在不同報導中呈現出複雜的樣貌，從外交訪問的戰略考量，到旅遊觀光中的文化地標展示，顯示該地區在國際視角下，既是能源合作的潛力夥伴，也面臨地緣政治與基礎設施建設的雙重挑戰。
對於中亞國家及新興市場而言，AI產業的發展與電力供給的矛盾，將直接影響其數位化程式。產業分析指出，若無法解決基載電力與綠電供應問題，新興市場恐將在AI競賽中邊緣化。另一方面，臺灣透過參與國際淨零永續展及外交倡議，試圖在綠色金融、迴圈經濟及檢驗認證領域建立標準，這不僅是產業佈局，更是透過技術輸出參與全球氣候治理的戰略手段。對於中亞而言，如何利用其能源優勢，並在國際外交中爭取技術與投資，將是未來十年經濟轉型的關鍵。
值得注意的是，儘管綠色科技被視為解決方案，但各方觀點仍存在限制。部分專家提醒，過度解讀單一技術（如微型水力發電）的普及性，可能忽略了大規模電力網建設的系統性難題。此外，地緣政治風險——如中東或中亞地區的潛在衝突與能源運輸安全——亦是不可忽視的變數。如近期發生的油輪遇襲事件，提醒了全球能源供應鏈在極端環境下的脆弱性。因此，在評估能源轉型政策時，必須區分「技術可行性」與「地緣政治現實」，避免將單一的產業願景視為解決所有能源瓶頸的萬靈丹。
後續觀察重點應聚焦於：第一，新興市場在AI電力需求與傳統電網改善之間的平衡策略；第二，中亞國家在國際能源供應鏈中的角色演變，特別是其與歐亞大國在能源轉型合作上的具體進展；第三，臺灣綠色產業技術在國際市場的實際落地情況，以及其參與全球氣候治理體系的進一步動態。這些指標將決定全球能源轉型在未來數年內的具體路徑與成效。
