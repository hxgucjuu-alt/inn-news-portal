  # Japanese Shipping Giants Implement Pay Raise Retention Plan in Response to Military Threats in the Middle East
  
  > Major Japanese shipping companies have reached an agreement with labor representatives to designate high-risk waters, such as the Strait of Hormuz, as "military operation zones," providing crew members with special allowances that effectively double their wages to address escalating military threats.
  
  The Japanese shipping industry is taking concrete labor-management measures in the face of global geopolitical instability. According to a report by Kyodo News on September 10, 2026, major shipping companies, including Nippon Yusen Kaisha (NYK Line) and Mitsui O.S.K. Lines (MOL), recently reached a consensus with labor representatives to implement a special salary allowance system for crew members navigating high-risk waters such as the Strait of Hormuz, the Persian Gulf, and the Gulf of Oman. This move stems from the persistent military threats and security concerns in the region, which have caused shipping companies to face significant challenges in recruiting and retaining crew members.
  
  To ensure the smooth operation of shipping activities and the personal safety of crew members, shipping operators have decided to use substantial financial compensation as a key means of retaining professional personnel.
  
  According to information cross-verified by multiple media outlets, this salary adjustment plan has been approved by both labor and management. The specific implementation details were negotiated between the shipping companies' affiliated International Seafarers' Labor Association and the International Transport Workers' Federation (ITF), formally designating the Strait of Hormuz, the Persian Gulf, and the Gulf of Oman as "military operation zones." Regarding the salary calculation standard, once a crew member enters the designated waters, the system will immediately issue an allowance equivalent to five days of basic wages; if the crew member's stay in the area exceeds the base period, an additional one-day allowance will be issued for each subsequent day starting from the sixth day.
  
  The implementation of this system effectively brings the income of crew members to a "double pay" level while navigating these waters, and the policy is retroactive, officially taking effect from March 2026.
  
  Although the implementation details of the "double pay" system have been reported by Kyodo News and widely cited, there are still differing views and unresolved questions regarding whether such high-risk allowances will be extended to other potential conflict zones, or whether this will lead to long-term changes in the global shipping cost structure. Some shipping analysts point out that while this move can effectively alleviate short-term crew shortages, it remains a focus of market attention whether more sea areas will be included in such "military operation zones" as global military conflict hotspots shift, thereby increasing the operational burden on shipping companies.
  
  Furthermore, there is currently no clear, specific explanation in public information regarding the adjustment mechanism for this system should the regional situation ease in the future; this part remains subject to further supplementation in subsequent labor-management negotiations.
  
  This policy has a significant impact on the global shipping industry and regional economies. First, for countries dependent on Middle Eastern energy transport, ensuring the stability of routes and the retention of crew members is crucial. Increasing allowances to maintain shipping operations demonstrates that shipping giants are willing to bear higher operational costs in the face of geopolitical risks to avoid supply chain disruptions. Second, this move may also have a demonstration effect on labor conditions in the global shipping industry, prompting shipping companies in other countries to re-evaluate their labor compensation standards for operations in high-risk waters, thereby affecting the allocation of human resource costs and salary levels across the global shipping industry and placing long-term pressure on the financial structures of related industries.
  
  However, interpretations of this policy vary. Some labor rights organizations believe that while this allowance is a reasonable compensation for the life risks faced by crew members, safety issues cannot be measured solely by money, and operators should still invest more resources in improving ship protection and risk-avoidance technology. On the other hand, some economists warn against over-interpreting this as a general wage hike for the global shipping industry, as it is merely a special compensation for specific high-risk areas. If interpreted broadly as a change in the overall industry salary structure, it could lead to misjudgments in shipping cost forecasts.
  
  Additionally, the limitation of this system lies in its high dependence on the stability of the regional situation; once the level of military threat changes drastically, it remains to be seen whether the current compensation standard still has room for flexible adjustments.
  
  Subsequent observations will focus on the following aspects: First, whether other international shipping companies will follow suit with similar "military operation zone" allowance policies, thereby forming global shipping labor standards; second, as the situation in the Middle East evolves, whether the scope of this "double pay" system will expand or shrink, and whether related costs will be passed on to terminal freight rates, thereby affecting global energy and commodity prices; third, whether the negotiation models between labor and management in the face of similar high-risk routes will become more institutionalized to cope with the increasingly complex global security environment. These factors will be important indicators for observing the stability of the global shipping industry and human resource management in the future.

# 日本海運巨頭因應中東軍事威脅，針對荷姆茲海峽航線實施加薪留才計畫

> 日本海運大型企業與勞方達成協議，將荷姆茲海峽等高風險水域列為軍事行動區域，並向船員發放特別津貼，實質薪資達雙倍，以應對日益嚴峻的軍事威脅。


日本海運產業在面對全球地緣政治動盪之際，正採取具體的勞資應對措施。根據日本媒體《共同社》（Kyodo News）於2026年9月10日的報導，日本郵船、商船三井等大型海運公司，近期與勞方代表經過談判後達成共識，針對航行於荷姆茲海峽、波斯灣及阿曼灣等高風險水域的船員，實施一項特別的薪資加給制度。此舉背景源於該地區持續存在的軍事威脅與安全隱憂，導致航運公司在招募與留任船員方面面臨極大挑戰。
為了確保航運作業的順暢與船員的人身安全，海運業者決定透過實質的經濟補償，作為留住專業人力的關鍵手段。
根據多方媒體交叉確認的資訊，這項薪資調整計畫已獲得勞資雙方同意。具體執行細節由海運公司所屬的國際船員勞務協會，與國際運輸勞連（ITF）進行協議，正式將荷姆茲海峽、波斯灣及阿曼灣劃定為「軍事行動區域」。在薪資計算標準方面，船員一旦進入上述指定海域，系統將立即加發相當於基本工資5日份的津貼；若船員在該區域的停留時間超過基本天數，則自第6日起，每多停留一日，便額外加發一日份的津貼。
這項制度的實施，使得船員在該水域航行期間的收入實質上達到「雙薪」水準，且該政策具有回溯效力，自2026年3月起正式生效。
儘管關於「雙薪」制度的實施細節已由《共同社》報導並獲廣泛引用，但目前各界對於此類高風險津貼是否會擴及其他潛在衝突海域，或是否會導致全球海運成本結構的長期改變，仍存在不同看法與尚待釐清之處。部分航運分析師指出，雖然此舉能有效緩解短期內的船員人力缺口，但隨著全球軍事衝突熱點的轉移，未來是否會有更多海域被列入此類「軍事行動區域」，進而加重航運業者的營運負擔，仍是市場關注的焦點。
此外，目前公開資訊中，對於該制度在未來若區域局勢緩和後的調整機制，尚未有明確的具體說明，這部分仍有待後續勞資談判的進一步補充。
這項政策對全球航運產業與區域經濟具有顯著影響。首先，對於依賴中東能源運輸的國家而言，確保航線的穩定與船員的留任至關重要。透過提高津貼來維持航運運作，顯示出海運巨頭在面對地緣政治風險時，願意承擔更高的營運成本，以避免供應鏈中斷。其次，此舉亦可能對全球海運業的勞動條件產生示範效應，促使其他國家的海運公司重新評估其在高風險海域作業的勞工補償標準，進而影響全球航運業的人力成本配置與薪資水平，對相關產業的財務結構造成長遠的壓力。
然而，針對此項政策的解讀，各界觀點不盡相同。部分勞工權益組織認為，這項津貼固然是對船員生命風險的合理補償，但不能僅以金錢衡量安全問題，業者仍應投入更多資源於船隻防護與避險技術的提升。另一方面，部分經濟學家則提醒，不應過度解讀此舉為全球海運業普遍性的加薪潮，因為這僅是針對特定高風險區域的特殊補償，若將其擴大解釋為整體產業薪資結構的變動，可能會導致對航運成本預測的誤判。
此外，這項制度的限制在於其高度依賴區域局勢的穩定性，一旦軍事威脅程度發生劇烈變化，現行的補償標準是否仍具備彈性調整空間，仍有待觀察。
後續觀察重點將聚焦於以下幾個方面：第一，其他國際海運公司是否會跟進類似的「軍事行動區域」津貼政策，進而形成全球性的航運勞動標準；第二，隨著中東局勢的演變，這項「雙薪」制度的適用範圍是否會擴大或縮減，以及相關成本是否會轉嫁至終端運費，進而影響全球能源與物資價格；第三，勞資雙方未來在面對類似高風險航線時的談判模式，是否會更加制度化，以因應日益複雜的全球安全環境。這些因素都將是未來觀察全球海運產業穩定性與人力資源管理的重要指標。
