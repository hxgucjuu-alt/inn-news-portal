  # Global AI Industry Expansion Triggers Debates on Energy Anxiety and Labor Taxation
  
  > As AI technology and hardware demand surge, the world faces challenges in energy supply and labor structure transformation. Bill Gates has advocated for taxing AI labor to sustain social welfare, while Taipower and the international community are adjusting to energy infrastructure needs and geopolitical shifts.
  
  The rapid iteration of artificial intelligence (AI) technology is profoundly altering global industrial structures and social operational models. According to recent market trends and industry analysis, the explosive growth in demand for AI computing power has not only driven a wave of production expansion in semiconductor devices and related hardware manufacturing—such as Qifu Technology’s entry into the emerging stock market at NT$120 per share on October 13, 2026—but has also highlighted the vulnerabilities of energy infrastructure and the labor market. In Europe, the EU is closely monitoring the impact of AI policies on the regional economy, while the recent U.S. withdrawal from the Council of Europe’s anti-corruption body reflects geopolitical shifts in the global political landscape against the backdrop of technological competition.
  
  These events collectively form the complex background of current global technological development, where the balance between technological progress and social carrying capacity is being tested as never before.
  
  Multiple sources confirm that the proliferation of AI and automation technology has become the core driver of industrial upgrading. Bill Gates recently advocated for taxing companies that replace human labor with robots and AI to support social safety nets and invest in worker retraining; this perspective directly addresses the potential tax revenue gap caused by AI replacing human labor. At the same time, energy issues have become a key bottleneck for the development of the AI industry. Taipower Chairman Tseng Wen-sheng pointed out on October 12, 2026, that with the explosion of AI computing power, energy has become the core driver of such power, and future challenges have shifted from simple "power supply volume" to "grid flexibility."
  
  This information indicates that whether regarding labor tax reform or energy supply strategies, major global economies have entered a stage where they must re-plan their industrial and social support mechanisms.
  
  However, there remain differences and uncertainties among sources regarding the specific paths for solutions. Regarding labor taxation, while Bill Gates advocates for establishing a "labor unit" standard and taxing AI tokens, there is currently no consensus on how such policies can be implemented in a globalized competitive environment to prevent capital flight. In the energy sector, although Taipower emphasizes that current power supply is sufficient to meet industrial growth, it has clearly stated that it will not shift the responsibility of power supply to manufacturers regarding issues such as whether the semiconductor industry needs to build its own power plants, reflecting a subtle interaction in responsibility between the public and private sectors.
  
  Furthermore, the attitude of the European region toward handling AI regulation and participation in international organizations is full of variables as the political situation changes. For instance, it remains to be seen whether the U.S. withdrawal from the Council of Europe’s anti-corruption body will affect future cooperation between Europe and the U.S. on technology governance and anti-corruption standards.
  
  The expansion of the AI industry has had a direct impact on regional economies and social policies. On the industrial side, the emerging market listing and expansion of companies like Qifu Technology reflect the high expectations of the capital market for the AI supply chain; on the policy side, Bill Gates' proposal has sparked a broad discussion on a "robot tax," which is directly related to the sustainability of future social welfare systems. For Europe, as a pioneer in global AI regulation, how to balance industrial innovation with labor rights and maintain regional stability against the backdrop of U.S. policy adjustments has become a major issue for EU policymakers. The flexibility of energy supply and grid upgrades have become the physical foundation supporting all the aforementioned developments, directly affecting the status of various countries in international technological competition.
  
  Although the productivity gains brought by AI are evident, there are still significant differences in perspectives among various sectors. Some viewpoints emphasize that AI will create new jobs and argue that imposing a robot tax could stifle technological innovation, leading to a decline in corporate competitiveness; another perspective worries that technological change is too rapid, and if the social safety net is not supported by sufficient tax revenue, it will trigger severe wealth inequality and social unrest. In addition, regarding the path of energy transition, countries have different considerations for green energy development and traditional energy allocation. These limitations and differences remind us not to over-interpret single policy proposals or single industry data, but to evaluate AI development within the multiple contexts of the global economy, politics, and society.
  
  Subsequent observations should focus on three aspects: First, whether governments will propose specific legislative drafts for AI labor taxation and how the business community will react to such tax policies; second, the progress of energy infrastructure upgrades, particularly whether grid resilience and the integration of diverse green energy can keep up with the demands of AI computing centers; and finally, the changes in the interaction between Europe and the U.S. regarding geopolitics and technology governance, especially the chain reaction following the U.S. withdrawal from international organizations, and whether this will affect the formulation and execution of global technology standards. These dynamics will determine the development environment and the level of social stability for the global technology industry in the coming years.

# 全球AI產業擴張引發能源焦慮與勞動稅制辯論

> 隨著AI技術與硬體需求爆發，全球面臨能源供應與勞動結構轉型挑戰。比爾·蓋茲倡議對AI勞動課稅以維繫社福，同時臺電與國際社會正針對能源基礎建設及地緣政治變動進行調整。


人工智慧（AI）技術的快速迭代，正深刻改變全球產業結構與社會運作模式。根據近期市場動態與產業分析，AI算力需求的爆發式成長，不僅帶動了半導體裝置與相關硬體製造的擴產浪潮，如啟賦科技於2026年10月13日以每股120元登入興櫃，亦凸顯出能源基礎設施與勞動市場的脆弱性。在歐洲地區，歐盟正密切關注AI政策對區域經濟的影響，而美國近期退出歐洲反貪腐委員會的舉動，亦反映出全球政治格局在科技競爭背景下的地緣變動。
這些事件共同構成當前全球科技發展的複雜背景，即技術進步與社會承載力之間的平衡正受到前所未有的考驗。
多項來源共同確認，AI與自動化技術的普及已成為推動產業升級的核心動力。比爾·蓋茲（Bill Gates）近期公開倡議，針對以機器人與AI取代人力之企業課稅，以支應社會安全網並投入勞工再培訓，此觀點直指AI取代人力後可能造成的稅收缺口。與此同時，能源問題成為AI產業發展的關鍵瓶頸。臺電董事長曾文生於2026年10月12日指出，隨著AI算力爆發，能源已成為驅動算力的核心，且未來挑戰已從單純的「供電量」轉向「電網彈性」。
這些資訊顯示，無論是勞動稅制改革還是能源供給策略，全球主要經濟體均已進入必須重新規劃產業與社會配套機制的階段。
然而，各來源對於解決方案的具體路徑仍存在差異與不確定性。在勞動稅制方面，比爾·蓋茲主張建立「勞動單位」標準並對AI Token課稅，但此類政策如何在全球化競爭中落實，避免資本外流，目前尚無定論。在能源領域，雖然臺電強調現階段供電足以因應產業成長，但對於半導體產業是否需自建電廠等議題，臺電明確表示不會將供電責任推給廠商，顯示出公部門與私部門在責任歸屬上的微妙互動。
此外，歐洲地區在處理AI監管與國際組織參與上的態度，亦隨著政治局勢變動而充滿變數，如美國退出歐洲反貪腐委員會的事件，是否會影響未來歐美在科技治理與反貪腐標準上的合作，仍有待觀察。
AI產業的擴張對區域經濟與社會政策產生了直接影響。在產業面，啟賦科技等企業的興櫃與擴產，反映出資本市場對AI供應鏈的高度期待；在政策面，比爾·蓋茲的倡議引發了關於「機器人稅」的廣泛討論，這直接關係到未來社會福利制度的永續性。對歐洲而言，作為全球AI監管的先行者，如何平衡產業創新與勞工權益，並在美國政策調整的背景下維持區域穩定，成為歐盟政策制定者的重大課題。能源供應的彈性化與電網升級，則成為支撐上述所有發展的物理基礎，直接影響到各國在國際科技競爭中的地位。
儘管AI帶來的生產力提升顯而易見，但各界觀點仍存在顯著分歧。部分觀點強調AI將創造新職位，認為課徵機器人稅可能抑制技術創新，導致企業競爭力下降；另一觀點則擔憂技術變革過快，社會安全網若無足夠稅收支撐，將引發嚴重的貧富差距與社會動盪。此外，對於能源轉型的路徑，各國在綠能發展與傳統能源配置上亦有不同考量。這些限制與分歧提醒我們，不應過度解讀單一政策建議或單一產業資料，必須將AI發展置於全球經濟、政治與社會的多重脈絡中進行評估。
後續觀察重點應聚焦於三個面向：首先是各國政府是否會針對AI勞動稅制提出具體立法草案，以及企業界對此類稅收政策的反應；其次是能源基礎設施的升級進度，特別是電網韌性與多元綠能整合是否能跟上AI算力中心的需求；最後是歐美在地緣政治與科技治理上的互動變化，特別是美國退出國際組織後的連鎖反應，是否會影響全球科技標準的制定與執行。這些動態將決定未來幾年全球科技產業的發展環境與社會穩定程度。
