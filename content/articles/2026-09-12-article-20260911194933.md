  # Overview of African Geographical Regionalization and Country Distribution: A Systematic Analysis of the Five Major Regions
  
  > This article organizes the standards for dividing Africa into geographical regions, categorizing them into five major blocks—North Africa, West Africa, East Africa, Central Africa, and Southern Africa—to outline the countries and geographical features of each, providing a foundational framework for regional governance and industrial analysis on the African continent.
  
  As the second-largest continent in the world, the division of Africa into geographical regions is crucial for understanding transnational policies, regional economic cooperation, and social development. Based on geographical orientation and climatic characteristics, Africa is typically divided into five major regions: North Africa, West Africa, East Africa, Central Africa, and Southern Africa. This classification is not only based on latitude and longitude but is also closely related to the geographical barrier of the Sahara Desert, the coastline of the Gulf of Guinea, and the formation of political and economic alliances. Through cross-referencing public data from sources such as Wikipedia, Sohu, and KKNews, the composition of countries in each region and their geographical positioning within the African continent can be clearly defined, providing a standardized geographical background for subsequent regional research.
  
  Comprehensive data from various geographical and statistical sources indicate that the division of Africa into five major regions has become a consensus among international organizations and academia. The North Africa region mainly covers countries from north of the Sahara Desert to the Mediterranean coast, including seven political entities: Egypt, Libya, Tunisia, Algeria, Morocco, Sudan, and Western Sahara. West Africa is concentrated on the northern shore of the Gulf of Guinea, bordering the Atlantic Ocean to the south and the Sahara Desert to the north, encompassing sixteen countries: Nigeria, Benin, Ghana, Togo, Ivory Coast, Liberia, Sierra Leone, Guinea, Guinea-Bissau, Senegal, The Gambia, Mauritania, Mali, Niger, Cape Verde, and Burkina Faso.
  
  These regional divisions are not merely geographical terms; they reflect significant differences in climate, culture, and economic development paths among the countries.
  
  However, there are still subtle classification differences between various sources regarding the specific definition of country affiliation, especially concerning countries that span multiple regions. For example, Sudan is classified as North Africa in some geographical literature, but because its geographical location lies at the intersection of North Africa and East Africa, its role may overlap in the classifications of certain economic cooperation organizations. Furthermore, there are discrepancies in the statistical standards of different institutions regarding the specific boundaries of "Central Africa" and "East Africa." This mainly stems from the varying degrees of participation by countries in regional economic communities (such as the East African Community or the Economic Community of Central African States), which may lead to adjustments in country affiliation based on policy needs in non-geographical statistics.
  
  The division of geographical regions has a direct impact on the industrial layout and public policy formulation of African countries. North African countries benefit from the Mediterranean trade circle, and their agricultural and energy development paths are vastly different from the tropical rainforest and coastal trade systems of West Africa. As one of the most densely populated regions in Africa, West Africa's agricultural development is closely linked to the climatic conditions of the Gulf of Guinea. With Nigeria as the economic leader of the region, its policy direction has a profound impact on the entire Economic Community of West African States (ECOWAS). This regional division helps international organizations allocate resources more precisely according to the climatic and geographical constraints of each region when promoting education, healthcare, and infrastructure construction.
  
  Although geographical regionalization provides a clear analytical framework, one must be wary of the risks brought by oversimplification. The social, cultural, and historical context of the African continent is extremely complex; classifying solely by geographical orientation can easily overlook the influence of transnational ethnic groups, historical colonial backgrounds, and cross-border economic flows. For instance, the Sahara Desert is not an absolute barrier; historically, trans-Saharan trade routes have always connected North Africa and West Africa. Therefore, when interpreting relevant policies, regional boundaries should not be viewed as rigid political barriers, but rather as dynamic references for understanding regional cooperation and conflict, avoiding stereotypes about the development potential of specific regions based on geographical labels.
  
  Subsequent observation should focus on the integration processes of Africa's various Regional Economic Communities (RECs) and the differential impacts of climate change on each geographical region. With the promotion of the "African Continental Free Trade Area" (AfCFTA), the interaction between traditional geographical regionalization and modern economic integration will become more frequent. Future attention should be paid to the progress of cooperation among countries in cross-regional infrastructure construction (such as the Trans-African Highway network) and how each region can seek resource complementarity through geographical advantages in issues such as energy transition and food security. This will be a key indicator for measuring the development effectiveness of Africa over the next decade.

# 非洲地理區域劃分與國家分佈概況：五大區域系統性解析

> 本文整理非洲地理區域劃分標準，依據北非、西非、東非、中非與南非五大區塊，梳理各區域涵蓋國家與地理特徵，提供非洲大陸區域治理與產業分析的基礎框架。


非洲大陸作為全球面積第二大的洲，其地理區域的劃分對於理解跨國政策、區域經濟合作及社會發展至關重要。根據地理方位與氣候特徵，非洲通常被劃分為北非、西非、東非、中非與南非五大區域。這種劃分方式不僅基於經緯度，更與撒哈拉沙漠的地理屏障、幾內亞灣的海岸線以及政治經濟聯盟的形成息息相關。透過維基百科、搜狐與每日頭條等公開資料的交叉比對，可以明確各區域的國家組成及其在非洲大陸中的地理定位，為後續的區域研究提供標準化的地理背景。
綜合多項地理與統計來源顯示，非洲五大區域的劃分已成為國際組織與學術界共識。北非區域主要涵蓋撒哈拉沙漠以北至地中海沿岸的國家，包括埃及、利比亞、突尼西亞、阿爾及利亞、摩洛哥、蘇丹以及西撒哈拉等七個政治實體。西非則集中於幾內亞灣北岸，南臨大西洋，北接撒哈拉沙漠，涵蓋奈及利亞、貝南、迦納、多哥、象牙海岸、賴比瑞亞、獅子山、幾內亞、幾內亞比索、塞內加爾、甘比亞、茅利塔尼亞、馬裡、尼日、維德角與布吉納法索等十六個國家。
這些區域的劃分不僅是地理名詞，更反映了各國在氣候、文化與經濟發展路徑上的顯著差異。
然而，在具體國家歸屬的界定上，不同來源之間仍存在細微的分類差異，特別是在部分跨區域國家的歸類問題上。例如，蘇丹在部分地理文獻中被歸入北非，但由於其地理位置處於北非與東非的交界地帶，在某些經濟合作組織的劃分中，其角色定位可能出現重疊。此外，對於「中非」與「東非」的具體邊界，不同機構的統計標準亦有出入，這主要源於各國對於區域經濟共同體（如東非共同體、中部非洲國家經濟共同體）的參與程度不同，導致在非地理性質的統計中，國家歸屬可能隨政策需求而有所調整。
地理區域的劃分對於非洲各國的產業佈局與公共政策制定具有直接影響。北非國家受惠於地中海貿易圈，其農業與能源產業發展路徑與西非的熱帶雨林及沿海貿易體系截然不同。西非地區作為非洲人口最密集的區域之一，其農業發展與幾內亞灣的氣候條件緊密相連，奈及利亞作為該區經濟領頭羊，其政策走向對整個西非經濟共同體具有深遠影響。這種區域劃分有助於國際組織在推動教育、醫療衛生及基礎設施建設時，能更精準地針對各區域的氣候與地理限制進行資源分配。
儘管地理區域劃分提供了清晰的分析框架，但必須警惕過度簡化所帶來的風險。非洲大陸的社會文化與歷史脈絡極為複雜，單純以地理方位進行歸類，容易忽略跨國族群、歷史殖民背景以及跨境經濟流動的影響。例如，撒哈拉沙漠並非絕對的隔離帶，歷史上跨撒哈拉的貿易路線始終連結著北非與西非。因此，在解讀相關政策時，不應將區域邊界視為僵化的政治隔閡，而應視為理解區域合作與衝突的動態參考，避免因地理標籤而對特定區域的發展潛力產生刻板印象。
後續觀察重點應聚焦於非洲各區域經濟共同體（RECs）的整合程式，以及氣候變遷對各地理區域帶來的差異性衝擊。隨著「非洲大陸自由貿易區」（AfCFTA）的推動，傳統地理區域劃分與現代經濟整合之間的互動將更為頻繁。未來需持續關注各國在跨區域基礎設施建設（如泛非公路網）的合作進展，以及各區域如何透過地理優勢，在能源轉型與糧食安全議題上尋求區域內的資源互補，這將是衡量非洲未來十年發展效能的關鍵指標。
