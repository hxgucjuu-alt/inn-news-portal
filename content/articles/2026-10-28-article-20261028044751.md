  # AI Economic Forecasts and Technical Security Risks: From Macroeconomic Growth to Micro-Privacy Threats
  
  > Anthropic’s founder predicts that AI will drive the U.S. economy to $44 trillion by 2030, while academia has uncovered a Wi-Fi beamforming vulnerability, illustrating that while AI and wireless technologies bring immense economic dividends and convenience, they also carry severe cybersecurity and privacy protection challenges.
  
  The evolution of artificial intelligence technology is at a critical turning point, not only reshaping the global economic landscape but also exerting a profound impact on infrastructure security and the software development community. According to perspectives shared by Anthropic co-founder Jack Clark at the International Monetary Fund (IMF) and World Bank annual meetings held in Bangkok in October 2026, the rapid advancement of AI capabilities will become the core driver of transformation across various fields. Simultaneously, academia and the technical community have recently issued warnings regarding privacy vulnerabilities in wireless communications and the impact of Large Language Models (LLMs) on open-source culture.
  
  These events collectively outline the complex challenges currently facing North American and global technology policy and public safety: how to effectively manage the risks of technological loss of control while pursuing economic growth.
  
  On the economic front, internal models at Anthropic suggest that, under specific conditions, artificial intelligence has the potential to push U.S. economic output to $44 trillion by 2030, an increase of nearly 50% from the current level of $30 trillion. Clark emphasized that scientific breakthroughs driven by AI will be the primary engine of economic growth, provided that global policymakers establish robust safety frameworks. In the realm of cybersecurity, research findings titled "BFId: Identity Inference Attacks Using Beamforming Feedback Information," presented by a German research team at the ACM Conference on Computer and Communications Security (ACM CCS) in Taipei, have exposed privacy vulnerabilities in Wi-Fi protocols.
  
  The study points out that attackers can extract "radio fingerprints" by intercepting beamforming feedback information, allowing for precise identification and tracking of specific individuals even in the absence of cameras. This discovery directly impacts the security architecture of wireless networks in public spaces.
  
  However, there are significant cognitive differences and conflicts regarding technological development across various fields. Within the software development and hacker communities, the proliferation of LLMs has triggered a serious crisis of trust. Renowned hacker Andy Nguyen (TheFlow) announced in September 2026 his withdrawal from the PS5 Linux development project and the cessation of support plans for the PS5 Pro. He publicly criticized the inferior code produced by LLMs for polluting the technical community, noting that some newcomers use AI tools to discover vulnerabilities and then privately claim bounties from vendors rather than following the traditional spirit of open-source sharing. This behavior has led to the collapse of community trust.
  
  This phenomenon stands in contrast to the "economic growth theory" proposed by Anthropic, suggesting that while AI enhances productivity, it may hinder long-term innovation in specific fields by undermining existing collaborative ethics and technical standards.
  
  These technological trends have a direct impact on North American industrial policy and social security. First, the expected growth in economic scale will prompt the U.S. government to increase investment in AI infrastructure, but the accompanying warnings regarding the shrinking "human control window" will force regulators to accelerate the formulation of AI safety laws. Second, the discovery of Wi-Fi vulnerabilities indicates that existing privacy protection regulations (such as GDPR or privacy laws in various U.S. states) may no longer cover the privacy infringement risks posed by the radio frequency spectrum, which will prompt the communications industry to re-examine the standardization specifications for beamforming technology.
  
  Finally, the turmoil in the hacker community serves as a reminder to the industry that over-reliance on AI-assisted coding may lead to the accumulation of technical debt and weaken the resilience of the software ecosystem.
  
  Despite the grand economic vision brought by AI, opinions on its side effects remain divided. Some argue that the productivity gains from AI are sufficient to offset the pains of the technological transition period; however, another camp worries that without strict auditing of LLM-generated content and mandatory protection of wireless communication privacy, society will face the dual threats of "invisible surveillance" and "stagnation in technological development." It is worth noting that current discussions regarding AI control are mostly focused on theoretical levels, and there remains a lack of global consensus on how to concretely implement "safety regulation."
  
  Furthermore, while the withdrawal of the hacker community is indicative, not all obstacles to technological progress should be blamed entirely on AI tools; this also involves structural contradictions between open-source culture and commercial interests.
  
  Subsequent observations should focus on: first, whether the U.S. government will propose specific "safety regulatory indicators" in response to Anthropic's warnings regarding AI economic growth targets; second, whether the Wi-Fi industry will mandate updates to wireless communication protocols to eliminate beamforming vulnerabilities in response to the "BFId" research results; and third, whether the technical community will establish new verification mechanisms or screening standards to repair the damaged development trust chain when facing the code quality and ethical challenges brought by LLMs. These developments will determine the balance between pursuing innovation and maintaining social security in the North American technology industry over the next few years.

# AI經濟預測與技術安全風險：從宏觀成長到微觀隱私威脅

> Anthropic創辦人預測AI將推動美國經濟於2030年達44兆美元，同時學界揭露Wi-Fi波束成形漏洞，顯示AI與無線技術在帶來巨大經濟紅利與便利的同時，亦伴隨嚴峻的資安與隱私防護挑戰。


人工智慧技術的演進正處於關鍵轉折點，不僅重塑全球經濟格局，亦對基礎設施安全與軟體開發社群造成深遠影響。根據Anthropic共同創辦人Jack Clark於2026年10月在曼谷舉行的國際貨幣基金（IMF）與世界銀行年會上提出的觀點，AI能力的迅速提升將成為推動各領域變革的核心動力。與此同時，學術界與技術社群近期亦針對無線通訊的隱私漏洞及大型語言模型（LLM）對開源文化造成的衝擊提出警示。
這些事件共同勾勒出當前北美及全球科技政策與公共安全所面臨的複雜挑戰，即如何在追求經濟成長的同時，有效管理技術失控的風險。
在經濟層面，Anthropic內部模型預測顯示，在特定條件下，人工智慧有望在2030年將美國經濟產出推升至44兆美元，較目前的30兆美元水準成長近50%。Clark強調，AI帶來的科學突破將是經濟成長的主要引擎，但前提是全球決策者必須建立完善的安全框架。而在資安領域，德國研究團隊在臺北ACM計算機與通訊安全會議（ACM CCS）發表的研究成果《BFId：利用波束成形反饋資訊的身份推斷攻擊》揭露了Wi-Fi協議的隱私漏洞。
該研究指出，攻擊者可透過側錄波束成形反饋資訊擷取「無線電指紋」，即便在沒有攝影機的情況下，也能對特定人員進行精準的身分辨識與追蹤，此發現直接衝擊了公共空間的無線網路安全架構。
然而，各領域的技術發展亦存在顯著的認知差異與衝突。在軟體開發與駭客社群中，LLM的普及已引發嚴重的信任危機。知名駭客Andy Nguyen（TheFlow）於2026年9月宣佈退出PS5 Linux開發專案，並中止對PS5 Pro的支援計畫。他公開批評LLM產出的劣質程式碼汙染了技術社群，且部分新進者利用AI工具挖掘漏洞後，私下向廠商領取賞金，而非遵循傳統的開源共享精神，這種行為導致了社群信任的崩解。
此現象與Anthropic提出的「經濟成長論」形成對比，顯示AI在提升生產力的同時，可能因破壞既有的協作倫理與技術標準，進而阻礙特定領域的長期創新發展。
這些技術趨勢對北美產業政策與社會安全具有直接影響。首先，經濟規模的預期成長將促使美國政府加大對AI基礎設施的投資，但隨之而來的「人類掌控視窗」縮減警告，將迫使監管機構加速制定AI安全法規。其次，Wi-Fi漏洞的發現顯示，現有的隱私保護法規（如GDPR或美國各州的隱私權法）可能已無法涵蓋無線電頻譜帶來的隱私侵害風險，這將促使通訊產業重新審視波束成形技術的標準化規範。
最後，駭客社群的動盪則提醒產業界，過度依賴AI輔助編碼可能導致技術債務的累積，並削弱軟體生態系統的韌性。
儘管AI帶來的經濟願景宏大，但各界對其副作用的看法仍存在分歧。部分觀點認為，AI帶來的生產力提升足以抵銷技術轉型期的陣痛；然而，另一派觀點則擔憂，若缺乏對LLM產出內容的嚴格審核，以及對無線通訊隱私的強制保護，社會將面臨「隱形監控」與「技術開發停滯」的雙重威脅。值得注意的是，目前關於AI控制權的討論多集中於理論層面，對於如何具體執行「安全監管」仍缺乏全球共識。
此外，駭客社群的退出事件雖具指標性，但亦不應將所有技術進步的阻礙全數歸咎於AI工具，這其中亦涉及開源文化與商業利益之間的結構性矛盾。
後續觀察重點應聚焦於：第一，美國政府是否會針對AI經濟成長目標，提出具體的「安全監管指標」以回應Anthropic的警告；第二，Wi-Fi產業是否會因應《BFId》研究結果，強制更新無線通訊協定以消除波束成形漏洞；第三，技術社群在面對LLM帶來的程式碼品質與倫理挑戰時，是否會建立新的驗證機制或篩選標準，以修復受損的開發信任鏈。這些發展將決定未來幾年北美科技產業在追求創新與維持社會安全之間的平衡點。
