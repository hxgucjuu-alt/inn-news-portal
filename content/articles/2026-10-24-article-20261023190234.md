  # Global Supply Chain Restructuring: Surge in Taiwan’s Investment in the U.S. and the Shift in Industrial Layout
  
  > Driven by geopolitical factors and the restructuring of global supply chains, Taiwan’s investment in the U.S. has climbed to $30.5 billion over the past five years, marking a growth rate of 284.1%. The Ministry of Economic Affairs has confirmed plans to establish its first industrial park in Texas, signaling a shift of Taiwanese capital from mainland China to the North American market.
  
  The global economic landscape is undergoing a significant structural adjustment, with geopolitical volatility and U.S.-China trade frictions serving as key catalysts for supply chain restructuring. According to the latest statistics from the Ministry of Economic Affairs, the overseas deployment strategies of Taiwanese firms have undergone a structural shift, with capital flows moving away from the historical concentration in mainland China toward the North American market, with the U.S. at its core. This trend not only reflects the emphasis enterprises place on supply chain resilience but also embodies the strategic movement of Taiwan’s semiconductor and server industries within the international division of labor.
  
  Minister of Economic Affairs Kung Ming-hsin recently confirmed that Taiwan will establish its first industrial park in Texas, USA. This move marks a new phase of substantive implementation for Taiwan-U.S. industrial cooperation and is expected to further strengthen bilateral ties within critical technology supply chains.
  
  According to data provided by the Ministry of Economic Affairs, Taiwan’s investment in the U.S. has soared to $30.5 billion over the past five years, representing a growth of 284.1% compared to the previous period. This data indicates that the U.S. share of Taiwan’s overseas investment layout has risen to 20.5%, significantly surpassing the ASEAN market and leading investment in mainland China by a wide margin. In fact, Taiwan’s investment in mainland China has shrunk substantially during the same period, with some statistics suggesting a decline of nearly 50%. This shifting capital flow clearly outlines the strategic adjustments made by Taiwanese enterprises to actively diversify risks and move closer to end markets in the face of changing global trade rules.
  
  As the leaders of this investment wave, the semiconductor and server industries’ reallocation of capital has directly influenced the long-term layout of Taiwan’s foreign trade and industrial competitiveness.
  
  Although official data clearly shows a shift in investment trends, some information regarding the operational details of various industries after landing in North America, as well as the investment attraction progress and supporting policies for the Texas park, remains to be disclosed. While sources show high consistency regarding investment growth data, media reports have focused on macroeconomic data regarding the "great capital migration." There has yet to be a comprehensive cross-evaluation of the long-term operational challenges faced by individual companies investing in the U.S., such as labor costs, tax compliance, and supply chain localization integration.
  
  Furthermore, while the investment figures are clear, the substantial output growth and local employment contributions these investments may bring over the next five years remain in the realm of industrial forecasting rather than realized economic outcomes.
  
  This investment trend will have a profound impact on the industrial ecosystem in North America, particularly in states like Texas that are actively courting semiconductor and high-tech manufacturing, which is expected to drive local infrastructure upgrades and cluster effects for upstream and downstream supply chains. For Taiwan, this is not merely an export of capital, but a transnational transplantation of industrial technology and management experience. However, this large-scale movement of capital is accompanied by policy risks, including potential changes in future U.S. trade policies, the impact of exchange rate fluctuations on corporate profits, and the cultural and management integration challenges faced after establishing factories in the U.S.
  
  These factors will directly affect the profitability and overall competitiveness of Taiwan’s manufacturing industry overseas, requiring policymakers and corporate decision-makers to strike a balance between pursuing supply chain resilience and maintaining profit margins.
  
  From an economic perspective, this single set of data should not be viewed as a signal of a total withdrawal of Taiwanese industry from other markets, but rather as a necessary adjustment under the "de-risking" strategy of global supply chains. Regarding limitations and unknown information, it is currently unclear whether this investment wave will trigger concerns about the "hollowing out" of Taiwan’s domestic industry, or what the long-term impact on Taiwan’s domestic R&D capacity will be following the supply chain shift. Additionally, the global economic environment remains highly uncertain; factors such as energy costs, inflationary pressures, and differences in subsidy policies for foreign investment across countries may all affect the execution efficiency of subsequent investments.
  
  Over-interpreting a single set of investment data may overlook the complexity of globalized layouts; enterprises must still consider the comprehensive benefits of diverse markets when allocating global resources.
  
  Future observation should focus on three aspects: First, whether the industrial park established by the Ministry of Economic Affairs in Texas can be completed on schedule and attract upstream and downstream supply chain firms; second, observing the industrial structure of Taiwan’s investment in the U.S. to see if it will expand beyond semiconductors and servers to other high-value-added industries; and finally, continuing to track the subsequent development of U.S.-China trade policies and how these policies continue to influence the resource allocation decisions of Taiwanese firms between North America and Asia. With the global trend toward net-zero carbon emissions and the demand for industrial transformation, whether Taiwan’s future investments in North America will incorporate green energy and sustainable supply chain standards will be another key indicator for evaluating the effectiveness of this investment wave.

# 全球供應鏈重組：臺灣對美投資激增與產業佈局轉向

> 受地緣政治與全球供應鏈重組影響，臺灣對美投資規模五年內攀升至305億美元，成長率達284.1%。經濟部證實將於德州設立首個產業園區，顯示臺灣資本正由中國大陸轉向北美市場。


全球經濟版圖正經歷顯著的結構性調整，地緣政治動盪與美中貿易摩擦成為推動供應鏈重組的關鍵催化劑。根據經濟部最新統計資料，臺灣廠商的海外佈局策略已發生結構性轉變，資金流向由過往高度集中於中國大陸，轉向以美國為核心的北美市場。這一趨勢不僅反映了企業對於供應鏈韌性的重視，更體現了臺灣半導體與伺服器產業在國際分工中的戰略性移動。
經濟部長龔明鑫日前公開證實，臺灣將在美國德州設立首個產業園區，此舉標誌著臺美產業合作進入實體落地的新階段，預計將進一步強化雙邊在關鍵科技供應鏈上的連結。
根據經濟部提供的資料，過去五年內，臺灣對美國的投資額飆升至305億美元，與前一期相比，成長幅度高達284.1%。這一資料顯示，美國在臺灣海外投資佈局中的比重已提升至20.5%，不僅大幅超越東協市場，更在投資規模上顯著領先對中國大陸的投資。事實上，同期臺灣對中國大陸的投資呈現大幅萎縮，部分統計顯示已接近「腰斬」程度。此消彼長的資金流向，清晰勾勒出臺灣企業在面對全球貿易規則變動時，採取了積極分散風險、貼近終端市場的策略調整。
半導體與伺服器產業作為此波投資潮的領頭羊，其資本配置的轉移，直接影響了臺灣對外貿易與產業競爭力的長期佈局。
儘管官方資料明確顯示投資趨勢的轉向，但針對各產業在北美具體落地後的營運細節，以及德州園區的招商進度與配套政策，目前仍有部分資訊待進一步公開。來源間對於投資成長的資料一致性極高，但在「資金大遷徙」背後的具體產業鏈細分層面，不同媒體報導側重於宏觀資料的呈現，對於個別企業在美投資的長期營運挑戰，如勞動力成本、稅務合規及供應鏈在地化整合等細節，尚未有全面性的交叉評估。
此外，雖然投資額資料明確，但這些投資在未來五年內能帶來的實質產值增長與在地就業貢獻，仍屬於產業預測範疇，而非已實現的經濟成果。
此波投資趨勢對北美地區的產業生態將產生深遠影響，特別是在德州等積極爭取半導體與高科技製造業進駐的州別，預計將帶動當地基礎設施升級與上下游供應鏈的群聚效應。對於臺灣而言，這不僅是資本的輸出，更是產業技術與管理經驗的跨國移植。然而，這種大規模的資金移動也伴隨著政策風險，包括美國未來貿易政策的潛在變動、匯率波動對企業獲利的影響，以及在美設廠後所面臨的文化與管理整合挑戰。
這些因素都將直接牽動臺灣製造業在海外的獲利能力與整體競爭力，政策制定者與企業決策層需在追求供應鏈韌性與獲利空間之間取得平衡。
從經濟學角度分析，不應將此單一資料視為臺灣產業全面撤離其他市場的訊號，而應解讀為全球供應鏈「去風險化」策略下的必要調整。限制與未知資訊方面，目前尚不清楚這波投資潮是否會引發臺灣本土產業的「空洞化」疑慮，以及供應鏈轉移後，對於臺灣本土研發能量的長遠影響。此外，全球經濟環境仍存在高度不確定性，包括能源成本、通膨壓力及各國針對外資的補貼政策差異，均可能影響後續投資的執行效率。
過度解讀單一投資資料可能忽略了全球化佈局的複雜性，企業在進行全球資源配置時，仍需考量多元市場的綜合效益。
後續觀察重點應聚焦於三個面向：首先，經濟部在德州設立的產業園區是否能如期完工並吸引供應鏈上下游廠商進駐；其次，觀察臺灣對美投資的產業結構，除了半導體與伺服器外，是否會擴散至其他高附加價值產業；最後，需持續追蹤美中貿易政策的後續發展，以及這些政策如何持續影響臺灣企業在北美與亞洲之間的資源配置決策。隨著全球淨零碳排趨勢與產業轉型需求，未來臺灣在北美的投資是否會結合綠色能源與永續供應鏈標準，將是評估此波投資成效的另一項關鍵指標。
