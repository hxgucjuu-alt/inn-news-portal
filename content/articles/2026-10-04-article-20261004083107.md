  # Five Central Asian Nations Promote Digital Market Integration, Aiming to Connect an Economic Zone of 80 Million People
  
  > The five Central Asian nations are drafting plans to establish a single digital market targeting a population of 80 million to foster regional economic integration and digital transformation; meanwhile, issues regarding cross-border tourism and labor mobility remain influenced by geopolitics and public health policies.
  
  Economic development and regional integration in Central Asia have recently become a focus of international attention. The five nations—Kazakhstan, Uzbekistan, Kyrgyzstan, Tajikistan, and Turkmenistan—are actively exploring the feasibility of establishing a single digital market. The goal is to digitally connect a market totaling over 80 million people to enhance trade efficiency and industrial competitiveness within the region. At the same time, Central Asian countries are showing a proactive attitude toward promoting the tourism industry, with nations such as Uzbekistan and Kazakhstan attracting international travelers by promoting the cultural heritage of historic ancient cities like Samarkand and Bukhara.
  
  However, labor mobility and cross-border movement in the region remain significantly affected by the public health policies and border control measures of neighboring major powers such as Russia, presenting a complex situation where visions of economic integration coexist with real-world geopolitical challenges.
  
  According to various media reports, experts and business leaders from the five Central Asian nations have formally proposed a digital integration initiative. The core of this plan lies in breaking down national borders and creating a seamless business environment for 80 million people through unified digital standards and infrastructure. Regarding the tourism industry, market information indicates that travel routes centered on Kazakhstan, Kyrgyzstan, and Uzbekistan are gaining attention from international travel agencies. In particular, in-depth cultural tourism to historic cities like Samarkand has become an important pillar for promoting regional economic diversification. Tourism operators emphasize the provision of high-quality accommodation and guide services in their itinerary planning, demonstrating that Central Asian countries are attempting to enhance their international tourism appeal through infrastructure upgrades.
  
  However, there are significant discrepancies among sources regarding the progress and practical challenges of regional integration. Regarding the integration of the digital market, current efforts remain largely at the stage of expert recommendations and initiatives; specific legal frameworks, cross-border data exchange agreements, and implementation timelines have yet to be clarified. On the issue of labor and personnel mobility, different sources have revealed a grim reality: due to pandemic fluctuations and border controls within Russia, a large number of Central Asian migrant workers are facing predicaments such as being stranded, experiencing financial shortages, and dealing with issues of legal status. This stands in stark contrast to the "openness and exchange" promoted by the tourism industry.
  
  There is currently a lack of consistent official statements regarding when borders will be fully opened, whether digital integration can be successfully implemented, and the priorities of various governments in handling labor rights and regional economic coordination.
  
  The specific impact of Central Asia's push for digital market integration will be reflected in the fields of logistics, fintech, and e-commerce. If successfully integrated, it will significantly lower the threshold for Central Asian enterprises to enter neighboring markets and attract foreign investment for basic digital infrastructure construction. For countries like Uzbekistan, which are committed to cultural heritage protection and tourism transformation, digitalization can not only optimize the travel booking process but also use big data analysis to accurately grasp the needs of international travelers, thereby driving the development of surrounding industrial chains. However, the success of this policy depends heavily on the degree of compromise among countries regarding digital sovereignty and the formulation of regional rules, as well as the ability to effectively resolve technical gaps in cross-border payments and digital identity authentication.
  
  Although the prospects for digital market integration and tourism development are promising, experts warn against over-interpreting current policy declarations. First, the political systems and levels of economic development among Central Asian countries vary greatly, and the progress of digital infrastructure construction is uneven, which will be a major limitation in promoting a single market. Second, personnel mobility within the region is deeply influenced by Russia's economy and policies; if the structural problems of cross-border labor mobility cannot be solved, simple digital integration may have limited help in improving the economic situation of grassroots citizens. Furthermore, the recovery and development of the tourism industry are also highly susceptible to regional security situations and public health emergencies; therefore, long-term investment in related industries should still be evaluated with caution regarding risks.
  
  Subsequent observations should focus on whether the five Central Asian nations can sign binding agreements on digital integration and the progress of coordination among governments on digital trade rules. At the same time, continued attention should be paid to adjustments in border management policies, especially regarding visa facilitation measures for labor mobility and cross-border tourism. In addition, technical assistance from the international community for Central Asia's digital transformation, and how countries balance national security with regional openness while promoting the digital economy, will be key indicators in the coming years to observe whether regional economic integration has substantive significance. As the global digitalization process accelerates, whether the five Central Asian nations can seize the opportunity to translate the market potential of 80 million people into substantive economic growth is worth continuous tracking.

# 中亞五國推動數位市場整合，旨在串聯八千萬人口經濟圈

> 中亞五國正研擬建立單一數位市場，目標涵蓋八千萬人口，以促進區域經濟整合與數位轉型；同時，區域內跨境旅遊與勞工流動議題，仍受地緣政治與公共衛生政策影響。


中亞地區的經濟發展與區域整合近期成為國際關注焦點。哈薩克、烏茲別克、吉爾吉斯、塔吉克及土庫曼等五國，正積極探討建立單一數位市場的可行性，目標是將總計超過八千萬人口的市場進行數位化串聯，以提升區域內的貿易效率與產業競爭力。與此同時，中亞各國在旅遊產業推廣上亦展現積極態度，特別是烏茲別克與哈薩克等國，透過推廣歷史古都如撒馬爾罕（Samarkand）與布哈拉（Bukhara）的文化遺產，吸引國際旅客。
然而，該地區的勞動力流動與跨境人員往來，仍受到俄羅斯等周邊大國的公共衛生政策及邊境管制措施的顯著影響，呈現出經濟整合願景與現實地緣政治挑戰並存的複雜局面。
根據多家媒體報導，中亞五國的專家與企業界人士已正式提出數位整合倡議。這項計畫的核心在於打破國界限制，透過統一的數位標準與基礎設施，為八千萬人口打造一個無縫接軌的商業環境。在旅遊產業方面，市場資訊顯示，以哈薩克、吉爾吉斯與烏茲別克為核心的旅遊路線正受到國際旅行社重視，特別是針對撒馬爾罕等歷史古城的文化深度旅遊，已成為推動該區域經濟多元化的重要支柱。旅遊業者在行程規劃中，強調提供高品質的住宿與導覽服務，顯示出中亞各國正試圖透過基礎建設升級，提升國際觀光吸引力。
然而，資訊來源之間對於區域整合的進展與現實挑戰存在顯著差異。關於數位市場的整合，目前多停留在專家建議與倡議階段，具體的法律框架、跨國資料交換協議及執行時間表尚未明確。在勞工與人員流動議題上，不同來源揭露了嚴峻的現實面：由於俄羅斯境內的疫情波動與邊境管制，大量中亞移工面臨滯留、資金短缺及身分合法性等困境。這與旅遊產業宣傳的「開放與交流」形成強烈對比。
各界對於邊境何時能全面開放、數位整合是否能順利落地，以及各國政府在處理勞工權益與區域經濟協調上的優先順序，目前仍缺乏一致性的官方說法。
中亞推動數位市場整合的具體影響，將體現在物流、金融科技與電子商務領域。若能成功整合，將大幅降低中亞企業進入鄰國市場的門檻，並吸引外資投入基礎數位設施建設。對於烏茲別克等致力於文化遺產保護與觀光轉型的國家而言，數位化不僅能最佳化旅遊預訂流程，還能透過大資料分析精準掌握國際旅客需求，進而帶動周邊產業鏈的發展。然而，這項政策的成功與否，高度依賴於各國在數位主權與區域規則制定上的妥協程度，以及能否有效解決跨境支付與數位身分認證的技術落差。
儘管數位市場整合與旅遊業發展前景看好，但各界專家提醒，不應過度解讀現階段的政策宣示。首先，中亞各國的政治體制與經濟發展水準差異巨大，數位基礎設施的建設進度不一，這將成為推動單一市場的主要限制。其次，區域內的人員流動深受俄羅斯經濟與政策影響，若無法解決勞工跨境流動的結構性問題，單純的數位整合對改善基層民眾經濟處境的幫助恐有限。此外，旅遊業的復甦與發展，亦極易受到區域安全形勢與公共衛生突發事件的幹擾，因此，相關產業的長期投資仍需審慎評估風險。
後續觀察重點應聚焦於中亞五國是否能針對數位整合簽署具約束力的協定，以及各國政府在數位貿易規則上的協調進度。同時，應持續關注各國邊境管理政策的調整，特別是針對勞工流動與跨境旅遊的簽證便利化措施。此外，國際社會對於中亞數位轉型的技術援助，以及各國在推動數位經濟時如何平衡國家安全與區域開放，將是未來幾年觀察該區域經濟整合是否具備實質意義的關鍵指標。隨著全球數位化程式加速，中亞五國能否把握機會，將八千萬人口的市場潛力轉化為實質的經濟成長，值得持續追蹤。
