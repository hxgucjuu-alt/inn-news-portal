  # IPAC Warns of Economic Impact of Taiwan Strait Crisis and Challenges for Japanese Firms in the Indian Market
  
  > The Inter-Parliamentary Alliance on China (IPAC) held its annual summit in The Hague, Netherlands, where participating lawmakers warned of the threat a potential Taiwan Strait crisis poses to the global economy. Simultaneously, Japanese enterprises face significant challenges in their expansion into the Indian market, highlighting the complex interplay between regional expansion and geopolitical risks for East Asian economies.
  
  The Inter-Parliamentary Alliance on China (IPAC) held its annual summit in The Hague, Netherlands, on November 6, 2026, bringing together 52 lawmakers from 30 countries to discuss transnational repression by the Chinese Communist Party and geopolitical risks. At the same time, the East Asian regional economic landscape is undergoing structural adjustments, as Japanese companies encounter varying degrees of success and failure while seeking to diversify their overseas markets in India. These events collectively reflect the multiple pressures and strategic considerations faced by East Asian nations as they address supply chain restructuring, geopolitical tensions, and slowing global economic growth.
  
  During the IPAC summit, Dutch Member of Parliament Jan Paternotte explicitly pointed out that Taiwan occupies a core position in the global semiconductor supply chain, and that international trade is highly dependent on the shipping security of the Taiwan Strait. Paternotte warned that if tensions in the Taiwan Strait were to escalate, the resulting economic crisis could far exceed the impact experienced during the COVID-19 pandemic. This perspective received widespread attention from the attending lawmakers, emphasizing that Taiwan Strait security is not merely a regional issue, but an indispensable component of global economic stability.
  
  Meanwhile, a 2025 survey by the Japan External Trade Organization (JETRO) indicated that 81.5% of Japanese companies have entered the Indian market and plan to expand their business footprint within the next one to two years, reflecting a strategic trend of Japanese firms actively seeking market shifts amid headwinds in the Chinese economy.
  
  However, there are significant differences in how various media outlets analyze these issues, and some information remains to be clarified. Regarding the development of Japanese firms in India, media reports point to a mix of successes and failures; for instance, Suzuki has achieved significant success through 40 years of deep cultivation in India, while companies such as NTT Docomo and Daiichi Sankyo have faced setbacks. Furthermore, regarding the economic outlook for the East Asian region, the World Bank has previously lowered its growth forecasts for developing East Asian countries, primarily attributing this to the slowdown in China's economic growth and shifts in U.S. monetary policy.
  
  These cross-analyses of history and current conditions show that the development of East Asian economies is not only influenced by regional policies but is also deeply constrained by the global macroeconomic environment and geopolitical tensions.
  
  For relevant industries and policies, the stability of the situation in the Taiwan Strait directly affects the continuity of the semiconductor supply chain, which poses a potential systemic risk to the global technology industry. The layout of Japanese firms in the Indian market reflects the practical difficulties of implementing a "China Plus One" strategy; even multinational corporations with mature technology and management experience must undergo long-term adjustments and adaptation when facing India's unique market environment, regulations, and cultural differences. These phenomena indicate that East Asian countries must balance geopolitical risk management with the depth and breadth of localized operations while promoting industrial transformation and market diversification.
  
  Although the IPAC meeting emphasized the severity of a potential Taiwan Strait crisis, there remain differing views regarding the probability of such a crisis, the specific paths of its impact, and the international community's capacity to respond. Some analyses suggest that overemphasizing geopolitical risks may lead to market sentiment volatility, which in turn could affect corporate long-term investment decisions. Furthermore, the success or failure of Japanese firms in the Indian market should not be simplified into a matter of market attractiveness alone; factors such as a company's industrial attributes, timing of entry, local partners, and government policy support are all critical variables influencing business outcomes. Therefore, when interpreting these economic and geopolitical trends, one should avoid oversimplifying cause-and-effect relationships and instead conduct a comprehensive assessment from multiple dimensions.
  
  Future observations will focus on several core areas: First, whether the policy recommendations following the IPAC summit will translate into concrete economic or security commitments toward Taiwan by various countries, and how these actions will affect regional economic exchanges. Second, the subsequent layout of Japanese companies in the Indian market, particularly the review and adjustment of previous failed cases, will serve as an important indicator for observing changes in the operating environment for foreign capital in India. Finally, as the global economic environment changes, how East Asian nations balance their economic and trade ties with China while deepening cooperation with other emerging markets will be a key observation point for regional economic development in the coming years.
  
  Various sectors also need to continue monitoring the dynamic adjustments of global financial institutions' economic growth forecasts for the East Asian region to grasp the pulse of the regional economy.

# IPAC示警臺海危機經濟衝擊與日企印度市場佈局挑戰

> 「對中政策跨國議會聯盟」（IPAC）於荷蘭海牙召開年度峰會，與會議員示警臺海潛在危機對全球經濟之威脅；同時，日企在印度市場的佈局面臨顯著挑戰，顯示東亞經濟體在區域擴張與地緣政治風險間的複雜博弈。


「對中政策跨國議會聯盟」（IPAC）於2026年11月6日在荷蘭海牙召開年度峰會，匯集來自30個國家的52名議員，共同探討中共跨國鎮壓行為及地緣政治風險。與此同時，東亞區域經濟格局正經歷結構性調整，日本企業在尋求海外市場多元化的過程中，於印度市場遭遇不同程度的成敗挑戰。這些事件共同反映出東亞國家在應對供應鏈重組、地緣政治壓力及全球經濟增長放緩等議題時，所面臨的多重壓力與戰略考量。
在IPAC峰會中，荷蘭國會議員帕特諾特（Jan Paternotte）明確指出，臺灣在全球半導體產業鏈中佔據核心地位，且國際貿易高度仰賴臺灣海峽的航運安全。帕特諾特警告，一旦臺海緊張情勢升級，其引發的經濟危機恐將遠超COVID-19疫情期間的衝擊。此觀點獲得與會議員的廣泛關注，強調了臺海安全不僅是區域性議題，更是全球經濟穩定不可或缺的一環。
與此同時，日本貿易振興機構（JETRO）的2025年度調查顯示，81.5%的日本企業已進軍印度市場，並計劃在未來一至兩年內擴大事業版圖，反映出日企在中國經濟面臨逆風之際，積極尋求市場轉移的戰略趨勢。
然而，各媒體對於上述議題的分析重點存在顯著差異，部分資訊尚待進一步釐清。關於日企在印度的發展，媒體指出成功與失敗案例並存，如鈴木汽車（Suzuki）深耕印度40年取得顯著成功，但NTT Docomo與第一三共（Daiichi Sankyo）等企業則面臨失利。此外，針對東亞地區的經濟前景，世界銀行（World Bank）曾針對東亞開發中國家的成長預測提出下修，主要原因歸咎於中國經濟成長放緩以及美國貨幣政策的變動。
這些歷史與現況的交叉分析顯示，東亞經濟體的發展不僅受到區域內政策影響，更深受全球宏觀經濟環境與地緣政治緊張局勢的制約。
對於相關產業與政策而言，臺海局勢的穩定性直接影響半導體供應鏈的連續性，這對全球科技產業構成潛在的系統性風險。日企在印度市場的佈局，則反映了企業在「中國加一」（China Plus One）策略下的實際執行困難，即便是擁有成熟技術與管理經驗的跨國企業，在面對印度獨特的市場環境、法規與文化差異時，仍需經歷長期的調整與適應。這些現象顯示，東亞國家在推動產業轉型與市場多元化的過程中，必須同時兼顧地緣政治風險管理與在地化經營的深度與廣度。
儘管IPAC會議強調了臺海危機的嚴重性，但各界對於危機發生的機率、影響的具體路徑以及國際社會的因應能力仍存在不同觀點。部分分析指出，過度強調地緣政治風險可能導致市場情緒波動，進而影響企業的長期投資決策。此外，日企在印度市場的成敗案例，亦不應被簡化為單一的市場吸引力問題，企業的產業屬性、進入時機、在地合作夥伴以及政府政策支援，皆為影響經營成果的關鍵變數。因此，在解讀這些經濟與地緣政治趨勢時，應避免過度簡化因果關係，需從多維度進行綜合評估。
後續觀察重點將聚焦於幾個核心領域：首先，IPAC峰會後續的政策建議是否會轉化為各國對臺的具體經貿或安全承諾，以及這些行動將如何影響區域內的經貿往來。其次，日本企業在印度市場的後續佈局，特別是針對先前失利案例的檢討與調整，將成為觀察外資在印度營運環境變化的重要指標。最後，隨著全球經濟環境的變動，東亞國家如何平衡與中國的經貿連結，同時深化與其他新興市場的合作，將是未來數年區域經濟發展的關鍵觀察點。
各界亦需持續關注全球金融機構對東亞地區經濟成長預測的動態調整，以掌握區域經濟脈動。
