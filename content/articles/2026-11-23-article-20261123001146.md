  # Taiwan’s Energy Transition and Geopolitical Defense Challenges: A Review of Nuclear Power Controversies and Asymmetric Strategy
  
  > Taiwan is currently facing dual challenges in energy policy and defense strategy. The National Nuclear Abolition Action Platform has questioned whether nuclear power can support the electricity demands of the AI industry, advocating instead for an accelerated development of renewable energy. Simultaneously, the defense sector is conducting in-depth research into the Ukrainian experience, exploring how asymmetric naval warfare strategies—utilizing low-cost unmanned vehicles—can achieve effective deterrence.
  
  Taiwan’s energy policy and national defense security issues have recently become the focus of public discussion. Regarding energy, in response to the No. 22 nuclear energy referendum, the National Nuclear Abolition Action Platform has presented counter-arguments, emphasizing that nuclear power is not a panacea for economic revitalization and questioning its cost-effectiveness and inability to provide immediate relief. At the same time, tensions in the geopolitical environment have prompted a reassessment of Taiwan’s defense strategy, particularly by drawing lessons from Ukraine’s use of low-cost unmanned surface vessels to sink high-value warships in the Black Sea, and exploring whether Taiwan can apply such asymmetric combat models to the defense of the Taiwan Strait to address potential regional military threats.
  
  In terms of energy policy progress, there has been intense debate over whether nuclear power can meet the growing electricity demands of the AI industry. The National Nuclear Abolition Action Platform points out that nuclear power construction cycles are long and costly, making it unable to respond to industrial needs in a timely manner, and potentially crowding out resources for the development of renewable energy. The platform believes that the global energy transition trend clearly points toward renewable energy, and if Taiwan misallocates resources to nuclear power, it risks dragging down the pace of its economic transformation.
  
  Regarding defense strategy, military analysis indicates that the case of Ukraine using unmanned vessels costing approximately $250,000 to sink a warship valued at $70 million has become a key subject of study for Taiwan’s military strategy. The focus lies in disrupting the enemy's pace of aggression and supply lines through the establishment of a resilient defense industry and logistics system, thereby achieving a deterrent effect.
  
  Regarding the aforementioned issues, there are significant differences in perspectives and information gaps across various sources. On energy policy, there is still a lack of consensus between pro-nuclear and anti-nuclear factions regarding whether nuclear energy qualifies as "green energy" and how costs should be calculated; the anti-nuclear side insists that nuclear power cannot meet the electricity demands of the AI era, while the pro-nuclear side emphasizes its function as a stable baseload power source. On the defense front, although Ukraine's unmanned vessel tactics have proven effective in actual combat, military experts remain reserved about whether this strategy can be fully transplanted to the Taiwan Strait.
  
  This is primarily due to the vast differences in the geographical environment of the Taiwan Strait, the complexity of enemy defense systems, and the structure of military forces between the two sides compared to the context of the Black Sea conflict. These factors mean that the argument for "directly copying" the Ukrainian model still requires more rigorous wargaming and verification.
  
  These policy and strategic shifts will have a profound impact on Taiwan’s industrial structure and social development. If the Taiwan government adopts a policy that prioritizes renewable energy, it will directly affect the layout of the energy supply chain and pose challenges to the power stability of energy-intensive AI and semiconductor industries. If the defense strategy shifts toward large-scale investment in unmanned vehicles and asymmetric combat capabilities, it will affect the allocation of the defense budget and the upgrading of the domestic defense industry.
  
  Furthermore, these issues have also received high levels of international attention. For example, the cautious attitude shown by Japan when handling issues regarding the Taiwanese flag at large sporting events reflects the complexity of dealing with sensitive political symbols in East Asia, which has indirectly influenced Taiwan’s space and interaction patterns in international geopolitics.
  
  However, the public should remain cautious when interpreting this information. Debates on energy policy are often intertwined with political stances; the calculation of the economic benefits of nuclear power is highly dependent on assumptions regarding nuclear waste disposal costs and decommissioning timelines, and should not be judged solely by power generation costs. Similarly, the assessment of defense strategy involves highly classified and complex military technologies. While Ukraine's successful experience is of reference value, over-mythologizing the results of unmanned vehicles while ignoring practical variables such as logistics maintenance, command chains, and electronic jamming could lead to strategic misjudgment. Additionally, the caution exercised by countries in the region, such as Japan, when dealing with Taiwan-related issues is largely due to geopolitical balancing considerations and should not be over-interpreted as a total shift in foreign policy.
  
  Looking ahead, there remain many unknowns on Taiwan’s dual paths of energy and defense. In terms of energy, whether Taiwan can meet its renewable energy share targets on schedule while maintaining a stable power supply will be the key to testing the success or failure of its energy policy. In terms of defense, the focus for future observation is whether Taiwan can successfully integrate unmanned vehicle technology into its existing joint combat system, rather than merely procuring the vehicles. Relevant policymakers need to strike a balance between international pressure, industrial demand, and defense budget constraints, while continuing to monitor the policy trends of other countries in the East Asian region to ensure that Taiwan can maintain long-term resilience and competitiveness in a volatile geopolitical environment.

# 臺灣能源轉型與地緣國防挑戰：核電爭議與不對稱戰略分析

> 臺灣近期面臨能源政策與國防戰略的雙重挑戰。全國廢核行動平臺質疑核電無法支撐AI產業用電，主張應加速發展再生能源；同時，國防界正深入研究烏克蘭經驗，探討如何透過不對稱海戰策略，以低成本無人載具達成有效嚇阻。


臺灣能源政策與國防安全議題近期成為公共討論焦點。在能源方面，針對第22案核能公投，全國廢核行動平臺提出反方論點，強調核電並非振興經濟的萬靈丹，並對其成本效益與緩不濟急的特性提出質疑。與此同時，地緣政治環境的緊張局勢，促使臺灣國防戰略重新評估，特別是借鑑烏克蘭在黑海戰場運用低成本無人艇擊沉高價軍艦的戰術，探討臺灣是否能將此類不對稱作戰模式應用於臺海防衛，以應對潛在的區域軍事威脅。
在能源政策的進展上，各界針對核電是否能滿足日益增長的AI產業用電需求進行了激烈辯論。全國廢核行動平臺指出，核電建設週期長且成本高昂，無法即時回應產業需求，反而可能排擠再生能源的發展資源。該平臺認為，全球能源轉型趨勢已明確指向再生能源，臺灣若將資源錯置於核電，恐將拖累經濟轉型腳步。
在國防戰略方面，軍事分析指出，烏克蘭以約25萬美元的無人艇擊沉價值7000萬美元軍艦的案例，已成為臺灣軍事戰略的重點研究物件，重點在於透過建立韌性國防產業與後勤體系，破壞敵方的侵略節奏與補給線，進而達成嚇阻效果。
關於上述議題，各來源資訊存在顯著的觀點差異與資訊落差。在能源政策上，擁核方與反核方對於核能是否屬於「綠能」及成本計算方式仍缺乏共識；反核方堅持核電無法應對AI時代的電力需求，但擁核方則強調其穩定基載電力功能。在國防層面，雖然烏克蘭的無人艇戰術在實戰中獲得成效，但軍事專家對於該戰略是否能完全移植至臺海仍持保留態度。
這主要源於臺海地理環境、敵軍防禦系統的複雜度，以及兩岸軍力結構與黑海衝突背景的巨大差異，這些因素均使得「直接照搬」烏克蘭模式的論點尚待更嚴謹的兵棋推演與驗證。
這些政策與戰略的變動，將對臺灣的產業結構與社會發展產生深遠影響。若臺灣政府採納再生能源優先的政策，將直接影響能源供應鏈的佈局，並對高耗能的AI與半導體產業產生電力穩定性的挑戰。若國防策略轉向大規模投入無人載具與不對稱戰力，則將牽動國防預算的分配與本土國防產業的升級。
此外，這些議題在國際間亦受到高度關注，例如日本在處理大型賽事中關於臺灣旗幟的議題時，展現出的謹慎態度，反映了東亞地區在處理敏感政治符號時的複雜性，這也間接影響了臺灣在國際地緣政治中的空間與互動模式。
然而，公眾在解讀這些資訊時應保持審慎。能源政策的爭論往往夾雜政治立場，核電的經濟效益計算極度依賴核廢料處理成本與除役時間的假設，不應單純以發電成本作為唯一判斷標準。同樣地，國防戰略的評估涉及高度機密與複雜的軍事技術，烏克蘭的成功經驗雖具參考價值，但若過度神話無人載具的戰果，忽略了後勤維護、指揮鏈與電子幹擾等實戰變數，可能導致戰略判斷的失準。此外，區域內如日本等國在處理涉臺議題時的謹慎，多出於地緣政治的平衡考量，不應將其單一事件過度放大為外交政策的全面轉向。
展望未來，臺灣在能源與國防的雙重路徑上，仍有諸多未知數。能源方面，臺灣能否在維持電力供應穩定的前提下，如期達成再生能源佔比目標，將是檢驗能源政策成敗的關鍵。國防方面，後續觀察重點在於臺灣能否成功將無人載具技術整合進現有的聯合作戰體系中，而非僅僅是採購載具。相關政策制定者需在國際壓力、產業需求與國防預算限制之間取得平衡，並持續關注東亞區域內其他國家的政策動向，以確保臺灣在動盪的地緣環境中，能維持長期的韌性與競爭力。
