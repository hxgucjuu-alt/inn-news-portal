  # US-Iran Conflict Disrupts Strait of Hormuz, Escalating Geopolitical Tensions and Accelerating Eurasia’s Energy Transition
  
  > Impacted by the US-Iran conflict, the Strait of Hormuz—a vital global energy artery—has effectively closed, forcing Eurasian nations to accelerate their transition to renewable energy to mitigate reliance on fossil fuels and address energy security challenges.
  
  The US-Iran conflict has now persisted for six months, with geopolitical tensions causing severe shocks to the global energy supply chain. According to international observers, the core of the conflict involves not only military standoffs between Iran and Israel but also directly affects the Strait of Hormuz, a lifeline for global energy transport. As a critical chokepoint for global oil and liquefied natural gas (LNG) shipments, the strait’s obstruction has directly impacted an estimated one-fifth of global oil and gas transport. Against this backdrop, the energy security architecture in Eurasia is being reshaped, forcing governments to reassess the resilience of their energy supplies and pivot toward the accelerated deployment of renewable energy to cope with the risk of long-term shortages.
  
  According to comprehensive reports from various media outlets and research institutions, Eurasian nations have reached a consensus on the necessity of reducing reliance on imported fossil fuels to counter price shocks and supply disruptions caused by geopolitical instability. In Europe, following previous fluctuations in Russian energy supplies, natural gas consumption in the EU has fallen by approximately 18%, indicating that energy transition has become a core policy priority. In Asia, countries are also actively adjusting their energy structures by increasing the share of renewables in their power systems, attempting to establish autonomous energy supply systems amidst a volatile international environment. Energy experts point out that this wave of energy transition is not only for environmental sustainability but also serves as a survival strategy for nations in the current conflict-ridden context.
  
  However, there remain discrepancies and unverified information regarding the specific impact of the conflict. Some media reports indicate that Iran has recently conducted strikes near major Israeli nuclear research centers, suggesting that the conflict is no longer limited to maritime blockades but involves sensitive military and nuclear issues, which adds a layer of complexity far beyond simple energy supply concerns. Furthermore, there is no consensus among parties on whether the "de facto closure" of the Strait of Hormuz is a comprehensive, permanent blockade or a phased military restriction.
  
  Energy market analysts also hold divergent views on the projected duration of the conflict. Some argue that if the conflict is not mitigated in the short term, the global energy market will face long-term structural inflationary pressure, which poses a significant economic challenge for emerging Asian markets that rely on energy imports.
  
  This energy crisis has had a profound impact on industries and policies across Eurasia. At the industrial level, traditional energy extraction and transportation sectors face extremely high operational risks, while renewable energy, energy storage technologies, and infrastructure construction have become focal points for government subsidies and investment. Policymakers are accelerating grid upgrades to accommodate more intermittent renewable power and are attempting to share supply risks through regional energy networks. For Europe, this means striking a delicate balance between maintaining economic operations and driving the green transition; for Asia, it requires making difficult decisions between ensuring energy security and controlling the costs of transition.
  
  Although the potential of renewable energy is widely recognized, there are limitations and aspects that should not be over-interpreted. Analysts caution that energy transition cannot be achieved overnight, and renewables still face numerous challenges in infrastructure construction, technical reserves, and cost-effectiveness. Moreover, energy transition policies in Eurasia are highly susceptible to geopolitical interference; if the scope of the conflict expands, it could lead to disruptions in raw material supply chains, thereby dragging down the speed of manufacturing and deploying renewable energy installations. Therefore, the current energy transition should not be viewed as a panacea for geopolitical conflict, but rather as a means of risk diversification in an uncertain environment.
  
  Future observations will focus on the evolution of the US-Iran conflict and whether there will be a turning point in the navigability of the Strait of Hormuz. The international community will closely monitor military interactions between Iran and Israel, as well as the effectiveness of individual nations' energy security policies. Additionally, the global energy market's reaction to oil and gas prices, and whether major energy-consuming nations adopt more aggressive energy substitution plans, will be key variables affecting Eurasian economic stability in the coming months. Subsequent reports from the International Energy Agency and relevant think tanks will also serve as important indicators of whether the global energy transition process is being "accelerated" or "stymied" by the conflict.

# 美伊衝突致荷莫茲海峽受阻，歐亞能源轉型與地緣局勢升溫

> 受美伊衝突影響，全球能源運輸要道荷莫茲海峽事實上關閉，迫使歐亞各國加速再生能源轉型，以減緩對化石燃料依賴，應對能源安全挑戰。


美伊衝突持續延燒已屆六個月，地緣政治緊張局勢對全球能源供應鏈造成嚴重衝擊。根據相關國際情勢觀察，衝突核心地帶不僅涉及伊朗與以色列周邊的軍事對峙，更直接波及全球能源運輸的命脈——荷莫茲海峽。該海峽作為全球石油與液化天然氣（LNG）運輸的關鍵通道，因衝突導致運輸量受阻，估計約五分之一的全球油氣運輸量受到直接影響。此一背景下，歐亞地區的能源安全架構面臨重塑，各國政府正被迫重新評估能源供應的韌性，並將目光轉向再生能源的加速佈局，以應對長期能源短缺的風險。
根據多家媒體與研究機構的綜合報導，歐亞各國目前已達成一致共識，即必須降低對進口化石燃料的依賴，以應對因地緣政治不穩定帶來的價格衝擊與供應中斷。在歐洲方面，歐盟地區在經歷先前俄羅斯能源供應波動後，天然氣使用量已下降約18%，顯示能源轉型已成為政策核心。而在亞洲，各國亦積極調整能源結構，透過提升再生能源在電力系統中的佔比，試圖在動盪的國際環境中建立自主的能源供給體系。能源專家指出，此波能源轉型不僅是為了環境永續，更是各國在當前衝突背景下的生存策略。
然而，關於衝突的具體影響層面，各來源間仍存在資訊差異與尚待確認之處。部分媒體報導指出，伊朗近期針對以色列主要核研究中心周邊區域進行打擊，顯示衝突已不僅限於海運封鎖，更涉及敏感的軍事與核子議題，這使得局勢的複雜度遠超單純的能源供應問題。此外，荷莫茲海峽的「事實上關閉」究竟是全面性的永久封鎖，還是階段性的軍事管制，目前各方說法尚未完全統一。
能源市場分析師對於衝突持續時間的預測亦有分歧，部分觀點認為，若衝突無法在短期內獲得緩解，全球能源市場將面臨長期的結構性通膨壓力，這對於依賴能源進口的亞洲新興市場國家而言，無疑是巨大的經濟挑戰。
此一能源危機對歐亞地區的產業與政策產生了深遠影響。在產業層面，傳統能源開採與運輸業面臨極高的營運風險，而再生能源、儲能技術及基礎設施建設則成為各國政府政策補貼與投資的重點。各國政策制定者正加速推動電網升級，以容納更多不穩定的再生能源電力，並試圖透過區域性的能源網際網路來分擔供應風險。對於歐洲而言，這意味著必須在維持經濟運作與推動綠色轉型之間取得微妙平衡；對於亞洲而言，則需在保障能源安全與控制轉型成本之間進行艱難的決策。
儘管各界普遍看好再生能源的發展潛力，但各方觀點仍存在限制與不應過度解讀之處。分析人士提醒，能源轉型並非一蹴可幾，再生能源在基礎設施建設、技術儲備與成本效益上仍面臨諸多挑戰。此外，歐亞地區的能源轉型政策極易受到地緣政治因素幹擾，若衝突範圍進一步擴大，可能導致原材料供應鏈中斷，進而拖累再生能源裝置的製造與部署速度。因此，不應將目前的能源轉型視為解決地緣政治衝突的萬靈丹，而應將其視為在不確定環境下的一種風險分散手段。
後續觀察重點將聚焦於美伊衝突的演變，以及荷莫茲海峽的通航狀態是否出現轉機。國際社會將密切關注伊朗與以色列之間的軍事互動，以及各國在能源安全政策上的具體執行成效。此外，全球能源市場對於油氣價格的反應，以及主要能源消費國是否會採取更激進的能源替代方案，將是未來數月影響歐亞經濟穩定的關鍵變數。國際能源總署與相關智庫的後續報告，亦將成為評估全球能源轉型程式是否因衝突而「加速」或「受挫」的重要指標。
