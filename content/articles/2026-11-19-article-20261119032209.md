  # Rising Global Supply Chain Risks: China’s Control Over Key Raw Materials and Challenges to Taiwan’s Semiconductor Industry Resilience
  
  > The author of "Chip War" has issued a warning that China controls 60% of global key raw materials, potentially endangering the supply of specialty gases for Taiwan's semiconductor industry. Faced with geopolitical tensions and ESG regulations, the electronics manufacturing sector is actively adjusting its supply chains to strengthen operational resilience and risk response capabilities.
  
  As the core of global technological development, the stability of the semiconductor industry's supply chain has long been a subject of intense international focus. Recently, Chris Miller, author of "Chip War," issued a stern warning, pointing out that China currently controls approximately 60% of the world's key raw materials. This is particularly true in the field of "specialty gases," which are indispensable in semiconductor manufacturing, where China holds 50% to 75% of the production capacity for various fluorinated gases. Should this supply chain be disrupted, it would directly threaten the operations of Taiwan's advanced wafer fabs. This issue concerns not only a single industry but also touches upon the fragility of global geopolitics and high-tech supply chains, prompting the industry to re-examine procurement strategies that have historically relied on a single market.
  
  According to cross-reporting by The News Lens and TechNews, there is a consensus among industry and academic circles that semiconductor manufacturing is extremely dependent on high-purity specialty gases, and any disruption in the supply chain could lead to a shutdown of wafer fabs. Currently, China's dominant position in the supply chains for fluorinated gases and upstream minerals has become a potential "disruption crisis" that Taiwan's semiconductor industry must face.
  
  At the same time, the global electronics manufacturing industry is in a critical period of supply chain restructuring. Large contract manufacturers such as Foxconn have begun to incorporate ESG (Environmental, Social, and Governance) criteria into their supply chain management, transforming it from a simple sustainability indicator into a strategic tool for strengthening operational resilience and risk management.
  
  However, there remain discrepancies and uncertainties in the information available at this stage. While experts have clearly pointed out China's control over raw materials, there are differing views regarding the specific timeline or trigger conditions for a "supply chain break." Some industry analyses suggest that companies can mitigate the impact of geopolitics to a certain extent through ESG transformation and supply chain diversification; however, the relocation of specialty gas supply chains involves high technical barriers and capital expenditures, which cannot be easily achieved in the short term. Furthermore, changes in international trade policies and increasingly stringent environmental regulations worldwide have added variables to supply chain adjustments, forcing companies into a complex trade-off between "cost efficiency" and "supply stability."
  
  This supply chain transformation has profound implications for East Asia and the world. As a global hub for semiconductor manufacturing, Taiwan will be in a passive position when facing geopolitical maneuvering if it cannot effectively establish a second source of supply. The electronics contract manufacturing industry must strike a balance between decarbonization and risk mitigation; this is not only an internal management challenge for companies but also involves the coordination of transnational supply chains. For the relevant industries, this means that the past cost-oriented globalization model has come to an end, replaced by localized or regionalized production networks centered on "resilience," which will directly change the procurement patterns and cost structures of electronic components in the coming years.
  
  Although the issue of supply chain resilience has sparked widespread discussion, experts warn that not all supply chain risks should be attributed to a single country or policy. The fragility of supply chains often stems from the long-term extreme division of global labor and over-reliance on specific geographic regions. Therefore, as companies promote ESG transformation, they must be wary of the dangers of "over-interpreting" a single risk indicator and should view supply chain diversification as a long-term strategy rather than a stopgap measure to cope with short-term geopolitical pressures. Additionally, the development of alternatives for key raw materials such as specialty gases still relies on international technical cooperation and policy support; it is unlikely that companies can achieve full autonomy on their own in the short term.
  
  Looking ahead, the focus for observing Taiwan's semiconductor and electronics manufacturing industries will be on the progress of establishing a "second supply chain" and the extent to which ESG standards are implemented in supply chain management. Whether the industry can successfully diversify supply chain risks to Southeast Asia, India, or Europe and the United States will be the key to watch over the next two to three years. At the same time, export control policies on semiconductor raw materials by various governments and international coordination mechanisms for critical mineral supply chains will continue to affect the stable development of the global technology industry. Relevant companies need to closely monitor international geopolitical dynamics and continue to invest in technological research and development to cope with potential supply chain fluctuations.

# 全球供應鏈風險升溫：中國關鍵原料掌控力與臺灣半導體產業韌性挑戰

> 《晶片戰爭》作者示警中國掌握全球六成關鍵原料，恐危及臺灣半導體特用氣體供應。面對地緣政治與ESG規範，電子製造業正積極調整供應鏈，以強化營運韌性與風險應變能力。


半導體產業作為全球科技發展的核心，其供應鏈穩定性長期受到國際高度關注。近期，《晶片戰爭》作者克里斯．米勒（Chris Miller）提出嚴正警告，指出中國目前掌控全球約六成關鍵原料，特別是在半導體製程中不可或缺的「特用氣體」領域，中國掌握了多種含氟氣體五成至四分之三的產能。若此供應鏈出現斷鏈，將直接威脅臺灣先進晶圓廠的運作。此一議題不僅關乎單一產業，更觸及全球地緣政治與高科技供應鏈的脆弱性，促使產業界重新審視過去依賴單一市場的採購策略。
根據《關鍵評論網》（The News Lens）與《科技新報》（TechNews）的交叉報導，產業界與學界已達成共識，即半導體製程極度依賴高純度特用氣體，任何供應鏈的中斷都可能導致晶圓廠停擺。目前，中國在含氟氣體及上游礦物供應鏈中佔據主導地位，這已成為臺灣半導體產業必須面對的潛在「斷鏈危機」。
同時，全球電子製造業正處於供應鏈重組的關鍵期，鴻海等大型代工廠已開始將ESG（環境、社會及公司治理）納入供應鏈管理，將其從單純的永續指標，轉化為強化營運韌性與風險應變的戰略工具。
然而，現階段資訊仍存在部分差異與不確定性。雖然專家明確指出中國在原料端的掌控力，但針對「斷鏈」發生的具體時間表或觸發條件，各界看法尚有落差。部分產業分析認為，企業透過ESG轉型與供應鏈多元化佈局，能在一定程度上緩解地緣政治造成的衝擊；但另一方面，特用氣體供應鏈的轉移涉及高昂的技術門檻與資本支出，並非短期內能輕易達成。此外，國際貿易政策的變動與各國日益嚴格的環境規範，也為供應鏈的調整增添了變數，使得企業在「成本效率」與「供應穩定」之間陷入更複雜的權衡。
這場供應鏈變革對東亞乃至全球的影響深遠。臺灣作為全球半導體製造重鎮，若無法有效建立第二供應來源，將在面對地緣政治博弈時處於被動地位。電子代工產業則必須在減碳與抗風險之間取得平衡，這不僅是企業內部的管理挑戰，更涉及跨國供應鏈的協調。對於相關產業而言，這意味著過去以成本為導向的全球化模式已走到盡頭，取而代之的是以「韌性」為核心的在地化或區域化生產網路，這將直接改變未來數年的電子零元件採購模式與成本結構。
儘管供應鏈韌性議題引發廣泛討論，但專家提醒，不應將所有供應鏈風險均歸咎於單一國家或單一政策。供應鏈的脆弱性往往來自於長期的全球分工極致化，以及對特定地理區域的過度依賴。因此，企業在推動ESG轉型時，必須警惕「過度解讀」單一風險指標的危險，應將供應鏈多元化視為長期策略，而非僅是因應短期地緣政治壓力的權宜之計。此外，特用氣體等關鍵原料的替代方案研發，仍需仰賴跨國技術合作與政策支援，單靠企業自身力量恐難以在短期內實現全面自主。
展望未來，臺灣半導體產業與電子製造業的後續觀察重點，將聚焦於「第二供應鏈」的建立進度以及ESG規範在供應鏈管理中的落實程度。產業界是否能成功將供應鏈風險分散至東南亞、印度或歐美地區，將是未來兩至三年內觀察的關鍵。同時，各國政府針對半導體原料的出口管制政策，以及國際間對於關鍵礦物供應鏈的協調機制，亦將持續影響全球科技產業的穩定發展。相關企業需密切監控國際地緣政治動態，並持續投入技術研發，以應對可能發生的供應鏈波動。
