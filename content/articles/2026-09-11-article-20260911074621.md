  # MOTC and Taichung Veterans General Hospital Collaborate to Prevent Motorcycle Accidents, While Addressing Elderly Anti-Fraud Issues in Kaohsiung
  
  > The Ministry of Transportation and Communications (MOTC) has partnered with Taichung Veterans General Hospital to release motorcycle safety guidelines, urging young riders to reduce speed to lower emergency trauma rates. Simultaneously, Kaohsiung City Councilor Lin Chih-hung has called on the city government to strengthen technological anti-fraud deployments in response to the high proportion of elderly citizens falling victim to AI deepfake scams.
  
  Taiwanese society is currently facing two major public safety challenges: traffic safety for young motorcyclists and anti-fraud issues for the elderly population. On September 10, 2026, the Ministry of Transportation and Communications (MOTC) announced a collaboration with Taichung Veterans General Hospital to issue warnings to newly licensed young motorcyclists by integrating emergency medical data and clinical trauma cases. At the same time, Kaohsiung City Councilor Lin Chih-hung raised a query regarding the structural problem of elderly individuals falling victim to fraud, demanding that the Kaohsiung City Government adopt more proactive preventive deployments against the threat posed by AI deepfake technology.
  
  Although these two issues belong to different fields, they both reflect Taiwan's active pursuit of strategic adjustments across ministries and local governments to maintain the safety of vulnerable groups.
  
  Regarding traffic safety, data from Taichung Veterans General Hospital’s emergency trauma department and 119 ambulance services indicate that the frequency and severity of traffic accidents among young people during the initial stage after obtaining their driver's licenses cannot be ignored. This collaboration between the MOTC and medical institutions aims to break the traditional model of one-way advocacy by transportation authorities, shifting instead to providing "graphic" injury data from frontline emergency medical personnel to concretely illustrate the long-term damage traffic accidents cause to individuals and their families.
  
  The two parties jointly released three guidelines, primarily centered on "slowing down" and "defensive driving," with the hope that the shock of real-world data will enhance young riders' awareness of riding safety, thereby reducing the number of trauma cases in emergency rooms caused by traffic accidents.
  
  On the issue of fraud, Kaohsiung City Councilor Lin Chih-hung cited statistics from the National Police Agency covering the period from July 2025 to June 2026, noting that the total number of fraud victims nationwide reached 179,507, with a significant proportion being seniors aged 65 and over. A cross-analysis of the six special municipalities shows that New Taipei City had the highest number of elderly victims at 2,803. When calculated by the proportion of elderly victims relative to the total number of victims in each city, Taipei City ranked first at 12.05%, while Kaohsiung City ranked third among the six municipalities.
  
  Lin Chih-hung emphasized that with the proliferation of AI deepfake technology, the traditional belief that "seeing is believing" is no longer applicable. As fraud techniques become increasingly sophisticated, the Kaohsiung City Government must deploy response mechanisms in advance to prevent the elderly from becoming the primary victims of technological fraud.
  
  These two issues have profound implications for the relevant regions and policies. In terms of traffic policy, the collaboration model between the MOTC and Taichung Veterans General Hospital is expected to become a benchmark for promoting traffic safety education. By emphasizing the emergency medicine perspective that "prevention is better than cure," it may encourage more counties and cities to follow suit by integrating resources with local medical institutions. Regarding social security policy, Lin Chih-hung’s inquiry highlights the passivity of local governments when facing emerging technological crimes. If the Kaohsiung City Government can implement AI anti-fraud deployments, it will help build a more resilient community safety net, particularly by providing more precise protective measures for the elderly who have weaker digital literacy, thereby reducing the negative socio-economic impact of fraud.
  
  However, there are still limitations and differing perspectives regarding these issues. While traffic safety advocacy can raise awareness, transforming "slowing down" and "defensive driving" into long-term habits for riders still relies on supporting road engineering designs and enforcement intensity, rather than relying solely on advocacy. Furthermore, there are varying views on the interpretation of fraud data; some experts argue that simply comparing the number or proportion of victims may overlook differences in population structure and the urban-rural digital divide across counties and cities. Therefore, when formulating anti-fraud policies, one should not over-interpret or allocate resources based solely on a single data indicator, but should instead consider the evolution of criminal behavior and the psychological needs of victims to avoid neglecting other potential criminal risks due to the promotion of a single policy.
  
  Future observations will focus on whether the collaboration project between the MOTC and Taichung Veterans General Hospital can effectively reduce trauma statistics for young riders in emergency rooms, and whether the relevant guidelines can enter campuses through the education system to generate substantial influence. In the field of anti-fraud, how the Kaohsiung City Government specifically implements the AI anti-fraud deployment requested by Lin Chih-hung through inter-departmental cooperation between the Social Affairs Bureau and the Police Bureau, as well as the penetration rate of relevant technological anti-fraud tools, will be important indicators for observing how local governments respond to emerging criminal threats. Furthermore, as technological and economic exchanges in the Asia-Pacific region become increasingly frequent, similar challenges in public safety and technological governance may become governance experiences that cities within the region can reference from one another.

# 交通部與臺中榮總聯手防制機車事故，並關注高雄高齡防詐議題

> 交通部攜手臺中榮總發布機車安全守則，呼籲年輕騎士減速以降低急診創傷率；同時，高雄市議員林智鴻針對長者遭AI深偽詐騙比例偏高，要求市府強化科技防詐部署。


近期臺灣社會面臨兩大公共安全挑戰，分別為年輕機車族的交通安全與高齡族群的防詐議題。交通部於2026年9月10日宣佈與臺中榮民總醫院合作，透過整合急診醫學資料與臨床創傷案例，向剛取得駕照的年輕機車族群提出警示。與此同時，高雄市議員林智鴻亦於同日針對高齡人口遭受詐騙的結構性問題提出質詢，要求高雄市政府針對AI深偽（Deepfake）技術帶來的詐騙威脅，採取更積極的預防性部署。
這兩項議題雖屬不同領域，但皆反映出當前臺灣在維護脆弱群體安全上，正積極尋求跨部會與地方政府的策略調整。
在交通安全方面，根據臺中榮總急診創傷與119救護車送醫資料顯示，年輕族群在考取駕照後的初期駕駛階段，發生交通事故的頻率與嚴重程度均不容忽視。交通部此次與醫療機構合作，旨在打破過去僅由交通主管機關單向宣導的模式，轉而由第一線急診醫護人員提供「血淋淋」的傷勢資料，藉此具體呈現交通事故對個人與家庭造成的長遠損害。
雙方共同發布了三項守則，主要核心在於「減速」與「防禦性駕駛」，期望透過真實資料的震撼教育，提升年輕騎士對騎乘安全的認知，進而降低急診室內因車禍導致的創傷病例。
在防詐議題上，高雄市議員林智鴻引用警政署自2025年7月至2026年6月間的統計資料指出，全國詐騙被害人總數已達17萬9,507人，其中65歲以上長者佔比顯著。針對六都的交叉分析顯示，新北市的受害長者人數居六都之冠，達2,803人；而若以長者受害比例佔該市總被害人比例計算，臺北市以12.05%位居首位，高雄市則名列六都第三。
林智鴻強調，隨著AI深偽技術的普及，傳統的「眼見為憑」已不再適用，詐騙手法日益精進，高雄市政府必須提前部署應對機制，避免長者成為科技詐騙下的主要受害者。
這兩項議題對於相關地區與政策的影響深遠。在交通政策上，交通部與臺中榮總的合作模式，預計將成為未來推動交通安全教育的指標性範例，強調「預防勝於治療」的急診醫學觀點，可能促使更多縣市跟進與當地醫療院所的資源整合。而在社會安全政策方面，林智鴻的質詢凸顯了地方政府在面對新興科技犯罪時的被動性。高雄市政府若能落實AI防詐部署，將有助於建立更具韌性的社群安全網，特別是針對數位能力較弱的高齡族群，提供更精準的防護措施，減少詐騙對社會經濟造成的負面衝擊。
然而，針對上述議題，仍存在部分限制與觀點落差。交通安全的宣導雖能提升意識，但如何將「減速」與「防禦性駕駛」轉化為騎士的長期習慣，仍需仰賴道路工程設計與執法強度的配套，而非單靠宣導。此外，針對詐騙資料的解讀，各界觀點不一；部分專家認為，單純比較受害人數或比例，可能忽略了各縣市人口結構與城鄉數位落差的差異，因此在制定防詐政策時，不應僅憑單一資料指標進行過度解讀或資源配置，而應考量犯罪行為的演變與被害人的心理需求，避免因單一政策的推動而忽略了其他潛在的犯罪風險。
後續觀察重點在於，交通部與臺中榮總的合作計畫是否能有效降低急診室內年輕騎士的創傷統計資料，以及相關守則是否能透過教育體系進入校園，產生實質影響力。在防詐領域，高雄市政府針對林智鴻提出的AI防詐部署要求，將如何具體落實於社會局與警察局的跨局處合作中，以及相關科技防詐工具的推廣普及率，將是未來觀察地方政府應對新興犯罪威脅的重要指標。此外，隨著亞太地區在科技與經貿交流日益頻繁，類似的公共安全與科技治理挑戰，亦可能成為區域內城市間相互參考的治理經驗。
