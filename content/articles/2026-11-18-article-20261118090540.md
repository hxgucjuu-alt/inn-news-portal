  # Deepening International Cooperation in Spatial Information Technology: Academia Sinica Exchanges Digital Governance Insights with Representatives from 18 Countries
  
  > Academia Sinica recently hosted a government delegation from 18 countries to exchange insights on the applications of Geographic Information Systems (GIS) and Geospatial Artificial Intelligence (GeoAI), exploring how spatial technology can be utilized to address global governance challenges such as climate change and public health.
  
  In mid-November 2026, the Research Center for Humanities and Social Sciences at Academia Sinica hosted participants of the "Digital Governance and Spatial Information Application Seminar," organized by the International Center for Land Policy Studies and Training under the Ministry of Agriculture. The delegation consisted of 18 government representatives from 18 different countries, with professional backgrounds spanning Geographic Information Systems, land-use planning, and related fields of digital governance.
  
  The core of this exchange focused on sharing Taiwan's R&D achievements in GIS and GeoAI, and exploring how these cutting-edge spatial technologies can be transformed into key tools for governments to promote "evidence-based governance" to address increasingly severe transnational public issues such as climate change, natural disaster prevention, and public health monitoring.
  
  According to a synthesis of multiple public information sources, the 18-country delegation was diverse, including five of Taiwan's diplomatic allies: Belize, Eswatini, Guatemala, Saint Vincent and the Grenadines, and Paraguay. Additionally, the delegation included government officials from countries such as Malaysia, Fiji, and Israel. During the exchange, Academia Sinica provided detailed demonstrations of specific applications of spatial technology in environmental monitoring and interdisciplinary research. These technologies are not only products of academic research but are also viewed as essential digital infrastructure for modern states to improve decision-making quality and resource allocation efficiency when facing global challenges.
  
  Through this seminar, representatives from various countries were able to gain an in-depth understanding of how Taiwan uses digital governance to translate complex geographic data into actionable policy recommendations.
  
  Regarding information details, various reports are highly consistent in their framing of the event and the background of the participating countries, emphasizing the integration of "digital governance" and "spatial technology." However, there are currently no further public details on how the representatives will specifically implement these technologies into their domestic policies following the seminar. Furthermore, differences in the foundational GIS and GeoAI technical capacities of each country, as well as whether there will be deeper international technical cooperation projects in the future, remain subjects for ongoing observation, with no clear information on signed bilateral or multilateral agreements.
  
  Some information sources focused on Academia Sinica's technical demonstrations, while specific feedback and technical needs from the representatives during the seminar await further information release from relevant authorities.
  
  From the perspective of regional governance and public policy impact, such international exchanges are significantly meaningful for promoting the global application of spatial information technology. For participating countries, learning from Taiwan's practical experience can assist in establishing more scientific assessment mechanisms for land-use planning and disaster early-warning systems. Especially for countries with fragile geographic environments or those highly susceptible to climate change, the introduction of GeoAI technology helps improve the precision of environmental monitoring, thereby reducing public health risks and economic losses.
  
  Furthermore, this knowledge-transfer-centered model of international cooperation helps strengthen Taiwan's substantive diplomatic relations with both diplomatic allies and non-allied countries, consolidating Taiwan's role as a technical contributor in the international governance system through professional exchanges.
  
  It is worth noting that while spatial technology plays a key role in digital governance, one must be cautious about over-interpreting its efficacy. The application results of GIS and GeoAI are deeply constrained by the quality of foundational data, hardware infrastructure, and local technical talent. When promoting digital governance, countries must still face practical challenges such as data privacy protection, the difficulty of cross-departmental data integration, and the costs of technology transfer. Therefore, the outcomes of this Academia Sinica exchange should be viewed as a starting point for technical inspiration rather than a panacea for all governance problems.
  
  In their subsequent policy planning, each country must consider its own digital transformation maturity; not all technical solutions can be directly replicated or applied, and they require moderate localization adjustments and long-term talent cultivation.
  
  Future observation will focus on whether the participating countries can formally incorporate what they learned during the seminar into their domestic land management or public health policy frameworks. Additionally, whether the International Center for Land Policy Studies and Training will launch longer-term technical training or talent exchange programs in the field of GeoAI applications will be a key indicator for evaluating whether the benefits of this exchange can be sustained. At the same time, whether Academia Sinica will further deepen research cooperation with these countries in the international export of spatial information technology is also worth tracking. As global climate change challenges intensify, this type of cross-border technological governance cooperation is expected to become an important trend in future international diplomacy and technical exchange.

# 空間資訊科技深化國際合作：中研院與18國代表交流數位治理

> 中央研究院近期接待來自18國的政府代表團，針對地理資訊系統（GIS）與地理空間人工智慧（GeoAI）的應用進行交流，探討如何運用空間科技解決氣候變遷與公共衛生等全球性治理挑戰。


中央研究院人文社會科學研究中心於2026年11月中旬，接待了一批由農業部國際土地政策研究訓練中心主辦的「數位治理與空間資訊應用研討班」學員。該訪團成員共計18人，由來自全球18個不同國家的政府部門代表組成，專業背景涵蓋地理資訊系統、國土規劃及相關數位治理領域。
此次交流的核心在於分享臺灣在GIS與GeoAI領域的研發成果，並探討如何將這些前瞻空間科技轉化為各國政府推動「循證治理」（Evidence-based Governance）的關鍵工具，以應對日益嚴峻的氣候變遷、天然災害防治以及公共衛生監測等跨國公共議題。
根據多個公開資訊來源綜合確認，此次參與交流的18國代表組成多元，涵蓋了臺灣的5個邦交國，包括貝里斯、史瓦帝尼、瓜地馬拉、聖文森及格瑞那丁以及巴拉圭；此外，訪團成員亦包含來自馬來西亞、斐濟與以色列等國的政府官員。中研院在交流過程中，詳細展示了空間科技在環境監測與跨領域研究中的具體應用案例。這些技術不僅是學術研究的成果，更被視為現代國家在面對全球性挑戰時，提升決策品質與資源分配效率的重要數位基礎設施。
透過此次研討班，各國代表得以深入瞭解臺灣如何透過數位治理手段，將複雜的地理資料轉化為可執行的政策建議。
在資訊細節方面，各報導來源對於此次活動的定位與參與國背景呈現高度一致，均強調了「數位治理」與「空間科技」的結合。然而，針對各國代表在研討班結束後，將如何具體將相關技術落實於本國政策，目前尚無進一步的公開細節。此外，關於各國在GIS與GeoAI技術匯入上的基礎差異，以及未來是否會有更深度的跨國技術合作計畫，目前仍屬於後續觀察範疇，尚未有明確的雙邊或多邊協議簽署訊息。
部分資訊來源側重於中研院的技術展示，而對於各國代表在研討班期間的具體反饋與技術需求，則有待相關單位後續發布更多資訊。
從區域治理與公共政策的影響層面來看，此類國際交流對於推動全球空間資訊科技的應用具有顯著意義。對於參與國而言，透過學習臺灣的實務經驗，能夠協助其在國土規劃與災害預警系統上建立更科學的評估機制。特別是針對地理環境脆弱或易受氣候變遷影響的國家，匯入GeoAI技術有助於提升環境監測的精確度，進而降低公共衛生風險與經濟損失。
此外，這種以知識轉移為核心的國際合作模式，亦有助於強化臺灣與友邦及非邦交國之間的實質外交關係，透過專業領域的交流，鞏固臺灣在國際治理體系中的技術貢獻者角色。
值得注意的是，儘管空間科技在數位治理中扮演關鍵角色，但過度解讀其效能仍需謹慎。GIS與GeoAI的應用效果深受基礎資料品質、硬體基礎建設以及在地化技術人才的限制。各國在推動數位治理時，仍需面對資料隱私保護、跨部門資料整合難度以及技術轉移成本等現實挑戰。因此，此次中研院的交流成果應被視為一個技術啟發的開端，而非解決所有治理問題的萬靈丹。
各國在後續政策規劃中，必須考量自身的數位轉型成熟度，並非所有技術方案皆能直接複製套用，必須進行適度的在地化調整與長期的人才培育。
後續觀察重點將聚焦於參與國是否能將此次研討班所學，正式納入其國內的國土管理或公共衛生政策框架中。此外，農業部國際土地政策研究訓練中心未來是否會針對GeoAI應用領域，推出更長期的技術培訓或人才交換計畫，將是評估此次交流效益是否能持續發酵的關鍵指標。同時，中研院在空間資訊科技的國際輸出上，是否會進一步深化與這些國家的研究合作，亦值得持續追蹤。隨著全球氣候變遷挑戰加劇，這類跨國界的科技治理合作，預計將成為未來國際外交與技術交流的重要趨勢。
