  # AI Governance and Geopolitical Games: East Asian Regional Order Faces Dual Challenges in Technology and Diplomacy
  
  > Recent global technological turbulence, ranging from Microsoft’s global service outage to AI safety concerns, compounded by the geopolitical maneuvering between the U.S., China, and Russia during the APEC summit, has placed the East Asian region at a critical crossroads of technological supply chain restructuring and shifting international order.
  
  Recent major events in the global technology and geopolitical spheres have highlighted the close connection between technological dependence and international diplomacy. In the technology sector, Microsoft’s 365 cloud services experienced a massive global failure in mid-November 2026, affecting over 70 countries and regions. The scope of the impact spanned government agencies, enterprises, and financial institutions, causing core services such as Outlook, Teams, and the Copilot AI assistant to remain offline for over 24 hours. Simultaneously, the political atmosphere in East Asia has become increasingly tense due to the convening of the APEC summit and the East Asia Summit (EAS).
  
  Russian Foreign Minister Sergey Lavrov has arrived in Manila, Philippines, to attend the 21st East Asia Summit, while interactions between the U.S. and China during APEC—particularly regarding Taiwan’s participation rights and chip export controls—have become the central focus of regional stability and industrial development.
  
  Multiple sources have confirmed that the governance capabilities of tech giants and the boundaries of national security are becoming increasingly blurred. Microsoft has officially confirmed that the global outage was not caused by external cyberattacks or an AI system malfunction, but rather by a technical operational error during the company's network capacity expansion. At the industry level, the resignation of NVIDIA CEO Jensen Huang is viewed as an indicator of a shift in Silicon Valley’s corporate governance model, reflecting the clash between U.S. board accountability mechanisms, differences in U.S.-China governance systems, and Washington’s high-end chip export control policies.
  
  These events point to a single fact: the decision-making of global tech giants can no longer be detached from geopolitical red lines. The era in which a founder’s personal will dominated a corporation is now under strict scrutiny by national security policies and regulatory mechanisms worldwide.
  
  However, there are significant discrepancies in how information sources interpret the nature of these events. Regarding the Microsoft outage, although Microsoft denied any connection to the U.S. Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) order to ground flights across the United States, public concern regarding the vulnerability of critical infrastructure has not been fully dispelled. Furthermore, there are divergent views among experts and media reports regarding AI risk assessment. Some experts point out that compared to the robot rebellions seen in science fiction, the real-world crises posed by AI are more concentrated on nuclear system miscalculations, cyber and biological attacks, and the high concentration of technological power resulting from the competition between the U.S. and China.
  
  These perspectives echo the political maneuvering in Beijing during the APEC summit, where it attempted to rewrite Taiwan’s participation rights by citing the "One China Principle," demonstrating that technological competition has been directly transformed into a bargaining chip for regional diplomacy.
  
  For the East Asian region, the specific impact of the aforementioned events has extended to supply chain security and regional participation rights. As a key node in the global AI and semiconductor supply chain, Taiwan faces pressure from the potential politicization of APEC membership rights. Maintaining its participation status and proposing AI rules that align with its own interests has become a top priority for current diplomatic and industrial policy. The Microsoft service disruption serves as a reminder to regional governments and enterprises dependent on a single cloud provider that they must re-examine the resilience of their digital infrastructure.
  
  Furthermore, the combination of NVIDIA’s leadership changes and export control policies may further compress the survival space for the East Asian tech industry between the U.S. and Chinese markets, forcing regional enterprises to accelerate their pursuit of technological autonomy and market diversification.
  
  Although there is high concern regarding AI runaway risks and geopolitical instability, analysts caution against over-interpreting isolated events. For example, directly linking Microsoft’s technical failure to AI safety threats, or viewing the personnel changes of a single company like NVIDIA as the endgame for the global chip industry, may overlook the complexity and self-correcting mechanisms within the tech industry. Experts emphasize that the core of the AI safety issue lies in "power concentration" and "systemic miscalculation" rather than mere technological upgrades. Therefore, when discussing regional order, one should distinguish between commercial governance risks and national security risks to avoid falling into emotional technological panic, and instead focus more on the substantive progress made by various countries in international forums regarding AI governance.
  
  Future observations will focus on several key dimensions: first, the specific declarations made by various countries regarding regional security and technological cooperation at the 21st East Asia Summit, particularly any shifts in the Russian position following Lavrov’s attendance; second, the changes in Taiwan’s influence on semiconductor supply chain rule-making following the APEC summit, and the further maneuvering between the U.S. and China regarding chip export controls; and finally, whether Microsoft and other large cloud service providers will face stricter transnational regulatory requirements following this major outage, and whether regional governments will adopt more decentralized risk management strategies for digital infrastructure. These developments will determine East Asia’s positioning and influence in the future global technological order.

# AI治理與地緣政治博弈：東亞區域秩序面臨科技與外交雙重挑戰

> 近期全球科技界動盪頻傳，從微軟全球服務中斷到AI安全隱憂，疊加APEC峰會期間的中美俄地緣角力，東亞地區正處於技術供應鏈重組與國際秩序變遷的關鍵十字路口。


近期全球科技與地緣政治領域出現多項重大事件，顯現出技術依賴與國際外交之間的緊密連結。在科技領域，微軟（Microsoft）旗下Microsoft 365雲端服務於2026年11月中旬發生全球性大規模故障，波及全球超過70個國家與地區，影響範圍涵蓋政府機關、企業及金融機構，導致Outlook、Teams及Copilot AI助理等核心服務停擺超過24小時。與此同時，東亞地區的政治氛圍亦因APEC峰會及東亞高峰會（EAS）的召開而趨於緊張。
俄羅斯外交部長拉夫羅夫（Sergey Lavrov）已抵達菲律賓馬尼拉出席第21屆東亞高峰會，而中美兩國在APEC期間的互動，特別是涉及臺灣的參與權利與晶片出口管制等議題，成為區域穩定與產業發展的核心焦點。
多方來源共同確認，科技巨頭的治理能力與國家安全邊界正日益模糊。微軟官方已證實，此次全球大當機並非源於外部網路攻擊或AI系統失控，而是與該公司進行網路容量擴充時的技術作業疏失有關。在產業層面，輝達（NVIDIA）執行長黃仁勳的辭任風波，被視為矽谷企業治理模式轉變的指標，反映出美國董事會問責機制、中美治理體系差異以及華府對高階晶片出口管制政策的交鋒。
這些事件共同指向一個事實：全球科技巨頭的決策已無法脫離地緣政治的紅線，創辦人個人意志主導企業的時代，正受到各國國安政策與監管機制的嚴格檢視。
然而，資訊來源在事件性質的解讀上存在顯著差異。針對微軟當機事件，儘管微軟否認其與美國聯邦航空總署（FAA）下令全美航班停飛有關聯，但公眾對於關鍵基礎設施脆弱性的擔憂仍未完全消弭。此外，關於AI的風險評估，專家觀點與媒體報導亦有分歧。部分專家指出，相較於科幻電影中的機器人反叛，AI帶來的現實危機更集中於核系統誤判、網路與生物攻擊，以及中美大國競逐導致的技術權力高度集中。
這些觀點與APEC峰會期間，北京試圖援引「一中原則」改寫臺灣參與權利的政治操作形成呼應，顯示科技競爭已直接轉化為區域外交的籌碼。
對於東亞地區而言，上述事件的具體影響已延伸至供應鏈安全與區域參與權。臺灣作為全球AI與半導體供應鏈的關鍵節點，在面臨APEC會員權利可能遭政治化處理的壓力下，如何維持參與資格並提出符合自身利益的AI規則主張，成為當前外交與產業政策的重中之重。微軟服務中斷事件則提醒了依賴單一雲端供應商的區域政府與企業，必須重新審視數位基礎設施的韌性。
此外，輝達高層異動與出口管制政策的疊加，恐將進一步壓縮東亞科技產業在美中兩大市場間的生存空間，迫使區域內企業加速尋求技術自主與市場多元化。
儘管各界對AI失控與地緣政治風險高度關注，但分析者提醒不應過度解讀單一事件。例如，將微軟技術故障與AI安全威脅直接連結，或將輝達單一企業的人事變動視為全球晶片產業的終局，均可能忽略了科技產業內部的複雜性與自我修正機制。專家強調，AI安全議題的核心在於「權力集中」與「系統性誤判」，而非單純的技術升級，因此在討論區域秩序時，應區分商業治理風險與國家安全風險，避免陷入情緒化的技術恐慌，並應更關注各國在國際論壇中針對AI治理所達成的實質規範進度。
後續觀察重點將聚焦於幾個關鍵維度：首先，第21屆東亞高峰會中，各國針對區域安全與科技合作的具體宣告，特別是拉夫羅夫與會後的俄方立場變化；其次，APEC峰會後，臺灣在半導體供應鏈規則制定上的話語權變化，以及中美雙方在晶片出口管制上的進一步博弈；最後，微軟與其他大型雲端服務供應商在經歷此次大當機後，是否會面臨更嚴格的跨國監管要求，以及區域內政府是否會針對數位基礎設施採取更分散的風險管理策略。這些發展將決定東亞在未來全球科技秩序中的定位與影響力。
