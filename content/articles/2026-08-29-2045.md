  # ASEAN Launches 2045 Vision Amid Regional Challenges: Economic Integration, the Myanmar Crisis, and Mekong Environmental Governance
  
  > The 46th ASEAN Summit was held in Kuala Lumpur, where the "Kuala Lumpur Declaration" was signed to launch the long-term "ASEAN 2045" vision, focusing on economic integration and reducing reliance on single trade partners. Meanwhile, the situation in Myanmar, the recovery of the automotive industry, and environmental pollution in the Mekong River have also drawn significant attention.
  
  Since its establishment in Bangkok on August 8, 1967, the Association of Southeast Asian Nations (ASEAN) has developed into the most important political and economic cooperation organization in the region. Its founding members include Indonesia, Malaysia, the Philippines, Singapore, and Thailand. Recently, ASEAN held its 46th Summit in Kuala Lumpur, Malaysia, where leaders from the 10 member states signed the "Kuala Lumpur Declaration," officially launching the "ASEAN 2045" long-term vision, which aims to map out a blueprint for regional integration over the next 20 years.
  
  The background of this meeting involves not only economic integration but also multiple dimensions such as geopolitical challenges and environmental governance, demonstrating ASEAN's strategic intent to strengthen regional resilience and domestic trade autonomy in the face of global changes.
  
  According to multiple confirmed sources, ASEAN countries reached several consensuses during this summit. First, Malaysian Prime Minister Anwar Ibrahim explicitly called for strengthening intra-ASEAN trade to reduce excessive reliance on single trade partners and wrote to U.S. President Donald Trump regarding tariff issues, requesting a special summit for communication. Second, in terms of industrial development, the automotive market within the ASEAN region is showing signs of recovery, with second-quarter sales increasing by approximately 11% year-on-year. Indonesia remains the largest market in the region, with demand for electric vehicles and related policy support serving as the primary growth drivers. Additionally, the Myanmar crisis has once again become a focal point of discussion, with ASEAN planning to establish relevant mechanisms to address the domestic situation.
  
  Regarding environmental issues, the Mekong River, as a vital regional waterway, has seen its basin's pollution problems draw close attention from local Thai civic groups and the international community.
  
  Although countries have shown unity in economic integration and vision planning, differences remain in how they handle specific affairs. On the Myanmar issue, Thai Prime Minister Anutin Charnvirakul advocates for a "pragmatic approach" to advance dialogue and has arranged to meet with Myanmar junta leader Min Aung Hlaing on the 6th and 7th to discuss bilateral cooperation and humanitarian aid. It remains to be seen whether this move is fully aligned with the potentially tougher sanctions or isolationist stances of some other ASEAN members. Furthermore, the governance of the Mekong River involves complex transnational water rights and ecological protection; while civic groups have issued warnings, there is currently no unified transnational action plan among the countries regarding the identification of pollution sources and specific improvement measures.
  
  Specific implementation details and budget allocations for "ASEAN 2045" are currently in the planning stage and require further detailed negotiations among various ministries.
  
  The policy direction of this ASEAN summit will have a profound impact on regional economic and social development. The growth of the automotive industry indicates that ASEAN is gradually becoming an important part of the global electric vehicle supply chain, and policy support will directly affect the manufacturing transformation in Indonesia and other member states. At the same time, successfully strengthening intra-regional trade will help mitigate the impact of external tariff policies and enhance ASEAN's bargaining power in the global supply chain. However, the uncertainty of the situation in Myanmar and the environmental crisis of the Mekong River not only threaten regional stability but also directly affect cross-border population flows and agricultural productivity. If these issues are not properly resolved, they may offset the positive benefits of economic growth and hinder ASEAN's regional integration process.
  
  Views on these developments vary. Some economists believe that breaking away from reliance on a single trade partner is key to ASEAN moving toward "strategic autonomy." However, other analyses point out that the economic development levels within ASEAN vary significantly, and achieving a high level of integration requires overcoming the limitations of protectionism and infrastructure gaps among member states. Furthermore, while the "pragmatic dialogue" path regarding Myanmar may maintain communication channels, failure to facilitate substantive democratic processes or humanitarian improvements could lead the international community to question ASEAN's ability to resolve regional conflicts. Therefore, the outside world should not over-interpret the effectiveness of a single meeting's declaration, but rather view it as the starting point for long-term negotiations rather than an immediately effective policy solution.
  
  Future observation will focus on several core aspects: first, the outcome of interactions between ASEAN and the U.S. regarding tariff issues, and whether the internal trade strengthening policy promoted by Anwar can be implemented. Second, the progress of dialogue between Thailand and the Myanmar military government will serve as a litmus test for how ASEAN balances its "non-interference" principle with its "regional stability" goal. Third, whether the Mekong River environmental pollution issue will be included in ASEAN's formal environmental protection agenda and lead to the establishment of a transnational governance mechanism will be an important indicator for measuring ASEAN's environmental governance capabilities.
  
  Finally, as the "ASEAN 2045" vision advances, the depth of cooperation among member states in digital transformation and green energy will determine whether ASEAN can transform from a simple trade alliance into an economic community with global influence over the next 20 years.

# 東協啟動2045願景與區域挑戰：經濟整合、緬甸危機與湄公河環境治理

> 東協第46屆高峰會於吉隆坡登場，簽署《吉隆坡宣言》啟動2045長期願景，聚焦經濟一體化與擺脫單一貿易依賴。同時，區域內緬甸局勢、汽車產業回溫及湄公河環境汙染議題亦引發高度關注。


東南亞國家協會（ASEAN，簡稱東協）自1967年8月8日於曼谷成立以來，已發展為區域內最重要的政治與經濟合作組織，創始會員國包括印尼、馬來西亞、菲律賓、新加坡及泰國。近期，東協於馬來西亞吉隆坡召開第46屆高峰會，會中10國領袖共同簽署《吉隆坡宣言》，正式啟動「東協2045」（ASEAN 2045）長期願景，旨在規劃未來20年的區域一體化藍圖。
此次會議背景不僅涉及經濟整合，更涵蓋了地緣政治挑戰與環境治理等多重面向，展現東協在面對全球變局時，試圖強化區域韌性與內貿自主的戰略意圖。
根據多方來源確認，東協各國在本次高峰會中達成多項共識。首先，馬來西亞首相安華明確呼籲強化東協內部貿易，以降低對單一貿易夥伴的過度依賴，並針對關稅議題致信美國總統川普，要求召開特別峰會進行溝通。其次，在產業發展方面，東協區域內的汽車市場呈現回溫趨勢，第二季銷量年增約11%，其中印尼穩居區域最大市場，電動車需求與相關政策支援成為主要成長動力。此外，緬甸危機再次成為討論焦點，東協計劃設立相關機制以應對國內局勢。
而在環境議題上，湄公河作為區域重要水系，其流域的環境汙染問題已引起泰國當地民團與國際社會的密切關注。
儘管各國在經濟整合與願景規劃上展現團結，但針對具體事務的處理方式仍存在差異。在緬甸議題上，泰國總理阿努廷主張採取「務實方式」推進對話，並安排於6日至7日會晤緬甸軍政府領導人敏昂萊，討論雙邊合作與人道援助，此舉與東協部分成員國可能採取的強硬制裁或孤立立場是否完全一致，仍有待觀察。此外，湄公河的治理涉及複雜的跨國水權與生態保護，雖然民團已發出警訊，但目前各國對於汙染源頭的認定與具體改善措施，尚未有統一的跨國行動方案。
關於「東協2045」的具體執行細節與各國預算分配，目前亦處於規劃階段，尚需後續各部會的細部談判。
此次東協的政策走向對區域經濟與社會發展影響深遠。汽車產業的成長顯示東協正逐步成為全球電動車供應鏈的重要一環，政策支援將直接影響印尼及其他成員國的製造業轉型。同時，若能成功強化區域內貿易，將有助於緩解外部關稅政策帶來的衝擊，提升東協在全球供應鏈中的議價能力。然而，緬甸局勢的不確定性與湄公河的環境生態危機，不僅威脅區域穩定，更直接影響跨境人口流動與農業生產力。這些議題若無法妥善解決，恐將抵銷經濟成長帶來的正面效益，並對東協的區域整合程式造成阻礙。
針對上述發展，各界觀點不盡相同。部分經濟學者認為，擺脫對單一貿易夥伴的依賴是東協邁向「戰略自主」的關鍵，但亦有分析指出，東協內部各國經濟發展程度差異巨大，要實現高度的一體化藍圖，需克服各國保護主義與基礎設施落差的限制。此外，針對緬甸問題的「務實對話」路徑，雖能維持溝通管道，但若無法促成實質的民主程式或人道改善，恐招致國際社會對東協解決區域衝突能力的質疑。因此，外界不應過度解讀單一會議的宣言效力，應將其視為長期談判的起點，而非立即見效的政策方案。
未來觀察重點將聚焦於幾個核心面向：首先是東協與美國針對關稅議題的互動結果，以及安華推動的內部貿易強化政策能否落實。其次，泰國與緬甸軍政府的對話進展，將成為檢驗東協「不干涉內政」原則與「區域穩定」目標如何取得平衡的試金石。再者，湄公河環境汙染議題是否會被納入東協正式的環境保護議程，並促成跨國治理機制的建立，將是衡量東協環境治理能力的重要指標。
最後，隨著「東協2045」願景的推進，各成員國在數位轉型與綠色能源領域的合作深度，將決定東協能否在未來20年內，從單純的貿易聯盟轉型為具備全球影響力的經濟共同體。
