  # NASA's 'Big Bang' Power-Saving Plan Succeeds in Extended Mission for Interstellar Probe
  
  > NASA's engineering team has successfully performed a power-saving adjustment codenamed "Big Bang" on Voyager 2, estimated to keep its three science instruments operational for at least another year, with plans to apply the same technique to Voyager 1.
  
  NASA recently confirmed that its engineering team has successfully executed a precision power-saving adjustment codenamed "Big Bang" on the Voyager 2 probe, which is traveling through interstellar space. The operation aimed to shut down certain power-consuming devices and activate low-power alternatives to extend the lifespan of the aging probe, which has been in service for more than 47 years. Voyager 2 is currently more than 20 billion kilometers from Earth and is one of the farthest-flying space probes in human history. Its three science instruments continue to transmit precious data on the interstellar medium, cosmic rays, and magnetic fields.
  
  According to cross-reports from multiple science media outlets, the operation codenamed "Big Bang" is actually a series of high-risk power-switching procedures. As the output of Voyager 2's radioisotope thermoelectric generator (RTG) has been declining year by year, the engineering team had to reallocate limited power resources without damaging the instruments. The operation simultaneously shut down three high-power-consuming devices, including some heaters and a tape recorder, and activated three lower-power alternatives. Through this power-scheduling strategy of "trading time for space," the probe can save nearly ten watts of power consumption annually, buying more operating time for the science instruments.
  
  Reports from Live Science and Scientific American both confirm that the operation was successfully executed in July at NASA's Jet Propulsion Laboratory (JPL) in Pasadena, California. The engineering team conducted meticulous simulations and risk assessments before execution, as any error in any step could result in loss of power supply or communication with the probe. Fortunately, the final results showed that Voyager 2's three science instruments are operating normally after the adjustment, with temperatures maintained within safe ranges, avoiding the risk of freezing due to the shutdown of heaters.
  
  This success also paves the way for the subsequent life-extension plan for Voyager 1.
  
  Regarding this event, media reports are highly consistent on the core facts but show subtle differences in details and expected benefits. Live Science emphasized that the operation "avoided shutting down another science instrument" and noted that the team plans to apply the same technique to Voyager 1 in the coming months; Scientific American explicitly stated that the adjustment is expected to extend the interstellar operating time of both Voyager probes by up to two years; while Smithsonian Magazine and CNN focused on the specific details of "shutting down heaters and the tape recorder" and confirmed at least one additional year of operation.
  
  These differences mainly stem from varying definitions of "extending lifespan" — some use full operation of all science instruments as the baseline, while others calculate based on the probe's overall functionality.
  
  This technological breakthrough has profound implications for deep-space scientific research. Voyager 1 and Voyager 2 are the only two human-made probes to have entered interstellar space, and the data they continuously transmit is the sole direct source for scientists to understand the heliosphere boundary, interstellar magnetic fields, and cosmic ray distribution. Every additional year of operation represents more precious data about the outer solar system environment. Furthermore, the successful validation of this power-saving technique provides an important engineering reference for future long-duration deep-space missions, particularly in energy management strategies after nuclear power systems decline. The innovative thinking and risk management capabilities demonstrated by NASA's engineering team under extreme conditions have become an important case study in the field of space engineering.
  
  However, the limitations of this achievement must be viewed with caution. First, the power decline of the Voyager probes is an irreversible physical process; power-saving measures can only delay, not prevent, the eventual shutdown of instruments one by one. Second, the "Big Bang" operation itself carries considerable risk, and not all devices can be shut down or switched without consequences; this success does not guarantee that Voyager 1 can replicate the same results, as the two probes have different device conditions and power consumption situations. Furthermore, current media reports largely cite NASA's official statements, with limited independent verification data; the actual power-saving benefits and precise lifespan extension duration still require subsequent observational data for confirmation.
  
  For general readers, "two-year extension" should not be interpreted as the probe fully restoring its functions, but rather as a limited extension of the scientific observation window.
  
  In the coming months, the global space science community will closely monitor the timeline and results of NASA's similar operation on Voyager 1. Voyager 1's power situation is more severe than Voyager 2's, and its success or failure will directly affect the timeline for mission termination. Additionally, whether the engineering team will develop further power-saving technologies, and how the science team will prioritize observation plans in the coming years, are key points worth tracking. Since their launch in 1977, the Voyager probes have been operating for nearly half a century, and every engineering miracle has rewritten the limits of human exploration of the universe. This successful "Big Bang" operation undoubtedly adds another chapter to this legendary journey.

# 星際探測器延壽大作戰 NASA「大霹靂」省電計畫成功

> NASA 工程團隊成功對航海家二號執行代號「大霹靂」的省電調整，預估可讓其三個科學儀器至少再運作一年，並計畫將相同手法應用於航海家一號。

美國國家航空暨太空總署（NASA）近日證實，工程團隊已成功對航行在星際空間的航海家二號（Voyager 2）探測器執行一項代號為「大霹靂」（Big Bang）的精密省電調整。這項操作旨在關閉部分耗電裝置，並啟用低功耗的替代方案，以延長這艘已服役超過四十七年的老舊探測器壽命。航海家二號目前距離地球超過二百億公里，是人類史上飛行最遠的太空探測器之一，其搭載的三個科學儀器仍持續回傳關於星際介質、宇宙射線與磁場的珍貴資料。
根據多家科學媒體交叉報導，這項名為「大霹靂」的操作實際上是一連串高風險的電力切換程式。由於航海家二號的放射性同位素熱電發電機（RTG）輸出功率逐年衰減，工程團隊必須在不損壞儀器的前提下，重新分配有限的電力資源。此次操作同時關閉了三個高耗電裝置，包括部分加熱器與一臺磁帶錄音機，並啟動三個功耗較低的替代裝置。透過這套「以時間換取空間」的電力排程策略，探測器每年可節省將近十瓦的電力消耗，為科學儀器爭取更多運作時間。
《生活科學》（Live Science）與《科學美國人》（Scientific American）的報導皆確認，這項操作已於今年七月在NASA位於加州帕薩迪納的噴射推進實驗室（JPL）成功執行。工程團隊在執行前經過縝密的模擬與風險評估，因為任何一個環節出錯，都可能導致探測器失去電力供應或通訊中斷。所幸最終結果顯示，航海家二號的三個科學儀器在調整後運作正常，且溫度維持在安全範圍內，避免了因加熱器關閉而導致的凍結風險。
這項成功也為航海家一號（Voyager 1）的後續延壽計畫鋪平了道路。
關於此事件的報導，各媒體在核心事實上的陳述高度一致，但在細節與預期效益上存在細微差異。《生活科學》強調此次操作「避免了關閉另一個科學儀器」，並指出團隊計畫在未來數月內對航海家一號執行相同手法；《科學美國人》則明確指出，這項調整預計可將兩艘航海家探測器的星際運作時間延長最多兩年；而《史密森尼雜誌》（Smithsonian Magazine）與《CNN》的報導則聚焦於「關閉加熱器與磁帶錄音機」的具體細節，並確認至少可延長一年的運作時間。
這些差異主要來自於對「延長壽命」的定義不同——有些以科學儀器全數運作為基準，有些則以探測器整體功能為計算範圍。
這項技術突破對深空科學研究具有深遠影響。航海家一號與二號是目前僅有兩艘進入星際空間的人類探測器，它們持續回傳的資料是科學家瞭解太陽圈邊界、星際磁場與宇宙射線分佈的唯一直接來源。每延長一年的運作，都代表著更多關於太陽系外圍環境的寶貴資料。此外，這套省電技術的成功驗證，也為未來長壽命深空任務提供了重要的工程參考，特別是在核能電力系統衰退後的能源管理策略上。NASA工程團隊在極端條件下展現的創新思維與風險控管能力，已成為太空工程領域的重要案例。
然而，必須審慎看待這項成果的侷限性。首先，航海家探測器的電力衰退是不可逆的物理過程，省電措施僅能延緩而非阻止儀器陸續關閉的命運。其次，「大霹靂」操作本身具有相當風險，並非所有裝置都能無痛關閉或切換；此次成功並不保證航海家一號能完全複製同樣的結果，因為兩艘探測器的裝置狀態與電力消耗情況不盡相同。再者，目前媒體報導多引用NASA官方說法，獨立驗證資料有限，實際節電效益與延長壽命的精確時間，仍需後續觀測資料佐證。
對於一般讀者而言，應避免將「延長兩年」解讀為探測器將全面恢復功能，而是理解為科學觀測視窗的有限延展。
接下來數月，全球太空科學界將密切關注NASA對航海家一號執行類似操作的時程與結果。航海家一號的電力狀況比二號更為嚴峻，其成功與否將直接影響任務終止的時間表。此外，工程團隊是否會進一步開發其他省電技術，以及科學團隊將如何優先排序未來數年的觀測計畫，都是值得追蹤的重點。航海家探測器自一九七七年發射以來，已持續運作近半世紀，每一次的工程奇蹟都在改寫人類探索宇宙的極限，而這次「大霹靂」的成功，無疑為這段傳奇旅程再添一頁。
