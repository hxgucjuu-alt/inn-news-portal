  # EU Launches Seven AI Superfactory Plan with Total Investment Expected to Reach 30 Billion Euros
  
  > The European Commission officially announced on the 30th the launch of a plan to build seven AI superfactories across Europe, mobilizing public funds and private investment with an overall scale expected to reach 30 billion euros, aiming to strengthen European technological sovereignty in the AI race against the United States and China.
  
  The European Commission held a press briefing on the 30th, officially announcing the launch of a plan to establish seven artificial intelligence (AI) superfactories across Europe to train advanced AI models. The plan is seen as a key step toward the EU's goal of becoming an "AI continent," and is also the most concrete cross-border investment initiative in AI infrastructure in Europe following the release of the "AI Continent Action Plan" in April 2025.
  
  According to the European Commission, the seven superfactories will be distributed across different member states, forming a cross-border collaborative AI computing network aimed at narrowing Europe's gap with the United States and China in AI computing capacity.
  
  Based on cross-referencing multiple media reports, the European Commission confirmed at the press briefing that the plan will mobilize public funds plus private investment, with the overall scale expected to reach 30 billion euros (approximately NT$1.12 trillion). Reuters reported that the EU will use 10 billion euros (approximately US$11.5 billion) in public funds as seed investment to attract private capital, ultimately forming a total investment scale of 30 billion euros. TechNews, citing the European Commission, emphasized that the seven AI superfactories will focus on training advanced AI models and will prioritize serving research institutions and startups within Europe.
  
  Notably, European Commission President von der Leyen had already articulated this vision at the Paris AI Action Summit in February 2025. The plan now formally entering the implementation phase indicates that the EU has shifted from strategic planning to concrete infrastructure investment in AI policy.
  
  Regarding the funding sources and implementation details of the plan, there are some discrepancies among different media outlets. Reuters clearly stated that the EU's public funding portion is 10 billion euros, emphasizing that this is a direct measure by the EU to respond to pressure from the United States and China in the AI race; TechNews used "overall scale expected to reach 30 billion euros" as its main reporting framework without clearly distinguishing the ratio between public funds and private investment. In addition, the "AI Continent Action Plan" document republished on the Chinese Ministry of Commerce website shows that the plan covers five core areas, including expanding AI computing infrastructure, but the document was released in April 2025 and does not include the specific locations or construction timelines of the seven superfactories.
  
  Information still pending confirmation includes: the specific locations of the seven superfactories, the division of labor among member states, and the actual timeline for private investment to materialize.
  
  This plan will have a profound impact on Europe's technology industry and policy direction. First, the establishment of seven AI superfactories will significantly increase AI computing supply within Europe, reducing the dependence of European research institutions and enterprises on US cloud service providers, which holds strategic significance for data sovereignty and technological autonomy. Second, the EU's choice to deploy in a "superfactory" model rather than a single large facility means that member states will form a new AI research and development division of labor system, potentially driving technology infrastructure investment in Southern and Eastern European countries.
  
  Furthermore, this plan will also affect Europe's AI talent cultivation and job market, as the operation of superfactories will require a large number of AI engineers, data scientists, and infrastructure management personnel, potentially intensifying talent competition between Europe and Asia.
  
  However, differing viewpoints and limitations are also worth noting. Some observers point out that Europe's lag in the AI field is not merely a computing power issue, but also involves structural factors such as the startup financing environment, regulatory frameworks, and the speed of commercial application deployment; simply building superfactories may not necessarily solve the overall competitiveness problem of Europe's AI industry. In addition, think tank reports from institutions such as the European Economic and Social Committee (EESC) have cautioned that the EU's development in generative AI remains constrained by regulatory uncertainty and difficulties in policy coordination among member states.
  
  The Reuters report also mentioned that the actual effectiveness of this plan will depend on whether private capital is willing to invest on a large scale, and whether the EU can establish effective cross-border governance mechanisms in the short term; if supporting measures are not put in place simultaneously, the superfactories may face risks of underutilization or shortages of technical talent.
  
  Key points for follow-up observation include: first, whether the European Commission will announce the specific locations of the seven superfactories and each country's contribution ratio in the near future; second, whether private investment will indeed reach the 20 billion euro target, and which industries the main investors come from; third, how this plan interacts with the EU's AI Act regulatory framework, particularly regarding the training and deployment of high-risk AI applications; fourth, how the United States and China will respond to this EU plan, and whether it will trigger a new round of global AI infrastructure investment competition.
  
  Overall, the EU's AI superfactory plan marks an important shift in Europe's role in the AI field from "regulator" to "builder," but its long-term effectiveness remains to be verified over time.

# 歐盟啟動七座AI超級工廠計畫，總投資上看300億歐元

> 歐盟執委會30日正式宣佈啟動歐洲七座AI超級工廠建設計畫，動用公共資金與民間投資，整體規模上看300億歐元，目標在與美國及中國的AI競賽中強化歐洲科技主權。

歐盟執委會於30日舉行新聞簡報會議，正式宣佈啟動在歐洲全境建立七座人工智慧（AI）超級工廠的計畫，用以訓練先進的人工智慧模型。這項計畫被視為歐盟實現「AI大陸」目標的關鍵步驟，同時也是繼2025年4月發布《人工智慧大陸行動計畫》（AI Continent Action Plan）之後，歐洲在AI基礎設施領域最具體的跨國投資行動。
根據歐盟執委會的說法，這七座超級工廠將分散於不同會員國，形成跨國協作的AI算力網路，目的在於縮小歐洲與美國、中國在AI運算能力上的差距。
根據多家媒體交叉比對，歐盟執委會在新聞簡報中確認，這項計畫將動用公共資金加上民間投資，整體規模預計上看300億歐元（約新臺幣1.12兆元）。路透社報導指出，歐盟將以100億歐元（約115億美元）的公共資金作為種子投資，吸引民間資本投入，最終形成總額達300億歐元的投資規模。科技新報則引述歐盟執委會的說法，強調這七座AI超級工廠將專注於訓練先進AI模型，並優先服務歐洲境內的研究機構與新創企業。
值得注意的是，歐盟執委會主席馮德萊恩（von der Leyen）早在2025年2月巴黎AI行動高峰會上即已闡述相關願景，如今這項計畫正式進入執行階段，顯示歐盟在AI政策上已從策略規劃轉向具體基礎設施投資。
關於計畫的資金來源與執行細節，不同媒體之間存在若干差異。路透社明確指出，歐盟公共資金部分為100億歐元，並強調這是歐盟在AI競賽中回應美國與中國壓力的直接措施；科技新報則以「整體規模上看300億歐元」為主要報導框架，並未明確區分公共資金與民間投資的比例。此外，中國商務部網站轉載的《人工智慧大陸行動計畫》檔案顯示，該計畫涵蓋五大核心領域，包括擴大AI計算基礎設施，但該檔案發布時間為2025年4月，並未包含本次七座超級工廠的具體地點與動工時程。
目前尚待確認的資訊包括：七座超級工廠的具體選址、各會員國的分工方式、以及民間投資的實際到位時程。
這項計畫對歐洲科技產業與政策走向將產生深遠影響。首先，七座AI超級工廠的建立將大幅提升歐洲境內的AI算力供給，減少歐洲研究機構與企業對美國雲端服務供應商的依賴，這對於資料主權與技術自主具有戰略意義。其次，歐盟選擇以「超級工廠」而非單一大型設施的模式佈局，意味著會員國之間將形成新的AI研發分工體系，可能帶動南歐與東歐國家的科技基礎設施投資。
此外，這項計畫也將影響歐洲AI人才培育與就業市場，因為超級工廠的營運需要大量AI工程師、資料科學家與基礎設施管理人員，可能進一步加劇歐洲與亞洲之間的人才競爭。
然而，不同觀點與限制條件也值得注意。部分觀察家指出，歐盟在AI領域的落後不僅是算力問題，還涉及新創融資環境、監管框架與商業應用落地速度等結構性因素；單純興建超級工廠未必能解決歐洲AI產業的整體競爭力問題。此外，歐洲經濟和社會委員會（EESC）等機構的智庫報告曾提醒，歐盟在生成式AI領域的發展仍受到監管不確定性與會員國之間政策協調困難的制約。
路透社報導中也提到，這項計畫的實際成效將取決於民間資本是否願意大規模投入，以及歐盟能否在短期內建立有效的跨國治理機制；若相關配套措施未能同步到位，超級工廠可能面臨利用率不足或技術人才短缺的風險。
後續觀察重點包括：第一，歐盟執委會是否會在近期公佈七座超級工廠的具體選址與各國出資比例；第二，民間投資是否確實達到200億歐元的目標，以及主要投資者來自哪些產業；第三，這項計畫與歐盟《人工智慧法》（AI Act）的監管框架如何互動，特別是在高風險AI應用的訓練與部署方面；第四，美國與中國對歐盟這項計畫的反應，是否會引發新一輪的全球AI基礎設施投資競賽。
整體而言，歐盟的AI超級工廠計畫標誌著歐洲在AI領域從「監管者」角色轉向「建設者」角色的重要轉變，但其長期成效仍需時間驗證。
