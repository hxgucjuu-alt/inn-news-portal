  # European Geopolitics and Regional Integration: From Trade Agreements to Changes in Organizational Membership
  
  > This article examines major geopolitical shifts in Europe in recent years, covering post-Brexit trade agreements, the expulsion of Russia from the Council of Europe, and the current status of travel and entry regulations for Bulgaria within the Schengen Area framework.
  
  The framework for European regional political and economic integration has undergone drastic changes over the past few years. From the United Kingdom's formal departure from the EU Single Market and Customs Union to the unprecedented expulsion of Russia by the Council of Europe, these events have profoundly impacted regional stability and cooperation mechanisms across the European continent. Meanwhile, travel and entry policies under the Schengen Area and EU regulations for countries such as Bulgaria have become key indicators of cross-border mobility and policy alignment in Europe. These events not only reflect the divergences among European nations regarding legal, economic, and human rights standards but also mark the restructuring process of the post-Cold War European security and cooperation order.
  
  Reports from various sources confirm that on December 24, 2020, then-British Prime Minister Boris Johnson announced that the UK and the EU had reached a trade agreement, which formally established the framework for bilateral economic and trade relations post-Brexit. Simultaneously, the Committee of Ministers of the Council of Europe took firm action against Russia, formally resolving to expel Russia from the human rights organization—the first time a member state has been ousted since the organization's inception.
  
  Furthermore, regarding entry regulations for countries like Bulgaria, official EU websites and relevant travel guidelines clearly state that travel and visa requirements within the Schengen Area are core policies of European regional integration, and that both citizens and foreign nationals must adhere to unified border management guidelines.
  
  Regarding the details and impacts of the aforementioned events, there are differences in focus among various sources. Concerning the UK-EU trade agreement, reports emphasize that both sides reached a consensus on Christmas Eve, ending the long-standing Brexit negotiation stalemate; however, there are still varying assessments regarding the long-term impact of the agreement on the UK's industrial chain. In the case of Russia's expulsion from the Council of Europe, reports confirm this as the first such instance in the organization's 26-year history, but the substantive impact on Russia's domestic rule of law and human rights situation, as well as Russia's legal countermeasures against this resolution, remain subjects of international legal dispute and have yet to be concluded.
  
  As for travel information regarding Bulgaria, sources primarily focus on border security and visa procedures, without addressing the direct impact of geopolitical conflicts, demonstrating the distinction between administrative travel regulations and high-level political decision-making.
  
  These geopolitical shifts have had a profound impact on European industries and society. The UK-EU trade agreement redefined the rules for the movement of goods and services between the two, forcing multinational corporations to adjust their supply chain layouts to cope with potential tariffs and administrative costs. The expulsion of Russia from the Council of Europe symbolizes an institutional rupture between European human rights oversight mechanisms and Russia; this not only affects the channels for Russian citizens to appeal to the European Court of Human Rights but also exacerbates the fragmentation of the European security architecture. As a member of the Schengen Area, the stability of Bulgaria's travel and entry policies is crucial for maintaining internal European labor mobility and the recovery of the tourism industry, reflecting the continued operation of the EU system in border management.
  
  Although the aforementioned events have sparked widespread discussion in European political circles, analysis should avoid over-interpretation. While the UK-EU trade agreement resolved the immediate risk of trade disruption, it did not entirely eliminate the long-term economic challenges brought about by Brexit; although Russia's expulsion is a significant political signal, the country's role in the European security architecture remains complex and should not be viewed simply as the beginning of a complete severance of relations with Europe. Furthermore, travel advice for countries like Bulgaria is primarily based on current local security and administrative requirements; while linked to the EU's overall regional security policy, specific implementation details remain subject to dynamic adjustments in national border policies, and travel guidelines should not be viewed as the sole barometer of the geopolitical situation.
  
  Subsequent observations should focus on the operation of dispute resolution mechanisms following the UK-EU agreement, as well as the evolution of domestic human rights protection mechanisms and the filling of international regulatory gaps in Russia following its withdrawal from the Council of Europe. At the same time, the expansion of the EU's Schengen Area and border management policies, particularly the degree of integration for countries like Bulgaria within the Schengen Area, will be key to the future of European regional integration. # 歐洲地緣政治與區域整合：從貿易協定到組織成員變動

> 本文梳理歐洲近年重大地緣政治變動，涵蓋英國脫歐後的貿易協定、俄羅斯遭歐洲理事會除名，以及保加利亞在申根區架構下的旅遊與入境規範現況。


歐洲區域政治與經濟整合架構在過去數年間經歷劇烈變動，從英國正式脫離歐盟單一市場與關稅同盟，到歐洲理事會針對俄羅斯採取史無前例的除名處分，皆深刻影響歐洲大陸的區域穩定與合作機制。與此同時，如保加利亞等國在申根區與歐盟規範下的旅遊與入境政策，亦成為歐洲跨國人員流動與政策對接的重要指標。這些事件不僅反映了歐洲各國在法律、經濟與人權標準上的分歧，也標誌著冷戰後歐洲安全與合作秩序的重組過程。
綜合各方報導確認，2020年12月24日，時任英國首相強森（Boris Johnson）宣佈英國與歐盟達成貿易協議，此舉正式確立了英國脫歐後的雙邊經貿關係架構。與此同時，歐洲理事會（Council of Europe）部長委員會亦針對俄羅斯採取了強硬行動，正式決議將俄羅斯從該人權組織中除名，這是該組織成立以來首次將成員國逐出。
此外，針對保加利亞等國的入境規範，歐盟官方網站與相關旅遊指引明確指出，申根區的旅遊與簽證要求為歐洲區域整合下的核心政策，各國公民與外籍人士須遵循統一的邊境管理準則。
關於上述事件的細節與影響，各來源間存在資訊側重差異。針對英國與歐盟的貿易協議，報導重點在於雙方在耶誕夜達成共識，結束了長期的脫歐談判僵局，但對於協議後續對英國產業鏈的長期衝擊，各界仍有不同評估。而在俄羅斯遭歐洲理事會除名的事件中，報導確認了這是該組織26年歷史上的首例，但關於此舉對俄羅斯國內法治與人權狀況的實質影響，以及俄羅斯對此決議的法律反制措施，目前仍處於國際法律爭議階段，尚未有定論。
至於保加利亞的旅遊資訊，各來源主要聚焦於邊境安全與簽證程式，並未涉及地緣政治衝突的直接影響，顯示出旅遊行政規範與高層政治決策間的區隔。
這些地緣政治變動對歐洲產業與社會產生了深遠影響。英國與歐盟的貿易協議重新定義了兩地的貨物與服務流通規則，迫使跨國企業調整供應鏈佈局，以應對潛在的關稅與行政成本。俄羅斯遭歐洲理事會除名，則象徵著歐洲人權監督機制與俄羅斯之間的制度性斷裂，這不僅影響了俄羅斯公民向歐洲人權法院申訴的管道，也加劇了歐洲安全架構的碎片化。保加利亞作為申根區成員國，其旅遊與入境政策的穩定性，對於維持歐洲內部勞動力流動與旅遊業復甦至關重要，反映了歐盟體制在邊境管理上的持續運作。
儘管上述事件在歐洲政壇引起廣泛討論，但分析時應避免過度解讀。英國與歐盟的貿易協議雖解決了即時的貿易中斷風險，但並未完全消除脫歐帶來的長期經濟挑戰；俄羅斯遭除名雖是重大政治訊號，但該國在歐洲安全架構中的角色仍具複雜性，不應單純視為與歐洲完全斷絕關係的開端。此外，關於保加利亞等國的旅遊建議，其主要依據為該國目前的治安與行政要求，與歐盟整體的區域安全政策雖有連動，但其具體執行細節仍需視各國邊境政策的動態調整而定，不宜將旅遊指引視為地緣政治局勢的唯一風向標。
後續觀察重點應聚焦於英國與歐盟協議後的爭端解決機制運作，以及俄羅斯在退出歐洲理事會後，其國內人權保障機制的演變與國際監管缺口的填補方式。同時，歐盟申根區的擴張與邊境管理政策，特別是針對保加利亞等國在申根區內的整合程度，將是未來歐洲區域整合程式的關鍵。INN新聞編輯部將持續追蹤歐洲各國在能源、安全與經濟政策上的協調進展，並密切關注這些跨國政策調整對歐洲民眾生活與產業發展的長遠影響。
