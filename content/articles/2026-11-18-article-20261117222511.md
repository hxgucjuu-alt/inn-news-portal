  # Announcement on Highlands County Court System Upgrades and Administrative Schedule Adjustments in Florida
  
  > The Highlands County Court in Florida has recently implemented adjustments to its administrative office hours, the "Clericus" court record system, and judicial space allocation, aiming to enhance digital service performance and administrative efficiency while ensuring that court record access complies with state government regulations.
  
  As an integral part of the Tenth Judicial Circuit Court of Florida, the Highlands County court system has long been responsible for maintaining public records, judicial administration, and upholding the public trust. According to the Florida Constitution, the roles of the Clerk of the Circuit Court and Comptroller are elected public trustees, whose core function is to ensure the transparency and accuracy of county administrative and judicial records through checks and balances.
  
  Recently, the county court system has made several adjustments to its administrative operations, involving not only changes to office hours but also the further promotion of upgrades to the court record digitization system to meet the increasing demand for public record inquiries.
  
  Progress confirmed by multiple sources indicates that the Highlands County court system is actively promoting digital transformation. At this stage, the county has implemented a court case maintenance system called "Clericus," which is designed to provide lawyers and relevant professionals with more convenient electronic access channels to retrieve various court records. Meanwhile, for public inquiry needs, the Highlands County Court maintains an online record retrieval service, through which the public can search for information such as court files, judgments, liens, and probate records via relevant platforms.
  
  In addition, to optimize judicial space allocation, the planning for the Highlands County Courthouse has also changed. Some of the Clerk’s record storage operations will be relocated to the new Government Center, while the original courthouse will continue to serve as the primary operational hub for the judicial system and the Clerk’s office.
  
  However, regarding the details of administrative operations, the information provided by various sources presents differences in specific implementation schedules and regulations. According to the latest announcement from the Highlands County Clerk’s Office, effective January 2, 2025, all public-facing Highlands County Clerk’s offices will close at 4:30 PM. This change contrasts with current digital access regulations; under Florida Supreme Court Administrative Order (AOSC 16-107), all online access to court records is strictly regulated, and users who need to obtain account credentials for online searches must contact the Clerk’s office directly.
  
  Furthermore, although online record search services are open to the public and free of charge, formal applications and requests for specific records still require the payment of prescribed fees. The fee schedules and application process details for these on different record platforms remain to be verified by users with the county court based on their actual needs.
  
  These administrative and digital system adjustments have had a direct impact on legal professionals, government administrative departments, and the general public in Highlands County. For the legal community, the expansion of the "Clericus" system is expected to significantly reduce the time cost of retrieving case data and improve the efficiency of judicial procedures. For the general public, although physical office hours have been shortened, the integration of the Online Court Records Search (OCRS) system allows the public to query certain public records outside of office hours, reflecting a strategic shift by local government in balancing human resource allocation with public service accessibility.
  
  Moreover, relocating some record storage operations to the Government Center not only alleviates space pressure on the original courthouse but also symbolizes a new stage in Highlands County's modernization of administrative resource integration.
  
  Although the system upgrade brings convenience, there are still limitations and aspects of the policy that should not be over-interpreted. First, digitization does not mean that all judicial records are publicly available without restriction; online access permissions are strictly controlled by state government administrative orders, meaning that highly sensitive or privacy-protected records must still be requested through traditional procedures. Second, the shortening of office hours reflects the reality of local administrative budgets and staffing considerations; the public should not interpret this as a decline in the quality of judicial services, but rather as a necessary means of optimizing administrative processes.
  
  In addition, the search interfaces and fee structures for various types of court records differ by case type; users should remain mindful of the terms of use for different platforms during operation to avoid affecting case progress due to misunderstandings of the rules.
  
  Subsequent observations will focus on public reaction following the implementation of the new office hours on January 2, 2025, as well as the stability of the "Clericus" system when handling large-scale case data. Furthermore, as some record storage operations move to the new Government Center, whether this space allocation adjustment can effectively improve the judicial operational efficiency of the courthouse, and whether more administrative tasks will be included in the scope of digital transformation in the future, will be key indicators for observing the effectiveness of Highlands County's judicial administrative reform. Relevant units should also continue to monitor public feedback on the online record retrieval system and adjust permission management and fee transparency in a timely manner to ensure the fairness and high efficiency of judicial services.

# 美國佛州海蘭郡法院系統升級與行政辦公時程調整公告

> 佛羅裡達州海蘭郡法院近期針對行政辦公時間、法院紀錄系統「Clericus」及司法空間配置進行調整，旨在強化數位化服務效能與行政效率，並確保法院紀錄存取符合州政府規範。


海蘭郡（Highlands County）法院系統作為佛羅裡達州第十司法巡迴法院的重要組成部分，長期承擔維護公共紀錄、司法行政及執行公眾信託的職責。根據佛羅裡達州憲法規定，海蘭郡書記官（Clerk of Circuit Court）與主計長（Comptroller）的角色是經由選舉產生的公共受託人，其核心職能在於透過制衡機制，確保郡級行政與司法紀錄的透明度與正確性。
近期，該郡法院系統針對行政運作進行了多項調整，不僅涉及辦公時間的變更，更進一步推動了法院紀錄數位化系統的升級，以因應日益增加的公共紀錄查詢需求。
多項來源共同確認的進展顯示，海蘭郡法院系統正積極推動數位化轉型。現階段，該郡已匯入名為「Clericus」的法院案件維護系統，該系統旨在提供律師及相關專業人士更為便捷的電子化存取管道，以檢索各類法院紀錄。同時，針對公眾查詢需求，海蘭郡法院亦維持了線上紀錄檢索服務，民眾可透過相關平臺查詢法院檔案、判決書、留置權及遺囑認證紀錄等資訊。
此外，為了最佳化司法空間配置，海蘭郡法院大樓的規劃亦有所變動，部分書記官的紀錄儲存業務將遷移至新建的政府中心（Government Center），原法院大樓則將持續作為司法系統與書記官辦公室的主要運作核心。
然而，在行政運作細節上，各來源提供的資訊呈現出具體的執行時程與規範差異。根據海蘭郡書記官辦公室的最新公告，自2025年1月2日起，所有面對公眾的海蘭郡書記官辦公室將統一於下午4時30分結束營業。此項變動與現行的數位化存取規範形成對比，根據佛羅裡達州最高法院行政命令（AOSC 16-107）的規定，所有法院紀錄的線上存取均受到嚴格規範，使用者若需獲取帳號密碼以進行線上查詢，必須直接聯絡書記官辦公室。
此外，儘管線上紀錄搜尋服務對公眾開放且免費，但若涉及特定紀錄的正式申請與調閱，則仍需支付相關規定的費用，此部分在不同紀錄平臺上的收費標準與申請流程細節，尚待使用者依據實際需求向該郡法院進一步核實。
此次行政與數位系統的調整，對海蘭郡的法律專業人士、政府行政部門及一般民眾產生了直接影響。對於律師群體而言，「Clericus」系統的擴充套件預期將大幅縮短案件資料調閱的時間成本，提升司法程式效率。對於一般民眾，雖然實體辦公室的服務時間縮短，但透過法院線上紀錄系統（OCRS）的整合，公眾在非辦公時間仍能查詢部分公開紀錄，這反映了地方政府在平衡人力資源配置與公眾服務可及性之間的策略轉向。
此外，將部分紀錄儲存業務遷移至政府中心，不僅緩解了原法院大樓的空間壓力，也象徵著海蘭郡在行政資源整合上邁向現代化管理的新階段。
儘管系統升級帶來了便利，但相關政策的推動仍存在限制與不應過度解讀之處。首先，數位化並不代表所有司法紀錄皆可無限制公開，線上存取許可權受到州政府行政命令的嚴格控管，這意味著高度敏感或受隱私保護的紀錄仍需透過傳統程式申請。其次，辦公時間的縮短反映了地方行政預算與人員配置的現實考量，民眾不應將此解讀為司法服務品質的下降，而應視為行政流程最佳化的必要手段。
此外，各類法院紀錄的查詢介面與收費標準因案件型別而異，使用者在操作時仍需留意不同平臺的使用條款，避免因誤解規則而影響案件進度。
後續觀察重點將聚焦於2025年1月2日新辦公時間實施後的公眾反應，以及「Clericus」系統在處理大規模案件資料時的穩定性。此外，隨著部分紀錄儲存業務遷移至新建政府中心，該項空間配置調整是否能有效提升法院大樓的司法運作效率，以及未來是否會有更多行政業務納入數位化轉型範疇，將是觀察海蘭郡司法行政改革成效的關鍵指標。相關單位亦應持續關注公眾對於線上紀錄檢索系統的使用回饋，適時調整許可權管理與收費透明度，以確保司法服務的公平性與高效能。
