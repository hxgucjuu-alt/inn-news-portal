  # Xi-Trump Summit to be Held in Washington: Global Trade Rivalry and Geopolitical Tensions Escalate
  
  > Xi Jinping and Donald Trump will meet in Washington on September 24, focusing on a tariff truce and trade balance. Meanwhile, Southeast Asia faces an ecological crisis triggered by Mekong River water resource development, while the global supply chain's high reliance on Taiwan further complicates the current international economic and trade landscape.
  
  The Greater Mekong Subregion (GMS) economic cooperation has long been an important framework for regional development in Southeast Asia, encompassing Cambodia, China, Laos, Myanmar, Thailand, and Vietnam, with the aim of promoting economic growth through regional cooperation. However, as regional economic integration advances, issues regarding ecology and resource allocation have become increasingly prominent. According to reports from the Central News Agency (CNA), the construction of the Don Sahong Dam in Laos is viewed by local fishermen and environmentalists as a "dam killer," which not only obstructs downstream water flow but also causes the loss of fish spawning habitats and the death of forests due to abnormal water levels, severely impacting the livelihoods of downstream countries such as Cambodia.
  
  Although the Chinese government emphasized a focus on innovative development and building a beautiful home together in the "Declaration of the 8th GMS Summit," the contradiction between infrastructure construction and environmental protection remains a major public issue for Southeast Asian nations, reflecting the tug-of-war between development and sustainability in regional economic cooperation.
  
  In terms of international trade, global attention is focused on the upcoming "Xi-Trump Summit" to be held in Washington on September 24. Combining reports from Reuters and Wealth Magazine, U.S. President Donald Trump is currently facing declining domestic approval ratings, rising living costs, and the dual geopolitical and economic pressures brought about by the war in Iran. By contrast, China's export engine remains strong, with its global trade surplus expected to exceed $1 trillion for the second consecutive year. U.S. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent and Chinese Vice Premier He Lifeng have already held talks in New York to pave the way for the summit, with discussion topics covering artificial intelligence safety guardrails and trade in non-sensitive products.
  
  Multiple media outlets have confirmed that the primary goal of this meeting is not to reach a major new agreement, but to strive for an extension of the tariff truce reached last year to avoid further severe shocks to the global economy.
  
  Although both sides have entered the pre-summit negotiation stage, differences and uncertainties remain regarding the scope of discussions on the Taiwan issue. Reuters analysis points out that due to Trump's political predicament in the quagmire of the Iran war, there is high concern over whether Taiwan might become a "bargaining chip" for the Chinese side at the negotiating table. Meanwhile, Raymond Greene, Director of the American Institute in Taiwan (AIT), recently emphasized publicly that Taiwan is at the core of critical global supply chains, and that the operation of the global economy is highly dependent on commercial and trade exchanges with Taiwan, rather than just energy issues.
  
  It remains to be confirmed whether the U.S. and China will include the Taiwan issue on the agenda during formal talks, and whether China, given its export advantage, will make substantive concessions on trade conditions proposed by the U.S. These are key variables in observing the outcome of this summit.
  
  The aforementioned economic and geopolitical dynamics have a profound impact on Southeast Asia and global industrial chains. As an important node in the global supply chain, Southeast Asian countries are not only indirectly impacted by changes in U.S.-China trade policies but are also directly threatened by the environmental impact of infrastructure construction within the region. If the U.S. and China cannot maintain the tariff truce, the stability of global supply chains will face challenges, subsequently affecting the export and manufacturing performance of Southeast Asia. Furthermore, Taiwan's critical position in the global supply chain means that any international political maneuvering involving Taiwan could trigger a chain reaction in global technology and manufacturing, making the balancing strategy of Southeast Asian nations between the two major powers even more difficult.
  
  Regarding the analyses and predictions from various sectors, experts remind that they should not be over-interpreted. The current focus of U.S.-China negotiations remains on extending the tariff truce and avoiding economic shocks, rather than seeking comprehensive structural changes. Expectations for Beijing to make major concessions are relatively low, primarily due to China's current trade surplus advantage and the limitations placed on Trump's negotiating leverage by domestic political pressure. Therefore, the substantive results of this Xi-Trump summit may be limited to maintaining the status quo. Additionally, the Mekong ecological crisis is a long-term regional governance issue; its solution involves not only the technical aspects of dam construction but also political negotiations over transnational water resource allocation, which is unlikely to be fundamentally improved through a single summit in the short term.
  
  Subsequent observations should focus on the joint declaration or statements made by both sides after the Xi-Trump summit on September 24, particularly regarding the specific direction of tariff policies and whether the Taiwan issue is mentioned. In Southeast Asia, it is necessary to continue monitoring the coordination progress among countries regarding Mekong water resource management and environmental protection under the GMS framework, as well as how ASEAN nations respond to the pressure of supply chain restructuring brought about by the U.S.-China trade game. The stability of the global economy will continue to be tested amidst the fragile balance of U.S.-China trade relations and the challenges of regional environmental issues.

# 川習會將於華府登場：全球貿易博弈與地緣政治角力升溫

> 習近平與川普將於9月24日在華府會晤，聚焦關稅休戰與貿易平衡。與此同時，東南亞地區面臨湄公河水資源開發引發的生態危機，以及全球供應鏈對臺灣的高度依賴，共同構成當前複雜的國際經貿局勢。


大湄公河次區域經濟合作（GMS）長期以來是東南亞區域發展的重要架構，涵蓋柬埔寨、中國、寮國、緬甸、泰國與越南等國，旨在透過區域合作促進經濟增長。然而，隨著區域經濟整合的推進，生態與資源分配問題日益凸顯。根據中央社報導，寮國境內的棟沙宏水壩建設，被當地漁民與環保人士視為「水壩殺手」，不僅導致下游水流受阻，更造成魚群產卵棲地消失、樹林因水位異常而枯死，嚴重衝擊柬埔寨等下游國家的生計。
儘管中國政府在《大湄公河次區域經濟合作第八次領導人會議宣言》中強調聚焦創新發展與共建美好家園，但區域內的基礎設施建設與環境保護之間的矛盾，仍是東南亞國家面臨的重大公共議題，這也反映出區域經濟合作在發展與永續之間的拉鋸。
在國際貿易層面，全球目光聚焦於即將在9月24日於華盛頓舉行的「習川會」。綜合路透社與財訊快報報導，美國總統川普目前面臨國內支援度下滑、生活成本上升，以及伊朗戰爭帶來的地緣政治與經濟雙重壓力。相較之下，中國出口引擎持續強勁，全球貿易順差預計將連續第二年突破1兆美元。美國財政部長貝森特與中國國務院副總理何立峰已於紐約進行會談，為元首峰會鋪路，討論議題涵蓋人工智慧安全護欄及非敏感產品貿易。
多方媒體共同確認，本次會晤的首要目標並非達成大型新協議，而是致力於延長去年達成的關稅休戰，以避免全球經濟遭受進一步的劇烈衝擊。
儘管雙方已進入會前談判階段，但針對臺灣議題的討論空間仍存在分歧與不確定性。路透社分析指出，由於川普在伊朗戰爭泥淖中的政治處境，外界高度關注臺灣是否可能成為中方談判桌上的「籌碼」。與此同時，美國在臺協會（AIT）處長谷立言近期公開強調，臺灣處於全球關鍵供應鏈的核心，全球經濟的運轉高度依賴與臺灣的商業及貿易往來，而非僅止於能源議題。
目前尚待確認的是，美中雙方是否會在正式會談中將臺灣議題納入討論議程，以及中方在出口優勢下，是否會對美國提出的貿易條件做出實質讓步，這些均是觀察本次峰會結果的關鍵變數。
上述經貿與地緣政治動態，對東南亞及全球產業鏈產生深遠影響。東南亞國家作為全球供應鏈的重要節點，不僅受到美中貿易政策變動的間接衝擊，更深受區域內基礎設施建設對環境影響的直接威脅。若美中雙方無法維持關稅休戰，全球供應鏈的穩定性將面臨挑戰，進而影響東南亞地區的出口與製造業表現。此外，臺灣在全球供應鏈中的關鍵地位，使得任何涉及臺灣的國際政治博弈，都可能引發全球科技與製造業的連鎖反應，這使得東南亞各國在美中兩大強權間的平衡策略變得更加艱難。
對於各界的分析與預測，專家提醒不應過度解讀。目前美中雙方的談判重點仍集中在延長關稅休戰與避免經濟衝擊，而非尋求全面性的結構改變。外界對於北京可能做出重大讓步的預期相對較低，主要原因在於中國目前的貿易順差優勢，以及美國國內政治壓力對川普談判籌碼的限制。因此，本次習川會的實質成果可能僅限於維持現狀。此外，湄公河的生態危機屬於長期的區域治理問題，其解決方案不僅涉及水壩建設的技術層面，更牽涉到跨國水資源分配的政治協商，短期內難以透過單一峰會獲得根本性的改善。
後續觀察重點應聚焦於9月24日習川會後的聯合宣告或雙方發言，特別是關於關稅政策的具體走向，以及是否提及臺灣問題。在東南亞方面，則需持續關注GMS框架下，各國對於湄公河水資源管理與環境保護的協調進度，以及東協（ASEAN）各國如何應對美中貿易博弈帶來的供應鏈重組壓力。全球經濟的穩定性，將在美中貿易關係的脆弱平衡與區域性環境議題的挑戰中，持續受到考驗。
