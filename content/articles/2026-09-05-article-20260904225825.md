  # Exploring Cold War Frontier Policies and Contemporary Geopolitics: Challenges and Opportunities for East Asian Regional Integration
  
  > This article examines the historical context of the Republic of China's frontier policies during the Cold War and contrasts them with the contemporary geopolitical landscape of East Asia. It explores Singapore's role as a bridge between ASEAN and Central Asia, analyzing the strategic choices and potential challenges of regional integration amidst trade disruptions and great power competition.
  
  During the Cold War, the frontier policy of the Republic of China (ROC) government played a pivotal role in regional politics. According to research by Wu Qina of the Institute of Modern History at Academia Sinica, the government at the time employed a series of frontier governance strategies that intertwined the virtual with the real, attempting to consolidate border stability and political legitimacy within the Cold War framework. This history not only reflects the ROC's perceptions of surrounding ethnic groups and geographical regions at the time but also serves as an important background for understanding the evolution of contemporary geopolitics surrounding China. The formulation and implementation of frontier policies have long been constrained by both the international Cold War framework and internal political needs, with their influence extending into discussions on contemporary ethnic relations and regional governance, making them a significant subject in the academic study of modern Chinese history.
  
  Various academic and political resources indicate that the political and economic landscape of East Asia and its surrounding regions is undergoing significant change. The extensive historical archives housed in the Chinese University of Hong Kong Library demonstrate that the evolution of China in history, politics, social sciences, and ethnic geography provides a solid documentary foundation for understanding modern East Asian regional relations. Meanwhile, with the fragmentation of global trade chains and the intensification of great power competition, interactions between East Asia and Central Asia are becoming increasingly frequent. There is a broad consensus that in the current complex international environment, countries in the region are seeking more diverse paths for cooperation to reduce their reliance on single markets or major powers, and to enhance their overall strategic autonomy through cross-regional economic integration.
  
  However, significant differences and uncertainties remain regarding the specific paths and effectiveness of regional integration. According to reports from Asia Government & Politics News Monitoring, Singapore is actively positioning itself as a bridge between ASEAN and Central Asia, attempting to establish closer economic ties between the two regions. Although the political will is clear, analysts point out that trade barriers, underdeveloped logistics infrastructure, and differences in the legal systems of various countries remain the primary challenges to promoting such integration.
  
  At present, whether Singapore's strategy can be effectively translated into substantive trade growth still depends on the internal political stability of Central Asian countries and the degree of coordination among ASEAN member states regarding this strategy; these variables remain inconclusive at this time.
  
  This geopolitical trend has a direct impact on regional industries and policies. With the restructuring of global supply chains, East Asian countries are reassessing their economic and trade ties with Central and Southeast Asia. As a financial and logistics hub, if Singapore can successfully play the role of a bridge, it will help facilitate capital flow and technology transfer within the region, thereby mitigating the economic shocks caused by great power competition. This involves not only the trade policy of a single country but also affects the economic security strategy of the entire East Asian region against the backdrop of the intertwining post-Cold War and new Cold War eras. By strengthening ties with Central Asia, East Asian countries are attempting to open up new market spaces beyond traditional trade routes to cope with increasingly severe global trade protectionism.
  
  In response to these developments, scholars and political observers have offered varying perspectives. Some scholars caution that over-interpreting the strategic significance of the "bridge" role may overlook the inherent contradictions within the region. The legacy of Cold War frontier policies may still influence the decision-making logic of various countries' foreign policies today. Furthermore, Singapore's strategic choices face potential pressure from major powers; how to balance the interests of all parties while maintaining a neutral stance is a major limitation in the implementation of the country's policies. Therefore, while the trend of regional integration is evident, its speed and depth remain constrained by complex geopolitical realities and should not be viewed as an immediate economic windfall.
  
  Subsequent observations should focus on the substantive progress of multilateral trade agreements between ASEAN and Central Asia. First, it is necessary to observe whether Singapore can effectively reduce transaction costs for cross-regional trade through bilateral trade agreements. Second, attention must be paid to how China's infrastructure investments in the region (such as railways and ports) compete or cooperate with Singapore's bridge strategy. Finally, it is essential to continue tracking how Cold War historical research can provide theoretical reflection for contemporary frontier and regional policies. These factors will collectively determine East Asia's position in future global geopolitics and its resilience in responding to trade disruptions and great power competition.

# 探討冷戰邊疆政策與當代地緣政治：東亞區域整合的挑戰與機遇

> 本文梳理冷戰時期中華民國邊疆政策的歷史脈絡，並對比當代東亞地緣政治變局。探討新加坡作為東協與中亞橋樑的角色，分析區域整合在貿易中斷與大國競爭背景下的戰略選擇與潛在挑戰。


冷戰時期，中華民國政府的邊疆政策在區域政治中扮演關鍵角色。根據中央研究院近代史研究所吳啟訥的研究，當時政府透過一系列虛擬與現實交織的邊疆治理策略，試圖在冷戰格局下鞏固邊境穩定與政治正當性。這段歷史不僅反映了當時中華民國對周邊民族與地理區域的認知，亦是理解當代中國周邊地緣政治演變的重要背景。邊疆政策的制定與執行，長期以來受到國際冷戰框架與內部政治需求的雙重製約，其影響力延伸至當代族群關係與區域治理的討論中，成為學術界研究中國現代史的重要課題。
多方學術與政治資源共同指出，東亞及周邊區域的政治經濟格局正經歷顯著變遷。香港中文大學圖書館收錄的豐富史料顯示，中國在歷史、政治、社會科學及民族地理等多方面的演變，為理解現代東亞的區域關係提供了堅實的文獻基礎。同時，隨著全球貿易鏈的斷裂與大國競爭加劇，東亞與中亞地區的互動日益頻繁。各界普遍認同，在當前複雜的國際環境下，區域內國家正尋求更多元的合作路徑，以降低對單一市場或大國的依賴，並透過跨區域的經濟整合來提升整體的戰略自主性。
然而，關於區域整合的具體路徑與成效，各界仍存在顯著差異與尚待確認之處。根據 Asia Government & Politics News Monitoring 的報導，新加坡正積極定位自身為東協（Asean）與中亞之間的橋樑，試圖在兩大區域間建立更緊密的經濟連結。雖然政治意願明確，但分析人士指出，貿易障礙、物流基礎設施的落後以及各國法律體系的差異，仍是推動此類整合的主要挑戰。
目前，新加坡的策略是否能有效轉化為實質的貿易增長，仍取決於中亞國家內部的政治穩定度，以及東協成員國對此戰略的協調程度，這些變數目前尚無法定論。
此一地緣政治趨勢對區域產業與政策產生直接影響。隨著全球供應鏈重組，東亞國家正重新評估其與中亞及東南亞的經貿連結。新加坡作為金融與物流中心，若能成功扮演橋樑角色，將有助於促進區域內的資本流動與技術轉移，進而減緩大國競爭帶來的經濟衝擊。這不僅涉及單一國家的貿易政策，更牽動整個東亞區域在後冷戰與新冷戰交織背景下的經濟安全戰略。透過強化與中亞的聯絡，東亞國家正試圖在傳統的貿易路徑之外，開闢新的市場空間，以應對日益嚴峻的全球貿易保護主義。
針對上述發展，學界與政治觀察家提出不同觀點。部分學者提醒，過度解讀「橋樑」角色的戰略意義可能忽略了區域內部固有的矛盾。冷戰時期的邊疆政策遺緒，在當代仍可能影響各國對外政策的決策邏輯。此外，新加坡的戰略選擇也面臨來自大國勢力的潛在壓力，如何平衡各方利益並維持中立立場，是該國政策執行上的主要限制。因此，雖然區域整合的趨勢明顯，但其發展速度與深度仍受限於複雜的地緣政治現實，不應將其視為一蹴可幾的經濟紅利。
後續觀察重點應聚焦於東協與中亞在多邊貿易協定上的實質進展。首先，觀察新加坡是否能透過雙邊貿易協定，有效降低跨區域貿易的交易成本；其次，需關注中國在該區域的基礎設施投資（如鐵路與港口）如何與新加坡的橋樑策略產生競合關係；最後，應持續追蹤冷戰歷史研究如何持續為當代邊疆與區域政策提供理論反思。這些因素將共同決定東亞在未來全球地緣政治中的定位，以及其在應對貿易中斷與大國競爭時的韌性表現。
