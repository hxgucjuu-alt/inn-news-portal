  # Southern Taiwan Science Park Revenue Growth and Central Asian Regional Development: The Dynamic Evolution of the Global Tech Landscape
  
  > In the first half of 2026, the Southern Taiwan Science Park (STSP) saw significant revenue growth, driven by demand for AI and high-performance computing. Simultaneously, Central Asia is demonstrating new momentum in energy, education, and cross-border cooperation, as global technology supply chains and regional geopolitics undergo a simultaneous transformation.
  
  The Southern Taiwan Science Park Administration released its latest statistics on September 29, 2026, indicating that the total revenue of the STSP reached NT$1.773908 trillion in the first half of 2026, a substantial increase of 28.57% compared to the same period in 2025. This growth is primarily attributed to the continued expansion of the global artificial intelligence (AI) boom and the strong leading role of the integrated circuit industry. Among the six major industries, the integrated circuit industry recorded a revenue of NT$1.560987 trillion, a growth rate of 30.22%, largely benefiting from the massive demand for high-performance computing (HPC) and AI applications. Meanwhile, the computer and peripheral industry grew by 40.6%, and the precision machinery industry also saw a growth of 44.87%.
  
  This data not only reflects the core position of Taiwan's high-tech industry within the supply chain but also demonstrates that global investment in semiconductor and AI infrastructure continues to intensify.
  
  At the same time, the Central Asian region is undergoing a significant strategic transformation, becoming a focal point of global geopolitical and economic attention. According to Reuters, South Korea held a summit in September 2026, placing critical minerals and energy cooperation at the heart of its engagement with Central Asian nations, marking a new phase in relations between Seoul and Central Asia.
  
  Furthermore, according to research by the CACI Analyst, educational mobility patterns in Central Asia are changing. The Kazakh government is actively promoting internationalization with a goal of attracting 150,000 international students by 2029, indicating that the region is shifting from a traditional source of talent to a regional education hub. Uzbekistan is also showing a trend of two-way interaction in cross-border education and policy. At the European Union level, the European Parliament signed an Enhanced Partnership and Cooperation Agreement (EPCA) with Uzbekistan in May 2026 and discussed the prevention of cross-border organized crime and terrorism, highlighting the increasing importance of Central Asia in international regulations and security cooperation.
  
  Despite the positive trends in STSP economic data and Central Asian regional development, concerns regarding technological development have also surfaced. The tech media outlet *Techbang* cited multinational research suggesting that generative artificial intelligence (LLM) could be viewed as a "cognitive virus." Over-reliance on AI for "outsourcing" thought processes may lead to the degradation of human cognitive abilities. The research team warns that although AI technology has rapidly permeated culture due to its powerful utility, humans must enhance their "cognitive immunity" through active critical thinking to defend their right to autonomous thought.
  
  This perspective contrasts with the description of strong AI demand in the STSP revenue report; the former emphasizes the explosive growth of the industrial economy, while the latter warns of the social and cognitive risks behind the widespread adoption of technology. Currently, specific quantitative indicators regarding the impact of AI on human cognition remain to be verified by academia, and it is not yet possible to determine the extent of its long-term negative impact on the quality of the global workforce.
  
  The operational growth of the STSP has directly driven the expansion of Taiwan's high-tech industry and further solidified its key position in the global AI supply chain. For Central Asia, policy interventions from South Korea and the EU will help in the development of the region's critical minerals and the upgrading of its education industry, thereby influencing the stability of the global energy supply chain. However, these developments are not isolated; the growth of the global tech industry and the development of Central Asian resources are both highly dependent on the influx of AI and digital tools. If the "cognitive virus" effect expands further in the future, it could affect the innovation capabilities of global high-tech talent, thereby posing a potential challenge to the long-term competitiveness of tech clusters like the STSP.
  
  Current information indicates that the growth of the STSP is mainly benefiting from a surge in hardware and computing demand, while the changes in Central Asia lean toward structural adjustments in geopolitical and educational policies. Although the two are in different geographical dimensions, both are driven by the global wave of digital transformation. It should be noted that the STSP revenue growth data represents concrete industrial performance, whereas the warning regarding the AI "cognitive virus" belongs to the realm of academic and socio-psychological analysis. Equating industrial growth directly with social progress or over-exaggerating AI risks may both overlook the multifaceted nature of technological development.
  
  Furthermore, whether the education and energy policies in Central Asia can be successfully translated into substantive economic growth will still depend on subsequent geopolitical stability and the effectiveness of international cooperation.
  
  Future observation will focus on three aspects: First, whether the STSP's revenue performance in the second half of the year can sustain the growth momentum driven by AI; second, whether the policy layouts of South Korea and the EU in Central Asia will further trigger global supply chain reliance on and competition for Central Asian critical minerals; and finally, whether academic research on the "cognitive virus" will prompt global policymakers to re-examine AI education and usage norms. These variables will collectively determine the future direction of the global tech industry and regional development, and are worthy of continued tracking.

# 南科營收成長與中亞區域發展：全球科技版圖的動態變遷

> 2026年上半年南科營收成長顯著，受惠於AI與高效能運算需求；與此同時，中亞地區在能源、教育及跨境合作上展現新動能，全球科技供應鏈與區域政治經濟正同步重塑。


南部科學園區管理局於2026年9月29日發布最新統計資料，指出2026年上半年南科整體營業額達到1兆7739.08億元，較2025年同期大幅成長28.57%。此一增長主要歸因於全球人工智慧（AI）熱潮的持續擴張，以及積體電路產業的強勁領航作用。在六大產業中，積體電路產業營業額達1兆5609.87億元，成長率達30.22%，主要受惠於高效能運算（HPC）與AI應用的龐大需求。同時，電腦及周邊產業成長40.6%，精密機械產業亦有44.87%的成長表現。
此資料不僅反映了臺灣高科技產業在供應鏈中的核心地位，亦顯示全球對於半導體與AI基礎建設的投資力道持續加大。
與此同時，中亞地區正經歷顯著的戰略轉型，成為全球地緣政治與經濟關注的焦點。根據路透社報導，南韓於2026年9月舉行高峰會，將關鍵礦產與能源合作置於與中亞國家交往的核心地位，標誌著首爾與中亞關係進入新階段。
此外，根據《中亞分析師》（CACI Analyst）的研究，中亞地區的教育流動性模式正在改變，哈薩克政府積極推動國際化，目標在2029年前吸引15萬名國際學生，顯示該地區正從傳統的人才輸出地轉變為區域教育中心，烏茲別克亦在跨境教育與政策上展現出雙向互動的趨勢。在歐盟層面，歐洲議會於2026年5月針對烏茲別克簽署強化夥伴關係與合作協定（EPCA），並討論跨境組織犯罪與恐怖主義防範，顯示中亞在國際法規與安全合作上的重要性日益提升。
儘管南科的經濟資料與中亞的區域發展呈現正向趨勢，但科技發展的隱憂亦浮上檯面。科技媒體《Techbang》引述跨國研究指出，生成式人工智慧（LLM）可能被視為一種「認知病毒」，過度依賴AI進行思維外包，恐導致人類心智認知能力退化。研究團隊警告，雖然AI技術憑藉強大實用性迅速滲透文化，但人類必須透過主動思辨來提升「認知免疫力」，以捍衛自主思考權。
此觀點與南科營收報告中對AI需求強勁的描述形成對比，前者強調產業經濟面的爆發，後者則提醒技術普及背後的社會與認知風險。目前關於AI對人類認知影響的具體量化指標仍待學界進一步驗證，尚無法斷定其對全球勞動力素質的長期負面衝擊程度。
南科的營運成長直接帶動了臺灣高科技產業的擴張，並進一步鞏固其在全球AI供應鏈中的關鍵地位。對於中亞而言，南韓與歐盟的政策介入，將有助於該地區關鍵礦產的開發與教育產業的升級，進而影響全球能源供應鏈的穩定性。然而，這些發展並非孤立，全球科技產業的成長與中亞資源的開發，皆高度依賴於AI與數位工具的匯入。若「認知病毒」效應在未來進一步擴大，可能影響全球高科技人才的創新能力，進而對南科等科技聚落的長期競爭力構成潛在挑戰。
目前的資訊顯示，南科的成長主要受惠於硬體與運算需求的激增，而中亞的變動則偏向地緣政治與教育政策的結構性調整。兩者雖處於不同地理維度，但皆受到全球數位轉型浪潮的驅動。應注意之處在於，南科的營收成長資料為具體的產業績效，而針對AI「認知病毒」的警告則屬於學術與社會心理層面的分析。將產業成長直接等同於社會進步或將AI風險過度誇大，皆可能忽略了技術發展的多面性。
此外，中亞地區的教育與能源政策是否能順利轉化為實質的經濟成長，仍需視後續的地緣政治穩定性與國際合作執行力而定。
後續觀察重點將聚焦於以下三個面向：首先，南科下半年的營收表現是否能延續AI帶動的成長動能；其次，南韓與歐盟在中亞的政策佈局，是否會進一步引發全球供應鏈對中亞關鍵礦產的依賴與競爭；最後，學界對於「認知病毒」的研究是否會引發全球政策制定者對AI教育與使用規範的重新檢視。這些變數將共同決定未來全球科技產業與區域發展的走向，值得持續追蹤。
