  # Middle East AI Investment and Panama Canal Rivalry: The Intersection of Global Technology and Geopolitics
  
  > Summary: Middle Eastern sovereign wealth funds are making massive investments in AI, with Gulf nations incorporating artificial intelligence into national strategies. Meanwhile, the Panama Canal, affected by Central American drought and US-China rivalry, highlights new variables in global supply chains and technology capital flows.
  
  The Persian Gulf and Oceania may seem far apart, but global technology capital and geopolitics are connecting the two regions. The Gulf Cooperation Council (GCC) countries have in recent years actively incorporated artificial intelligence (AI) into national development strategies, with sovereign wealth funds making large-scale investments in AI infrastructure. At the same time, the Panama Canal has become a critical node in international trade and technology supply chains due to climate change and great-power competition. According to a report released by McKinsey & Company, Middle Eastern enterprises have moved from the experimental stage to scaling in AI adoption, focusing on five key strategies—talent, operating models, technology, data, and change management—in an attempt to build new growth engines beyond the oil economy.
  
  This trend may seem unrelated to the US-China rivalry over the Panama Canal, but both reflect deep structural changes in global supply chain restructuring and technology capital flows.
  
  In terms of AI investment, reports from Cnyes indicate that Middle Eastern sovereign wealth funds actively bet on artificial intelligence in 2025, with Abu Dhabi-led MGX partnering with OpenAI and Microsoft to plan investments of hundreds of billions of dollars in AI infrastructure. Saudi Arabia and the United Arab Emirates have further incorporated AI into national strategies to promote economic diversification. McKinsey's analysis corroborates this trend, emphasizing that Gulf enterprises are no longer just piloting AI but are truly embedding AI into core business operations by adjusting operating models and organizational design to create measurable value. These investments and strategic deployments show that the Middle East is transforming from energy-exporting countries into an important source of global technology capital and a testing ground for AI applications.
  
  However, different sources vary in their assessments of the scale and impact of Middle East AI investment. Cnyes focuses on the capital movements of sovereign wealth funds, emphasizing infrastructure investments at the scale of hundreds of billions of dollars; McKinsey approaches from the corporate execution level, pointing out that most Gulf enterprises still face challenges in data governance, talent shortages, and change management, and not all AI projects can quickly generate returns.
  
  Additionally, BBC Chinese's report focuses on the Panama Canal, noting that this 80-kilometer artificial waterway has become a bottleneck for global trade due to climate drought and US-China rivalry; Panamanian President José Raúl Mulino emphasized Panama's sovereignty over the canal and described the canal's neutrality as key to addressing individual and global threats. These three sources cover different topics, but they collectively point to one phenomenon: global infrastructure—whether digital or physical—is becoming a focal point of great-power competition and capital deployment.
  
  Regarding specific impacts on relevant regions, Middle East AI investment will directly change labor markets and industrial structures in Gulf countries. McKinsey points out that the key to AI scaling lies in talent and operating models, which means Saudi Arabia and the UAE will increase investment in education and technical training and attract international tech talent. At the same time, sovereign wealth fund-led investments may strengthen the state's role in the economy, forming a "state capitalism" style of technology development path.
  
  As for the Panama Canal, its drought problems have led to a decline in vessel transits, forcing shipping companies to reroute, which in turn affects supply chain schedules in Oceania and the Asia-Pacific region; the competition between the United States and China for influence over the canal has also elevated the geopolitical strategic status of Central American and Pacific island nations. For Oceania countries such as New Zealand and Australia, canal transit efficiency directly affects transportation costs for their agricultural and mineral exports.
  
  However, these reports also have limitations and should not be over-interpreted. First, McKinsey's report is based on corporate interviews and data analysis, which may lean toward the perspectives of large multinational corporations and state-owned enterprises, failing to fully reflect the AI adoption difficulties of small and medium-sized enterprises. Cnyes' report may over-focus on the "big spending" of sovereign wealth funds, ignoring risks of investment failure or delays. Although BBC Chinese's report highlights US-China rivalry, it provides limited coverage of the diverse voices within Panama's domestic politics and society regarding canal governance. Additionally, the candidate materials do not provide specific investment amounts, employment figures, or official statistics on canal transits, so any quantitative conclusions should be treated with caution.
  
  It is worth noting that some candidate sources (such as repelisflix.com) are unrelated to this topic and cannot serve as verifiable sources of information, and therefore should be excluded during cross-referencing.
  
  Key points for follow-up observation include: First, whether the hundred-billion-dollar investment projects by Middle Eastern sovereign wealth funds (such as MGX) with OpenAI and Microsoft materialize as planned, and whether their infrastructure site selection involves regions beyond Oceania or the Middle East. Second, whether Saudi Arabia and the UAE's national AI strategies will prompt other Gulf countries (such as Qatar and Kuwait) to follow suit, forming a regional AI ecosystem. Third, whether the Panama Canal's drought problem will become normalized due to climate change, leading to intensified infrastructure investment and diplomatic competition between China and the United States around the canal.
  
  Fourth, how Oceania countries (such as Australia and New Zealand) respond to the dual impacts of supply chain rerouting and technology capital flows, particularly in the deployment of digital infrastructure such as data centers and submarine cables. These issues will continue to evolve in the coming months and deserve close monitoring.

# 中東AI投資與巴拿馬運河角力：全球科技與地緣政治交織

> 摘要：中東主權基金大舉投資AI，海灣國家將人工智慧納入國家戰略；同時巴拿馬運河因中美洲乾旱與美中角力，凸顯全球供應鏈與科技資本流動的新變數。

波斯灣與大洋洲看似遙遠，但全球科技資本與地緣政治正在將兩地串連。中東海灣國家（GCC）近年積極將人工智慧（AI）納入國家發展戰略，主權基金大規模投資AI基礎設施；與此同時，巴拿馬運河因氣候變遷與大國競爭，成為國際貿易與科技供應鏈的關鍵節點。根據麥肯錫公司（McKinsey & Company）發布的報告，中東企業在AI採用上已從實驗階段邁向規模化，聚焦於人才、營運模式、技術、資料與變革管理等五大策略，試圖在石油經濟之外建立新的成長引擎。
這項趨勢與巴拿馬運河的中美角力看似無直接關聯，但兩者皆反映出全球供應鏈重組與科技資本流動的深層結構變化。
在AI投資方面，鉅亨網報導指出，2025年中東主權基金積極押注人工智慧，其中由阿布達比主導的MGX公司與OpenAI、微軟合作，計畫投資千億美元於AI基礎設施。沙烏地阿拉伯與阿拉伯聯合大公國更將AI納入國家戰略，推動經濟多元化。麥肯錫的分析也佐證此趨勢，強調海灣國家企業不再只是試行AI，而是透過調整營運模式與組織設計，真正將AI嵌入核心業務，以創造可衡量的價值。這些投資與策略佈局，顯示中東正從能源出口國轉型為全球科技資本的重要來源與AI應用的試驗場。
然而，不同來源對中東AI投資的規模與影響評估存在差異。鉅亨網側重主權基金的資本動向，強調千億美元級別的基礎設施投資；麥肯錫則從企業執行層面切入，指出多數海灣企業仍面臨資料治理、人才短缺與變革管理的挑戰，並非所有AI專案都能迅速變現。
此外，BBC中文網的報導聚焦巴拿馬運河，指出這條全長80公里的人工水道因氣候乾旱與中美角力，成為全球貿易的瓶頸；巴拿馬總統荷西·勞爾·穆利諾（José Raúl Mulino）強調巴拿馬對運河擁有主權，並形容運河的中立地位是應對個別與全球威脅的關鍵。這三個來源的議題範疇不同，但共同指向一個現象：全球基礎設施（無論是數位或實體）正成為大國競爭與資本佈局的焦點。
對相關地區的具體影響方面，中東AI投資將直接改變海灣國家的勞動市場與產業結構。麥肯錫指出，AI規模化的關鍵在於人才與營運模式，這意味著沙烏地與阿聯將加大對教育與技術培訓的投入，並吸引國際科技人才進駐。同時，主權基金主導的投資可能強化國家在經濟中的角色，形成「國家資本主義」式的科技發展路徑。
至於巴拿馬運河，其乾旱問題已導致船舶通行量下降，迫使航運公司改道，進而影響大洋洲與亞太地區的供應鏈時程；美國與中國對運河影響力的競爭，也讓中美洲與太平洋島國的地緣戰略地位上升。對紐西蘭與澳洲等大洋洲國家而言，運河通行效率直接關係到其農產品與礦產出口的運輸成本。
不過，這些報導也存在限制與不應過度解讀之處。首先，麥肯錫的報告以企業訪談與資料分析為基礎，可能偏向大型跨國公司與國營企業的觀點，未能全面反映中小企業的AI採用困境。鉅亨網的報導則可能過度聚焦於主權基金的「大手筆」，忽略投資失敗或延宕的風險。BBC中文網的報導雖點出中美角力，但對巴拿馬國內政治與社會對運河治理的多元聲音著墨有限。此外，候選素材中並未提供具體的投資金額、就業數字或運河通行量的官方統計，因此任何量化結論都應謹慎看待。
值得注意的是，部分候選來源（如repelisflix.com等）與本議題無關，無法作為可查證的資訊來源，因此在交叉比對時應排除。
後續觀察重點包括：第一，中東主權基金（如MGX）與OpenAI、微軟的千億美元投資案是否如期落地，以及其基礎設施選址是否涉及大洋洲或中東以外的地區。第二，沙烏地與阿聯的AI國家戰略是否會帶動其他海灣國家（如卡達、科威特）跟進，形成區域性的AI生態系。第三，巴拿馬運河的乾旱問題是否因氣候變遷而常態化，導致中美兩國在運河周邊的基礎設施投資與外交博弈升溫。
第四，大洋洲國家（如澳洲、紐西蘭）如何因應供應鏈改道與科技資本流動的雙重影響，特別是在資料中心與海底電纜等數位基礎設施的佈局。這些議題將在未來數月持續演變，值得密切追蹤。
