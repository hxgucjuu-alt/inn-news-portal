  # The Global Air Pollution Crisis and Net-Zero Transition: Health and Industrial Challenges Driven by Climate Change
  
  > The EU’s Copernicus Atmosphere Monitoring Service has warned that one-third of the global population is exposed to extremely high concentrations of ozone. Meanwhile, Taiwan is actively seeking to participate in global climate governance and technical cooperation through international net-zero sustainability exhibitions and diplomatic channels.
  
  The environmental impact of climate change is intensifying, not only directly affecting global public health but also forcing nations to accelerate industrial transformation. According to the latest analysis report released by the European Union's "Copernicus Atmosphere Monitoring Service" (CAMS) on September 30, 2026, approximately 34% of the global population had been exposed to "extremely poor" levels of ground-level ozone pollution as of September 15 this year. This data reveals the complex and dangerous interaction between extreme heatwaves, frequent wildfires, and anthropogenic pollutant emissions. When high temperatures and intense sunlight occur, gases from vehicles, industrial emissions, and wildfires undergo chemical reactions to form ground-level ozone, posing a severe challenge to global ecosystems and human health.
  
  Multiple sources confirm that the world faces structural dilemmas in addressing climate change and air pollution. Although the international community has achieved phased results in reducing some major air pollutants over the past few decades, the rate of improvement for ground-level ozone pollution remains relatively slow. The EU analysis report clearly points out that this pollutant carries high health risks, including damage to lung tissue and the induction of respiratory diseases and asthma.
  
  Meanwhile, Taiwan is also taking active measures. On September 24, Representative to New Zealand Joanne Ou publicly called for attention, emphasizing Taiwan's advantages in green technology, renewable energy, and climate resilience, and advocating that Taiwan should meaningfully participate in the "United Nations Framework Convention on Climate Change" (UNFCCC) to contribute to global carbon reduction goals.
  
  Currently, there are discussions and differences at various levels regarding the response pathways to climate change. The EU report focuses on the direct link between atmospheric monitoring data and environmental health, highlighting the intractability of ozone pollution; whereas Taiwan's policy perspective focuses on a dual strategy of "participation in international organizations" and "technology export." Furthermore, the industrial demand for net-zero transition is becoming increasingly urgent. The upcoming "2026 Taiwan International Net-Zero Sustainability Exhibition" demonstrates that the business sector is attempting to transform climate challenges into opportunities for industrial upgrading through technical means such as CBAM compliance, deep energy conservation, and green finance.
  
  However, whether these technical solutions can effectively mitigate atmospheric pollution, and whether the international political landscape will allow Taiwan to participate more deeply in global climate governance, remain key variables yet to be confirmed.
  
  These environmental and policy changes have had a profound impact on global industries and public policy. At the industrial level, global enterprises are accelerating their alignment with international carbon reduction trends. The exhibition brings together iconic companies such as Taipower, Delta Electronics, and Siemens, showing that green power trading and carbon management have become core strategies for corporate survival. At the social level, the spread of ozone pollution directly threatens the health of one-third of the global population; this is not only a public health issue but may also increase the burden on national healthcare systems. Policymakers must strike a balance between economic development and environmental protection; how to maintain strict environmental standards while reducing tariffs to promote trade has become a common challenge for governments worldwide.
  
  Although there is a consensus on the urgency of climate governance, it is necessary to avoid over-interpreting the effectiveness of any single technology or policy. For example, while green technology and renewable energy are important tools for carbon reduction, their promotion speed is still limited by international geopolitical and economic conditions. Furthermore, although the EU's monitoring data is indicative, the formation mechanism of ozone pollution is extremely complex; simple emission reduction policies, if lacking cross-border cooperation and systematic climate resilience construction, are unlikely to be effective in the short term. When promoting net-zero transitions, countries should carefully evaluate the actual environmental benefits after technology implementation, rather than stopping at industrial declarations.
  
  Future observations will focus on several key aspects: First, whether the concentration of global ozone pollution will slow down with the fading of seasonal heatwaves, and whether the EU will subsequently propose more mandatory cross-border emission reduction guidelines for this phenomenon; second, Taiwan's diplomatic progress in striving to participate in the UN system, and whether it can establish deeper climate cooperation mechanisms with the international community through technology exports; finally, whether the substantive cooperation results of companies from various countries in fields such as micro-hydropower and carbon management at the upcoming International Net-Zero Sustainability Exhibition can be effectively transformed into commercially viable climate solutions will be an important indicator for observing changes in the global climate industry chain.

# 全球空汙危機與淨零轉型：氣候變遷引發健康與產業挑戰

> 歐盟哥白尼大氣監測服務示警，全球三分之一人口暴露於極高濃度臭氧中；與此同時，臺灣透過國際淨零永續展及外交途徑，積極尋求參與全球氣候治理與技術合作。


氣候變遷帶來的環境衝擊正日益加劇，不僅直接影響全球公共衛生，也迫使各國加速產業轉型。根據歐盟「哥白尼大氣監測服務」（CAMS）於2026年9月30日發布的最新分析報告，今年截至9月15日，全球約有34%的人口曾暴露於「極差」等級的近地面臭氧汙染環境中。此項資料揭示了極端熱浪、頻繁野火以及人為排放汙染物之間，存在著複雜且危險的互動作用。當高溫與強烈陽光照射下，車輛、工業排放物與野火產生的氣體發生化學反應，便會形成近地面臭氧，對全球生態與人類健康構成嚴峻挑戰。
多方來源共同確認，全球在應對氣候變遷與空氣汙染的程式中，面臨著結構性的困境。儘管過去數十年來，國際社會在削減部分主要空氣汙染物方面已取得階段性成果，但近地面臭氧汙染的改善速度卻相對緩慢。歐盟的分析報告明確指出，這種汙染物具有高度的健康風險，包括損害肺部組織、誘發呼吸道疾病及氣喘等。
與此同時，臺灣方面亦積極採取行動，駐紐西蘭代表歐江安於9月24日公開呼籲，強調臺灣在綠色科技、再生能源及氣候韌性方面的優勢，主張臺灣應有意義地參與「聯合國氣候變化綱要公約」（UNFCCC），以貢獻全球減碳目標。
目前各界對於氣候變遷的應對路徑仍存在不同層次的討論與差異。歐盟報告側重於大氣監測資料與環境健康的直接關聯，強調臭氧汙染的難解性；而臺灣的政策觀點則聚焦於「參與國際組織」與「技術輸出」的雙重策略。此外，產業端對於淨零轉型的需求亦日益迫切，即將登場的「2026臺灣國際淨零永續展」顯示，企業界正試圖透過CBAM因應、深度節能及綠色金融等技術手段，將氣候挑戰轉化為產業升級的契機。
然而，這些技術解決方案能否有效緩解大氣汙染，以及國際政治格局是否允許臺灣更深入參與全球氣候治理，仍是目前尚待確認的關鍵變數。
這些環境與政策變動對全球產業及公共政策產生了深遠影響。在產業層面，全球企業正加速接軌國際減碳趨勢，展會中匯聚了臺電、臺達電子、西門子等指標性企業，顯示出綠電交易與碳管理已成為企業生存的核心戰略。在社會層面，臭氧汙染的擴散直接威脅到全球三分之一人口的健康，這不僅是公共衛生議題，更可能加重各國醫療體系的負擔。政策制定者必須在經濟發展與環境保護之間取得平衡，如何在降低關稅以促進經貿往來的同時，維持嚴格的環保標準，成為各國政府面臨的共同難題。
儘管各界對於氣候治理的緊迫性已有共識，但仍需避免過度解讀單一技術或單一政策的成效。例如，雖然綠色科技與再生能源是減碳的重要工具，但其推廣速度仍受限於國際地緣政治與經濟條件的制約。此外，歐盟的監測資料雖具指標意義，但臭氧汙染的形成機制極為複雜，單純的減排政策若缺乏跨國合作與系統性的氣候韌性建設，恐難以在短期內見效。各國在推動淨零轉型時，應審慎評估技術落地後的實際環境效益，而非僅止於產業宣示。
後續觀察重點將聚焦於幾個關鍵面向：首先，全球臭氧汙染的濃度變化是否會隨季節性熱浪的消退而趨緩，以及歐盟後續是否會針對此現象提出更具強制性的跨國減排指引；其次，臺灣在爭取參與聯合國體系過程中的外交進展，以及能否透過技術輸出與國際社會建立更深層的氣候合作機制；最後，即將舉行的國際淨零永續展中，各國企業在微型水力發電、碳管理等領域的實質合作成果，是否能有效轉化為具備商業可行性的氣候解決方案，將是未來觀察全球氣候產業鏈變化的重要指標。
