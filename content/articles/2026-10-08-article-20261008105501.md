  # UN Fiji Climate Talks Warn: Extreme Heat Poses Serious Threat to the Health of Pregnant Women and Newborns
  
  > The United Nations Framework Convention on Climate Change (UNFCCC) held a pre-COP meeting in Fiji, issuing a strong warning regarding the health risks posed to pregnant women and newborns by extreme heat triggered by climate change, and calling on the international community to address the impact of the climate crisis on public health.
  
  The United Nations Framework Convention on Climate Change (UNFCCC) held a critical pre-COP meeting in Nadi, Fiji, to conduct in-depth discussions on global public health issues stemming from climate change. A core focus of this meeting was exploring how extreme heat caused by global warming poses a direct threat to the health of pregnant women and newborns. As global temperatures continue to rise, climate change is no longer merely an environmental issue; it has evolved into a severe public health challenge. For the pregnant population, who possess weaker physiological regulatory capabilities, high-temperature environments have become a key factor leading to pregnancy complications and health risks for newborns.
  
  According to reports from various media outlets and information from relevant UN agencies, UNFCCC Executive Secretary Simon Stiell issued a clear warning during the Fiji talks. It is a collectively confirmed fact that extreme heat environments not only affect the physical condition of pregnant women but are also significantly linked to premature births, stillbirths, and newborn health issues. Long-term monitoring data from international organizations, including the World Health Organization (WHO) and the UN Secretariat, indicate that the increasing frequency and intensity of heatwaves caused by climate change have burdened medical resources. In vulnerable regions lacking basic cooling facilities and medical safeguards, the mortality risk and complication rates for pregnant women have risen significantly.
  
  Although UN agencies have reached a high level of consensus regarding the health crisis caused by climate change, differences remain among sources regarding specific data presentation and the details of response strategies. Some reports focus on the content of Executive Secretary Stiell’s speech, emphasizing global coordination at the policy level; other sources prioritize the necessity of cross-organizational cooperation, mentioning the roles of the Global Environment Facility (GEF) and the World Food Programme (WFP) in responding to climate disasters.
  
  Information currently awaiting confirmation concerns the uneven progress of various countries in implementing specific carbon reduction policies and medical subsidy programs. Furthermore, there is no unified global statistical data on the proportion of "climate adaptation plans" for pregnant women within national public health budgets, leaving room for discussion on how to concretely implement protective measures.
  
  This issue has a profound impact on global public health policy and social structure. At the industrial level, the healthcare system must re-evaluate obstetric care standards under extreme climate conditions, including the establishment of heat alert systems, the upgrading of cooling devices in delivery rooms, and the formulation of emergency rescue protocols for high-risk pregnant women. For policymakers, this means that climate policy must be integrated across departments with public health policy; the two can no longer be viewed as independent fields. Additionally, this warning may spark social concern regarding environmental justice, particularly in low-income countries or regions severely affected by climate change, where ensuring that pregnant women have access to basic medical rights has become a new focus for international humanitarian aid.
  
  However, experts caution against attributing all pregnancy complications to a single climate factor. While extreme heat is a clear risk factor, maternal health is still influenced by multiple factors, including nutritional status, medical history, access to healthcare, and socioeconomic status. Over-interpreting climate change as the sole cause of disease may lead to the neglect of other necessary public health interventions. Furthermore, current limitations exist in the connection between climate models and epidemiological data, and the lack of standardization in how countries collect relevant health data limits the international community's ability to provide more precise preventive recommendations for specific regions.
  
  Looking ahead, future observation will focus on whether countries will include "maternal and newborn health" in the official climate adaptation agenda at the upcoming climate summit. Whether the international community can utilize UN mechanisms to integrate resources from the Global Environment Facility and the World Health Organization to provide substantive medical infrastructure support to vulnerable regions will be a key indicator for evaluating the effectiveness of the Fiji talks. Additionally, academic research on the long-term effects of high temperatures on fetal development, as well as how countries build climate-resilient obstetric medical systems, will be important observation directions in the global public health field over the coming years.

# 聯合國斐濟氣候會談示警：極端高溫嚴重威脅孕婦與新生兒健康

> 聯合國氣候變化綱要公約（UNFCCC）於斐濟召開會前會，針對氣候變遷引發的極端高溫對孕產婦及新生兒造成的健康風險提出強烈警告，呼籲國際社會正視氣候危機對公共衛生的衝擊。


聯合國氣候變化綱要公約（UNFCCC）於斐濟納迪（Nadi）召開重要會前會（pre-COP），針對氣候變遷帶來的全球公共衛生議題進行深入討論。本次會議的核心焦點之一，在於探討氣候暖化導致的極端高溫現象，如何對孕婦及新生兒的健康構成直接威脅。隨著全球氣溫持續攀升，氣候變遷已不再僅是環境問題，更演變為嚴峻的公共衛生挑戰，特別是對於生理調節能力較弱的孕產婦群體，高溫環境已成為導致妊娠併發症與新生兒健康風險的關鍵因子。
根據多方媒體報導與聯合國相關機構的資訊指出，聯合國氣候變化綱要公約執行秘書斯帝爾（Simon Stiell）在本次斐濟會談中提出明確示警。各界共同確認的事實顯示，極端高溫環境不僅會影響孕婦的身體狀況，更與早產、死產及新生兒健康問題存在顯著的關聯性。世界衛生組織（WHO）與聯合國秘書處等國際機構長期監測資料顯示，氣候變遷引發的熱浪頻率與強度增加，已導致醫療資源負擔加重，特別是在缺乏基礎降溫設施與醫療保障的脆弱地區，孕產婦面臨的死亡風險與併發症比例顯著上升。
儘管聯合國各機構對於氣候變遷造成的健康危機已達成高度共識，但各來源在具體資料呈現及應對策略的細節上仍存在差異。部分報導聚焦於斯帝爾秘書長的演說內容，強調政策層面的全球協調；另一部分來源則側重於跨組織合作的必要性，提及全球環境基金（GEF）與世界糧食計畫署（WFP）在應對氣候災害時的角色。
目前尚待確認的資訊在於，各國在執行具體減碳政策與醫療補貼計畫的進度不一，且針對孕產婦的「氣候適應計畫」在各國公共衛生預算中的佔比尚未有統一的全球資料統計，這使得各界對於如何具體落實保護措施仍有討論空間。
此次議題對全球公共衛生政策與社會結構產生深遠影響。在產業層面，醫療保健體系必須重新評估極端氣候下的產科照護標準，包括建立高溫警報系統、提升產房降溫裝置，以及針對高風險孕婦群體制定緊急救護程式。對於政策制定者而言，這意味著氣候政策必須與公共衛生政策進行跨部門整合，不能再將兩者視為獨立領域。此外，這項警訊也可能引發社會對環境正義的關注，特別是在低收入國家或氣候變遷受災嚴重的地區，如何確保孕產婦能享有基本的醫療權利，成為國際人道救援的新重點。
然而，專家提醒不應將所有妊娠併發症皆歸因於單一氣候因素。雖然極端高溫是明確的風險因子，但孕產婦健康仍受營養狀況、既往病史、醫療可近性及社會經濟地位等多重因素影響。過度解讀氣候變遷為唯一致病原，可能導致忽略其他必要的公共衛生介入措施。此外，目前的限制在於氣候模型與流行病學資料的連結仍有精進空間，各國在蒐集相關健康資料時的標準化程度不一，這限制了國際社會針對特定地區提出更精準的預防建議。
展望未來，後續觀察重點將聚焦於各國在即將到來的氣候峰會中，是否會將「孕產婦與新生兒健康」列入正式的氣候適應議程。國際社會是否能透過聯合國機制，整合全球環境基金與世界衛生組織的資源，為脆弱地區提供實質的醫療基礎設施支援，將是評估本次斐濟會談成效的關鍵指標。此外，學界對於高溫對胎兒發育的長期影響研究，以及各國如何建立具備氣候韌性的產科醫療體系，將是未來數年全球公共衛生領域的重要觀察方向。
