  # Labor Rights Dispute Involving Taiwanese Enterprises Overseas: First National Contact Point Complaint Officially Filed
  
  > Addressing labor rights disputes regarding the overseas operations of Taiwanese enterprises, the Environmental Rights Foundation, alongside Indonesian workers, has submitted Taiwan's first OECD National Contact Point (NCP) complaint to the Ministry of Economic Affairs, calling on the government to establish a mechanism as soon as possible.
  
  The "Guidelines for Multinational Enterprises on Responsible Business Conduct," established by the Organisation for Economic Co-operation and Development (OECD), require national governments to establish a "National Contact Point (NCP)" to serve as a non-judicial dispute resolution and coordination platform, assisting enterprises in mitigating overseas operational risks and implementing responsible business conduct.
  
  However, although Taiwan is a vital link in the global supply chain and the Ministry of Economic Affairs is the designated unit for the NCP, it has yet to announce specific acceptance procedures or operational mechanisms. On September 1, 2026, members of TTNC WATCH and the Environmental Rights Foundation, accompanied by affected local Indonesian communities and advocacy groups, officially submitted Taiwan's first NCP complaint to the Ministry of Economic Affairs. Democratic Progressive Party (DPP) Legislator Lin Yue-chin also attended to show her support, demanding that the government end its passive "still in planning" attitude and immediately initiate the acceptance process.
  
  According to cross-referenced sources, the core of this complaint lies in the protection of labor rights by Taiwanese enterprises operating overseas. Although the Ministry of Economic Affairs has stated that the relevant mechanism is in the planning stage, civil society groups argue that as Taiwanese investment activities in Latin America, the Caribbean, and Indonesia become increasingly frequent—particularly in the development of strategic resources such as copper, lithium, and rare earths—corporate responsibility has become an unavoidable issue. A report by the International Organization for Migration (IOM) points out that the Central American and Caribbean regions are not only hotspots for strategic resource development but also critical hubs and sources for global migration; local labor rights and corporate responsible conduct directly impact regional stability and supply chain security.
  
  Currently, there is a significant discrepancy in information regarding the "progress of the complaint handling" across various sources. Civil society groups emphasize that filing the complaint is intended to fill gaps in existing legal remedies and implement international standards for corporate responsibility set by the OECD. However, the government has yet to provide a clear timeline for when the acceptance mechanism will be officially implemented. Furthermore, regarding the specific names of the enterprises involved and the details of the labor rights violations, some information remains undisclosed. There is high uncertainty among external observers as to whether the complaint will proceed to a substantive investigation stage, pending further response and clarification from the Ministry of Economic Affairs.
  
  The emergence of such complaints has profound implications for the international image and operational policies of Taiwan's industrial chains. With global demands for "responsible business conduct" becoming increasingly stringent, especially in resource-rich regions like Central America and the Caribbean, Taiwanese enterprises may face higher legal risks and international public pressure if they lack comprehensive complaint and mediation mechanisms. Experts point out that this is not merely an isolated labor rights incident; it reflects the necessity for Taiwan to simultaneously strengthen domestic administrative supporting mechanisms while participating in the formulation of international supply chain rules to address increasingly complex transnational labor and environmental disputes.
  
  Opinions on this incident are divided. Supporters believe that handling disputes through the NCP mechanism can effectively reduce litigation costs and enhance the international competitiveness of Taiwanese enterprises. However, some industry insiders worry that mechanisms that are too stringent or lack sufficient communication could increase the administrative burden and operational costs for businesses. Additionally, some commentators have noted that this single complaint should not be over-interpreted as evidence of widespread violations by Taiwanese enterprises, but that the international community's expectations regarding the regulation of Taiwan's overseas investment must be taken seriously. The current limitation of this case is that the Taiwanese NCP mechanism is not yet officially operational; how to ensure the fairness and transparency of the complaint process remains a focus of concern for all parties.
  
  The key focus for future observation will be whether the Ministry of Economic Affairs establishes a task force for this case and announces clear operational details for the NCP in the near future. Furthermore, whether civil society groups and international labor rights organizations will continue to expand their oversight of Taiwanese enterprises' overseas investments, and whether this case will become a landmark precedent for handling similar cross-border labor disputes, will be important indicators of Taiwan's implementation of OECD corporate responsibility standards. All sectors will closely monitor how the government balances corporate interests and labor protections in the face of international standards and domestic industrial development pressures.

# 臺灣企業海外勞權爭議：首件國家聯絡點申訴案正式遞交

> 針對臺灣企業海外營運的勞權爭議，環境權保障基金會偕同印尼勞工向經濟部遞交臺灣首件OECD國家聯絡點申訴案，呼籲政府儘速建立機制。


經濟合作暨發展組織（OECD）所制定的《多國籍企業指導綱領與責任企業行為》，旨在要求各國政府設立「國家聯絡點（National Contact Point, NCP）」，作為訴訟外的爭端解決與協調平臺，協助企業降低海外營運風險並落實責任商業行為。
然而，臺灣作為全球供應鏈重要一環，經濟部雖為NCP專責單位，卻遲遲未公佈具體的受理流程與運作機制。2026年9月1日，TTNC WATCH成員、環境權保障基金會正式偕同印尼在地受影響社群與倡議團體，前往經濟部遞交臺灣首件NCP申訴案，民進黨立委林月琴亦親自到場聲援，要求政府終結「仍在規劃」的消極態度，立即啟動受理程式。
根據多方來源交叉比對，此次申訴案的核心在於臺灣企業在海外的勞工權益保障問題。儘管臺灣經濟部對外表示相關機制正處於規劃階段，但民間團體認為，隨著臺灣企業在拉丁美洲、加勒比海及印尼等地的投資活動日益頻繁，特別是在銅、鋰、稀土等戰略資源開發領域，企業責任已成為不可迴避的議題。根據國際移民組織（IOM）的報告指出，中美洲與加勒比海地區不僅是戰略資源開發熱點，同時也是全球移民遷徙的重要樞紐與來源地，當地的勞工權益與企業責任行為，直接影響區域穩定與供應鏈安全。
目前各來源對於「申訴案處理進度」的資訊存在明顯落差。民間團體強調，遞交申訴案是為了填補現行法律救濟的不足，並落實OECD對企業責任的國際標準；然而，官方對於何時能正式實施受理機制，目前仍未給出明確的時間表。此外，針對申訴案中涉及的具體企業名稱與勞權侵害細節，目前公開資訊中尚有部分細節未完全披露，外界對於該申訴案是否能進入實質調查階段，仍存在高度不確定性，尚待經濟部後續的回應與說明。
此類申訴案的出現，對臺灣產業鏈的國際形象與營運政策具有深遠影響。隨著全球對於「負責任商業行為」的要求日益嚴格，特別是在中美洲與加勒比海等開發資源豐富的地區，臺灣企業若缺乏完善的申訴與調解機制，恐面臨更高的法律風險與國際輿論壓力。相關專家指出，這不僅是單一勞權事件，更反映出臺灣在參與國際供應鏈規則制定時，必須同步強化國內的行政配套機制，以應對日益複雜的跨國勞工與環境爭議。
針對此事件，各界觀點存在分歧。支持者認為，透過NCP機制處理爭議，能有效降低訴訟成本，並提升臺灣企業的國際競爭力；然而，部分產業界人士則擔憂，過於嚴苛或未經充分溝通的機制，可能增加企業的行政負擔與營運成本。此外，亦有評論指出，不應將此單一申訴案過度解讀為臺灣企業普遍存在違規現象，但必須正視國際社會對於臺灣在海外投資行為的監管期待。目前該案的限制在於，臺灣NCP機制尚未正式運作，如何確保申訴過程的公正性與透明度，仍是各界關注的焦點。
後續觀察重點在於經濟部是否會針對此案設立專案小組，並於近期內公佈明確的NCP運作細則。此外，民間團體與國際勞權組織是否會持續擴大對臺灣企業海外投資行為的監督，以及此案是否會成為未來處理類似跨境勞權爭議的指標性案例，將是觀察臺灣落實OECD企業責任行為的重要指標。各界將密切關注政府在面對國際標準與國內產業發展壓力下，如何平衡企業權益與勞工保障。
