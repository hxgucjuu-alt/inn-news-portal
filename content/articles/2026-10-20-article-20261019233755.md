  # The Link Between the Internet and Global Secularization: Transnational Study Reveals Decline in Religious Organization Influence
  
  > Recent research from the University of Maribor in Slovenia indicates a significant correlation between internet penetration and global secularization. Data shows that the internet weakens the organizational framework of traditional churches, leading to the decentralization of faith rather than the simple eradication of individual spiritual pursuits.
  
  As a core infrastructure of modern society, the internet’s impact on human psychology and social structures has long been a subject of intense academic interest. Recently, a research team at the University of Maribor in Slovenia released a transnational panel data analysis that delves into the relationship between internet penetration and shifts in religious belief. The study seeks to clarify whether the internet is a byproduct of modernization or an independent driving force reshaping the human spiritual landscape. The research background is built upon long-standing sociological debates regarding "secularization"—specifically, whether the decline in religious influence stems from economic development, the spread of education, or the process of urbanization.
  
  Through large-scale statistical analysis, the study presents a key finding: after excluding traditional confounding variables such as economic level, educational attainment, and urbanization, internet penetration remains the most stable indicator for predicting the decline of religious organizations.
  
  Confirmed by multiple transnational data analyses, the spread of the internet is steadily driving the process of global secularization. The study points out that the expansion of the internet has not directly led to the total disappearance of individual faith, but has significantly weakened the organizational framework and authoritative status of traditional religious institutions. In the past, religious organizations often played a central role in social identity and the transmission of values. However, in the digital age, the decentralization of information access allows individuals to detach from specific church systems and instead pursue personalized spiritual quests. This shift not only changes the forms of participation in religious activities but also drastically reduces the influence of "church identity labels" in modern society, demonstrating the structural impact of technology on traditional religious organizational structures.
  
  Although research data shows a strong correlation between internet penetration and the decline of religious organizations, there are still subtle differences in how this phenomenon is interpreted across various sources, and some information requires further clarification. The current focus of the debate is whether this "decentralized" spiritual pursuit will lead to a loss of social cohesion or if it is merely an evolution of religious forms. Furthermore, the research team emphasizes that due to the vast differences in cultural backgrounds and internet usage habits across global regions, it remains to be seen whether these findings can be universally applied to all religious systems, necessitating subsequent comparative analyses tailored to different religious and cultural spheres.
  
  The academic community currently maintains a cautious attitude regarding whether the internet is the sole cause of secularization, suggesting that more diverse socio-psychological variables must be considered.
  
  These findings have concrete implications for social policy and educational systems in North America and globally. In North America, religious organizations have long played a significant role in public affairs, educational institutions, and community services. If internet penetration continues to weaken the organizational influence of traditional religions, it may force governments and non-profit organizations to re-evaluate how social safety nets are constructed and to consider how to fill the social support functions previously provided by traditional religious organizations within a decentralized digital environment. For the education industry, this also means that the channels for value transmission have fundamentally changed; educators must confront the high degree of autonomy and fragmented characteristics students exhibit in the process of acquiring information and establishing faith identities.
  
  However, scholars also caution against equating the "decline of religious organizations" directly with the "demise of spiritual faith." The study explicitly states that the internet does not unilaterally eliminate faith but rather reshapes the spiritual landscape of modern individuals. The limitation of this shift is that data analysis often struggles to capture the true changes in an individual's inner spiritual experiences and religious sentiments. Therefore, when using internet penetration as a predictive indicator, one must avoid over-interpreting it as a "clash between technology and faith." Sociologists suggest that this phenomenon should be viewed as an adaptive adjustment of religion in the digital age, rather than a simple linear process of decline.
  
  Subsequent observation will focus on how the trend of internet decentralization affects future social mobilization capabilities and civic engagement. As the influence of traditional religious organizations wanes, whether new digital collective identity mechanisms will emerge remains a key point for future study. Researchers suggest that follow-up studies should continue to track long-term changes in internet usage behavior and religious participation across different generations, particularly by conducting in-depth interviews with "Digital Natives" regarding their faith models, in order to clarify how humans construct and maintain their spiritual pillars and value systems in an information-saturated environment.

# 網際網路與全球世俗化關聯：跨國研究揭示宗教組織影響力衰退

> 斯洛維尼亞馬裡博爾大學近期研究指出，網路普及與全球世俗化程式存在顯著關聯。資料顯示網路削弱了傳統教會的組織框架，使信仰趨向去中心化，而非單純消滅個人精神追求。


網際網路作為現代社會的核心基礎設施，其對人類心理與社會結構的影響長期受到學界高度關注。近期，斯洛維尼亞馬裡博爾大學（University of Maribor）的研究團隊發布了一項跨國面板資料分析，針對網路普及率與宗教信仰變遷之間的關係進行了深入探討。該研究試圖釐清網路究竟是現代化的附屬產物，還是重塑人類精神版圖的獨立驅動力。研究背景建立在過去社會學界對於「世俗化」的長期爭論之上，即宗教影響力的減弱究竟是源於經濟發展、教育普及，還是都市化程式。
透過大規模資料統計，該研究提出了一個關鍵發現：在排除經濟水平、教育程度及都市化等傳統幹擾變數後，網路普及率依然是預測宗教組織衰退最為穩定的指標。
根據多項跨國資料分析的共同確認，網路的普及正穩定地推動全球世俗化程式。研究指出，網際網路的擴張並未直接導致個人信仰的完全消失，而是顯著削弱了傳統宗教機構的組織框架與權威地位。過去，宗教組織往往扮演著社會身分認同與價值傳遞的核心角色，然而在數位時代，資訊獲取的去中心化使得民眾能夠脫離特定教會的體系，轉而尋求個人化的精神追求。這種轉變不僅改變了宗教活動的參與形式，更使得「教會身分標籤」在現代社會中的影響力大幅下降，顯示出科技對傳統宗教組織結構的結構性衝擊。
儘管研究資料顯示了網路普及與宗教組織衰退之間的強相關性，但不同來源對於此現象的解讀仍存在細微差異，且部分資訊尚待進一步釐清。目前的爭論焦點在於，這種「去中心化」的精神追求是否會導致社會凝聚力的流失，抑或僅是宗教形式的演化。此外，研究團隊強調，由於全球各地區的文化背景與網路使用習慣存在巨大差異，該研究結果是否能一體適用於所有宗教體系，仍需後續針對不同宗教文化圈進行更細緻的比較分析。
目前學界對於「網路是否為世俗化的唯一主因」仍持有審慎態度，並認為需考慮更多元化的社會心理變數。
此項發現對於北美及全球的社會政策與教育體系具有具體影響。在北美地區，宗教組織長期以來在公共事務、教育機構及社群服務中扮演重要角色。若網路普及持續削弱傳統宗教的組織影響力，將可能迫使政府與非營利組織重新評估社會安全網的建構方式，並思考如何在去中心化的數位環境中，填補傳統宗教組織過去所提供的社會支援功能。對於教育產業而言，這也意味著價值觀傳遞的管道已發生根本性改變，教育工作者需面對學生在獲取資訊與建立信仰認同過程中的高度自主性與碎片化特徵。
然而，學界同時提醒，不應將「宗教組織衰退」直接等同於「精神信仰的消亡」。研究中明確指出，網際網路並非單向消滅信仰，而是重塑了現代人的精神版圖。這種轉變的限制在於，資料分析往往難以捕捉個人內心深處的靈性體驗與宗教情感的真實變化。因此，將網路普及率作為預測指標時，必須避免過度解讀為「科技與信仰的對立」。社會學家建議，應將此現象視為宗教在數位時代的一種適應性調整，而非簡單的線性衰退過程。
後續觀察重點將聚焦於網路去中心化趨勢如何影響未來的社會動員能力與公民參與。隨著傳統宗教組織影響力的減弱，社會是否會出現新的數位化集體認同機制，將是未來觀察的重點。研究人員建議，後續研究應持續追蹤不同世代在網路使用行為與宗教參與度上的長期變化，特別是針對數位原生代（Digital Natives）的信仰模式進行深度訪談，以釐清在資訊爆炸的環境下，人類如何構建並維持其精神支柱與價值體系。
