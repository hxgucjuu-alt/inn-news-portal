  # AI Development and Geopolitical Conflict Risks: Tech Security Controversies and Attacks on Energy Facilities
  
  > Recent intense debates within the global tech community regarding the risks of AI spiraling out of control, coupled with information censorship in East Asia and drone strikes in Ukraine, have sparked heightened international concern over energy security and the flow of information.
  
  Jacob Coxon, a former core technical researcher at Anthropic and OpenAI, resigned in late September 2026 and issued a stern warning regarding the development of artificial intelligence (AI). Coxon alleged that a "doomsday mentality" is prevalent among practitioners within cutting-edge laboratories, with many genuinely believing that AI could lead to human extinction before 2030. This event occurs against the backdrop of an intensifying artificial intelligence race, as major tech giants invest massive amounts of capital and computing resources in hopes of gaining a lead in the development of superintelligence.
  
  Meanwhile, the geopolitical landscape remains volatile. According to a report by Liveuamap on September 21, 2026, a drone attack launched by Ukraine on September 20 resulted in the disruption of operations at relevant energy facilities, highlighting the vulnerability of global energy infrastructure amidst conflict.
  
  Multiple media sources have confirmed that there is a serious divide within the tech industry regarding risk assessments for the future of AI. Coxon’s resignation announcement explicitly stated that tech giants are wagering the lives of all humanity on the superintelligence race, ignoring potential catastrophic risks. The tech community’s reaction to this allegation has been polarized; a camp led by the open-source platform Hugging Face has expressed strong skepticism, arguing that current concerns regarding AI doomsday theories lack empirical evidence. They have even likened the discourse to "air conditioning repairmen talking about climate change," implying that some engineers may be exaggerating the social impact of technological development while lacking a comprehensive understanding of macro-systems.
  
  However, significant differences remain in how various sources assess AI risks, and some information remains to be clarified. Coxon’s argument focuses on the psychological expectations and potential technical loss of control among internal practitioners, yet he has not proposed a specific path to technical destruction. Conversely, the rebuttal from the CEO of Hugging Face emphasizes an alternative perspective from the open-source community on technology governance, suggesting that fears surrounding closed-source development may actually hinder transparency and safety.
  
  Furthermore, regarding the information environment in East Asia, a report by Voice of America (VOA) noted that the distributor for Reuters had faced information filtering issues, including news related to protests in Hong Kong; even some reports from China’s official news agency, Xinhua, had been blocked. Such restrictions on the dissemination of information lead to vastly different perceptions of AI policy and geopolitical conflicts across different regions.
  
  These events have had tangible impacts on global energy, industrial policy, and social stability. First, the Ukrainian drone attacks on energy facilities directly threaten the stability of transnational energy supply chains and may indirectly affect price volatility for energy-importing nations in East Asia. Second, the controversy over the safety of AI research has forced governments worldwide to re-examine their regulatory frameworks for artificial intelligence. If tech giants fail to effectively address concerns from their own engineers regarding "AI spiraling out of control," it may trigger stricter administrative interventions, which would in turn affect the pace of R&D and capital flow in the global AI industry.
  
  Moreover, restricted information flow, particularly in East Asia, not only affects the public's right to know about major public issues but also deepens the divide within the international community regarding technology governance and political transparency.
  
  Despite the heated discussion surrounding AI doomsday theories, there are clear limitations to the perspectives involved, and they should not be over-interpreted. Coxon’s view reflects the anxiety of some top researchers, but this does not mean that AI will inevitably lead to destruction, nor can it serve as the sole basis for dismissing technological progress. Similarly, while the analogy of air conditioning repairmen discussing climate change is ironic, it may also overlook the critical role that technology developers play in system architecture.
  
  Additionally, the attacks on energy facilities during geopolitical conflicts are the result of specific military actions. While they share the category of global security issues with AI development risks, there is no direct causal link between the two, and observers should avoid conflating them to prevent misjudgments of the global situation.
  
  Future observations should focus on three aspects: First, whether cutting-edge AI laboratories will adjust their R&D transparency policies in response to internal waves of resignations and public opinion pressure; second, whether international coordination mechanisms for the protection of energy infrastructure can effectively address the threats posed by drone attacks; and third, how East Asia will adjust its digital governance strategy in the face of dual pressures from information blockades and technological competition. These issues will continue to influence the path of global technological development and geopolitical stability in the coming years, and are worthy of close attention.

# AI發展與地緣衝突風險：科技安全爭議與能源設施受襲

> 近期全球科技界針對 AI 失控風險爆發激烈辯論，同時東亞資訊審查與烏克蘭無人機襲擊事件，亦引發國際對能源安全與資訊流通的高度關注。


前 Anthropic 與 OpenAI 核心技術研究員雅各布・考克森（Jacob Coxon）於 2026 年 9 月下旬公開離職，並針對人工智慧（AI）發展發出嚴厲警告。考克森指控，目前尖端實驗室內部的從業者普遍存在一種「末日心態」，即真心相信 AI 可能在 2030 年前導致人類滅絕。此事件發生在人工智慧競賽白熱化的背景下，各大科技巨頭正投入巨額資本與運算資源，以期在超級智慧研發上取得領先。
與此同時，地緣政治領域亦不平靜，根據 Liveuamap 於 2026 年 9 月 21 日的報導，烏克蘭於 9 月 20 日發動的無人機攻擊，已造成相關能源設施運作中斷，顯示全球能源基礎設施在衝突下的脆弱性。
多個媒體來源共同確認，科技界對於 AI 未來發展的風險評估已出現嚴重分歧。考克森的離職宣告明確指出，科技巨頭正將全人類的生命安全作為超級智慧競賽的賭注，忽視了潛在的毀滅性風險。針對此項指控，科技圈反應兩極，以開源平臺 Hugging Face 為首的陣營對此表達了強烈的質疑，認為目前針對 AI 末日論的擔憂缺乏實證基礎，甚至將其比喻為「冷氣維修工談論氣候變遷」，意指部分工程師可能過度誇大了技術發展的社會衝擊，卻缺乏對宏觀系統的完整理解。
然而，各來源對於 AI 風險的評估仍存在顯著差異，且部分資訊尚待釐清。考克森的說法側重於內部從業者的心理預期與潛在的技術失控，但並未提出具體的技術毀滅路徑；而 Hugging Face 執行長的反駁則強調了開源社群對技術治理的另一種觀點，即認為封閉式的研發恐懼反而阻礙了技術的透明度與安全性。
此外，在東亞地區的資訊環境中，根據美國之音（VOA）的報導，路透社的發行商曾面臨資訊過濾問題，包括香港示威相關新聞在內，甚至連中國官方通訊社新華社的部分報導也曾遭到封鎖。這種資訊傳播的限制，使得全球對於 AI 政策與地緣政治衝突的認知，在不同地區呈現出截然不同的樣貌。
這些事件對全球能源、產業政策及社會穩定產生了具體影響。首先，烏克蘭無人機對能源設施的攻擊，直接威脅到跨國能源供應鏈的穩定性，並可能對東亞能源進口國的價格波動造成間接影響。其次，AI 研發的安全性爭議，已迫使各國政府重新審視人工智慧的監管框架。若科技巨頭無法有效回應內部工程師對「AI 失控」的擔憂，未來可能會引發更嚴格的行政幹預，進而影響全球 AI 產業的研發節奏與資本流向。
此外，資訊流通的受限，特別是在東亞地區，不僅影響了公眾對重大公共議題的知情權，也加深了國際社會在科技治理與政治透明度上的隔閡。
儘管各界對 AI 末日論的討論熱烈，但各方觀點仍存在明顯限制，不應過度解讀。考克森的觀點反映了部分頂尖研究人員的焦慮，但這並不代表 AI 必然會走向毀滅，亦不能作為否定技術進步的唯一依據。同樣地，將 AI 風險比喻為冷氣維修工談氣候變遷，雖具備諷刺意味，卻也可能忽略了技術開發者在系統架構中的關鍵角色。
此外，地緣政治衝突中的能源設施受襲，是具體的軍事行動結果，與 AI 發展的風險雖同屬全球安全議題，但兩者在因果關係上並無直接連結，觀察者應避免將其混為一談，以免造成對全球局勢的誤判。
後續觀察重點應聚焦於三個面向：第一，尖端 AI 實驗室是否會因應內部離職潮與輿論壓力，調整其研發透明度政策；第二，國際社會針對能源基礎設施保護的協調機制，是否能有效因應無人機攻擊帶來的威脅；第三，東亞地區在面對資訊封鎖與科技競爭的雙重壓力下，將如何調整其數位治理策略。這些議題將持續影響未來數年的全球科技發展路徑與地緣政治穩定，值得各界密切關注其動態發展。
