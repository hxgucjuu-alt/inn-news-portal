  # APEC Sub-Committee on Customs Procedures Meeting Concludes in Dalian, Focusing on Asia-Pacific Trade Security and Technological Innovation Collaboration
  
  > The APEC Sub-Committee on Customs Procedures (SCCP) held a meeting in Dalian in August 2026, focusing on technology empowerment and trade facilitation, aiming to strengthen supply chain security and customs procedure standardization in the Asia-Pacific region to promote regional economic integration.
  
  The Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation (APEC) Sub-Committee on Customs Procedures (SCCP) held its second plenary meeting of the year from August 22 to 24, 2026, in Dalian, Liaoning, China. This meeting was regarded as one of the key agendas for the 2026 APEC "China Year" in the customs sector. The primary context is the current global trade environment, which faces challenges from geopolitics and supply chain restructuring; member economies urgently need to enhance trade facilitation within the region through technological innovation and standardized procedures. The core objective of the meeting was to implement APEC's vision of building an Asia-Pacific community and to conduct in-depth discussions on the digital transformation of customs supervision, trade security mechanisms, and convenient clearance procedures for cross-border e-commerce.
  
  According to publicly available information and meeting records, the consensus of this SCCP meeting focused on promoting "technology and innovation empowerment." Participating representatives collectively emphasized that in the face of increasingly complex global trade logistics, simple administrative review is no longer sufficient. It is necessary to introduce artificial intelligence, big data analytics, and blockchain technology to achieve real-time sharing of customs data and risk early warning. All member economies agreed that customs declaration procedures should be further simplified, trade barriers reduced, and a closer regional cooperation network established to ensure the resilience of critical supplies and supply chains during public health emergencies or natural disasters.
  
  However, there remain significant differences among member economies regarding the specific timeline and degree of standardization for technology implementation. While a consensus was reached on the general direction of enhancing trade facilitation, some developing member economies expressed concerns regarding the financial burden and technology transfer issues associated with adopting high-cost digital supervision systems. Furthermore, regarding security and privacy protection standards for cross-border data flows, customs authorities in various countries still have different legal regulations and regulatory frameworks in practice. This makes the process of fully realizing "Single Window" interconnection subject to further negotiation in terms of technical integration and legal compatibility. It is currently impossible to confirm the specific details of data-sharing agreements signed by various countries, and this part still requires subsequent bilateral or multilateral talks to be clarified.
  
  The conclusions of this meeting have a significant impact on the industrial chain layout in the Asia-Pacific region. Especially for small and medium-sized enterprises that rely on cross-border logistics, the optimization of customs procedures means a reduction in operating costs and an improvement in clearance efficiency. If digital mutual recognition of customs within the region can be successfully promoted in the future, it will help the Asia-Pacific region form a more competitive digital trade ecosystem. For multinational corporations, this policy orientation will prompt them to re-evaluate the configuration of logistics centers in the Asia-Pacific region to utilize a more convenient customs clearance environment to optimize supply chain management, thereby strengthening the position of the Asia-Pacific market in the global economic system.
  
  Although the meeting released positive signals of cooperation, external analysts pointed out that one should not over-interpret the meeting's role in easing geopolitical tensions. Customs technical cooperation belongs to the technical aspect of trade facilitation, and its effectiveness highly depends on political mutual trust and the willingness of member economies to implement it. Against the backdrop of the complex security environment in the Asia-Pacific region, natural disasters faced by many countries (such as the reconstruction challenges following the recent earthquake in the Philippines), and intense competition in regional economic and trade rules, customs collaboration is only a small part of regional integration.
  
  A limiting factor is that the balance point between national security and trade openness varies from country to country, which limits the possibility of complete unification of customs procedures. Overly optimistic expectations may ignore the autonomy and regulatory constraints of various countries in trade policy.
  
  Subsequent observation will focus on whether member economies can translate the technical consensus reached at the SCCP meeting into concrete action plans or regional agreements before the APEC Leaders' Meeting at the end of this year. In particular, investment projects for digital customs infrastructure and how countries address port and logistics disruptions caused by climate change will be the focus of attention for all sectors in the coming months. In addition, the progress of coordination among countries in handling cross-border e-commerce taxation and supervision will also reflect the depth of cooperation among Asia-Pacific economies in digital economic governance. We will continue to track the implementation of relevant policies and the actual execution data of various countries regarding trade facilitation initiatives.

# APEC海關分委會大連會議落幕，聚焦亞太貿易安全與技術創新協作

> 亞太經合組織（APEC）海關手續分委會於2026年8月在大連召開會議，聚焦技術賦能與貿易便利化，旨在強化亞太區域供應鏈安全與海關程式標準化，推動區域經濟整合。


亞太經合組織（APEC）海關手續分委會（SCCP）於2026年8月22日至24日在中國遼寧大連舉行了該年度的第二次全體會議。本次會議被視為2026年APEC「中國年」在海關領域的重要議程之一，主要背景在於當前全球貿易環境面臨地緣政治與供應鏈重組的挑戰，各成員體亟需透過技術創新與標準化程式，提升區域內的貿易便利化水平。會議的核心目標在於落實APEC關於建設亞太共同體的願景，並針對海關監管的數位化轉型、貿易安全機制以及跨境電商的便捷通關程式進行深入研討。
根據多方公開資訊與會議記錄顯示，本次SCCP會議的共識重點在於推動「技術與創新賦能」。與會代表共同強調，面對日益複雜的全球貿易物流，單純的行政審查已不足以應對，必須引入人工智慧、大資料分析與區塊鏈技術，以實現海關資料的即時共享與風險預警。各成員體一致同意，應進一步簡化海關申報手續，減少貿易壁壘，並透過建立更為緊密的區域合作網路，確保在突發公共衛生事件或自然災害期間，關鍵物資與供應鏈的韌性不會中斷。
然而，關於技術落實的具體時程與標準化程度，各成員體間仍存在顯著差異。雖然在提升貿易便利化的大方向上達成共識，但部分開發中成員體對於採用高成本數位監管系統的財務負擔與技術轉移問題表示擔憂。此外，針對跨境資料流動的安全性與隱私保護標準，各國海關在實務操作上仍有不同的法律規範與監管框架，這使得全面實現「單一視窗」互聯互通的程式，在技術對接與法律相容性上仍有待進一步協商。目前尚無法確認各國在資料共享協議上的具體簽署細節，這部分仍需後續的雙邊或多邊會談予以釐清。
本次會議的結論對亞太地區的產業鏈佈局具有顯著影響。特別是對於依賴跨境物流的中小型企業而言，海關程式的最佳化意味著營運成本的降低與通關效率的提升。若未來能成功推動區域內的海關數位互認，將有助於亞太地區形成更具競爭力的數位貿易生態圈。對於跨國企業而言，這項政策導向將促使其重新評估在亞太地區的物流中心配置，以利用更為便利的通關環境來最佳化供應鏈管理，進而強化亞太市場在全球經濟體系中的地位。
儘管會議釋出了積極的合作訊號，但外界分析指出，不應過度解讀此次會議對地緣政治緊張局勢的緩解作用。海關技術合作屬於貿易便利化的技術性層面，其成效高度依賴於各成員體間的政治互信與執行意願。在當前亞太地區安全環境複雜、多國面臨自然災害（如近期菲律賓地震後的重建挑戰）及區域經貿規則競爭激烈的背景下，海關協作僅是區域整合的一小部分。
限制因素在於，各國在國家安全與貿易開放之間的平衡點不盡相同，這限制了海關程式完全統一的可能性，過度樂觀的預期可能會忽視各國在貿易政策上的自主許可權制。
後續觀察重點將集中在今年年底APEC領袖會議前，各成員體是否能將SCCP會議達成的技術共識轉化為具體的行動計畫或區域協定。特別是針對數位海關基礎設施的投資專案，以及各國如何應對氣候變遷導致的港口與物流中斷問題，將是未來幾個月內各界關注的焦點。此外，各國海關在處理跨境電商稅收與監管方面的協調進度，亦將反映出亞太經濟體在數位經濟治理上的合作深度。我們將持續追蹤相關政策的落地情形，以及各國在貿易便利化倡議上的實際執行資料。
