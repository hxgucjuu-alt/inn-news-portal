  # Eastern German State Government Seeks to Cut Funding for "Anti-German Art," Reigniting Debate Over Cultural Subsidy Policy
  
  > The Alternative for Germany (AfD) parliamentary group in Saxony-Anhalt has proposed cutting public funding for "anti-German art," sparking debate within Germany over the boundaries of cultural subsidies and artistic freedom.
  
  The Alternative for Germany (AfD) party's cultural policy moves in the Saxony-Anhalt state parliament have recently become a focus of attention in German cultural and political circles. According to a report by Deutsche Welle (DW), the party's cultural policy spokesperson in the Saxony-Anhalt state parliament, Hans-Thomas Tillschneider, made remarks in the state parliament in late June, clearly stating that the party has declared in its governing platform that it no longer intends to use public funds to support so-called "anti-German art."
  
  This statement directly touches on Germany's long-standing sensitivities regarding public cultural funding, and has prompted outsiders to re-examine the influence of far-right parties on cultural policy within local governing coalitions.
  
  Saxony-Anhalt is located in eastern Germany and is one of the federal states of the former East Germany. The state is currently governed by a coalition of the Christian Democratic Union (CDU), the Social Democratic Party (SPD), and the AfD, with the AfD holding considerable party strength and political visibility in the state. The fact that the party's cultural policy spokesperson proactively raised the issue of cutting funding for specific art forms is seen as a concrete move to assert dominance over cultural policy within the coalition government. According to the report, Tillschneider's remarks in the state parliament were not a personal opinion but cited provisions of the party's already-adopted governing platform, indicating that this policy direction has formed an official consensus within the party rather than being an ad hoc individual proposal.
  
  Based on currently available information, the DW report is the only media source to have directly interviewed and reported on this event. The report explicitly quotes Tillschneider's remarks and notes that they were made in the state parliament. However, more specific details regarding the proposal—such as draft legislation, the projected budget amount to be cut, the list of affected arts institutions or projects, and the initial responses of other coalition parties in the state (CDU and SPD)—are not fully disclosed in the report. Additionally, the report does not clarify whether this policy proposal has entered the formal legislative process in the state parliament or remains at the stage of a party political initiative.
  
  These unspecified details make it difficult for outsiders to fully assess the actual scope of impact and the timeline for advancing the proposal.
  
  The potential impact of this cultural subsidy policy move is not limited to arts organizations in Saxony-Anhalt. Under Germany's federal system, states enjoy a high degree of autonomy over cultural affairs, and local cultural subsidy policies often have a demonstration effect. If Saxony-Anhalt takes the lead in passing a resolution to cut art subsidies on the grounds of being "anti-German," it could encourage the AfD to pursue similar proposals in other state parliaments. For German artists, museums, theaters, and independent cultural institutions, public funding is a vital pillar for maintaining operations. Once subsidy criteria shift from artistic quality to political stance review, it would place direct pressure on creative freedom and cultural diversity.
  
  Contemporary artworks that critically engage with German history, national identity, or social issues would be particularly vulnerable.
  
  Regarding this event, there is a spectrum of differing views within German society. The position supporting subsidy cuts is primarily based on taxpayer rights and national identity, arguing that public funds should not be used to support artistic works that are hostile to or disparage one's own culture. This view holds appeal among some conservative and nationalist-leaning groups. However, mainstream German cultural and academic circles are generally highly vigilant, arguing that the definition of "anti-German art" is vague and subjective, and that once a precedent of reviewing art subsidies by political standards is established, it would seriously erode artistic autonomy and potentially stifle reflection and criticism of history.
  
  It is worth noting that current reports are primarily based on the AfD's unilateral statements, and no formal responses from other state government parties, the Ministry of Culture, or affected institutions have yet been seen. Therefore, this proposal should not be viewed as a finalized policy direction, nor can it be confirmed whether it will ultimately be implemented as concrete budget cuts.
  
  For observing the subsequent development of this event, several key indicators are worth tracking. First, whether the AfD will formally submit this proposal as a state parliament bill and seek support from other parties in the coalition government will be the first step in testing the political feasibility of the proposal. Second, whether cultural institutions and artist communities in Saxony-Anhalt will launch public opposition or advocacy campaigns, and whether federal-level cultural ministries and leaders of other parties will comment on the matter, will all influence public opinion. Additionally, the AfD's overall election strategy ahead of the 2025 federal election, as well as the party's cultural policy positions in other states, will provide broader political context for this event.
  
  Until more official documents, cross-party responses, and specific budget data are made public, it can only be confirmed at this stage that the AfD has proposed this policy direction in the Saxony-Anhalt state parliament, and its subsequent development remains to be further observed.

# 德東邦府擬削減「反德藝術」補助 文化補助政策再掀論戰

> 德國選擇黨薩克森-安哈爾特州議會黨團提出削減公共資金對「反德藝術」的資助，引發德國境內對於文化補助界限與藝術自由的辯論。


德國另類選擇黨（AfD，臺灣媒體多譯為「德國選擇黨」）在薩克森-安哈爾特州議會的文化政策動向，近期成為德國文化圈與政治輿論的關注焦點。根據德國之聲（DW）的報導，該黨薩克森-安哈爾特州議會黨團文化政策發言人漢斯-託馬斯‧蒂爾施奈德（Hans-Thomas Tillschneider）於6月下旬在州議會發表談話，明確表示該黨在執政綱領中已宣告，不再打算動用公共資金來支援所謂的「反德藝術」。
此一表態直接觸及德國長期以來對於公共文化資助的敏感神經，也讓外界重新檢視極右翼政黨在地方執政聯盟中對文化政策的影響力。
薩克森-安哈爾特位於德國東部，是前東德地區的聯邦州之一。該州目前由基民盟（CDU）、社民黨（SPD）與德國選擇黨組成的聯合政府執政，德國選擇黨在該州具有相當的政黨實力與政治能見度。此次由該黨文化政策發言人主動丟擲削減特定藝術補助的議題，被視為其在聯合政府內爭取文化政策主導權的具體行動。根據報導內容，蒂爾施奈德在州議會的發言並非個人意見，而是引用該黨已透過的執政綱領條文，顯示此政策方向在黨內已形成正式共識，而非臨時提出的個別主張。
從目前已公開的資訊來看，德國之聲的報導是唯一直接針對此事件進行採訪與報導的媒體來源。報導中明確引述了蒂爾施奈德的發言內容，並指出其發言場合為州議會。然而，關於該提案更具體的法律草案細節、預計削減的預算金額、受影響的藝術機構或專案清單，以及州內其他執政聯盟政黨（基民盟與社民黨）的初步回應，目前報導中並未完整揭露。此外，報導亦未說明此項政策主張是否已進入州議會的正式立法程式，或是仍處於政黨政治倡議的階段。
這些未明的細節，使得外界尚難全面評估該提案的實際影響範圍與推動時程。
這項文化補助政策動向的潛在影響，不僅限於薩克森-安哈爾特州的藝文團體。德國聯邦體制下，各州對文化事務擁有高度自治權，地方的文化補助政策往往具有示範效應。若薩克森-安哈爾特州率先透過以「反德」為由削減藝術補助的相關決議，可能鼓舞德國選擇黨在其他聯邦州議會跟進類似主張。對於德國的藝術家、博物館、劇場及獨立文化機構而言，公共資金是維持營運的重要支柱，一旦補助標準從藝術品質轉向政治立場審查，將對創作自由與文化多樣性構成直接壓力。
特別是對於那些以批判性視角處理德國歷史、國家認同或社會議題的當代藝術作品，更可能首當其衝。
針對此事件，德國社會內部存在不同觀點的光譜。支援削減補助的立場，主要從納稅人權利與國家認同出發，主張公共資金不應用於資助敵視或詆毀本國文化的藝術創作，此觀點在部分保守派與民族主義傾向的群體中具有號召力。然而，德國主流文化界與學術界普遍對此抱持高度警覺，認為「反德藝術」的定義模糊且主觀，一旦開啟以政治標準審查藝術補助的先例，將嚴重侵蝕藝術自主性，並可能箝制對歷史的反思與批判。
值得注意的是，目前相關報導仍以德國選擇黨的單方面主張為主，尚未見到州政府其他政黨、文化部或受影響機構的正式回應，因此不宜將此提案視為已成形的政策方向，也無法確認其最終是否會落實為具體的預算刪減。
後續觀察此事件的發展，有幾個關鍵指標值得追蹤。首先，德國選擇黨是否會將此主張正式提交為州議會法案，並爭取聯合政府內其他政黨的支援，將是檢驗該提案政治可行性的第一步。其次，薩克森-安哈爾特州的文化機構與藝術家社群是否會發起公開反對或倡議活動，以及德國聯邦層級的文化部會與其他政黨領袖是否會就此表態，都將影響輿論走向。此外，德國選擇黨在2025年聯邦選舉前的整體選舉策略，以及該黨在其他州的文化政策主張，也將提供此事件更廣泛的政治脈絡。
在更多官方檔案、跨黨派回應與具體預算資料公開之前，現階段僅能確認德國選擇黨在薩克森-安哈爾特州議會提出了此項政策方向，其後續發展仍有待進一步觀察。
