  # Rising International Visibility of African Contemporary Art: Curatorial Shifts from Cape Town to the Venice Biennale
  
  > The influence of African contemporary art in the international art world continues to rise, with the curatorial practices of South Africa's Zeitz MOCAA museum serving as a key indicator. Meanwhile, Cameroonian-born curator Koyo Kouoh has been appointed artistic director of the 2026 Venice Biennale, marking the entry of African art perspectives into the global mainstream.
  
  South Africa's Zeitz Museum of Contemporary Art Africa (Zeitz MOCAA) in Cape Town has in recent years consistently advanced curatorial practices and the reconceptualization of artistic perspectives in African contemporary art. Located in the Victoria & Alfred Waterfront area of Cape Town, the museum has, since its opening, held as its core mission the exhibition of contemporary art from Africa and its diaspora. According to a report by "Museums Island," a platform under Taiwan's Ministry of Culture, the museum's curatorial strategy focuses not only on the display of artworks but also on redefining the position and narrative of African art within global art history.
  
  These efforts have sparked widespread discussion in the international art community, particularly against a backdrop where the global art market and major biennale mechanisms have long been dominated by Europe and the United States. Zeitz MOCAA's practice is regarded as a significant force for structural change.
  
  At the same time, the visibility of African art on the international stage is manifesting in concrete ways. The report notes that Claire Breukel's observations at Art Basel Miami Beach show that the presence of contemporary African artworks at the international art fair has noticeably increased, reflected not only in the number of participating galleries but also in the level of attention collectors and curators pay to African art. These observations echo Zeitz MOCAA's curatorial direction, indicating that market and academic discussions of African contemporary art are heating up in tandem.
  
  Notably, these developments are not isolated events but are closely tied to a broader restructuring of global art power dynamics.
  
  Beyond institutional practice, the influence of individual curators has also become a key factor. According to the official Venice Biennale announcement cited by "Museums Island," Cameroonian-born curator Koyo Kouoh has been appointed artistic director of the 2026 Venice Biennale (Biennale Arte 2026). Kouoh has long enjoyed a distinguished reputation in African and international art circles. She founded the RAW Material Company art center in Dakar and served as director of Kunsthalle Basel. Her curatorial career spans Africa and Europe, with a profound impact on the international dissemination of African art.
  
  This appointment is seen as formal recognition of African curatorial perspectives by the Venice Biennale—an international exhibition with more than a century of history, often hailed as the "Olympics of Art."
  
  However, behind these encouraging developments, there are certain discrepancies and points awaiting confirmation among different sources. First, regarding the specific curatorial cases and exhibition details at Zeitz MOCAA, the "Museums Island" report provides an overall framework but does not delve into the curators, participating artist lists, or run dates of individual exhibitions. Second, although Kouoh's appointment has been confirmed by the official Venice Biennale announcement, the report does not mention the theme of the 2026 Biennale, the number of participating national pavilions, or specific exhibition plans.
  
  Moreover, the observations from Art Basel Miami Beach come from the perspective of a single commentator, Claire Breukel, and their representativeness and scope still require cross-verification through more independent sources. These information gaps remind readers that the current understanding of the internationalization of African art remains provisional, and a complete picture will only emerge after more official data is released.
  
  The impact of these developments on the African art ecosystem is multi-layered. At the industry level, the increased visibility of African artworks at international fairs and auction markets directly affects the economic returns and career trajectories of African artists. In the past, many African artists had to rely on European and American gallery representation to enter the international market. Today, with the growth of local African galleries and art institutions, artists have a wider range of options. At the policy level, government support for the cultural sector has become a key variable. South Africa, Senegal, Nigeria, and other countries have in recent years strengthened investment in contemporary art infrastructure, with Zeitz MOCAA itself being a typical case of the public-private partnership model.
  
  At the social level, the internationalization of African art has also changed how younger generations in Africa perceive their own cultural heritage. Contemporary art is no longer seen as a Western import but as an important vehicle for the expression of African subjectivity.
  
  Despite the clear trends, interpretations of these developments still require caution. First, Africa is a vast continent comprising 54 countries and thousands of languages and cultures; the achievements of any single institution or individual cannot represent the entirety of African art. Zeitz MOCAA is located in South Africa—one of the most economically developed countries on the continent—and its resources and influence cannot be easily replicated in other African nations. Second, whether the international art market's interest in African art represents a long-term structural shift or merely a short-term fad remains undetermined. Historically, African art has attracted attention in the Western world on multiple occasions, but such attention has often failed to translate into sustained systemic support.
  
  Furthermore, while Kouoh's appointment carries symbolic significance, the actual operations of the Venice Biennale remain constrained by complex factors such as funding, politics, and international relations, and the final results can only be assessed after the exhibition opens in 2026.
  
  Future observation should focus on the following aspects: first, whether Zeitz MOCAA will launch more ambitious transnational collaborative exhibitions in the next two years, and how these exhibitions respond to the differences in artistic practice across various regions within Africa. Second, how Kouoh will leverage her position as artistic director of the Venice Biennale—whether she will channel curatorial resources to African local institutions and establish sustainable North-South cooperation mechanisms.
  
  Third, whether the share of African art participation at international fairs such as Art Basel Miami Beach will continue to grow, and whether this will boost international participation in African local art fairs (such as South Africa's FNB Art Joburg and Senegal's Dakar Biennale). Fourth, whether governments and international organizations will adjust cultural policies and funding allocations in response to this wave of attention, particularly in art education and talent development. These indicators will help determine whether the internationalization of African contemporary art is truly entering a phase of structural transformation or is merely part of cyclical fluctuations.

# 非洲當代藝術國際能見度提升：從開普敦到威尼斯雙年展的策展變革

> 非洲當代藝術在國際藝壇影響力持續上升，南非Zeitz MOCAA美術館的策展實踐成為重要觀察指標，同時喀麥隆裔策展人Koyo Kouoh獲任命為2026年威尼斯雙年展藝術總監，標誌非洲藝術觀點進入全球主流視野。

南非開普敦的Zeitz MOCAA當代藝術博物館（Zeitz Museum of Contemporary Art Africa）近年來持續推動非洲當代藝術的策展實踐與藝術觀點重構，這座位於開普敦維多利亞阿爾弗雷德碼頭區的博物館，自開館以來便以展出非洲及其僑民當代藝術為核心使命。根據臺灣文化部轄下「博物之島」平臺的報導，該館的策展策略不僅關注藝術作品的展示，更著重於重新定義非洲藝術在全球藝術史中的位置與敘事方式。
這項努力在國際藝術界引起廣泛討論，特別是在全球藝術市場與大型雙年展機制長期由歐美主導的背景下，Zeitz MOCAA的實踐被視為一股重要的結構性變革力量。
與此同時，非洲藝術在國際舞臺上的能見度正以具體方式呈現。報導中指出，Claire Breukel在Art Basel Miami Beach的觀察顯示，來自非洲的當代藝術作品在該國際藝術博覽會中的存在感明顯增強，這不僅反映在參展畫廊的數量上，也體現在收藏家與策展人對非洲藝術的關注程度。這項觀察與Zeitz MOCAA的策展方向形成呼應，顯示非洲當代藝術的市場與學術討論正同步升溫。
值得注意的是，這些發展並非孤立事件，而是與更廣泛的全球藝術權力結構重組密切相關。
在機構實踐之外，個人策展人的影響力亦成為關鍵因素。根據「博物之島」引述的威尼斯雙年展官方公告，喀麥隆裔策展人Koyo Kouoh已被任命為2026年威尼斯雙年展（Biennale Arte 2026）的藝術總監。Koyo Kouoh長期以來在非洲與國際藝術界享有盛譽，她曾創辦位於達卡的RAW Material Company藝術中心，並在巴塞爾美術館（Kunsthalle Basel）擔任館長，其策展生涯橫跨非洲與歐洲，對非洲藝術的國際傳播有深遠影響。
這項任命被視為威尼斯雙年展——這個擁有超過百年歷史、被譽為「藝術奧林匹克」的國際展覽——對非洲策展觀點的正式肯定。
然而，在這些令人振奮的發展背後，不同來源之間的資訊存在若干差異與尚待確認之處。首先，關於Zeitz MOCAA的具體策展案例與展覽細節，「博物之島」的報導提供了整體框架，但並未深入說明個別展覽的策展人、參展藝術家名單或展期。其次，Koyo Kouoh的任命雖經威尼斯雙年展官方公告確認，但報導中未提及2026年雙年展的主題、參展國家館數量或具體展覽規劃。
此外，Art Basel Miami Beach的觀察來自單一評論者Claire Breukel的視角，其代表性與涵蓋範圍仍有待更多獨立來源交叉驗證。這些資訊缺口提醒讀者，目前對非洲藝術國際化的理解仍屬階段性，完整影象需待更多官方資料發布後方能拼湊。
這些發展對非洲藝術生態系統的影響是多層面的。在產業層面，非洲藝術作品在國際博覽會與拍賣市場的能見度提升，直接影響非洲藝術家的經濟收益與職業發展路徑，過去許多非洲藝術家需依賴歐美畫廊代理才能進入國際市場，如今隨著非洲本土畫廊與藝術機構的成長，藝術家有了更多元的選擇。在政策層面，各國政府對文化產業的支援態度成為關鍵變數，南非、塞內加爾、奈及利亞等國近年來均加強對當代藝術基礎設施的投資，Zeitz MOCAA本身即是公私合夥模式的典型案例。
在社會層面，非洲藝術的國際化也改變了非洲年輕世代對自身文化遺產的認知，當代藝術不再被視為西方進口品，而是非洲主體性表達的重要載體。
儘管趨勢明顯，但對這些發展的解讀仍需保持謹慎。首先，非洲是一個包含54個國家、數千種語言與文化的廣闊大陸，任何單一機構或個人的成就都不能代表整體非洲藝術的樣貌。Zeitz MOCAA位於南非——非洲大陸經濟最發達的國家之一——其資源與影響力難以直接複製到其他非洲國家。其次，國際藝術市場對非洲藝術的興趣是否為長期結構性轉變，抑或僅是短期熱潮，目前尚無定論。歷史上，非洲藝術曾多次在西方世界引起關注，但往往未能轉化為持續的系統性支援。
此外，Koyo Kouoh的任命雖具象徵意義，但威尼斯雙年展的實際運作仍受制於經費、政治與國際關係等複雜因素，其最終成果有待2026年展覽開幕後方能檢驗。
後續觀察的重點應放在以下幾個面向：第一，Zeitz MOCAA在未來兩年內是否會推出更具野心的跨國合作展覽，以及這些展覽如何回應非洲內部不同地區的藝術實踐差異。第二，Koyo Kouoh將如何運用其威尼斯雙年展藝術總監的身分，是否會將策展資源引導至非洲本土機構，並建立可持續的南北合作機制。
第三，Art Basel Miami Beach等國際博覽會中非洲藝術的參展比例是否持續成長，以及這是否帶動非洲本土藝術博覽會（如南非的FNB Art Joburg、塞內加爾的Dakar Biennale）的國際參與度提升。第四，各國政府與國際組織是否會因應這波關注，調整文化政策與資金分配，特別是在藝術教育與人才培育方面。這些觀察指標將有助於判斷非洲當代藝術的國際化是否真正邁入結構性轉變階段，抑或僅是週期性波動的一部分。
