  # Two Travel Agencies and a Tour Guide Have Licenses Revoked Over Forced Shopping; Travel Industry Authority Steps Up Inspections Ahead of National Day
  
  > The Travel Industry Authority (TIA) has recently imposed severe penalties on two travel agencies and one tour guide by revoking their licenses. The primary reason for this action is their involvement in coercing tourists into shopping during inbound tours, which constitutes a serious breach of professional conduct. The authority emphasized that it will intensify enforcement efforts ahead of the National Day Golden Week to eradicate such malpractice.
  
  The Travel Industry Authority (TIA) has recently taken significant enforcement action regarding market order in the tourism industry. Following investigations into two complaints involving forced shopping during inbound tours, the TIA decided to revoke the licenses of two travel agencies and one tour guide. According to the investigation results, the agencies involved, "Guoming Travel" and "Earl Travel," failed to fulfill their responsibilities as licensed entities while hosting tour groups from mainland China in February and April of this year, respectively. They failed to take all reasonable steps to ensure that tourists were not subjected to forced shopping. Such behavior severely damages the reputation of the tourism industry and violates relevant guidelines issued by the authority to protect the rights and interests of tourists. Consequently, the TIA decided to impose the most severe administrative penalty: the revocation of their licenses, prohibiting them from continuing operations.
  
  Based on information from various sources, the two travel agencies clearly failed to properly supervise the behavior of their tour guides when handling tourist itineraries, leading to undue pressure on tourists during their trips. In one instance, the tour guide involved made threatening remarks to tourists while leading the group, forcing them to purchase specific products. The TIA confirmed during its investigation that the tour guide's actions constituted forced shopping. As the managing entity, the travel agencies failed to effectively stop or prevent such violations and must bear the corresponding administrative responsibility.
  
  The outcomes of these two cases demonstrate the regulatory body's determination to combat improper sales practices in the tourism industry, maintain fair market competition, and safeguard the safety and experience of tourists. It also serves as a clear warning to the industry that any behavior that harms the rights and interests of tourists will face legal and administrative sanctions.
  
  Currently, there are still discrepancies in the information reported by various parties regarding the specific details and the amounts involved in the two cases. Although the TIA clearly pointed out that the violations by the two travel agencies occurred in February and April, there is no further public information regarding the details of the employment relationship between the tour guide and the travel agency, nor whether the guide was involved in other undisclosed violations. Furthermore, the exact number of affected tourists, the types of products involved, and the final compensation plans are still in the post-investigation processing stage and have not been fully disclosed to the public.
  
  The public and the industry are still waiting to see if the authorities will conduct a more in-depth review of the past records of the relevant travel agencies to clarify whether there is a systemic pattern of violations, rather than just the individual behavior of a single tour guide.
  
  This incident has profound implications for the tourism industry and public policy in the relevant regions. First, it directly impacts the integrity system of the tourism industry. Especially for the inbound tourism market, forced shopping often creates a negative international image and affects the willingness of tourists to return. For the government, this enforcement action is not only to punish violators but also to reshape market order ahead of peak travel seasons such as the National Day Golden Week. The TIA has clearly stated that it will coordinate with other law enforcement departments to strengthen inspections and strictly regulate high-risk shopping spots and travel itineraries. This will force the tourism industry to re-examine its business models, shifting from reliance on shopping commissions to providing high-quality tourism services to meet increasingly stringent regulatory requirements.
  
  However, scholars and industry experts also remind the public not to over-interpret such incidents as a total collapse of the tourism industry. Although forced shopping still occurs from time to time in some areas, these are the actions of a few "black sheep" and do not represent the norm for the entire tourism industry. At the same time, the authority and enforcement efficiency of regulatory bodies face challenges. How to strike a balance between protecting the rights of tourists and maintaining the flexible operation of the tourism industry is a topic that future policymakers need to reflect upon. In addition, consumers should remain vigilant when participating in low-cost tours to avoid falling into shopping traps due to the desire for cheap prices. Consumer education and regulatory enforcement must go hand in hand to effectively eradicate such social problems.
  
  Future observations will focus on market reactions during the National Day Golden Week. Whether the TIA can effectively curb violations through continuous inspections and high-intensity enforcement will be a key indicator of the effectiveness of these penalties. At the same time, the industry's reaction to the license revocations, and whether travel agencies will proactively adjust itineraries to avoid controversial shopping spots, are also worth noting. In the long run, whether the authorities will further amend relevant regulations to increase criminal liability for forced shopping, or promote a more transparent tourism pricing mechanism, will become an important direction for future tourism policy reform. The public should continue to pay attention to the latest announcements from the TIA to ensure their travel rights are protected.

# 兩間旅行社及導遊涉威迫購物遭撤照 旅監局加強國慶巡查

> 旅遊業監管局近日對兩間旅行社及一名導遊祭出重罰，撤銷其牌照，主因是涉嫌在接待入境旅行團期間威迫旅客購物，嚴重違反專業操守。當局強調將在國慶黃金周前夕加強執法，杜絕同類惡行。


旅遊業監管局（旅監局）近期針對旅遊業市場秩序採取重大執法行動，針對兩宗涉及入境旅行團的威迫購物投訴進行調查，並最終決定撤銷兩間旅行社及一名導遊的牌照。根據調查結果，涉事的「幗名旅遊」及「伯爵旅行社」分別在今年二月及四月接待來自中國大陸的旅遊團期間，未能履行其作為持牌機構應盡的責任，未能採取一切合理步驟確保旅客不受威迫購物。此類行為嚴重損害旅遊業聲譽，亦違反了當局對於保障旅客權益的相關指引，因此旅監局決定採取最嚴厲的行政處分，即撤銷相關牌照，禁止其繼續營運。
綜合各方資訊，兩間旅行社在處理旅客行程時，顯然未有適當監管導遊行為，導致旅客在行程中遭受不當壓力。其中，涉事的導遊在帶團過程中，對旅客作出了具威脅性的言論，強迫旅客購買特定商品。旅監局在調查中確認，該導遊的行為已構成威迫購物的事實，且旅行社作為管理方，未能有效制止或預防此類違規事件發生，需承擔相應的行政責任。
這兩宗案件的處理結果，顯示了監管機構對於打擊旅遊業不正當銷售手法、維護市場公平競爭及保障旅客安全體驗的決心，亦向業界發出明確警示，任何損害旅客權益的行為都將面臨法律與行政制裁。
目前，關於兩宗案件的具體細節與涉案金額，各方報導仍存在部分資訊落差。雖然旅監局明確指出兩間旅行社分別於二月及四月發生違規行為，但對於涉案導遊與旅行社之間的僱傭關係細節，以及該導遊是否同時涉及其他未曝光的違規行為，目前尚無進一步的公開資訊。此外，受影響的旅客具體人數、涉及的商品種類以及最終的賠償方案，目前仍處於調查後的處理階段，尚未完全對外披露。
公眾與業界仍在等待當局是否會針對相關旅行社的過往紀錄進行更深入的審查，以釐清是否存在系統性的違規模式，而非單一的導遊個人行為。
此事件對旅遊產業及相關地區的公共政策影響深遠。首先，這直接衝擊了旅遊業的誠信體系，特別是針對入境旅遊市場，威迫購物行為往往會造成負面的國際形象，影響遊客重遊意願。對於政府而言，這項執法行動不僅是為了懲處違規者，更是為了在國慶黃金周等旅遊旺季前夕，重塑市場秩序。旅監局已明確表示，將聯合其他執法部門加強巡查，針對高風險的購物點及旅遊行程進行嚴格監管，這將迫使旅遊業界重新審視其營運模式，從依賴購物佣金轉向提供高品質的旅遊服務，以符合日益嚴格的監管要求。
然而，學界與業界專家亦提醒，對於此類事件不應過度解讀為旅遊業全面崩壞。儘管威迫購物行為在部分地區仍時有發生，但這屬於少數害群之馬的違規行為，並不代表整體旅遊產業的常態。同時，監管機構的許可權與執法效率亦面臨挑戰，如何在保障旅客權益與維持旅遊業靈活營運之間取得平衡，是未來政策制定者需要深思的課題。此外，消費者在參與低價團時，亦應提高警覺，避免因貪圖便宜而陷入購物陷阱，消費者教育與監管執法需雙管齊下，才能有效根除此類社會問題。
後續觀察重點將集中在國慶黃金週期間的市場反應。旅監局是否能透過持續的巡查與高強度的執法，有效遏止違規行為的發生，將是檢驗此次處分有效性的關鍵指標。同時，業界對於此次撤照事件的反應，以及是否會出現旅行社主動調整行程安排以避開爭議購物點，亦值得關注。長期而言，當局是否會進一步修訂相關法規，加重對於威迫購物行為的刑事責任，抑或是推動更為透明的旅遊定價機制，將成為未來旅遊政策改革的重要觀察方向。公眾應持續關注旅監局的最新公告，以確保自身的旅遊權益獲得保障。
