  # Geopolitical and Cybersecurity Risks in Eurasia: Arctic Strategic Rivalry and Supply Chain Security Crises
  
  > As geopolitical tensions in Eurasia escalate, strategic competition in the Arctic intensifies. Simultaneously, global supply chain cybersecurity vulnerabilities are frequently reported, with enterprises in Ireland, Japan, and South Korea suffering successive hacks, highlighting the severe challenges facing digital infrastructure and cross-border policy coordination.
  
  In the current Eurasian geopolitical landscape, the Arctic region has become a new focal point for strategic competition among nations. According to a report by *Eurasia Review*, U.S. Department of Defense officials have explicitly pointed out that although China is not an Arctic nation, its increasingly frequent activities in the region are viewed as a potential threat to homeland security. The U.S. advocates for the use of multi-layered defense systems and cutting-edge technology to deter, protect against, and defeat threats directed at the homeland. This context reflects the Arctic's status as a strategic pivot point, serving not only as a potential area for logistics and energy development but also as a critical battlefield for great power competition.
  
  Meanwhile, political turmoil within Europe cannot be ignored. Large-scale protests have recently erupted in Slovakia, where citizens have expressed strong dissatisfaction with Prime Minister Robert Fico’s pro-Russian stance. This indicates deep divisions within the Eurasian region regarding foreign policy and national positioning, and these political frictions further increase uncertainty regarding regional stability.
  
  At the level of digital infrastructure, the global supply chain is facing severe cybersecurity challenges. According to the latest cybersecurity weekly report from *iThome*, there has been a recent trend of cascading cyberattacks on global third-party suppliers. The common characteristic of these incidents is "single-point intrusion, chain-reaction impact," where a single IT supplier being hacked often leads to dozens or even hundreds of corporate clients being victimized.
  
  For example, the Irish banking IT service provider Wellington IT was hacked, affecting approximately 40 credit unions; the KARTE platform under the Japanese marketing technology firm PLAID was breached, leaving dozens of companies including JINS, DHC, and Mitsubishi UFJ Bank facing the risk of personal data leaks; and South Korean customer service outsourcing firm MetaM, as well as corporate collaboration platforms Dooray! and DaouOffice, have also reported successive cybersecurity incidents. These events demonstrate that reliance on cross-border digital services has become a shared vulnerability for modern enterprises and government departments.
  
  Regarding the aforementioned events, there are significant differences in fact-checking across various media sources. Concerning supply chain cybersecurity incidents, *iThome* provided a specific list of victimized enterprises and the scope of industrial impact, confirming the connections between multiple cases in Ireland, Japan, and South Korea. However, regarding geopolitical issues, the focus of each source varies. *Eurasia Review* focuses on strategic deployment at the military and defense levels, emphasizing the necessity of the Arctic as a defensive frontline; whereas *Euronews* focuses on social unrest and public backlash within Europe, reporting on the protests by Slovakian citizens against the Prime Minister's diplomatic stance.
  
  As for the issue of precision medicine patent grants reported by *TradingView*, it shows that despite the volatile political and cybersecurity environment, the Eurasian region continues to engage in cross-border technical cooperation and policy promotion within the biotech industry, which forms a sharp contrast to the aforementioned conflict-ridden issues.
  
  These events have had a profound impact on industries and policies in the Eurasian region. First, in the field of cybersecurity, enterprises are being forced to re-evaluate risk management mechanisms for outsourced service providers, and government departments have begun to strengthen scrutiny of critical infrastructure supply chains to prevent digital infiltration. Second, in foreign policy, the tense situation in the Arctic forces nations to seek a balance between energy development, shipping lanes, and national defense security. The political protests in Slovakia reflect that as Eurasian nations handle their relationships with Russia, the rift between domestic public opinion and government decision-making is constantly widening, which will directly affect policy consistency within the European Union and may have a ripple effect on future regional security architectures.
  
  Although these issues have garnered high attention, it is necessary to avoid over-interpretation. Regarding the Arctic issue, while defense officials have issued warnings, the current development and military activities in the region remain limited by complex international law and climatic conditions, and it is not merely a simple military confrontation. Similarly, while supply chain cybersecurity incidents are frequent, most enterprises have initiated contingency procedures, and the scale of victimization and actual losses remain subject to further investigation. Furthermore, while the protests in Slovakia show a divergence in public opinion, there is still high uncertainty as to whether this will lead to a change in government or a fundamental shift in foreign policy. Analysts should distinguish between "strategic intent" and "substantive action" to avoid escalating single events into comprehensive regional conflicts.
  
  Follow-up observations should focus on three aspects: First, whether global supply chain cybersecurity vulnerabilities will trigger stricter cross-border regulatory laws, particularly regarding cybersecurity certification requirements for third-party service providers. Second, whether the substantive interaction between nations regarding energy extraction and military deployment in the Arctic will shift from diplomatic friction to more concrete military confrontation as climate change leads to the opening of shipping lanes. Third, whether the public backlash against pro-Russian policies within Europe will spread to other Central and Eastern European countries, thereby affecting the European Union's collective action capability in energy supply and geopolitics. These variables will determine the future development of the Eurasian region in terms of digital security and geopolitics.

# 歐亞地區地緣政治與資安風險：北極戰略角力與供應鏈資安危機

> 隨著歐亞地緣政治緊張升溫，北極地區戰略競爭加劇，同時全球供應鏈資安漏洞頻傳，愛爾蘭、日本與韓國企業接連受駭，凸顯數位基礎設施與跨國政策協調的嚴峻挑戰。


在當前的歐亞地緣政治版圖中，北極地區已成為各國戰略競爭的新焦點。根據《Eurasia Review》的報導，美國國防部官員曾明確指出，儘管中國並非北極國家，但其在該地區的活動日益頻繁，被視為對國土安全構成潛在威脅。美方主張透過多層次防禦體系與尖端技術，以嚇阻、保護並擊敗針對國土的威脅。此背景反映了北極作為戰略要衝，不僅是物流與能源開發的潛在區域，更是大國博弈的關鍵戰場。
與此同時，歐洲內部的政治動盪亦不容忽視，斯洛伐克近期爆發大規模抗議活動，民眾針對總理羅伯特·菲喬（Robert Fico）的親俄立場表達強烈不滿，顯示歐亞地區在外交政策與國家定位上存在深刻的分歧，這些政治摩擦進一步增加了區域穩定性的不確定性。
在數位基礎設施層面，全球供應鏈正面臨嚴峻的資安挑戰。根據《iThome》的最新資安週報指出，近期全球第三方供應商遭駭事件呈現連環爆發趨勢。這些事件的共同特徵在於「單點入侵、連鎖波及」，即單一IT供應商遭駭後，往往導致數十甚至數百家企業客戶受害。
例如，愛爾蘭銀行IT服務商Wellington IT遭駭，波及約40家信用合作社；日本行銷科技業者PLAID旗下的KARTE平臺遭入侵，導致JINS、DHC及三菱UFJ銀行等數十家企業面臨個資外洩風險；韓國的客服委外業者MetaM及企業協作平臺Dooray!、DaouOffice亦接連傳出資安事故。這些事件顯示，跨國數位服務的依賴性已成為現代企業與政府部門的共同脆弱點。
針對上述事件，各媒體來源在事實確認上存在顯著差異。關於供應鏈資安事件，《iThome》提供了具體的受害企業清單與產業影響範圍，確認了愛爾蘭、日本與韓國多起案例的關聯性。然而，針對地緣政治議題，各來源的關注點則有所不同。《Eurasia Review》聚焦於軍事與國防層面的戰略部署，強調北極作為防禦前線的必要性；而《Euronews》則側重於歐洲內部的社會動盪與民意反彈，報導斯洛伐克民眾對總理外交立場的抗議。
至於《TradingView》報導的精準醫療專利授予議題，則顯示在動盪的政治與資安環境下，歐亞地區在生技產業仍持續進行跨國技術合作與政策推動，這與前述的衝突性議題形成了鮮明對比。
這些事件對歐亞地區的產業與政策產生了深遠影響。首先，在資安領域，企業被迫重新評估委外服務商的風險管理機制，政府部門亦開始加強對關鍵基礎設施供應鏈的審查，以防範數位滲透。其次，在外交政策上，北極地區的緊張局勢迫使各國必須在能源開發、航運通道與國防安全之間尋求平衡。斯洛伐克的政治抗議則反映出，歐亞國家在處理與俄羅斯關係時，國內民意與政府決策之間的裂痕正不斷擴大，這將直接影響歐盟內部的政策一致性，並可能對未來的區域安全架構產生連鎖效應。
儘管各界對上述議題高度關注，但仍需避免過度解讀。針對北極問題，雖有國防官員提出警告，但該地區目前的開發與軍事活動仍受限於複雜的國際法與氣候條件，並非單純的軍事對抗。同樣地，供應鏈資安事件雖頻繁，但多數企業已啟動應變程式，受害規模與實際損失尚待進一步調查。此外，斯洛伐克的抗議活動雖顯示民意分歧，但其是否會導致政府更迭或外交政策的根本轉向，仍存在高度不確定性。各界在分析時應區分「戰略意圖」與「實質行動」的差異，避免將單一事件無限上綱為區域性的全面衝突。
後續觀察重點應聚焦於三個面向：第一，全球供應鏈資安漏洞是否會引發更嚴格的跨國監管法規，特別是針對第三方服務商的資安認證要求。第二，北極地區在氣候變遷導致航道開放後，各國在能源開採與軍事佈署上的實質互動，是否會從外交摩擦轉向更具體的軍事對峙。第三，歐洲內部對於親俄政策的民意反彈，是否會擴散至其他中東歐國家，進而影響歐盟在能源供應與地緣政治上的集體行動能力。這些變數將決定未來歐亞地區在數位安全與地緣政治上的發展走向。
