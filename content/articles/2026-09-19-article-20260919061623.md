  # Geography and Regional Definition of Oceania: A Comprehensive Analysis from Historical Context to Modern Transnational Scope
  
  > This article examines the geographical definition and regional scope of Oceania, explores the positioning of Australia, New Zealand, and Pacific Island nations within historical and modern geopolitics, and clarifies the differences between various academic and practical definitions.
  
  Oceania, as a unique regional concept in global geography, has long been viewed as a "maritime continent" composed of the Australian continental platform and the island groups of Melanesia, Micronesia, and Polynesia. According to the foundational definitions in geography and historical research, this region encompasses not only Australasia (Australia and New Zealand) but also Pacific island nations and territories such as Fiji, Papua New Guinea, Easter Island, and Hawaii.
  
  The historical development of Oceania is closely linked to its geographical characteristics. As most nations in the region are island states lacking traditional land borders, the advancement of humanitarian, security, and public policy initiatives often relies on transnational cooperation mechanisms and is constrained by the unique nature of the marine environment.
  
  Multiple public sources confirm that the geographical scope of Oceania is not a single, rigid definition. Data sources such as Factsinstitute and Wikipedia consistently point out that the core composition of Oceania includes Australia, New Zealand, and the three major Pacific island groups. In a historical context, these areas have formed a close and complex interactive network due to colonial history, indigenous cultures, and the establishment of modern sovereign states. As the economic and political core of the region, Australia and New Zealand play key roles in regional security issues and serve as primary drivers for various international organizations and public health policies.
  
  For example, the New Zealand government utilizes official information platforms to systematically integrate information and issue policies regarding public health challenges within the region (such as the response to the COVID-19 pandemic), demonstrating a high demand for information collaboration when facing transnational public crises.
  
  However, there are significant cognitive differences between various sources regarding the definition of Oceania's boundaries, which remains a key point to be clarified in current geographical and geopolitical discussions. According to the broad definition provided by Simple English Wikipedia, the scope of Oceania can even extend between the Asian and American continents, including the Japanese archipelago, Taiwan, and the Aleutian Islands in the Pacific Rim. This "broadest" definition creates a significant discrepancy with traditional geographical definitions that focus solely on Australia and the Pacific islands.
  
  Furthermore, the use of "Oceania" by commercial entities such as Oceania Cruises tends to favor geographical marketing for travel routes, which is vastly different from the rigorous geographical boundary demarcations used in academia. These discrepancies in definition indicate that the term "Oceania" may simultaneously refer to geographical plates, cultural regions, or commercial service scopes depending on the context; readers should distinguish between these application scenarios when referencing the term.
  
  The geographical characteristics of Oceania have a direct impact on the region's industrial development and public policy. Because most countries in the region are islands, transportation and logistics costs are key factors affecting economic development. Taking the tourism industry as an example, transnational cruise services view Oceania as an important travel destination, which not only promotes the regional tourism economy but also drives the construction of related port infrastructure. However, this reliance on maritime transport also makes the region face more severe challenges than continental countries when dealing with climate change, rising sea levels, and the spread of infectious diseases.
  
  Policymakers must strike a balance between promoting regional openness and maintaining border security, especially in the scheduling of public health responses and humanitarian aid supplies; geographical dispersion remains a major constraint on policy implementation.
  
  Regarding regional studies of Oceania, there are various perspectives in academia and public policy, and a single geographical definition should not be viewed as absolute truth. Some perspectives emphasize the "maritime nature" of Oceania, arguing that the collective interests of Pacific Island nations should be placed at the core; another perspective tends to view Australia and New Zealand as bridges connecting to Asia. The limitation is that due to the lack of a unified international standard definition, countries often adjust their scope based on their own needs when compiling statistical data and planning regional cooperation. Therefore, when reading related reports, it is essential to note whether the defined scope includes the Pacific island chain or is limited to the traditional South Pacific region, to avoid misinterpreting regional geopolitics due to confusion over definitions.
  
  Future observations should focus on the evolution of Oceania within international climate change agreements and regional security frameworks. As Pacific Island nations increase their focus on the issue of rising sea levels, the role of Australia and New Zealand in humanitarian aid within the region will become increasingly significant. Furthermore, with the popularization of digital information platforms, such as transnational information-sharing mechanisms like the New Zealand health information platform, whether these can be further integrated into a regional public health monitoring network will be an important indicator for observing Oceania's regional governance capabilities. It is recommended to continuously track the latest reports released by relevant government departments and international organizations to grasp Oceania's changing position in global geopolitics.

# 大洋洲地理與區域定義：從歷史脈絡至現代跨國範疇的綜合分析

> 本文梳理大洋洲的地理定義與區域範疇，探討澳洲、紐西蘭及太平洋島國在歷史與現代地緣政治中的定位，並釐清不同學術與實務定義間的差異。


大洋洲（Oceania）作為全球地理學中獨特的區域概念，長期以來被視為由澳洲大陸平臺以及美拉尼西亞、密克羅尼西亞與玻里尼西亞等群島組成的「海洋大陸」。根據地理學界與歷史研究的基礎定義，該區域不僅涵蓋了澳洲與紐西蘭（Australasia），亦包含斐濟、巴布亞紐幾內亞、復活節島及夏威夷等太平洋島國與領地。
大洋洲的歷史發展與其地理特性緊密相連，由於該區域多數國家為島嶼國家，缺乏傳統陸地邊界，這使得其人道、安全與公共政策的推動，往往需仰賴跨國合作機制，並受制於海洋環境的特殊性。
多個公開來源共同確認，大洋洲的地理範疇並非單一且僵化的定義。Factsinstitute 與 Wikipedia 等資料來源一致指出，大洋洲的核心組成包含澳洲、紐西蘭以及太平洋三大群島區。在歷史脈絡中，這些區域因殖民歷史、原住民文化及現代主權國家的建立，形成了緊密且複雜的互動網路。澳洲與紐西蘭作為該區域的經濟與政治核心，不僅在區域安全議題上扮演關鍵角色，同時也是各類國際組織與公共衛生政策的主要推動者。
例如，紐西蘭政府透過官方資訊平臺，針對區域內的公共衛生挑戰（如 COVID-19 疫情應對）進行系統性的資訊整合與政策發布，顯示出該區域在面對跨國公共危機時，具有高度的資訊協作需求。
然而，關於大洋洲的邊界定義，不同來源之間存在顯著的認知差異，這也是目前地理與地緣政治討論中尚待釐清的重點。根據 Simple English Wikipedia 的廣義定義，大洋洲的範疇甚至可延伸至亞洲大陸與美洲之間，包含環太平洋地區的日本列島、臺灣以及阿留申群島。這種「最廣義」的定義方式，與傳統地理學中僅聚焦於澳洲及太平洋島嶼的定義產生了顯著落差。
此外，商業機構如 Oceania Cruises 對於「大洋洲」的使用則偏向旅遊航線的地理行銷，與學術界嚴謹的地理邊界劃分目的迥異。這些定義上的分歧，顯示出「大洋洲」一詞在不同語境下，可能同時指涉地理板塊、文化區域或商業服務範疇，讀者在參考時需區分其應用情境。
大洋洲的地理特性對該區域的產業發展與公共政策產生了直接影響。由於區域內多數國家為島嶼，交通運輸與物流成本成為影響經濟發展的關鍵因素。以旅遊產業為例，跨國郵輪服務將大洋洲視為重要的旅遊目的地，這不僅促進了區域內的觀光經濟，也帶動了相關港口基礎設施的建設。然而，這種對海洋交通的依賴，同時也使該區域在面對氣候變遷、海平面上升及傳染性疾病傳播時，面臨比大陸型國家更嚴峻的挑戰。
政策制定者必須在促進區域開放與維護邊境安全之間取得平衡，特別是在公共衛生應對與人道救援物資的排程上，地理上的分散性始終是政策執行的主要限制。
針對大洋洲的區域研究，學界與公共政策領域存在多種觀點，不應將單一的地理定義視為絕對真理。部分觀點強調大洋洲的「海洋性」，認為應將太平洋島國的集體利益置於核心；另一觀點則傾向將澳洲與紐西蘭視為與亞洲連結的橋樑。限制在於，由於缺乏統一的國際標準定義，各國在統計資料與區域合作規劃時，往往會根據自身需求調整範疇。因此，在閱讀相關報導時，必須留意其定義範圍是否包含環太平洋島鏈，或是僅限於傳統的南太平洋區域，避免因定義混淆而對區域地緣政治產生錯誤解讀。
後續觀察重點應聚焦於大洋洲在國際氣候變遷協議與區域安全架構中的演變。隨著太平洋島國對海平面上升議題的關注度提升，澳洲與紐西蘭在區域內的人道援助角色將更為吃重。此外，隨著數位化資訊平臺的普及，如紐西蘭健康資訊平臺等跨國資訊共享機制，未來是否能進一步整合為區域性的公共衛生監控網路，將是觀察大洋洲區域治理能力的重要指標。建議持續追蹤相關政府部門與國際組織發布的最新報告，以掌握大洋洲在變動中的全球地緣政治地位。
