  # Supermarket Refrigeration Systems Across Seven European Countries Hacked, Leading to Food Spoilage
  
  > On November 2, refrigeration units at over 115 supermarkets across seven European countries were compromised by hackers, who manipulated temperature control systems to cause food spoilage. This major cybersecurity incident highlights global vulnerabilities in IoT device security and has sparked international concern regarding public health and supply chain resilience.
  
  A large-scale cybersecurity attack occurred in Europe on November 2, affecting over 115 supermarkets across seven countries where refrigeration and freezing control systems were breached by hackers. According to a report by the cybersecurity media outlet *Ithome*, hackers successfully gained remote control of this critical infrastructure and deliberately raised the temperature settings of the refrigeration units, causing large quantities of fresh food stored in the supermarkets to spoil due to abnormal temperature control. This incident not only caused direct economic losses for retailers but also raised serious questions about the security protection mechanisms of smart Internet of Things (IoT) devices, given the implications for food safety and public health.
  
  Currently, the affected supermarket operators are working with cybersecurity experts to clarify the specific methods of the intrusion and are handling the spoiled food to prevent potential threats to consumer health.
  
  According to preliminary investigations and summaries by *Ithome*, the core of this attack lies in remote access vulnerabilities within Industrial Control Systems (ICS). The hackers did not directly damage the hardware; instead, they manipulated the software interface to adjust the cold chain system's temperature settings to an unsafe range, leaving food exposed to high temperatures for an extended period. Although no specific hacker group has claimed responsibility, this type of attack on critical public infrastructure is highly similar to the ransomware attack patterns seen in recent years targeting supply chains and logistics systems. Governments and cybersecurity regulatory agencies have intervened in the investigation and have required relevant operators to strengthen device authentication and network isolation measures to prevent similar incidents from recurring.
  
  Regarding the details of the incident, there are still discrepancies and points awaiting confirmation among various parties. While *Ithome* clearly stated that the scope of the impact covers seven European countries and over 115 supermarkets, the specific list of countries involved, the brands of the attacked devices, and the technical details of the intrusion (such as whether it involved supply chain software update vulnerabilities or weak password vulnerabilities) are still under investigation, and no detailed technical report has been released to the public. Furthermore, supermarket operators in various countries have yet to provide unified statistics on the specific quantity of spoiled food and the total economic loss.
  
  Because food safety regulations vary across European countries, the subsequent attribution of compensation liability and public health tracking procedures are expected to become the focus of cross-border coordination.
  
  This incident has had a direct impact on the retail industry and public health policies in Europe. First, as the final nodes in the food supply chain, the stability of supermarket cold chain systems is directly related to public dietary safety; this hacker attack exposed serious deficiencies in the cybersecurity protection of smart management systems, forcing the retail industry to re-evaluate its deployment strategies for IoT devices. Second, health departments in various countries have initiated emergency inspection procedures for the affected supermarkets to ensure that spoiled food has not entered the market and have called on relevant operators to conduct immediate system security reviews.
  
  This event may also prompt the European Parliament to accelerate the formulation of stricter cybersecurity standards for smart buildings and Industrial IoT devices, elevating the digital resilience of infrastructure to the level of national security.
  
  However, the public should remain cautious in interpreting such events and avoid excessive panic. Cybersecurity experts point out that although this incident caused food loss, it does not mean that all smart refrigeration systems are at high risk. Many supermarket systems were designed with offline backup mechanisms; the large-scale damage may stem from system vulnerabilities in specific software providers rather than a general technical flaw in the industry. Furthermore, there is currently no substantive evidence to support linking this event to other international geopolitical events (such as the results of the U.S. midterm elections) or interpreting it in an overly politicized manner.
  
  The public should focus on whether device manufacturers can provide patches in a timely manner and whether companies are implementing the supervisory responsibilities of their Chief Information Security Officers (CISO), rather than viewing a single cybersecurity incident as an indicator of comprehensive social collapse.
  
  Subsequent observation will focus on three aspects: first, the technical tracing of the hacker intrusion, specifically whether it involved a "supply chain attack" targeting a specific software provider; second, the food safety disposal processes of the affected supermarkets and how governments can conduct cross-border judicial cooperation regarding this new type of cybersecurity attack; and third, adjustments to cybersecurity procurement specifications for IoT devices in the global retail industry. As technology evolves, with the increasing popularity of AI next-generation communication technologies in various fields as reported by *Techbang*, ensuring that these innovative technologies do not become backdoors for hackers while improving efficiency will be a key challenge for future global public health and digital governance.

# 歐洲七國超市冷凍系統遭駭客遠端入侵導致食品損毀

> 歐洲七國逾百家超市冷凍裝置於11月2日遭駭客操控，透過竄改溫控系統導致食品變質。此重大資安事件凸顯全球物聯網裝置安全漏洞，亦引發國際對公共衛生與供應鏈韌性的高度關注。


歐洲於11月2日爆發大規模資安攻擊事件，影響範圍橫跨七個國家，超過115家超市的冷藏與冷凍控制系統遭到駭客入侵。根據資安媒體《Ithome》報導，駭客成功取得這些關鍵基礎設施的遠端控制權，並蓄意調高冷凍裝置的溫度設定，導致超市內儲存的生鮮食品因溫控異常而大規模損毀。此事件不僅造成零售業者的直接經濟損失，更因涉及食品安全與公共衛生，引發各界對於智慧連網裝置（IoT）安全防護機制的強烈質疑。
目前，受影響的超市業者正與資安專家合作，試圖釐清駭客入侵的具體途徑，並針對受損食品進行後續處理，以防範對消費者健康造成潛在威脅。
根據《Ithome》的初步調查與彙整，此次攻擊事件的核心在於工業控制系統（ICS）的遠端存取漏洞。駭客並非直接破壞硬體，而是透過操控軟體介面，將冷鏈系統的溫度設定調整至不安全範圍，導致食品長期暴露於高溫環境。儘管目前尚未有具體的駭客組織出面承認責任，但此類針對關鍵民生基礎設施的攻擊手法，與近年來針對供應鏈及物流系統的勒索軟體攻擊模式高度相似。各國政府與資安監管機構已介入調查，並要求相關業者加強裝置的身份驗證與網路隔離措施，以防止類似事件再次發生。
關於事件細節，目前各方資訊仍存在部分差異與待確認之處。雖然《Ithome》明確指出受影響範圍為歐洲七國及超過115家超市，但具體涉及的國家名單、遭受攻擊的裝置品牌，以及駭客入侵的技術細節（如是否涉及供應鏈軟體更新漏洞或弱密碼漏洞），目前仍處於調查階段，尚未對外公開詳細技術報告。此外，對於食品受損的具體數量與經濟損失金額，各國超市業者尚未給出統一的統計資料。
由於歐洲各國對食品安全監管法規不盡相同，後續的賠償責任歸屬與公共衛生追蹤程式，預計將成為跨國協調的重點。
此次事件對歐洲地區的零售業與公共衛生政策產生了直接影響。首先，連鎖超市作為食品供應鏈的末端節點，其冷鏈系統的穩定性直接關係到民眾的飲食安全；此次駭客攻擊暴露了智慧化管理系統在資安防護上的嚴重不足，迫使零售產業必須重新評估物聯網裝置的部署策略。其次，各國衛生部門已針對受影響超市啟動緊急檢查程式，確保變質食品未流入市面，並呼籲相關業者立即進行系統安全性審查。
這場事件亦可能促使歐洲議會加速針對智慧建築與工業物聯網裝置制定更嚴格的資安標準，將基礎設施的數位韌性提升至國家安全層級。
然而，公眾對於此類事件的解讀應保持謹慎，不宜過度恐慌。資安專家指出，雖然此次事件造成了食品損失，但並不代表所有智慧冷凍系統皆處於高風險狀態。許多超市的系統在設計時已具備離線備援機制，此次大規模受損可能源於特定軟體供應商的系統漏洞，而非產業通用的技術缺陷。此外，將此事件與其他國際地緣政治事件（如美國期中選舉結果）進行直接連結或過度政治化解讀，目前並無實質證據支援。
公眾應關注的是裝置製造商是否能及時提供修補程式，以及企業內部是否落實了資安長（CISO）的監督職責，而非將單一資安事件視為全面性的社會崩潰指標。
後續觀察重點將集中在三個面向：第一，駭客入侵的技術溯源，特別是是否涉及針對特定軟體供應商的「供應鏈攻擊」；第二，受影響超市的食品安全處置流程，以及各國政府如何針對此類新型態資安攻擊進行跨國司法協作；第三，全球零售業對於物聯網裝置資安採購規範的調整。隨著技術演進，如《Techbang》所報導的AI次世代通訊技術在各領域的應用日益普及，如何確保這些創新技術在提升效率的同時，不會成為駭客入侵的後門，將是未來全球公共衛生與數位治理的關鍵挑戰。
