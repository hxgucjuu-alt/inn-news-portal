  > Wildfires continue to rage across France and Spain, with the cumulative number of evacuees surpassing 300,000. The blazes are wreaking havoc in Gironde and around Madrid, while high temperatures and strong winds are hampering firefighting efforts.
  
  France and Spain are facing their most severe wildfire crisis in recent years. According to multiple international media reports, as of late July, the total number of people forced to evacuate due to the wildfires in the two countries has exceeded 300,000, with France's southwestern Gironde region and the areas surrounding Spain's capital Madrid being the hardest hit. The wildfires, accompanied by rare extreme heat and dry weather in Southern Europe, have spread rapidly, prompting both governments to issue large-scale evacuation orders and deploy thousands of firefighters and aerial firefighting equipment for rescue operations. The fires remain not fully under control, and the disaster continues to draw international attention.
  
  According to reports from CNN, BBC, Reuters, and Al Jazeera, French authorities ordered approximately 55,000 more residents to evacuate on Saturday night, significantly raising the cumulative number of evacuees in France. Meanwhile, the wildfire situation in Spain is equally severe, with the blaze in Guadalajara province having scorched an estimated 32,000 hectares of land, making it Spain's most severe single wildfire event this year.
  
  Fire departments in both countries stated that due to strong winds and persistently high temperatures, the fires are extremely difficult to control, with some fire lines even spreading in a leaping pattern, threatening more villages and towns.
  
  It is worth noting that media reports vary slightly in the timing and scope of the total evacuation figures. Both CNN and BBC reported an evacuation scale of "over 300,000 people," while Al Jazeera, in its compiled report, emphasized the magnitude of "hundreds of thousands" and noted that the areas around Bordeaux and Madrid are among "the most severe fire zones in Europe in recent years." Additionally, Reuters' report was published earlier, when the recorded number of evacuees had not yet reached 300,000, indicating that the disaster deteriorated sharply within a matter of days. Regarding the specific affected area and the number of damaged buildings, sources have not yet provided complete and consistent official statistics, and some data still await further confirmation from national authorities.
  
  The wildfires have had a direct socioeconomic impact on France and Spain. The large-scale evacuations have temporarily displaced hundreds of thousands of residents, disrupted multiple major highways and railway lines, and severely affected tourism and agricultural activities. In France's Gironde region, the natural and agricultural resources renowned for pine forests and wine are facing devastating losses; in central Spain, livestock farming and olive cultivation have also suffered significant damage from the fires. Both governments have activated emergency response mechanisms, mobilized military personnel to assist in relief efforts, and opened temporary shelters to accommodate displaced residents. The European Union has also expressed willingness to provide cross-border assistance to help the affected countries through the crisis.
  
  However, experts and some local officials have cautioned against oversimplifying the link between the causes of the fires and climate change. While high temperatures and drought have indeed exacerbated the spread of the fires, the points of origin for some blazes remain under investigation, and the proportion of human versus natural factors has yet to be clarified. Moreover, the evacuation figures and affected areas reported by various media are mostly preliminary estimates, and the actual numbers may be adjusted as the disaster evolves. Al Jazeera also noted in its report that communication disruptions in some remote areas could lead to underestimation of disaster statistics, so the current data should be viewed as "at least" rather than "precise" figures of the disaster's scale.

The French and Spanish governments are expected to release more complete damage assessments and reconstruction plans in the near future, and whether the EU-level climate disaster response mechanism will be activated will also become a focus of international attention. Additionally, as the number of evacuees continues to rise, long-term issues such as the resettlement of disaster victims and psychological support are expected to become major challenges that both countries must confront in the days ahead.

# 南歐野火延燒逾30萬人撤離 法西兩國全力救災

> 法國與西班牙野火持續延燒，累計疏散人數突破30萬，火勢在吉倫特省與馬德里周邊肆虐，高溫強風助長救災困難。

法國與西班牙正面臨近年來最嚴重的野火危機。根據多家國際媒體報導，截至7月下旬，兩國因野火被迫撤離的總人數已超過30萬人，其中法國西南部吉倫特省（Gironde）與西班牙首都馬德里周邊地區受災最為嚴重。這場野火伴隨南歐罕見的極端高溫與乾燥天氣，火勢蔓延迅速，迫使兩國政府發布大規模疏散令，並投入數千名消防人員與空中滅火裝置進行搶救。目前火勢仍未完全受控，災情持續引發國際社會關注。
綜合美國有線電視新聞網（CNN）、英國廣播公司（BBC）、路透社（Reuters）與半島電視臺（Al Jazeera）等媒體報導，法國當局在週六晚間再度下令約5.5萬名居民撤離，使得法國境內累計疏散人數大幅上升。與此同時，西班牙的野火災情同樣嚴峻，其中位於瓜達拉哈拉省（Guadalajara）的火場已燒毀估計約3.2萬公頃土地，成為西班牙今年以來最嚴重的單一野火事件。
兩國消防部門表示，由於風勢強勁且氣溫持續偏高，火勢極難控制，部分火線甚至出現跳躍式蔓延，威脅更多村莊與城鎮。
值得注意的是，各媒體對於撤離總人數的統計時間點與範圍略有差異。CNN與BBC均報導「超過30萬人」的疏散規模，但半島電視臺在彙整報導中則強調「數十萬人」的數量級，並指出波爾多（Bordeaux）與馬德里周邊是「歐洲近年最嚴重」的火場之一。此外，路透社的報導時間較早，當時統計的疏散人數尚未達到30萬，顯示災情在數日內急劇惡化。關於具體受災面積與建築物損毀數量，目前各來源尚未提供完整一致的官方統計，部分資料仍待各國當局進一步確認。
這場野火對法國與西班牙的社會經濟造成直接衝擊。大規模疏散導致數十萬居民暫時失去家園，多條主要公路與鐵路交通中斷，旅遊業與農業活動亦受到嚴重影響。在法國吉倫特省，當地以松林與葡萄酒聞名的自然與農業資源面臨毀滅性打擊；西班牙中部地區的畜牧業與橄欖種植也因火災蒙受重大損失。兩國政府已啟動緊急應變機制，動員軍隊協助救災，並開放臨時收容中心安置災民。歐盟也表示願意提供跨境援助，協助受災國度過難關。
然而，專家與部分地方官員提醒，對於火災成因與氣候變遷的關聯性，目前不宜過度簡化解讀。雖然高溫與乾旱確實加劇了火勢蔓延，但部分火場的起火點仍在調查中，人為因素與自然因素的比例尚待釐清。此外，各媒體報導的疏散人數與受災面積多為初步估計，實際數字可能隨災情發展而調整。半島電視臺在報導中也指出，部分偏遠地區的通訊中斷可能導致災情統計低估，因此現有資料應視為「至少」而非「精確」的災害規模。
後續觀察重點將集中於火勢是否能在未來數日內獲得控制，以及各國氣象部門預測的降溫或降雨是否如期到來。法國與西班牙政府預計將在近期公佈更完整的災損評估與重建計畫，而歐盟層級的氣候災害應對機制是否啟動，也將成為國際社會關注焦點。此外，隨著疏散人數持續攀升，災民安置與心理健康支援等長期議題，預料將成為兩國接下來必須面對的重大考驗。
