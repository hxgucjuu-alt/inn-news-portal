  # Rising Geopolitical Risks: Nations Strengthen Supply Chain Security and Semiconductor Export Controls
  
  > As global geopolitical risks intensify, the UK Parliament has warned that a Taiwan Strait crisis in 2028 could lead to a semiconductor supply disruption. Meanwhile, Taiwan’s Legislative Yuan is deliberating amendments to the "Trade Act," proposing strict controls on the export of critical integrated circuits to balance national security with industrial compliance.
  
  The global geopolitical situation has recently had a profound impact on the semiconductor supply chain. In early November 2026, the UK Parliament’s Business, Innovation, Science and Trade (BIST) Committee released a 100-page investigation report, issuing a stern warning regarding China's attempts to alter the status quo in the Taiwan Strait. The report indicates that with Taiwan’s presidential election scheduled for 2028, the period between 2028 and 2030 could be the "highest risk period" for a conflict in the Taiwan Strait.
  
  At the same time, internal legislative discussions regarding semiconductor export controls are underway in Taiwan. On November 2, the Legislative Yuan’s Economics Committee held a public hearing on the draft amendments to the "Trade Act." The focus was on adding Article 13-2 and Article 27-3, which propose stricter control measures for the export of critical integrated circuits to mainland China, including potential criminal penalties of up to 10 years to plug loopholes in transshipment.
  
  According to confirmed public information, the UK parliamentary report explicitly lists the situation in the Taiwan Strait as a major national security and economic risk for the UK. It estimates that a blockade or conflict would result in a devastating global economic loss of at least $2 trillion, with the disruption of the semiconductor chip supply chain being the primary impact point. In Taiwan, the legislative process for the "Trade Act" has entered the public hearing stage. Iconic semiconductor companies, including TSMC, have dispatched legal and import/export management engineers to attend as observers, signaling the industry's high level of concern regarding these regulatory changes. This move aims to respond to international pressure to prevent the illegal transfer of high-end technology to China and to strengthen Taiwan's compliance role in the global supply chain.
  
  However, regarding the details and implementation of the amendments, source information indicates that there are differing perspectives and areas requiring clarification. Legal experts and scholars at the public hearing expressed reservations about the necessity and practical implementation of the proposed provisions, primarily fearing that harsh criminal penalties could "accidentally harm" compliant manufacturers. Experts suggest that legislation should clearly define the boundary between "malicious evasion" and "general administrative negligence" to avoid excessive compliance burdens on companies due to vague definitions, which could even affect normal business operations. Furthermore, the "2028 Taiwan Strait risk" mentioned in the UK report falls within the scope of expert assessment; while viewed as an important geopolitical warning, the timing and potential scale of a conflict remain speculative and are not established facts.
  
  Such shifts in geopolitical and legal policies directly affect the layout strategies of the global semiconductor industry. For North American and global technology industries, Taiwan serves as a critical source of chip supply; any fluctuations in legislation or regional risk will affect supply chain stability. If Taiwan implements stricter controls on exports to China, it will force relevant companies to re-evaluate their global export compliance processes and may accelerate the "de-risking" of supply chains.
  
  Furthermore, it is becoming increasingly difficult for governments to balance national security with economic interests. How to maintain export competitiveness—such as South Korea's recent economic performance with exports exceeding $900 billion—while aligning with geopolitical controls has become a core challenge for governments worldwide.
  
  It is worth noting that interpretations of "de-coupling" policies should not be oversimplified. Although the UK parliamentary report emphasizes the need to guard against economic risks, this does not equate to an immediate, comprehensive economic decoupling. Legal experts remind that the purpose of the amendment is to close transshipment loopholes, not to ban legitimate trade entirely. Therefore, when discussing relevant policies, it is essential to distinguish between "national security protection" and "normal business activities" to prevent unnecessary market panic caused by over-interpretation.
  
  For businesses, the future challenge lies in ensuring the legality and resilience of operations within an increasingly complex international regulatory environment, while simultaneously coping with sudden policy reversals driven by geopolitics. For instance, changes in Pakistan's airspace control policy toward Taiwan demonstrate that uncertainty in international transportation and trade environments is increasing.
  
  Future observation will focus on the final text of Taiwan's "Trade Act" amendment, particularly the definition of the scope of criminal liability, and how the industry communicates with the government through trade associations to minimize interference with legitimate trade. At the same time, whether the international community's assessment of the Taiwan Strait situation will further influence global tech investment decisions, and whether Western nations like the UK will adopt specific economic and trade measures in response to the report, are indicators worth tracking closely in the coming months. Additionally, how countries handle economic dependence on China while promoting "national interest diplomacy" will be a key variable affecting changes in the global trade landscape.

# 地緣政治風險升溫：各國強化供應鏈安全與半導體出口管制

> 隨全球地緣政治風險加劇，英國國會示警2028年臺海危機恐致晶片斷供，臺灣立法院則研議修正《貿易法》，擬嚴格管制關鍵積體電路出口，平衡國安與產業合規。


全球地緣政治情勢近期對半導體供應鏈產生深遠影響。英國國會「商業、創新、科學暨貿易事務委員會」（BIST）於2026年11月初發布一份長達百頁的調查報告，針對中國企圖改變臺海現狀提出嚴厲警告。報告指出，隨著臺灣將於2028年舉行總統大選，2028年至2030年間恐為臺海衝突的「最高風險期」。
與此同時，臺灣內部亦針對半導體出口管制展開立法討論，立法院經濟委員會於11月2日召開《貿易法》部分條文修正草案公聽會，重點聚焦於增訂第13條之2與第27條之3，擬針對關鍵積體電路輸往中國大陸採取更嚴格的管制措施，並研擬祭出最高10年刑責，以防堵轉運漏洞。
根據各方公開資訊確認，英國國會報告明確將臺海局勢列為英國面臨的重大國家安全與經濟風險，並評估一旦發生封鎖或戰事，全球經濟將面臨至少2兆美元的毀滅性損失，其中半導體晶片供應鏈中斷為主要衝擊點。臺灣方面，立法院針對《貿易法》的修法程式已進入公聽會階段，臺積電等指標性半導體企業已派出法務及進出口管理工程師出席旁聽，顯示產業界對於相關法規變動的高度關注。此舉旨在回應國際對於防堵高階技術非法轉移至中國的壓力，強化臺灣在國際供應鏈中的合規角色。
然而，在修法細節與執行層面上，來源資訊顯示存在不同觀點與待釐清之處。法律界專家與學者在公聽會中對專設條文的必要性與實務執行表達保留態度，主要擔憂嚴苛的刑責可能「誤傷」合規廠商。專家建議，立法時應明確劃分「惡意規避」與「一般行政疏失」的界線，避免因定義模糊導致企業在合規成本上過度負擔，甚至影響正常商業營運。此外，英國報告中所述的「2028年臺海風險」屬於專家評估範疇，儘管被視為重要的地緣政治警訊，但該時間點與潛在衝突規模仍屬預測性質，並非已發生的既定事實。
此類地緣政治與法律政策的變動，直接影響全球半導體產業的佈局策略。對於北美及全球科技產業而言，臺灣作為關鍵晶片供應源，任何修法或區域風險的波動都將牽動供應鏈穩定性。若臺灣實施更嚴格的輸中管制，將迫使相關企業重新評估其全球出口合規流程，並可能加速供應鏈的「去風險化」（de-risking）程式。
此外，各國政府在追求國家安全與經濟利益之間的平衡日益困難，如何在維持出口競爭力（如南韓近期出口額突破9千億美元的經濟表現）與配合地緣政治管制之間取得平衡，已成為各國政府的核心課題。
值得注意的是，各界對於「脫中」政策的解讀不應過度簡化。雖然英國國會報告強調需防範經濟風險，但這並不等同於立即性的全面經濟脫鉤。法律專家提醒，修法目的在於填補轉運漏洞，而非全面禁止合法貿易。因此，在討論相關政策時，必須區分「國安防護」與「正常商業活動」的界線，避免市場因過度解讀而產生不必要的恐慌。
對於企業而言，未來的挑戰在於如何在日益複雜的國際法規環境下，確保業務運作的合法性與韌性，同時應對地緣政治帶來的突發性政策反覆，如巴基斯坦對我國領空管制政策的變動，皆顯示出國際交通與貿易環境的不確定性正在增加。
後續觀察重點將聚焦於臺灣《貿易法》修正草案的最終條文內容，特別是關於刑責適用範圍的界定，以及產業界如何透過公會與政府進行溝通，以降低對合法貿易的幹擾。同時，國際社會對於臺海局勢的評估是否會進一步影響全球科技業的投資決策，以及英國等西方國家是否會針對報告內容採取具體的經貿配套措施，皆是未來數月值得密切追蹤的指標。此外，各國在推動「國益外交」的同時，如何處理與中國之間的經貿依賴關係，也將是影響全球貿易版圖變化的關鍵變數。
