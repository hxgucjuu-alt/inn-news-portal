  # Calls for African Economic Autonomy and Infrastructure Governance Challenges in South Africa Proceed in Parallel
  
  > African entrepreneur Aliko Dangote has urged African nations to proactively develop their economic potential rather than relying on foreign aid; meanwhile, the South African city of Durban has lost a lawsuit regarding sewage treatment, highlighting mounting pressures on public health and infrastructure governance.
  
  Economic development and public governance issues on the African continent have recently become a focus of international attention. Aliko Dangote, Africa's richest man and founder of the Dangote Group, recently stated on the BBC program "Africa Enterprise" that African nations must take responsibility for developing their own economies and should not wait for external forces to create opportunities.
  
  At the same time, urban governance in South Africa is facing severe challenges. In particular, the eThekwini Municipality in KwaZulu-Natal recently lost a court case regarding its failure to properly manage untreated sewage leaks, underscoring the urgency of infrastructure maintenance and environmental governance during the rapid urbanization process in Africa.
  
  According to various media reports and public information, Dangote emphasized in the interview that Africa possesses immense potential, but the key lies in how to translate that potential into tangible economic results. He pointed out that African governments and the business community must adopt a more proactive attitude, addressing long-standing development bottlenecks through internally driven industrial upgrading and economic reform. Meanwhile, the legal case in Durban, South Africa, reflects structural problems in the country's public service management. The court ruled against the city for its failure to effectively curb sewage leaks, which not only affected the quality of life for local residents but also sparked external concerns regarding the execution capabilities of South African local governments in public health and environmental protection.
  
  Currently, discussions regarding African economic development and the plight of local governance in South Africa present different dimensions of focus in terms of information sources. Dangote's perspective primarily centers on macroeconomic policy and the agency of entrepreneurs, emphasizing the importance of "self-development," while reports on Durban focus on specific legal rulings and public health crises. Although both belong to development issues in the African region, the former pertains to a strategic economic vision, while the latter concerns specific execution and legal disputes. Furthermore, South Africa is currently facing a chronic disease crisis, such as diabetes, which, together with environmental governance issues like sewage treatment, constitutes a challenge for the country's public policy.
  
  It remains to be confirmed what specific remedial measures the eThekwini Municipality will take following its defeat in court, and how much political and administrative resistance Dangote's discourse on economic autonomy will face during the implementation of specific national policies.
  
  These two events have profound implications for Africa. If Dangote's call receives policy responses from various governments, it will help promote trade integration and industrialization within the African continent, reduce reliance on international aid, and enhance Africa's bargaining power in the global economic system. On the other hand, the sewage treatment lawsuit in Durban serves as a wake-up call for other rapidly developing cities in Africa. If infrastructure construction cannot keep pace with the speed of urbanization, it will directly threaten public health and subsequently increase the burden on the medical system. For South Africa, how to strike a balance between economic growth and improving infrastructure governance has become a major public issue that the current administration must prioritize.
  
  However, there are different interpretations and limitations regarding Dangote's views. Some commentators argue that while "self-development" is the correct long-term direction, African nations face immense challenges in transforming solely through internal forces in the absence of capital, technology, and a stable political environment; overemphasizing internal responsibility may overlook the constraints imposed on African development by the international financial system and trade structures. Similarly, regarding the environmental governance issue in Durban, it should not be viewed merely as a local administrative failure, but as a systemic dilemma in the resource allocation and prioritization of South Africa's overall public governance system. Over-interpreting a single court ruling may mask more complex structural problems in South Africa's medical and environmental policies.
  
  Subsequent observations will focus on two aspects: First, whether Dangote's call will trigger substantive policy dialogues between the business community and governments across African nations, thereby promoting specific industrial protection or investment policies. Second, whether the eThekwini Municipality will initiate large-scale infrastructure upgrade plans following its legal defeat, and whether the national government will propose a more comprehensive national strategy addressing the diabetes crisis and environmental sanitation issues. These developments will directly affect the effectiveness of Africa's future economic autonomy and public governance, and serve as important indicators for assessing the overall development trend of the African continent.

# 非洲經濟自主呼籲與南非基礎建設治理挑戰並進

> 非洲企業家丹格科呼籲非洲各國應主動開發經濟潛力，而非仰賴外援；同時，南非德班市因汙水處理問題敗訴，公共衛生與基礎設施治理壓力持續升高。


非洲大陸的經濟發展與公共治理議題，近期成為國際關注焦點。非洲首富、丹格科集團（Dangote Group）創辦人阿里科·丹格科（Aliko Dangote）近期於英國廣播公司（BBC）節目《Africa Enterprise》中公開表示，非洲國家必須肩負起發展自身經濟的責任，不應等待外部力量創造機會。
與此同時，南非的城市治理亦面臨嚴峻挑戰，特別是位於誇祖魯-納塔爾省的德班市（eThekwini Municipality），近期因未妥善處理未經處理的汙水外洩問題，在法院訴訟中敗訴，凸顯了非洲地區在快速城市化過程中，基礎設施維護與環境治理的迫切性。
根據多方媒體報導與公開資訊，丹格科在訪談中強調，非洲擁有巨大的潛力，但關鍵在於如何將這些潛力轉化為現實的經濟成果。他指出，非洲各國政府與企業界必須採取更為積極的態度，透過內部驅動的產業升級與經濟改革，來解決長期以來的發展瓶頸。與此同時，南非德班市的法律訴訟案，則反映了該國在公共服務管理上的結構性問題。德班市因未能有效遏止汙水外洩，遭法院裁定敗訴，這不僅影響了當地居民的生活品質，也引發了外界對於南非地方政府在公共衛生與環境保護執行力上的擔憂。
目前，關於非洲經濟發展的討論與南非地方治理的困境，在資訊來源上呈現出不同的關注維度。丹格科的觀點主要聚焦於宏觀經濟政策與企業家的主體責任，強調「自我開發」的重要性；而針對德班市的報導，則側重於具體的法律裁決與公共衛生危機。兩者雖均屬於非洲地區的發展議題，但前者屬於戰略層面的經濟願景，後者則是具體的執行層面與法律糾紛。此外，南非目前亦面臨糖尿病等慢性病危機，這與汙水處理等環境治理問題共同構成了該國公共政策的挑戰。
尚待確認之處在於，德班市在敗訴後，將採取何種具體的補救措施，以及丹格科的經濟自主論述，在各國具體政策落實上將面臨多大的政治與行政阻力。
這兩起事件對非洲的影響深遠。丹格科的呼籲若能獲得各國政府的政策響應，將有助於推動非洲大陸內部的貿易整合與工業化程式，減少對國際援助的依賴，並提升非洲在全球經濟體系中的談判籌碼。另一方面，南非德班市的汙水處理敗訴案，則對非洲其他正在快速發展的城市敲響了警鐘。基礎設施的建設若無法跟上城市化速度，將直接威脅公共衛生，進而加重醫療體系的負擔。對於南非而言，如何在經濟成長與改善基礎設施治理之間取得平衡，已成為當前執政當局必須優先解決的重大公共議題。
然而，對於丹格科的觀點，外界亦存在不同的解讀與限制。部分評論認為，儘管「自我開發」是正確的長期方向，但非洲各國在缺乏資金、技術與穩定政治環境的情況下，單靠內部力量轉型存在極大挑戰；過度強調內部責任可能忽略了國際金融體系與貿易結構對非洲發展的制約。同樣地，針對德班市的環境治理問題，不應將其單純視為地方行政疏失，而應視為南非整體公共治理體系在資源分配與優先順序上的系統性困境。過度解讀單一法院判決，可能掩蓋了南非在醫療與環境政策上更複雜的結構性問題。
後續觀察重點將聚焦於以下兩方面：首先，丹格科的呼籲是否會引發非洲各國商界與政府在政策上的實質對話，進而推動具體的產業保護或投資政策。其次，南非德班市政府在敗訴後，是否會啟動大規模的基礎設施升級計畫，以及該國政府是否會針對糖尿病危機與環境衛生問題，提出更為完整的國家級戰略。這些發展將直接影響非洲未來在經濟自主與公共治理上的成效，也是評估非洲大陸整體發展趨勢的重要指標。
