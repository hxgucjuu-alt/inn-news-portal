  # International Governance and Urban Development Trends: Global Challenges from Smart Governance to Geopolitics
  
  > With the release of the 2026 global urban governance indicators and the commencement of international summits, nations are actively addressing geopolitical shifts and regional development challenges through smart city certifications and transnational economic linkages, seeking new paradigms for local governance.
  
  In October 2026, global urban governance and international political economy entered a period of high dynamism. In terms of urban development, several counties and cities in Taiwan have demonstrated international competitiveness. On October 13, Yunlin County Magistrate Chang Li-shan led a delegation to the United States to participate in the "2026 Intelligent Community Forum (ICF) Global Summit" held in Columbus, Ohio. The Yunlin County Government noted that the county was selected as one of the Top 7 Intelligent Communities globally for the second time since 2024 and achieved full ICF Intelligent Community certification, becoming the only city in Taiwan to be selected this year. This trip aimed to share the governance experience of "Intelligence Beyond Agriculture."
  
  Meanwhile, Hsinchu City ranked in the top 16% globally in the "2026 Global Cities Index" released by Oxford Economics, and secured the top spot in Taiwan in the "Quality of Life" dimension, indicating that Taiwan's local governments have gradually established internationally credible assessment models in the dimensions of economy, human capital, and governance.
  
  Regarding transnational governance and regional cooperation, the governance models in Latin America and the Caribbean have also seen significant progress. According to information from the United Nations Economic Commission for Latin America and the Caribbean (ECLAC), the region has officially established the "Network of Subnational Governments for Productive Development."
  
  This mechanism is positioned as a permanent technical and political space aimed at strengthening the capacity of subnational governments to design and implement productive development policies and promoting the coordination of cross-border public issues. This, combined with the trend of Taiwan's local governments actively participating in international forums to strengthen urban governance capabilities, constitutes the two pillars of global local governance transformation.
  
  However, shifts in the international political and economic landscape continue to add uncertainty to regional development. Recent remarks by former U.S. President Donald Trump regarding the political future of Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy have sparked international discussions about a potential shift in U.S. foreign policy. Professor Wen Li-chung, based in the U.S., pointed out that Trump's remarks indicate that U.S. foreign commitments may be adjusted based on "realistic interests," which serves as a warning for all countries. This perspective coincides with the issues to be discussed at the "3rd Cathay Asset Management Summit," which will be held in Taipei on October 30.
  
  The summit focuses on the impact of global geopolitics, tariff policies, and AI development on international financial markets, demonstrating that beyond smart governance and local development, the volatility of the global political and economic situation has become a risk variable that governments and enterprises must face together.
  
  For relevant regions and industries, these trends have brought concrete impacts. In terms of public governance, the cases of Yunlin County and Hsinchu City prove that local governments can effectively enhance urban visibility and attract investment through international certification and objective indicators. On the industrial front, the hosting of the Cathay Asset Management Summit reflects that the financial sector is actively seeking to build robust investment paradigms in the "new normal" through dialogues with international experts from firms such as BlackRock and JPMorgan. For the Latin American and Caribbean region, the establishment of the Network of Subnational Governments for Productive Development helps overcome regional resource inequality and enhances overall economic resilience through cross-border policy coordination.
  
  Although various indicators and summits provide a development blueprint, different perspectives remind the outside world not to over-interpret them. For example, while urban indices provide an objective reference, there is still room for discussion on whether their assessment models are fully applicable to the unique cultural and social backgrounds of different countries. Furthermore, Professor Wen Li-chung's warning also points out that over-reliance on the commitments of a single superpower carries high risks; while pursuing international cooperation, countries must strengthen their own "standing" capabilities rather than merely hoping for external support. Regarding smart city certification, while it is an important affirmation of governance, how to translate certification into long-term social dividends, rather than just remaining at the level of short-term international publicity, is a long-term challenge faced by local governments.
  
  Looking ahead, the focus of observations on international governance will center on two aspects: first, how local governments can substantively translate ICF smart city certification and international network connections into momentum for local industrial upgrading; and second, the actual impact of global geopolitical risks on regional economic cooperation. With the convening of the Cathay Asset Management Summit at the end of October and the ongoing responses of various countries to changes in U.S. policy, the international community will more frequently examine the linkage between "local governance" and "global geopolitics." As governments and subnational units pursue international integration, they will inevitably have to seek a more precise balance between "deepening international partner connections" and "building autonomous development resilience."

# 國際治理與城市發展趨勢：從智慧治理到地緣政治的全球挑戰

> 隨著2026年全球城市治理指標出爐及國際高峰會展開，各國正積極透過智慧城市認證與跨國經濟連結，應對地緣政治變動與區域發展挑戰，尋求在地治理的新典範。


2026年10月，全球城市治理與國際政治經濟進入高度動態期。在城市發展層面，臺灣多個縣市展現了國際競爭力，雲林縣長張麗善於10月13日率團赴美，參與在俄亥俄州哥倫布市舉行的「2026年國際智慧城市論壇（ICF）全球高峰會」。雲林縣政府指出，該縣繼2024年後再度獲選為全球七大智慧城市（Top 7），並取得ICF完整智慧城市社群認證，成為今年臺灣唯一入選城市，此行旨在分享「農業之上的智慧」治理經驗。
與此同時，新竹市在牛津經濟研究院發布的「2026全球城市指數」中，擠進全球前16%，並在「生活品質」維度奪下全臺第一，顯示臺灣地方政府在經濟、人力資本與治理維度上，已逐步建立具備國際公信力的評估模型。
在跨國治理與區域合作方面，拉丁美洲與加勒比海地區的治理模式亦有重大進展。根據聯合國拉丁美洲暨加勒比海經濟委員會（CEPAL）的資訊，該地區已正式建立「次國家級政府生產力發展網路」（Network of Subnational Governments for Productive Development）。
此機制被定位為永久性的技術與政治空間，旨在強化各次國家級政府在設計與實施生產力發展政策上的能力，促進跨境公共議題的協調，這與臺灣地方政府積極參與國際論壇、強化城市治理能力的趨勢，共同構成全球地方治理轉型的兩大支柱。
然而，國際政經局勢的變動仍為區域發展增添不確定性。近期美國前總統川普針對烏克蘭總統澤倫斯基的政治去留發表言論，引發國際對於美國外交政策轉向的討論。旅美教授翁履中指出，川普的言論顯示美國對外承諾可能隨「現實利益」而調整，這對各國而言是一項警訊。此觀點與即將於10月30日在臺北舉辦的「第三屆國泰資產管理高峰會」所關注的議題不謀而合。
該高峰會聚焦於全球地緣政治、關稅政策及AI發展對國際金融市場的衝擊，顯示出在智慧治理與地方發展之外，全球政經局勢的波動已成為各國政府與企業必須共同面對的風險變數。
對於相關地區與產業而言，這些趨勢帶來了具體的影響。在公共治理上，雲林縣與新竹市的案例證明，地方政府透過國際認證與客觀指標，能有效提升城市能見度並吸引投資。在產業面，國泰資產管理高峰會的舉辦，反映出金融界正積極尋求在「新常態」下，透過與貝萊德、摩根大通等國際專家對話，打造穩健的投資典範。對於拉丁美洲與加勒比海地區，生產力發展網路的建立則有助於克服區域內資源分配不均，透過跨境政策協調，提升整體經濟韌性。
儘管各類指標與高峰會提供了發展藍圖，但不同觀點仍提醒外界不應過度解讀。例如，雖然城市指數提供了客觀參考，但其評估模型是否完全適用於各國特殊的文化與社會背景，仍存在討論空間。此外，翁履中教授的警示也指出，過度依賴單一強權的承諾具有高度風險，各國在追求國際合作的同時，必須強化自身「站得住」的實力，而非僅僅寄望於外部支援。對於智慧城市認證，雖然是重要的治理肯定，但如何將認證轉化為長期的社會紅利，而非僅停留在短期的國際宣傳，是地方政府面臨的長期挑戰。
展望後續，國際治理的觀察重點將聚焦於兩大面向：首先是地方政府如何將ICF的智慧城市認證與國際網路連結，實質轉化為在地產業升級的動力；其次是全球地緣政治風險對區域經濟合作的實際衝擊。隨著10月底國泰資產管理高峰會的召開，以及各國對美國政策變動的持續應對，國際社會將更頻繁地檢視「在地治理」與「全球地緣政治」之間的連動性。各國政府與次國家級單位在追求國際接軌的同時，勢必得在「深化國際夥伴連結」與「建立自主發展韌性」之間，尋求更為精確的平衡。
