  # The 30th Taipei Innovative Textile Application Show (TITAS) Opens Amidst Global AI Economic Stability Risk Assessment
  
  > The 30th Taipei Innovative Textile Application Show (TITAS) officially opened this Tuesday, focusing on sustainable circularity. Simultaneously, the ASEAN+3 Macroeconomic Research Office (AMRO) has issued a warning that if the global AI boom were to reverse, Asian economies could face severe financial and real economic shocks.
  
  The 30th Taipei Innovative Textile Application Show (TITAS) officially opened on October 6, 2026. As a significant annual event for the global textile industry, this year's exhibition coincides with the global wave of net-zero carbon emissions and green transformation, as the industrial chain accelerates toward deep innovation. The organizers noted that sustainable circularity has become the core theme of this year's exhibition, reflecting how the global textile industry is actively adjusting its production models in the face of climate change and environmental regulatory pressures.
  
  Meanwhile, global financial markets are also being influenced by shifts in the technology sector. The ASEAN+3 Macroeconomic Research Office (AMRO) released its "2026 Financial Stability Report" on October 4, issuing a stern warning regarding the potential risks of the global AI boom, emphasizing that while major Asian economies are highly dependent on technology supply chains, they are also exposed to the risks of market volatility.
  
  According to public information, the 30th Taipei Innovative Textile Application Show has brought together 410 companies from 12 countries, including Taiwan, the United States, Italy, Germany, Switzerland, Sweden, Israel, Japan, South Korea, Vietnam, Malaysia, and mainland China, with a total of 977 exhibition booths. Leading domestic companies such as Formosa Plastics, Far Eastern New Century, Lealea Group, Eclat Textile, Hao Fang, New Wide Group, Everlight Chemical, Chia-Fung, Zig Sheng, Nan Ya Plastics, Nan-Woven, and Taiwan Fu Hsing have actively participated, showcasing their latest research and development achievements in functional textiles and green manufacturing processes.
  
  On the other hand, the report released by AMRO points out that because Southeast Asia, China, Japan, and South Korea are at the core of the global AI supply chain and are increasingly integrated into AI-related financial markets, these regions would bear the brunt of any disorderly correction in the AI boom, facing more severe chain reactions than other economies.
  
  Regarding these two issues, current information presents a situation where industrial development and macroeconomic risks coexist. For the Taipei Textile Show, media reports have focused on the scale of the exhibition and trends in sustainable transformation; specific order performance and actual profit expectations for individual companies remain subject to further statistics during the remainder of the event. Regarding the AMRO warning report, there are still varying interpretations across sectors concerning the definition and timing of an "AI boom reversal."
  
  The report clearly states that risk transmission channels include declining exports of technology products, investment portfolio losses, capital outflows, and refinancing pressures on highly leveraged technology and infrastructure companies. However, there are subtle differences in the analytical models and viewpoints of various financial institutions regarding the specific market indicators that would trigger a correction, and no unified global market forecast has been formed.
  
  The global economy and trade environment are at a critical stage of transformation. As a representative of traditional manufacturing, the textile industry is attempting to seek new growth momentum in the green economy through sustainable transformation, which is a necessary means for export-dependent Asian countries to maintain industrial competitiveness. However, AMRO's warning highlights the structural risks of over-concentration in high-tech industries. If AI-related infrastructure and financial markets experience significant volatility, it could not only lead to a sharp drop in regional technology product export values but also potentially impair regional financial stability due to capital outflows.
  
  This dual pressure—the need to invest significant capital in green and digital transformation on one hand, and the need to guard against the financial risks of a bursting technology bubble on the other—will become a severe challenge that Asian governments and enterprises must face together in the coming period.
  
  Although the AI supply chain has driven economic growth in the Asian region over the past few years, market analysts and policymakers emphasize that one should not over-interpret the growth potential of a single market and must also be wary of the aftereffects of market overheating. For traditional industries such as textiles, although the direct impact of an AI bubble is lower than that of technology manufacturing, they will still be indirectly affected by weak global consumer demand and tightening financial conditions. Therefore, industrial resilience depends not only on the level of green technology leadership but also on the ability to manage risks associated with global macroeconomic volatility. Experts suggest that while pursuing technological upgrades, companies should maintain robust financial structures and avoid excessive leverage to cope with potential market shocks.
  
  Subsequent observations will focus on two major aspects: first, the actual procurement willingness and price acceptance of international buyers for sustainable textiles during the Taipei Textile Show, which will reflect the true demand of the global end-consumer market under inflation and economic uncertainty; second, whether central banks and financial regulatory agencies will adopt more conservative monetary policies or capital control measures in response to the risk assessments in the AMRO report. Additionally, changes in capacity utilization and inventory levels within the AI supply chain will be key indicators for determining whether the global technology industry is entering a correction phase.
  
  Observers will continue to track this data to assess the resilience of the global supply chain in the face of external shocks and the effectiveness of the transformation of Asian economies following this wave of the technology boom.

# 第30屆臺北紡織展揭幕與全球AI經濟穩定風險評估

> 第30屆臺北紡織展於本週二正式開幕，聚焦永續迴圈議題；同時，東協與中日韓宏觀經濟研究辦公室（AMRO）示警，若全球AI熱潮出現逆轉，亞洲經濟體恐面臨嚴峻的金融與實體經濟衝擊。


第30屆臺北紡織展（TITAS）於2026年10月6日正式開展，作為全球紡織產業的重要年度盛會，本屆展會適逢全球淨零碳排與綠色轉型浪潮，產業鏈正加速邁向深度革新。主辦單位指出，永續迴圈已成為本屆參展的核心主題，顯示出全球紡織業在面對氣候變遷與環保法規壓力下，正積極調整生產模式。
與此同時，全球金融市場亦受到科技產業變遷的牽動，東協與中日韓宏觀經濟研究辦公室（AMRO）於10月4日發布《2026年金融穩定報告》，針對全球AI熱潮的潛在風險提出嚴正警告，強調亞洲主要經濟體在高度依賴科技供應鏈的同時，也暴露於市場波動的風險之中。
根據公開資訊顯示，第30屆臺北紡織展匯聚了來自臺灣、美國、義大利、德國、瑞士、瑞典、以色列、日本、韓國、越南、馬來西亞及中國大陸等12個國家，共計410家廠商參與，展出攤位數達977個。國內指標性企業如臺塑、遠東新、力麗集團、儒鴻、昊紡、旭榮集團、宏遠、佳紡、展頌、集盛、新纖、南緯及臺灣富網等均積極投入，展示在機能性紡織品與綠色製程上的最新研發成果。
另一方面，AMRO發布的報告則指出，由於東南亞、中國、日本與韓國均處於全球AI供應鏈的核心地位，且與AI相關的金融市場整合度日益加深，一旦AI熱潮出現失序式修正，上述地區將首當其衝，面臨比其他經濟體更為劇烈的連鎖反應。
關於上述兩項議題，目前各方資訊呈現出產業發展與總體經濟風險並存的態勢。針對臺北紡織展，各媒體報導重點均集中於參展規模與永續轉型趨勢，對於個別廠商的具體訂單表現與實際獲利預期，尚待後續展會期間的進一步統計。而在AMRO的預警報告中，各界對於「AI熱潮逆轉」的定義與時間點仍存有不同解讀。
報告明確指出，風險擴散管道包括科技產品出口下滑、投資組合損失、資本外流，以及高槓桿科技與基礎設施企業面臨再融資壓力等，然而對於具體觸發修正的市場指標，各金融機構的分析模型與觀點尚存在細微差異，尚未形成全球市場的統一預測。
全球經濟與貿易環境正處於轉型關鍵期。紡織產業作為傳統製造業的代表，透過永續轉型試圖在綠色經濟中尋求新的成長動能，這對於依賴出口的亞洲國家而言，是維持產業競爭力的必要手段。然而，AMRO的示警則提醒了過度集中於高科技產業的結構性風險。若AI相關基礎設施與金融市場出現大幅波動，不僅會導致區域內科技產品出口值驟降，更可能因資本外流導致區域金融穩定性受損。
這種雙重壓力——一方面需投入大量資本進行綠色與數位轉型，另一方面需防範科技泡沫破裂的金融風險——將成為未來一段時間內，亞洲各國政府與企業必須共同面對的嚴峻課題。
雖然AI供應鏈在過去幾年帶動了亞洲區域的經濟成長，但市場分析師與政策制定者均強調，不應過度解讀單一市場的成長潛力，亦需警惕市場情緒過熱帶來的後遺症。對於紡織等傳統產業而言，雖然其受AI泡沫直接衝擊的程度較科技製造業低，但仍會受到全球消費需求疲軟與金融環境緊縮的間接影響。因此，產業的韌性不僅取決於綠色技術的領先程度，更取決於對全球總體經濟波動的風險控管能力。各界專家建議，企業在追求技術升級的同時，應保持財務結構的穩健，避免過度槓桿，以應對可能出現的市場震盪。
後續觀察重點將聚焦於兩大面向：首先是臺北紡織展期間，國際買主對於永續紡織品的實際採購意願與價格接受度，這將反映出全球終端消費市場在通膨與經濟不確定性下的真實需求；其次是各國央行與金融監管機構針對AMRO報告所提出的風險評估，是否會採取更為保守的貨幣政策或資本管制措施。此外，AI供應鏈的產能利用率與庫存水位變化，將是判斷全球科技產業是否進入修正期的關鍵指標。
觀察家將持續追蹤這些資料，以評估全球供應鏈在面對外部衝擊時的抗壓性，以及亞洲經濟體在經歷這波科技熱潮後的轉型成效。
