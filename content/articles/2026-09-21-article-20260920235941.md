  # Asia-Pacific Technology and Economic Landscape: Industrial Challenges and Policy Planning Amid the AI Investment Wave
  
  > As global demand for artificial intelligence surges, SoftBank has expanded its Arm-backed loans to bolster its AI footprint, while memory shortages impact terminal industries. Simultaneously, the Asia-Pacific region faces dual challenges: the crowding out of geopolitical resources and the complexities of distributing benefits from localized infrastructure projects.
  
  In mid-September 2026, the global technology industry and the Asia-Pacific geopolitical landscape underwent multiple shifts. In the realm of technology investment, SoftBank Group announced it had reached an agreement with creditor banks to increase its margin loan facility—secured by its shares in Arm—by an additional $5 billion, bringing the total to $25 billion. The move is aimed at fully committing to AI infrastructure, chip, and model development. Meanwhile, the global memory market has fallen into a severe shortage due to the crowding-out effect of AI demand; this supply-demand imbalance is expected to persist through 2027 or even 2028, placing operational pressure on small-scale electronic device manufacturers.
  
  Regarding policy and geopolitics, U.S. military officials warned Congress that conflicts in the Middle East have led to the diversion of certain resources, thereby impacting deterrence capabilities in the Asia-Pacific region. Furthermore, the Philippine government, addressing the U.S.-supported "Luzon Corridor" project, emphasized the need to ensure local benefits and compliance with relevant regulations, indicating that Asia-Pacific economic cooperation is facing tests of localization and regulatory integration during its implementation.
  
  SoftBank Group’s capital scheduling strategy clearly reflects the strong ambitions of multinational tech giants in the AI sector. According to public reports, SoftBank founder Masayoshi Son is actively building an AI ecosystem, with his investment commitment to OpenAI approaching $65 billion. This move contrasts with the strained global memory supply chain, where memory prices have surged by over 90% in the first quarter of this year and are expected to continue climbing this quarter.
  
  Market research firms TrendForce and Counterpoint Research have pointed out that the supply chain crisis has forced small-scale electronic device manufacturers to redesign products, significantly increasing the risk of higher retail prices. It is estimated that global smartphone shipments will decline to 1.08 billion units this year, marking the largest drop in history.
  
  In terms of policy advancement in the Asia-Pacific, there is a gap between the expectations for multinational cooperation and reality. The Philippine government, regarding the Luzon Corridor project under the U.S.-led "Free and Open Indo-Pacific" framework, has explicitly demanded that local benefits be realized. It also noted that current Philippine regulations lack specific ordinances for large-scale single projects, which has become a major variable in promoting multinational investment. On the other hand, U.S. strategic deployment in the Asia-Pacific is also under pressure; military officials admitted to Congress that the consumption of military resources due to situations such as the war in Iran has led to a certain degree of crowding out of deterrence in the Asia-Pacific. Additionally, recent reports of economic and diplomatic fluctuations between the U.S. and India reflect the complex interactions within the Asia-Pacific region.
  
  The aforementioned events have had a direct impact on regional industries and policies. In the technology sector, the memory shortage is not merely a supply chain issue but has evolved into a survival challenge for terminal products, with small businesses facing the dual pressure of cost pass-throughs and declining product competitiveness. At the policy level, the Taiwan government is also attempting to seek a balance between technology and culture. During the 2026 National Cultural Conference, President Lai Ching-te emphasized that technology should advance alongside dignity, with the core philosophy that "technology allows Taiwan to move fast, while culture allows Taiwan to go far." This attempt to address global environmental changes through cultural subjectivity amidst the wave of digital transformation shows that Asia-Pacific nations are beginning to value soft power and local cultural assets while pursuing technological growth.
  
  However, interpretations of these trends should remain cautious. While SoftBank's large-scale financing demonstrates confidence in AI, it remains to be seen whether its high-leverage operations will create risks as the market fluctuates. Similarly, the claim regarding the impairment of U.S. military deterrence in the Asia-Pacific is a strategic assessment provided by the military to Congress; whether this will result in a substantive security vacuum or is merely a tactical reallocation of resources remains to be seen, depending on subsequent U.S. military exercises and deployment dynamics in the region.
  
  Furthermore, the Philippines' demands regarding the benefits of the Luzon Corridor reflect the insistence of developing nations on economic sovereignty and local development when participating in the strategic cooperation of major powers. Such negotiation processes are often lengthy and full of variables, and it would be inappropriate to over-interpret a single negotiation requirement as a breakdown in cooperation.
  
  Future observation will focus on three key points: First, whether the memory shortage will be alleviated in 2027 through capacity adjustments, or if it will further trigger a structural reorganization of the global electronics industry. Second, whether SoftBank's massive investments in the AI field can be converted into concrete profit models within the next two years to support its enormous financing scale. Finally, whether the U.S. strategic resource allocation in the Asia-Pacific will be adjusted in line with the evolution of the Middle East situation, and how countries like the Philippines respond to the localization needs of multinational economic cooperation through domestic regulatory amendments, will be key indicators in determining the extent to which the Indo-Pacific strategy is implemented.

# 亞太科技與經貿局勢：AI投資浪潮下的產業挑戰與政策佈局

> 隨著全球人工智慧需求激增，軟銀擴大Arm抵押貸款以加碼AI佈局，記憶體短缺衝擊終端產業；同時，亞太地區面臨地緣政治資源排擠與基礎建設在地化利益分配的雙重挑戰。


2026年9月中旬，全球科技產業與亞太地緣政治格局出現多重變動。在科技投資領域，軟銀集團（SoftBank）宣佈與債權銀行完成協議，將以旗下安謀（Arm）持股作為擔保的保證金貸款額度，再增加50億美元，總額達到250億美元，旨在全力投入AI基礎設施、晶片及模型開發。與此同時，全球記憶體市場因AI需求排擠效應陷入嚴重短缺，預計供需失衡將延續至2027年甚至2028年，對小型電子裝置業者造成營運壓力。
在政策與地緣層面，美國軍方官員向國會示警，指中東地區的衝突已導致部分資源被挪用，進而影響亞太地區的嚇阻能力；此外，菲律賓政府針對美方支援的「呂宋走廊」計畫，強調需確保在地利益與符合相關法規，顯示亞太經貿合作在推動過程中，正遭遇在地化與法規整合的考驗。
軟銀集團的資金排程策略，明確反映了跨國科技巨頭對AI領域的強烈企圖心。根據公開報導，軟銀創辦人孫正義正積極佈局AI生態系，其中針對OpenAI的承諾投資規模已逼近650億美元。此舉與全球記憶體供應鏈的緊張情勢形成對比，記憶體價格在今年第一季已暴漲逾九成，且本季預計持續攀升。
市場研究機構集邦科技（TrendForce）與Counterpoint Research指出，供應鏈危機已迫使小型電子裝置業者重擬產品設計，終端售價上漲風險大增，且預估今年全球智慧型手機出貨量將下滑至10.8億支，創下史上最大跌幅。
在亞太地區的政策推進上，各國對於跨國合作的期待與現實存在落差。菲律賓政府針對美方倡議的「自由開放印太」架構下的呂宋走廊計畫，明確要求必須落實在地利益，並指出菲律賓現行法規缺乏針對單一大型計畫的專屬條例，這成為推動跨國投資的一大變數。另一方面，美國在亞太地區的戰略部署亦面臨壓力，軍方官員向國會坦言，由於伊朗戰爭等中東局勢消耗了軍事資源，導致對亞太地區的嚇阻力受到一定程度的排擠。此外，美國與印度之間近期亦傳出經濟與外交層面的波動，反映出亞太區域內部的複雜互動。
上述事件對區域產業與政策產生直接影響。科技產業方面，記憶體短缺不僅是供應鏈問題，更演變為終端產品的生存挑戰，小型業者面臨成本轉嫁與產品競爭力下降的雙重夾擊。在政策層面，臺灣政府亦試圖在科技與文化間尋求平衡，總統賴清德於2026年全國文化會議中強調，科技應與尊嚴並進，並以「科技讓臺灣走得快，文化讓臺灣走得遠」為核心，試圖在數位轉型浪潮中，透過文化主體性來應對全球環境變局，這顯示出亞太國家在追求科技成長的同時，亦開始重視軟實力與在地文化價值的儲存。
然而，各界對上述趨勢的解讀應保持審慎。軟銀的大規模融資雖展現對AI的信心，但其高槓桿操作是否會隨市場波動產生風險，仍有待觀察。同樣地，關於美軍在亞太地區嚇阻力受損的說法，屬於軍方對國會的戰略評估，是否會造成實質安全真空，抑或是戰術上的資源調配，尚需視後續美軍在該區域的軍事演訓與部署動態而定。
此外，菲律賓對於呂宋走廊的利益要求，反映了開發中國家在參與大國戰略合作時，對於經濟主權與在地發展的堅持，這類談判過程往往漫長且充滿變數，不宜將單一的談判要求過度解讀為合作破局。
後續觀察重點將聚焦於以下三點：首先，記憶體短缺是否會隨產能調整在2027年獲得緩解，或將進一步引發全球電子產業的結構性重組；其次，軟銀在AI領域的鉅額投資能否在未來兩年內轉化為具體的獲利模式，以支撐其龐大的融資規模；最後，美國在亞太地區的戰略資源配置，是否會隨著中東局勢的演變而出現調整，以及菲律賓等國如何透過國內法規修正，來回應跨國經貿合作的在地化需求，將是決定印太戰略落實程度的關鍵指標。
