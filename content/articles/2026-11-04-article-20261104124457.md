  # New Taipei City Launches Four-Year Aerospace Talent Cultivation Program, Aiming to Develop Satellites and Rockets by 2030
  
  > The New Taipei City Education Department has announced a budget of NT$10 million to collaborate with Academia Sinica and the Taiwan Space Agency (TASA) on an aerospace talent cultivation program, with the goal of completing the development of "New Taipei Satellite 1" and "New Taipei Rocket 1" by 2030.
  
  On November 3, 2026, the New Taipei City Education Department officially announced an investment of NT$10 million for a four-year aerospace talent cultivation program. The initiative aims to establish a comprehensive aerospace technology education system through cross-sector collaboration between industry, government, academia, and research institutions. The core objective of the program is to integrate resources from Academia Sinica, the Taiwan Space Agency, universities, and the private sector to train professionals with practical skills in rocket manufacturing, satellite research and development, space data reception, and subsequent applications.
  
  As the inaugural activity of the program, the New Taipei City Government held the "New Taipei Future Space Talent and 2026 Space Centennial Education Exhibition" at the City Hall auditorium from November 3 to 4. Through expert lectures, interactive experiences, and thematic displays, the event showcased the current status and future trends of space technology to local students, aiming to enhance their interest in the aerospace field.
  
  According to Chang Ming-wen, Director of the New Taipei City Education Department, the program adopts a phased and systematic cultivation strategy to ground aerospace education at the grassroots level while extending it upward. Specifically, the Education Department will begin with vocational exploration courses at the junior high school level, gradually transitioning to advanced courses and project-based learning in high schools and vocational schools, and further connecting with the forward-looking research resources and industrial practical applications of universities. This vertical integration model is designed to provide students with opportunities to engage with space technology at different learning stages, allowing them to accumulate practical experience from building foundational knowledge to participating in actual satellite and rocket R&D projects.
  
  The ultimate vision of this program is to successfully develop "New Taipei Satellite 1" and "New Taipei Rocket 1" by 2030, marking a milestone for New Taipei City's participation in national space industry development at the local level.
  
  Currently, regarding the progress and execution details of the program, stakeholders have primarily confirmed the budget scale, partners, and clear phased goals. However, specific technical specifications for "New Taipei Satellite 1" and "New Taipei Rocket 1," the choice of launch vehicles, satellite orbital configurations, and the division of R&D responsibilities between central and local authorities during the execution process have not yet been detailed in public information. Furthermore, although the Education Department emphasized the importance of industry-academia cooperation, the list of private enterprises participating in the program and the roles these companies will play in talent cultivation and R&D remain to be disclosed in future policy details.
  
  These uncertainties will serve as key indicators for observers to determine whether the program can be implemented as scheduled.
  
  This program has profound implications for the aerospace industry chain in New Taipei City and Taiwan as a whole. First, at the policy level, it symbolizes the local government's active response to national space development policies, using educational investment to reserve a large pool of technical talent for the future aerospace industry, which helps alleviate the current talent shortage. Second, in terms of industrial impact, the successful development of satellites and rockets could drive the transformation and upgrading of local supply chains in New Taipei City, shifting from traditional manufacturing to high-value-added aerospace technology. For the education system, this program provides a concrete practical field, allowing vocational and higher education to align more closely with industrial needs and enhancing the employment competitiveness of students.
  
  Despite the ambitious vision, the program faces numerous technical and resource constraints. First, the threshold for aerospace R&D is extremely high, involving the integration of multiple fields such as precision machinery, communication engineering, and materials science. Whether a four-year cultivation cycle and a NT$10 million budget are sufficient to support the complete development of satellites and rockets remains to be carefully evaluated. Additionally, the development of space technology is highly dependent on international regulations and national launch permits. How New Taipei City, as a local government, will coordinate with the Taiwan Space Agency for subsequent launch and verification work will be a major challenge in the program's execution.
  
  Therefore, it is inappropriate for the public to interpret this program as a task that can be completed independently by a single local government; rather, it should be viewed as a component of local education and talent development within the national aerospace development blueprint.
  
  Subsequent observation should focus on the program's performance over the next four years, including the number of participating students, specific outcomes of project-based learning, and whether it can attract more private aerospace companies to invest resources. Furthermore, whether the government will regularly release progress reports on the R&D of "New Taipei Satellite 1" and "New Taipei Rocket 1," and how it will align with the annual plans of the Taiwan Space Agency, will be key to assessing whether the program can achieve its expected goals. Stakeholders will closely monitor how New Taipei City overcomes technical and regulatory challenges while promoting aerospace education, and how it translates this talent into actual industrial momentum.

# 新北市啟動四年航太人才培育計畫，目標2030年研製衛星與火箭

> 新北市政府教育局宣佈投入千萬預算，攜手中央研究院與國家太空中心，推動航太人才培育計畫，目標於2030年完成「新北衛星1號」與「新北火箭1號」研製。


新北市政府教育局於2026年11月3日正式宣佈，將投入新臺幣1,000萬元預算，展開為期四年的航太人才培育計畫，旨在透過產、官、學、研的跨界合作，建立完整的航太科技教育體系。此計畫的核心目標，是結閤中央研究院、國家太空中心以及各大學與產業資源，針對火箭製造、衛星研發、太空資料接收及後續應用領域，培養具備實作能力的專業人才。
作為計畫啟動的首波活動，新北市政府於11月3日至4日期間，在市府6樓大禮堂舉辦「新北市未來新空人才暨2026太空百年教育展」，透過專家講座、互動式體驗與主題展示，向在地學子展示太空科技的發展現況與未來趨勢，藉此提升學生對於航太領域的興趣。
根據新北市政府教育局局長張明文的說明，該計畫採取階段性與系統性的培育策略，將航太教育向下紮根並向上延伸。具體而言，教育局將從國中階段的職業試探課程開始，逐步銜接至高中職的深化課程與專題實作，並進一步串聯大學的前瞻研究資源與產業實務應用。這種垂直整合的模式，旨在讓學生在不同的學習階段，都能獲得接觸太空科技的機會，從基礎知識的建立到參與衛星與火箭的實際研發專案，逐步累積實務經驗。
此計畫的最終願景，是期望在2030年時，能成功研製出「新北衛星1號」與「新北火箭1號」，標誌著新北市在地方層級參與國家太空產業發展的里程碑。
目前關於此計畫的進度與執行細節，各界主要確認了預算規模、合作物件及明確的階段性目標。然而，針對「新北衛星1號」與「新北火箭1號」的具體技術規格、發射載具的選擇、衛星軌道配置，以及在計畫執行過程中如何分配中央與地方的研發權責，目前公開資訊中尚未詳述。此外，雖然教育局強調了產學合作的重要性，但具體參與計畫的民間企業名單，以及這些企業在人才培育與研發過程中將扮演的角色，目前仍有待後續相關政策細節的進一步揭露。
這些未定之處，將是外界觀察該計畫能否如期落實的關鍵指標。
此項計畫對新北市乃至臺灣的航太產業鏈具有深遠的影響。首先，在政策層面，這象徵著地方政府積極響應國家太空發展政策，透過教育資源的投入，為未來航太產業儲備大量技術人才，有助於緩解目前航太產業面臨的技術人才缺口。其次，在產業影響方面，若能成功研製出衛星與火箭，將能帶動新北市在地相關供應鏈的轉型升級，從傳統製造業向高附加價值的航太科技轉向。對於教育體系而言，這項計畫亦提供了一個具體的實作場域，讓技職教育與高等教育能更緊密地與產業需求接軌，提升學生的就業競爭力。
儘管該計畫願景宏大，但仍面臨諸多技術與資源上的限制。首先，航太科技研發門檻極高，涉及精密機械、通訊工程、材料科學等多領域整合，僅憑四年的培育週期與千萬預算，是否足以支撐衛星與火箭的完整研製，仍需審慎評估。此外，太空科技發展高度依賴國際法規與國家級的發射許可，新北市在地方政府的層級下，如何協調國家太空中心進行後續的發射與驗證工作，將是計畫執行的一大挑戰。
因此，外界不宜過度解讀此計畫為單一地方政府即可獨立完成的任務，而應將其視為國家航太發展藍圖中，地方教育與人才養成的一環。
後續觀察重點應聚焦於該計畫在未來四年內的執行成效，包括參與計畫的學生人數、專題實作的具體成果，以及是否能吸引更多民間航太企業投入資源。此外，政府是否會針對「新北衛星1號」與「新北火箭1號」的研發進度，定期發布階段性報告，以及如何與國家太空中心的年度計畫進行對接，將是評估該計畫是否能達成預期目標的關鍵。各界將密切關注新北市在推動航太人才教育的同時，如何克服技術與法規上的挑戰，並將這些人才轉化為實際的產業動能。
