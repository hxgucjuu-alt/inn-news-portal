  # Central Asian Diplomatic Strategy Heats Up: Kazakhstan and Uzbekistan Become Focus of International Cooperation
  
  > Kazakhstan and Uzbekistan have recently become hotspots for international diplomatic and economic cooperation. Hong Kong Chief Executive John Lee and Italian Prime Minister Giorgia Meloni have successively led delegations to visit the region, while the European Union has announced a new era in its relations with Central Asia, focusing on energy, digital transformation, and infrastructure development.
  
  More than thirty years after the collapse of the Soviet Union, the five Central Asian nations are gradually shedding their history of border disputes and isolation to become a key region in global geopolitical and economic planning. As the economic leader of Central Asia, Kazakhstan not only possesses abundant oil and mineral resources but is also the birthplace of the "Belt and Road" initiative. Recently, it has actively established a Ministry of Digital Development, Innovations and Aerospace Industry to promote large-scale data center construction. Uzbekistan, as the most populous country in Central Asia, is leveraging its historical status as a hub of the ancient Silk Road to actively seek international capital and encourage its enterprises to list overseas.
  
  In the past, the two countries experienced long-term border closures due to territorial issues. However, in recent years, with the signing of border agreements and improvements in infrastructure (such as roads in the Pamir Mountains), cross-border logistics and the movement of people within the region have significantly increased.
  
  Multiple media reports confirm that Kazakhstan and Uzbekistan have recently hosted a series of intensive international diplomatic visits. Italian Prime Minister Giorgia Meloni led a delegation to both countries at the end of May, explicitly identifying Central Asia as a priority for Italian foreign policy, with the aim of increasing Italy's visibility and influence in the region. Meanwhile, Hong Kong Chief Executive John Lee also plans to lead a delegation to Kazakhstan and Uzbekistan, focusing on promoting bilateral cooperation in finance, corporate listings, and technology. Furthermore, the European Union has publicly stated that its relationship with Central Asian countries has entered a "new era." Although the EU maintains reservations regarding the progress of reform programs in Central Asian nations, it has clearly expressed its willingness to strengthen partnerships.
  
  However, there are subtle differences in how various sources assess the prospects for cooperation. The EU emphasizes that while Central Asian countries have shown a willingness to cooperate and have made some progress, the EU still expects to see more concrete reform results and substantive actions. In contrast, reports from Hong Kong and Italy focus on the alignment of economic interests; for example, Hong Kong highlights the willingness of Uzbek enterprises to list in Hong Kong, as well as the investment potential of Kazakhstan's Ministry of Digital Development. This information indicates that Central Asian countries are adopting diversified diplomatic strategies when dealing with different partners, attempting to strike a balance between traditional regional powers and emerging international investors.
  
  For the Central Asian region, these diplomatic and economic collaborations will have profound impacts. First, at the industrial level, Kazakhstan's digital transformation and data center projects are expected to drive the upgrading of regional technological infrastructure. Second, Uzbekistan's financial opening policies may provide local enterprises with more international financing channels. Furthermore, with the reopening of border crossings and the optimization of transportation networks, the cost of cross-border trade among the five Central Asian countries is expected to decrease further. This not only facilitates regional economic integration but also provides new alternative routes for global supply chains. However, the extent to which these policies are implemented still depends on the administrative efficiency and legal environment within each country.
  
  Although international attention toward Central Asia has increased significantly, the outside world should not over-interpret the immediate effectiveness of these diplomatic visits. First, the political systems and market maturity of Central Asian countries vary, and international companies entering these markets still face challenges such as opaque legal regulations and complex bureaucratic systems. Second, the "reform progress" issue mentioned by the EU suggests that structural limitations regarding democratization and human rights protection still exist in the region, which could become a hidden concern for deepening EU-Central Asia cooperation in the future. Therefore, these diplomatic actions are currently mostly in the stage of intent and preliminary engagement; the actual return on investment and policy effects will still depend on whether specific contracts are signed and large-scale projects are implemented in the coming years.
  
  Follow-up observations should focus on three areas: First, whether Kazakhstan's "Ministry of Digital Development" can successfully attract international tech giants and translate this into actual economic growth momentum; second, whether the process of Uzbek enterprises listing in Hong Kong can become a landmark case for Central Asian companies to utilize international financial centers for financing; and third, whether the cooperation agreements between the EU and Central Asia regarding energy security and infrastructure will be adjusted due to changes in the geopolitical situation. These indicators will determine whether the five Central Asian countries can truly achieve a strategic transformation from being "landlocked" to becoming a "regional hub."

# 中亞外交戰略升溫：哈薩克與烏茲別克成國際合作焦點

> 近期哈薩克與烏茲別克成為國際外交與經濟合作熱點，香港特首李家超及義大利總理梅洛尼相繼率團出訪，歐盟亦宣佈與中亞開啟關係新時代，聚焦能源、數位轉型及基礎建設發展。


中亞五國在蘇聯解體三十多年後，正逐漸擺脫過往的邊界糾紛與交通封閉，成為全球地緣政治與經濟佈局的關鍵區域。哈薩克作為中亞經濟領頭羊，不僅擁有豐富的石油與礦產資源，更是「一帶一路」倡議的發源地，近期更積極成立人工智慧與數字發展部，推動大規模資料中心建設。烏茲別克則作為中亞人口最多的國家，憑藉其古絲路重鎮的歷史地位，正積極尋求國際資本挹注，並鼓勵企業赴海外上市。
過去兩國間曾因邊界問題長期封鎖關口，但近年隨著邊界協議的簽署與基礎設施（如帕米爾高原道路）的改善，區域內的跨境物流與人員流動已顯著提升。
多方媒體報導共同確認，哈薩克與烏茲別克近期迎來密集的國際外交訪問。義大利總理梅洛尼於五月底率團出訪兩國，明確將中亞視為義大利外交政策的優先事項，旨在提升義大利在該區域的能見度與影響力。與此同時，香港特首李家超亦計畫率團訪問哈薩克與烏茲別克，重點推動雙邊在金融、企業上市及科技領域的合作。此外，歐盟官方亦公開表示，與中亞國家的關係已進入「新時代」，雖然歐盟對中亞各國在改革程式上的表現持保留態度，但已明確表達加強夥伴關係的意願。
然而，各來源對於合作前景的評估存在細微差異。歐盟方面強調，儘管中亞國家已展現合作意願並取得部分進展，但歐盟仍期待看到更具體的改革成果與實質行動。相比之下，香港與義大利的報導則側重於經濟利益的對接，例如香港強調烏茲別克企業來港上市的意願，以及哈薩克在數字發展部的投資潛力。這些資訊顯示，中亞國家在面對不同合作夥伴時，採取了多元化的外交策略，試圖在傳統的區域強權與新興的國際投資者之間取得平衡。
對於中亞地區而言，這些外交與經濟合作將帶來深遠影響。首先，在產業層面，哈薩克的數位轉型與資料中心計畫，有望帶動區域內科技基礎設施的升級；其次，烏茲別克的金融開放政策，可能為當地企業提供更多國際融資管道。此外，隨著邊界口岸的重新開放與交通網路的最佳化，中亞五國的跨境貿易成本有望進一步降低，這不僅有利於區域經濟整合，也為全球供應鏈提供了新的替代路徑。然而，這些政策的落實程度仍取決於各國國內的行政效率與法治環境。
儘管國際社會對中亞的關注度顯著提升，但外界不應過度解讀這些外交訪問的即時成效。首先，中亞各國的政治體制與市場成熟度各異，國際企業在進入當地市場時，仍需面對法律規範不透明、官僚體系複雜等挑戰。其次，歐盟所提到的「改革進度」問題，暗示了該區域在民主化與人權保障方面仍存在結構性限制，這可能成為未來歐盟與中亞深化合作的隱憂。因此，這些外交行動目前多處於意向與初步對接階段，實際的投資回報與政策效應，仍需觀察未來數年內是否有具體的合約簽署與大型專案落地。
後續觀察重點應聚焦於三方面：第一，哈薩克推動的「數字發展部」是否能成功吸引國際科技巨頭進駐，並轉化為實際的經濟成長動能；第二，烏茲別克企業赴港上市的程式，是否能成為中亞企業利用國際金融中心進行融資的指標性案例；第三，歐盟與中亞在能源安全與基礎建設方面的合作協議，是否會因地緣政治局勢的變動而產生調整。這些指標將決定中亞五國能否真正實現從「內陸封閉」走向「區域樞紐」的戰略轉型。
