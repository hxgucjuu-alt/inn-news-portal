  # Taoyuan Promotes Space Science Education: Large-Scale Stage Play "A Century of Dreams" Depicts the Legends of Rocket Pioneers
  
  > The Taoyuan City Government Department of Education, in collaboration with Chung Yuan Christian University and National Taiwan University, presented the scientific-artistic stage play "A Century of Dreams" on the 17th and 18th of this month. The production promotes interdisciplinary science education by artistically interpreting the legendary stories of rocket pioneers Robert Goddard and Wernher von Braun.
  
  On the 17th and 18th of this month, the Taoyuan City Government Department of Education hosted a large-scale scientific-artistic stage play titled "A Century of Dreams: The Legendary Stories of Goddard and von Braun, the Ladder to the Moon" at the Taoyuan Arts Center. The event aimed to promote space science education to the public through the medium of performing arts. This production was co-planned and created by the Space Science and Technology Research Center of the College of Science at Chung Yuan Christian University and the Department of Physics at National Taiwan University, with support from the Taoyuan City Government, relevant science education foundations, and corporate entities. The performance combined music, singing, dance, and multimedia technology, attempting to transform complex space science knowledge into an engaging artistic work to achieve the interdisciplinary educational goal of "watching art while learning science."
  
  The event was held on a non-profit basis with no tickets sold to the public. Students and parents were able to gain admission through ticket reservation and on-site queuing, demonstrating Taoyuan City's determination to promote popular science education.
  
  According to multiple reports, the core narrative of the stage play focuses on the legendary lives of two rocket science pioneers: Robert Goddard and Wernher von Braun. Goddard is hailed as the father of modern rocketry, while von Braun holds a pivotal position in the history of space exploration. Through the interpretations of professional theater actors and vocalists, the production team brought historical scientific breakthroughs and personal struggles to the stage.
  
  This collaboration combined the academic resources of domestic higher education institutions with professional performing arts teams, indicating that Taiwan is gradually adopting innovative, interdisciplinary, and multimedia-integrated models for promoting science communication, using soft storytelling to lower the threshold for public engagement with space technology.
  
  Although the event received positive feedback for its promotion of science education, there are currently no detailed critical reports regarding the play's historical accuracy or how it balances "scientific facts" with "artistic fiction." Furthermore, as the event was non-profit, there is a lack of quantitative data supporting the number of participants and the actual effectiveness of scientific knowledge absorption after viewing. Some media coverage has focused on the educational significance of such activities, without further academic exploration or in-depth reporting on the historical controversies or scientific details presented in the play.
  
  The long-term influence of such science education activities combined with performing arts, and whether they can effectively stimulate students' interest in space science, remains subject to subsequent evaluation and observation by the Department of Education.
  
  This event has had a tangible impact on the science education environment in the Taoyuan area. By integrating resources from academia and local government, it not only enhanced the cultural and educational functions of the Taoyuan Arts Center but also provided a practical case study for interdisciplinary teaching in Taiwan. Regarding educational policy, such activities demonstrate a trend toward "de-professionalizing" scientific knowledge, moving space science beyond campus laboratories or textbooks and into the community through popular culture. This helps cultivate an initial understanding of the space technology industry among local students and may indirectly influence future talent development paths, laying a deeper social foundation for Taiwan's development in the field of space technology.
  
  However, it must be noted that the presentation of scientific-artistic stage plays has certain limitations. Artistic creation is inherently subjective, and audiences should not view the plot as strictly rigorous scientific history. There remains room for different evaluations and discussions in academia regarding the contributions and historical positioning of pioneers Goddard and von Braun. Over-interpreting the plot of the stage play may lead the public to have a simplified understanding of the complex history of scientific development. Therefore, the primary value of the event should be positioned as inspiring interest and promoting the scientific spirit, rather than replacing formal history of science education. Participants should maintain critical thinking and view the stage play as a starting point, rather than an end, for understanding the history of science.
  
  The focus for subsequent observation will be whether the Taoyuan City Government Department of Education will normalize this type of interdisciplinary science education model and expand it to other scientific fields. Additionally, whether the collaboration between Chung Yuan Christian University and National Taiwan University can continue to produce more works with scientific depth is also worth noting. With the rapid development of the global space industry, whether such educational activities can be transformed into substantial improvements in scientific literacy and further stimulate the willingness of local students to engage in space technology research will be key to future educational policy assessment. Furthermore, whether relevant units will track the effectiveness of this event and make the results public will help optimize strategies and directions for future science education promotion.

# 桃園推廣太空科學教育：大型舞臺劇《百年逐夢》演繹火箭先驅傳奇

> 桃園市政府教育局攜手中原大學與臺灣大學，於本月17、18日推出科學藝術舞臺劇《百年逐夢》，透過藝術演繹火箭先驅戈達德與馮布朗的傳奇故事，推廣跨域科學教育。


桃園市政府教育局於本月17日與18日，在桃園展演中心演藝廳舉辦大型科學藝術舞臺劇《百年逐夢：攀登月亮的天梯戈達德與馮布朗的傳奇故事》。該活動旨在透過藝術表演形式，向大眾推廣太空科學教育。此次演出由中原大學理學院太空科技研究中心，與臺灣大學物理系共同策劃創作，並獲得桃園市政府、相關科學教育基金會及企業單位的支援。演出形式結合了音樂、歌唱、舞蹈及多媒體技術，試圖將艱澀的太空科學知識轉化為具備觀賞性的藝術作品，實現「看的是藝術，學的是科學」的跨域教育目標。
活動採取公益索票方式，不對外售票，開放學生及家長透過索票與現場排隊方式入場，展現桃園市推動科學普及教育的決心。
根據多方報導確認，該舞臺劇的核心敘事主軸聚焦於兩位火箭科學先驅——羅伯特·戈達德（Robert Goddard）與韋恩·馮·布朗（Wernher von Braun）的生平傳奇。戈達德被譽為現代火箭之父，而馮·布朗則在太空探索史上具有舉足輕重的地位。製作團隊透過專業劇團演員與歌者的詮釋，將歷史上的科學突破與個人奮鬥過程搬上舞臺。
此次合作結合了國內高等學府的學術資源與專業表演藝術團隊，顯示出臺灣在推動科學傳播時，正逐漸採取跨學科、多媒體整合的創新模式，藉由軟性敘事降低大眾接觸太空科技的門檻。
儘管活動在推廣科學教育方面獲得正面評價，但針對該劇在歷史細節的考究程度，以及如何平衡「科學史實」與「藝術虛構」之間的比例，目前尚無詳細的評論報導。此外，由於該活動為公益性質，參與人數與實際觀影後的科學知識吸收成效，目前亦缺乏量化資料支援。部分媒體對此類活動的關注點集中於其教育推廣意義，對於劇中呈現的歷史爭議或科學細節，尚未有進一步的學術探討或深度報導。
公眾對於此類結合表演藝術的科學教育活動，其長期影響力與是否能有效激發學生對太空科學的興趣，仍有待後續教育局的評估與觀察。
此次活動對桃園地區的科學教育環境具有具體影響。透過整合學術界與地方政府資源，不僅提升了桃園展演中心的文化與教育功能，也為臺灣的跨域教學提供了實踐案例。對於教育政策而言，這類活動展示了將科學知識「去專業化」的趨勢，使太空科學不再侷限於校園實驗室或教科書，而是透過大眾文化的形式進入社群。此舉有助於培養在地學子對於太空科技產業的初步認知，並可能間接影響未來相關人才的培育路徑，為臺灣在太空科技領域的發展奠定更深厚的社會基礎。
然而，必須指出的是，科學藝術舞臺劇的呈現方式存在一定的限制。藝術創作本質上帶有主觀詮釋，觀眾在觀賞時不應將劇中情節完全視為嚴謹的科學史實。對於戈達德與馮·布朗兩位先驅的貢獻與其歷史定位，學術界仍有不同的評價與討論空間。過度解讀舞臺劇中的情節，可能導致大眾對複雜的科學發展史產生簡化認知。因此，該活動的主要價值應定位於啟發興趣與推廣科學精神，而非取代正規的科學史教育。參與者應保持批判性思考，將舞臺劇視為認識科學歷史的起點，而非終點。
後續觀察重點在於桃園市政府教育局是否會將此類跨域科學教育模式常態化，並擴大至其他科學領域。此外，中原大學與臺灣大學的合作模式是否能持續產出更多具備科學深度的藝術作品，亦值得關注。隨著全球太空產業的快速發展，此類教育活動能否轉化為實質的科學素養提升，並進一步激發在地學生投入太空科技研究的意願，將是未來教育政策評估的關鍵。此外，相關單位是否會針對此次活動進行成效追蹤，並將結果公開，將有助於最佳化未來科學教育推廣的策略與方向。
