  # Geopolitical Conflict and Shifts in Energy Supply: Multiple Challenges Facing Northeast Asian Energy Security
  
  > Impacted by supply chain tightening triggered by the US-Israeli war, Asian liquefied natural gas (LNG) demand has declined for two consecutive years. Northeast Asia is seeking a balance between nuclear expansion and the recovery of fossil fuel exports, as geopolitical risks profoundly reshape the regional energy landscape.
  
  As a core region for global energy consumption and industrial manufacturing, Northeast Asia is currently facing severe energy supply challenges. According to a Reuters report, influenced by the global supply chain tightening caused by the US-Israeli war, Asian liquefied natural gas (LNG) demand has shown a downward trend for two consecutive years. This phenomenon not only reflects the impact of energy price fluctuations on the regional economy but also highlights the vulnerability of Northeast Asian countries to geopolitical risks, given their high dependence on imported energy.
  
  At the same time, regional infrastructure security and energy transition strategies have become a focus of attention. Particularly regarding issues such as nuclear energy policy and the stability of large-scale water conservancy facilities, countries are adopting different paths to address the increasingly complex international energy situation.
  
  According to a cross-analysis by Reuters and ThinkChina, countries in Northeast Asia are adopting diverse strategies to stabilize energy supplies. On one hand, refineries in South Korea and Taiwan showed signs of export recovery in April 2026; data indicates that diesel and jet fuel exports in May rebounded compared to April, demonstrating that the regional refining industry retains a certain degree of resilience and export capacity when facing supply chain pressures. On the other hand, ThinkChina points out that China is leading the expansion of nuclear energy construction in Northeast Asia. Whether this shift is intended to address the energy crisis triggered by the Iran war or is an inevitable result of long-term energy transition strategies has become a focus of regional policy discussions.
  
  These adjustments in energy deployment indicate that Northeast Asian countries are attempting to alleviate the pressure of unstable external supplies through a dual-track approach of nuclear energy and fossil fuel exports.
  
  However, there remain information gaps and differences in interpretation among various sources regarding the assessment of regional energy security and infrastructure. Regarding the motivations behind China's nuclear expansion, some perspectives interpret it as a direct response to geopolitical crises, while other analyses suggest it is the result of long-term adjustments to the regional energy structure. Furthermore, regarding infrastructure security risks, Asia Times previously reported on non-structural deformations of the Three Gorges Dam. Although operators emphasized the overall safety of the dam, the potential connection between the stability of such large-scale water facilities and regional energy security remains a variable of concern for observers.
  
  It is currently impossible to confirm whether these sporadic infrastructure issues will cause a chain reaction in the overall energy supply chain of Northeast Asia; relevant information awaits further technical assessment and official data verification.
  
  The shift in energy supply has had a profound impact on the Northeast Asian region. First, the continuous decline in LNG demand has forced governments to re-evaluate the diversification of energy import sources to reduce reliance on single supply chains. Second, while the recovery in fuel exports from South Korea and Taiwan has eased short-term industrial pressure, their profitability remains constrained by fluctuations in international oil prices and transportation costs. Furthermore, while the acceleration of nuclear construction in various countries helps improve energy autonomy, it has also sparked policy debates regarding safety standards, environmental impacts, and nuclear waste disposal.
  
  These changes not only affect the energy industry but also directly impact the cost structure of manufacturing within the region, thereby influencing Northeast Asia's competitiveness in the global supply chain.
  
  Regarding current energy policies and the geopolitical situation, there are significant differences in perspectives, and the impact of single events should not be over-interpreted. Some analysts believe that nuclear expansion is a necessary means to address the global energy crisis, while others question its flexibility in dealing with short-term supply disruptions. Additionally, although refining export data shows a short-term recovery, this does not mean that the energy supply chain has fully returned to stability, as the duration and scale of the US-Israeli war remain highly uncertain. Therefore, observers caution that when evaluating the energy outlook for Northeast Asia, one must distinguish between short-term market fluctuations and long-term strategic adjustments, avoiding the direct equation of regional industrial data with the stability of the overall energy security system.
  
  Subsequent observation will focus on several key indicators: first, the long-term impact of the US-Israeli war on the global natural gas market and whether Asian LNG demand will see a third year of decline; second, the progress of Northeast Asian countries in nuclear expansion policies and how they balance nuclear development with public safety concerns; and finally, the continued adaptability of the regional refining industry in the face of international energy price fluctuations. As the second half of 2026 approaches, subsequent reports from the International Energy Agency (IEA) and regional energy think tanks will become key benchmarks for judging whether the energy policy transition in Northeast Asia is successful.
  
  The trade-offs made by national governments between energy security and economic development will continue to influence the future geopolitical status and industrial development paths of Northeast Asia.

# 地緣衝突與能源供應變局：東北亞能源安全面臨多重挑戰

> 受美以戰爭引發的供應鏈緊縮影響，亞洲液化天然氣需求連續兩年下滑。東北亞地區在核能擴張與化石燃料出口復甦之間尋求平衡，地緣政治風險正深刻重塑區域能源版圖。


東北亞地區作為全球能源消費與產業製造的核心地帶，近期面臨嚴峻的能源供應挑戰。根據路透社報導，受美以戰爭（US-Israeli war）導致的全球供應鏈緊縮影響，亞洲液化天然氣（LNG）需求已連續兩年呈現下滑趨勢。此一現象不僅反映了能源價格波動對區域經濟的衝擊，也凸顯了東北亞國家在高度依賴進口能源的背景下，面臨地緣政治風險時的脆弱性。
與此同時，區域內的基礎設施安全與能源轉型策略也成為各界關注焦點，特別是在核能政策與大型水利設施穩定性等議題上，各國正採取不同路徑以應對日益複雜的國際能源局勢。
根據路透社與《ThinkChina》的交叉分析，東北亞各國正採取多元策略以穩定能源供應。一方面，南韓與臺灣的煉油廠在二○二六年四月展現了出口復甦的跡象，資料顯示五月份柴油與航空燃油的出口量較四月有所回升，顯示區域內煉油產業在面對供應鏈壓力時，仍保有一定的彈性與外銷能力。另一方面，《ThinkChina》指出，中國正引領東北亞地區的核能建設擴張，這項轉變究竟是為了應對伊朗戰爭引發的能源危機，抑或是長期能源轉型戰略的必然結果，已成為區域政策討論的焦點。
這些能源佈局的調整，顯示出東北亞各國正試圖透過核能與化石燃料出口的雙軌並行，緩解外部供應不穩的壓力。
然而，針對區域能源安全與基礎設施的評估，各來源間仍存在資訊落差與詮釋差異。關於中國核能擴張的動機，部分觀點將其解讀為對地緣政治危機的直接反應，但亦有分析認為這是區域能源結構長期調整的結果。此外，針對基礎設施的安全風險，亞洲時報（Asia Times）曾報導三峽大壩出現非結構性變形的訊息，儘管營運方強調大壩整體安全，但此類大型水利設施的穩定性與區域能源安全之間的潛在關聯，仍是觀察家關注的變數。
目前尚無法確認這些零星的基礎設施問題是否會對東北亞整體的能源供應鏈造成連鎖效應，相關資訊仍待進一步的技術評估與官方資料佐證。
能源供應的變局對東北亞地區產生了深遠影響。首先，液化天然氣需求的持續下滑，迫使各國政府重新評估能源進口來源的多元化，以降低對單一供應鏈的依賴。其次，南韓與臺灣在燃油出口上的復甦，雖然緩解了短期內的產業壓力，但其獲利能力仍受制於國際油價與運輸成本的波動。此外，各國在核能建設上的加速，雖有助於提升能源自主性，但也引發了關於安全標準、環境影響及核廢料處理的政策辯論。
這些變動不僅影響能源產業，更直接衝擊了區域內的製造業成本結構，進而影響東北亞在全球供應鏈中的競爭力。
針對目前的能源政策與地緣政治局勢，各界觀點存在顯著差異，且不應過度解讀單一事件的影響。部分分析人士認為，核能擴張是應對全球能源危機的必要手段，但也有觀點質疑其在應對短期供應中斷時的靈活性。此外，雖然煉油出口資料顯示短期復甦，但這並不代表能源供應鏈已完全恢復穩定，因為美以戰爭的持續時間與規模仍充滿高度不確定性。因此，觀察家提醒，在評估東北亞能源前景時，必須區分短期市場波動與長期戰略調整，避免將區域性的產業資料直接等同於整體能源安全體系的穩定。
後續觀察重點將聚焦於幾個關鍵指標：首先是美以戰爭對全球天然氣市場的長期影響，以及亞洲液化天然氣需求是否會出現第三年的下滑；其次是東北亞國家在核能擴張政策上的進展，以及各國如何平衡核能發展與公眾安全疑慮；最後則是區域內煉油產業在面對國際能源價格波動時的持續適應能力。隨著二○二六年下半年的到來，國際能源署（IEA）與區域能源智庫的後續報告，將成為判斷東北亞能源政策轉型是否成功的關鍵依據。
各國政府在能源安全與經濟發展之間的權衡，將持續影響東北亞未來的地緣政治地位與產業發展路徑。
