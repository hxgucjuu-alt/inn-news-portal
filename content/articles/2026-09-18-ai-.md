  # Global Venture Capital Shifts Toward Hardware Deep Tech: AI Infrastructure Demand Drives Billions in Investment
  
  > As artificial intelligence technology evolves, Silicon Valley venture capital is shifting on a large scale toward deep tech fields such as semiconductors and robotics. This year, investments in hardware startups have accounted for one-third of total U.S. venture capital, indicating that the demand for AI computing power is reshaping the industrial investment landscape.
  
  The rapid iteration of global artificial intelligence (AI) technology is profoundly changing the capital allocation logic of Silicon Valley venture capital (VC). According to a recent report by "Crypto Briefing," the global venture capital market is experiencing a "return to hardware" boom, with billions of dollars shifting from pure software applications to semiconductors, robotics, and other fields classified as "deep tech." Because these fields are characterized by long research and development cycles and capital intensity, they were previously often viewed as high-barrier investments. However, with the extreme demand for computing power from large language models (LLMs), hardware infrastructure has become the core battlefield for AI development.
  
  According to cross-referenced sources, there has been a significant shift in the flow of Silicon Valley venture capital, with hardware companies accounting for nearly one-third of total U.S. venture capital allocation so far this year. The core driver of this trend is the urgent demand for advanced chips, data center hardware, and automated robotics in AI training and inference processes. At the same time, multinational corporations such as Bayer are actively undergoing global digital transformation through artificial intelligence and advanced supply chain planning systems (such as OMP's Unison Planning™), demonstrating the comprehensive penetration of AI from underlying hardware computing power to upper-level corporate decision-making systems.
  
  Despite the clear hardware investment boom, there are still discrepancies in how various media outlets describe the details of capital flows. Some reports emphasize that funding is concentrated in semiconductors and robotics, while corporate-level applications focus more on the integration of software and decision-making systems. Furthermore, there is currently no definitive market consensus on whether the "return to hardware" will crowd out funding for software startups, and the return on investment (ROI) cycles for specific deep tech fields will depend on the implementation of technology over the next few years. These detailed differences reflect the significant gap in investment risks and expected returns faced by the AI industry chain at different levels, from infrastructure to the application end.
  
  This wave of capital shifting has had a profound impact on the North American technology industry. First, semiconductor and data center hardware suppliers have become darlings of the venture capital market, which will further promote North America's leading position in AI computing infrastructure. Second, for enterprises, integrating AI technology to optimize global supply chains has become a necessary condition for maintaining competitiveness. For example, Bayer plans to share how it has rebuilt its global planning system through artificial intelligence at the 2026 Gartner Supply Chain Planning Summit in London, showing that large multinational corporations are actively integrating AI into daily operations to cope with increasingly complex global supply chain challenges.
  
  However, investors and industry observers also caution that deep tech investment is characterized by high risk and long cycles and should not be over-interpreted as a decline in the software industry. The return to hardware reflects the maturity of the AI ecosystem, meaning that software development has reached a stage that requires more powerful hardware support. Furthermore, although venture capital is pouring into the hardware sector, the market remains cautious about whether these investments can be quickly converted into commercial profits. Investors should be aware that the R&D threshold for deep tech is extremely high, and not all funded companies can successfully bridge the gap to technical commercialization; concerns about market overheating leading to a bubble remain.
  
  Subsequent observation will focus on three key areas: first, whether the enthusiasm of Silicon Valley venture capital for hardware investment can continue beyond 2026; second, whether enterprises can significantly improve decision-making efficiency and quality as expected after importing AI supply chain planning systems; and finally, whether a new wave of technological breakthroughs will emerge in the AI application layer as hardware infrastructure becomes complete. The market will continue to monitor the cross-validation of corporate practical cases and venture capital market trends at the 2026 Gartner Supply Chain Planning Summit to assess the overall development momentum of the AI industry chain.

# 全球創投轉向硬體深科技：AI 基礎建設需求推動數十億美元資金投入

> 隨著人工智慧技術演進，矽谷創投資金正大規模轉向半導體與機器人等深科技領域，今年硬體新創獲得的投資佔比已達美國創投總額的三分之一，顯示 AI 算力需求正重塑產業投資版圖。


全球人工智慧（AI）技術的快速迭代，正深刻改變矽谷創投（VC）的資金配置邏輯。根據《Crypto Briefing》近期報導，全球創投市場正掀起一股「硬體回歸」熱潮，數十億美元的資金正從純軟體應用轉向半導體、機器人以及其他被歸類為「深科技」（deep tech）的領域。這些領域因具備長期研發週期與資本密集特性，過去常被視為高門檻投資，但隨著大型語言模型（LLM）對算力的極致需求，硬體基礎建設已成為 AI 發展的核心戰場。
根據多方來源交叉比對，目前矽谷創投的資金流向已出現顯著變化，硬體公司在美國創投總資金分配中，今年以來已佔據近三分之一的份額。此一趨勢的核心驅動力在於 AI 訓練與推論過程對先進晶片、資料中心硬體及自動化機器人的迫切需求。與此同時，跨國企業如拜耳（Bayer）也正積極透過人工智慧與先進供應鏈規劃系統（如 OMP 的 Unison Planning™）進行全球數位轉型，顯示出 AI 從底層硬體算力到上層企業決策系統的全面滲透。
儘管硬體投資熱潮明確，但不同媒體對於資金流向的細節描述仍存在差異。部分報導強調資金集中於半導體與機器人，而企業層面的應用則更側重於軟體與決策體系的整合。此外，關於「硬體回歸」是否會排擠軟體新創的資金，目前尚無確切的市場共識，且針對特定深科技領域的投資回報週期，仍需視未來幾年的技術落地情況而定。這些細節差異反映了 AI 產業鏈在不同層級（從基礎設施到應用端）所面臨的投資風險與預期回報存在顯著落差。
此波資金轉向對北美科技產業產生了深遠影響。首先，半導體與資料中心硬體供應商成為創投市場的寵兒，這將進一步推動北美在 AI 算力基礎設施上的領先地位。其次，對於企業而言，整合 AI 技術以最佳化全球供應鏈已成為維持競爭力的必要條件。例如，拜耳計畫於 2026 年在倫敦舉行的 Gartner 供應鏈規劃峰會上，分享其如何透過人工智慧重建全球規劃體系，這顯示出大型跨國企業正積極將 AI 整合進日常營運，以應對日益複雜的全球供應鏈挑戰。
然而，投資人與產業觀察家也提醒，深科技投資具有高風險與長週期的特質，不應過度解讀為軟體產業的衰退。硬體回歸反映的是 AI 生態系統的成熟，即軟體發展已達到需要更強大硬體支撐的階段。此外，雖然創投資金湧入硬體領域，但市場對於這些投入是否能迅速轉化為商業獲利仍持審慎態度。投資者應注意，深科技的研發門檻極高，並非所有獲投企業都能順利跨越技術商業化的鴻溝，市場過熱可能導致泡沫化的隱憂依然存在。
後續觀察重點將聚焦於以下三方面：首先，矽谷創投對硬體投資的熱度是否能持續至 2026 年之後；其次，企業在匯入 AI 供應鏈規劃系統後，能否如預期般顯著提升決策效率與品質；最後，隨著硬體基礎建設的完備，AI 應用層是否會出現下一波技術爆發。市場將持續關注 2026 年 Gartner 供應鏈規劃峰會中，企業實務案例與創投市場趨勢的交叉驗證，以評估 AI 產業鏈的整體發展動能。
