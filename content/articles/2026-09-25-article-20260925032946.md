  # Venezuela's Political and Economic Turmoil Triggers Energy Crisis; Central America and the Caribbean Seek Energy Security Transition
  
  > Instability in Venezuela is impacting regional energy supplies, prompting officials from Central American and Caribbean nations to gather in Washington to discuss enhancing energy security and cross-border cooperation in response to potential supply chain risks and regional geopolitical shifts.
  
  Central America and the Caribbean have long been highly dependent on regional energy supply chains, particularly Venezuela's oil exports and preferential energy agreements. However, as Venezuela's domestic political and economic situation remains volatile, the country's energy production and export capabilities face severe challenges, directly threatening the economic stability of neighboring Central American and Caribbean nations that rely on its energy assistance. In response, officials from the relevant countries have scheduled an energy summit in Washington, hosted by then-U.S. Vice President Joe Biden, aimed at alleviating regional energy security anxieties caused by Venezuela's instability through expanded international cooperation and the promotion of energy transition.
  
  According to multiple public sources, government representatives from Central American and Caribbean nations will attend the energy summit in Washington on May 3rd and 4th. The core issue of this meeting is to assess the long-term impact of Venezuela's political situation on the regional energy landscape. The focus shared by the attending representatives includes: how to reduce reliance on a single supply source, how to build more resilient regional power grids, and how to attract more diverse energy investments through international multilateral mechanisms to ensure that countries can maintain basic livelihoods and industrial operations in the event of disruptions to regional supply chains.
  
  Although a consensus has been reached regarding the need for energy security, source information indicates differing emphases on specific implementation paths and resource allocation. Some reports point out that energy security issues are structurally linked to the region's long-standing problems with violent crime, gangs, and drug trafficking, particularly in the Northern Triangle of Central America, where security issues often interfere with the construction and maintenance of infrastructure. Furthermore, regarding the assessment of the situation in Venezuela, there remain policy disagreements among nations between adopting tough sanctions or maintaining dialogue to safeguard energy supplies.
  
  It remains to be confirmed whether the countries will be able to reach specific energy mutual assistance agreements after the meeting, as well as the scale of U.S. funding and the details of technology transfer involved.
  
  The impact of this energy crisis on Central America and the Caribbean is profound. First, fluctuations in energy prices have directly impacted the price levels in these countries, thereby exacerbating social instability. Second, the uncertainty of energy supplies has forced governments to re-examine their national energy policies, elevating energy transition and security to the level of national security. For industries, rising energy costs may lead to a decline in manufacturing competitiveness, especially in Caribbean island nations that rely on power-intensive industries, where energy transition has become key to maintaining economic survival. At the policy level, countries are actively seeking cooperation with the United States and other international institutions to promote the development of renewable energy to reduce reliance on traditional fossil fuels.
  
  However, it must be noted that it is inappropriate to oversimplify regional energy security issues into a single factor. While Venezuela's turmoil is the current catalyst, the energy plight of Central America and the Caribbean is also intertwined with aging infrastructure, poor governance, and severe violent crime within the region. Analysts warn that energy security should not be viewed as a panacea for all the region's problems. If the focus is solely on adjusting energy supply chains while ignoring regional security and the rule of law, energy facilities themselves may become targets for gangs and violent organizations, thereby offsetting the positive benefits of energy transition.
  
  Subsequent observations will focus on the implementation of specific policies following the Washington energy summit. First, whether countries can successfully attract multinational energy companies to invest in infrastructure upgrades will be an indicator of the meeting's effectiveness. Second, the progress of the Regional Energy Grid construction and how to balance the costs of energy transition with the burden on the public will test the governing capabilities of each nation. Finally, the degree of international intervention in the security issues of the Northern Triangle of Central America will directly affect whether energy security strategies can be effectively implemented in the region. The INN editorial department will continue to track the subsequent developments of regional energy policies and geopolitical changes.

# 委內瑞拉政經動盪引發能源危機，中美洲與加勒比海地區尋求能源安全轉型

> 委內瑞拉局勢不穩衝擊區域能源供應，中美洲與加勒比海各國官員齊聚華盛頓，商討提升能源安全及跨境合作，應對潛在的供應鏈風險與區域地緣政治變動。


中美洲與加勒比海地區長期以來高度依賴區域內的能源供應鏈，特別是委內瑞拉的石油出口與優惠能源協議。然而，隨著委內瑞拉國內政經局勢持續動盪，該國的能源生產與出口能力面臨嚴峻挑戰，直接威脅到依賴其能源援助的中美洲及加勒比海鄰國的經濟穩定。為此，相關國家官員已安排於華盛頓召開能源高峰會，由時任美國副總統喬·拜登主持，旨在透過擴大國際合作與推動能源轉型，緩解因委國局勢不穩而引發的區域能源安全焦慮。
根據多方公開資訊確認，中美洲與加勒比海各國政府代表將於五月三日至四日出席在華盛頓舉辦的能源高峰會。此次會議的核心議題在於評估委內瑞拉政局對區域能源景觀的長期影響。與會代表共同關注的焦點包括：如何減少對單一供應源的依賴、如何建立更具韌性的區域電網，以及如何透過國際多邊機制，吸引更多元的能源投資，以確保在區域供應鏈出現斷鏈時，各國仍能維持基本的民生與產業運作。
儘管各界對於能源安全的需求達成共識，但在具體執行路徑與資源分配上，來源資訊呈現出不同的側重點。部分報導指出，能源安全問題與該地區長期存在的暴力犯罪、幫派與毒梟活動存在結構性關聯，特別是在中美洲北部三角地帶（Northern Triangle），治安問題往往會干擾基礎設施的建設與維護。此外，針對委內瑞拉局勢的評估，各國在採取強硬制裁還是維持對話以保障能源供應之間，仍存在政策上的分歧。
目前尚待確認的是，各國在會後是否能達成具體的能源互助協議，以及美國在其中的資金挹注規模與技術轉移細節。
此次能源危機對中美洲與加勒比海地區的影響深遠。首先，能源價格的波動直接衝擊了這些國家的物價水平，進而加劇社會不穩定。其次，能源供應的不確定性迫使各國政府重新檢視其國家能源政策，將能源轉型與安全提升至國家安全層級。對於產業而言，能源成本的攀升可能導致製造業競爭力下降，尤其是在依賴電力密集型產業的加勒比海島國，能源轉型已成為維持經濟生存的關鍵。政策層面上，各國正積極尋求與美國及其他國際機構合作，推動再生能源發展，以降低對傳統化石燃料的依賴。
然而，必須指出的是，將區域能源安全問題過度簡化為單一因素的結果是不適當的。雖然委內瑞拉的動盪是當前的催化劑，但中美洲與加勒比海地區的能源困境，亦與基礎設施老舊、治理效能不彰以及區域內嚴重的暴力犯罪問題交織在一起。分析人士提醒，不應將能源安全視為解決區域所有問題的萬靈丹。若僅關注能源供應鏈的調整，而忽略了區域治安與法治建設，能源設施本身恐將成為幫派與暴力組織的攻擊目標，進而抵銷能源轉型的正面效益。
後續觀察重點將集中在華盛頓能源高峰會後的具體政策落實情況。首先，各國是否能成功吸引跨國能源企業投入基礎設施更新，將是檢驗此次會議成效的指標。其次，各國在區域能源網際網路（Regional Energy Grid）的建設進度，以及如何平衡能源轉型成本與民生負擔，將考驗各國政府的執政能力。最後，國際社會對於中美洲北部三角地帶治安問題的介入程度，將直接影響能源安全策略能否在該地區有效落地。INN新聞編輯部將持續追蹤此區域能源政策的後續發展與地緣政治變化。
