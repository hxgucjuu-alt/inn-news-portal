  # The Dual Challenge of Technology and Education: Opportunities and Crises of AI Application in the Asia-Pacific Region
  
  > As artificial intelligence technology expands rapidly across the Asia-Pacific region, significant progress is being seen in everything from infrastructure construction to medical applications. However, a recent OECD study warns that over-reliance on AI will lead to a decline in students' cognitive abilities, while the arts and cultural sectors have also begun to reflect on the soulless nature of AI.
  
  The development of artificial intelligence (AI) technology is profoundly changing the industrial landscape and social structure of the Asia-Pacific region. At the infrastructure level, the Asian Development Bank (ADB) recently announced a major plan worth up to $70 billion, aimed at comprehensively upgrading energy and digital infrastructure in the Asia-Pacific region by 2035. Key focuses include strengthening power grid connectivity, promoting cross-border electricity trade, and expanding broadband coverage. This policy serves not only as a cornerstone for regional economic development but also provides the hardware support necessary for the application of digital technology.
  
  Meanwhile, the U.S. government continues to reiterate its commitment to regional economic, trade, and technology partnerships to Asia-Pacific leaders, emphasizing the importance of maintaining high standards of trade and cooperation during the digital transformation process, demonstrating the determination of major powers in the region's technological competition and strategic layout.
  
  In terms of industrial application, the penetration rate of AI has shifted from the consumer end to professional fields. According to recent reports, global tech giants and startups are racing to develop "personal AI agents," such as the Muse application launched by Meta, which attempts to handle complex tasks by automatically understanding user intent.
  
  In the medical field, the clinical value of AI is also highly regarded. United Orthopedic gathered over 200 experts from around the world at the 18th Global User Meeting for Joint Reconstruction (ARC) held in Kuala Lumpur, Malaysia, to focus on the application of robot-assisted surgery and AI in digital orthopedics, showing that AI has become a key tool for improving medical precision and surgical efficiency.
  
  Although AI shows potential in industrial applications, its negative impacts are gradually emerging. The Organization for Economic Co-operation and Development (OECD) conducted the "Programme for International Student Assessment" (PISA) survey on over 760,000 15-year-old students across 91 countries, finding that excessive use of generative AI leads to "cognitive outsourcing" among students, which in turn causes a significant decline in scientific literacy. The study points out that students who are accustomed to handing over their thinking processes to AI are seeing their deep learning abilities eroded.
  
  Furthermore, in the field of culture and art, Studio Ghibli producer Toshio Suzuki has publicly criticized generative AI imagery, arguing that it lacks the vitality and warmth of hand-drawn work by craftsmen found in Hayao Miyazaki's films. This also explains why the once-popular "Ghibli-style" AI image trend quickly faded in a short period of time.
  
  The aforementioned developments represent both opportunities and severe challenges for the Asia-Pacific region. At the policy level, governments across the Asia-Pacific need to formulate corresponding educational and ethical norms while promoting digital infrastructure to address the cognitive risks brought by AI. In terms of industry, although the development models of AI assistants are diverse, their security and competitive strategies remain to be tested by the market. While the medical community is actively embracing digital transformation, clinical applications must still strictly follow evidence-based medicine to ensure that technological upgrades truly benefit patients rather than merely blindly pursuing automation.
  
  It is worth noting that there are clear differences in attitudes toward AI across different fields. The tech industry tends to view AI as a productivity tool, dedicated to increasing the level of automation; however, the education and art sectors are more concerned with the subjectivity of "humans." OECD data reminds us that AI may weaken basic human cognitive abilities, while Toshio Suzuki's perspective emphasizes the essence that technology cannot replace human emotion and soul. These views remind the public not to over-interpret the omnipotence of AI, and even more so, not to ignore the "degeneration" risks that humans may face in the learning and creative processes after technology becomes widespread.
  
  Subsequent observations should focus on the implementation efficiency of digital infrastructure in the Asia-Pacific region, as well as how national education policies are adjusted to cope with the phenomenon of "cognitive outsourcing." In addition, as the personal AI agent market enters a polarized development phase, the competition between large tech giants and startups regarding privacy protection and system security will be the key to determining whether AI can develop healthily. All sectors should continue to monitor empirical data on these technologies in clinical and educational environments to balance the relationship between technological innovation and core human capabilities.

# 科技與教育的雙重挑戰：AI 應用在亞太地區的機遇與危機

> 隨著人工智慧技術在亞太地區的快速擴張，從基礎設施建設到醫療應用均見顯著進展。然而，OECD 最新研究警告過度依賴 AI 將導致學生認知能力下滑，而藝術與文化界亦開始反思 AI 缺乏靈魂的本質。


人工智慧（AI）技術的發展正深刻改變亞太地區的產業格局與社會結構。在基礎設施層面，亞洲開發銀行（ADB）於近期宣佈一項高達 700 億美元的重大計畫，旨在 2035 年前全面提升亞太地區的能源與數位基礎建設，重點包括強化電網連結、促進跨境電力貿易及擴大寬頻覆蓋，這項政策不僅是區域經濟發展的基石，更為數位科技的應用提供了硬體支撐。
與此同時，美國政府亦持續向亞太領袖重申其對區域經貿與科技夥伴關係的承諾，強調在數位轉型過程中，維持高標準貿易與合作的重要性，顯示出大國在該區域科技競爭與佈局的決心。
在產業應用方面，AI 的滲透率已從消費端轉向專業領域。根據近期報導，全球科技巨頭與新創公司正競相開發「個人 AI 代理」，如 Meta 推出的 Muse 應用程式，試圖透過自動化理解使用者意圖來處理複雜任務。
在醫療領域，AI 的臨床價值同樣受到重視，聯合骨材（United Orthopedic）在馬來西亞吉隆坡舉辦的第 18 屆全球關節重建使用者大會（ARC）中，集結了全球逾 200 位專家，重點探討機器人輔助手術與 AI 在數位骨科中的應用，顯示 AI 已成為提升醫療精準度與手術效率的關鍵工具。
儘管 AI 在產業應用上展現潛力，但其帶來的負面影響也逐漸浮現。經濟合作暨發展組織（OECD）針對全球 91 個國家、超過 76 萬名 15 歲學生進行的「國際學生能力評量計畫」（PISA）調查發現，過度使用生成式 AI 會導致學生的「認知外包」，進而造成科學素養顯著滑落。研究指出，習慣將思考過程交給 AI 的學生，其深層學習能力正遭受侵蝕。
此外，在文化藝術領域，吉卜力工作室製作人鈴木敏夫亦公開批評生成式 AI 影象，認為其缺乏宮崎駿作品中那種由匠人親手繪製的生命力與溫度，這也解釋了為何曾經風靡全球的「吉卜力風格」AI 影象熱潮，在短時間內迅速退燒。
上述發展對亞太地區而言，既是機遇也是嚴峻挑戰。在政策層面，亞太各國政府需在推動數位基建的同時，制定相應的教育與倫理規範，以應對 AI 帶來的認知風險。產業方面，AI 助理的發展模式雖呈現多元，但其安全性與競爭策略仍待市場檢驗。醫療界雖然積極擁抱數位轉型，但臨床應用仍需嚴格遵循實證醫學，確保技術升級能真正造福患者，而非僅是盲目追求自動化。
值得注意的是，不同領域對 AI 的態度存在明顯分歧。科技產業傾向於將 AI 視為生產力工具，致力於提升自動化程度；然而，教育界與藝術界則更關注「人」的主體性。OECD 的資料提醒我們，AI 可能會削弱人類的基礎認知能力；而鈴木敏夫的觀點則強調了技術無法取代人類情感與靈魂的本質。這些觀點提醒大眾，不應過度解讀 AI 的全能性，更不應忽視技術普及後，人類在學習與創作過程中可能面臨的「退化」風險。
後續觀察重點應聚焦於亞太地區在數位基建落實後的執行效率，以及各國教育政策如何調整以應對「認知外包」現象。此外，隨著個人 AI 代理市場進入兩極化發展，大型科技巨頭與新創公司在隱私保護與系統安全上的競爭，將是決定 AI 是否能健康發展的關鍵。各界應持續關注這些技術在臨床與教育環境中的實證資料，以平衡技術創新與人類核心能力之間的關係。
