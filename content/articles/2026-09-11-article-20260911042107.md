  # Geopolitical Rivalry in the Pacific Intensifies: U.S., New Zealand, and Australia Strengthen Blue Pacific Strategic Cooperation
  
  > The geopolitical significance of the Pacific region has increased significantly in recent years, particularly the strategic triangle formed by the Solomon Islands, Australia, and New Zealand, which has become a focal point for international power competition. According to the "Building a Blue Pacific Agenda for the Twenty-First Century" symposium, co-hosted by the Center for Strategic and International Studies (CSIS) and the embassies of Australia and New Zealand in the United States, the Pacific Islands Forum (PIF) has become a core arena for nations to compete for regional influence. Such high-level dialogues involve not only traditional national defense and security but also extend to substantive gamesmanship in economic frameworks and foreign policy, highlighting the region's critical position in the Indo-Pacific strategic landscape.
  
  During the symposium, U.S. representative Kurt Campbell explicitly stated that the United States must work closely with traditional allies such as Australia and New Zealand to jointly address challenges under the Indo-Pacific economic framework. Media reports indicate that control and discourse power within the Pacific Islands Forum are seen as key to countering the expansion of regional forces. Participating scholars emphasized that the lessons learned from the Solomon Islands' diplomatic shift—caused by past strategic negligence—have prompted the U.S., Australia, and New Zealand to re-evaluate their level of engagement with Pacific Island nations to ensure the strategic stability of the region.
  
  Observing from a geographical perspective, the Solomon Islands are situated precisely between Australia and New Zealand, making their strategic value self-evident. Furthermore, Papua New Guinea, as the host of the 2018 APEC summit, has also become a focal point for regional power competition, demonstrating that the stability of this triangle directly affects the balance of power in the Indo-Pacific region.
  
  Although all parties share the goal of strengthening Pacific strategy, different perspectives remain regarding specific implementation strategies and priorities. Some analyses point out that the Solomon Islands often employ diverse diplomatic means when facing competition between major powers, which requires the U.S., Australia, and New Zealand to consider the autonomy and development needs of the island nations themselves when promoting the "Blue Pacific" agenda. In addition, regarding how to concretize the impact of "countering the Indo-Pacific economic framework," countries are currently still in the policy discussion stage concerning the specific details of economic aid and security commitments, and have not yet formed a fully unified set of action guidelines.
  
  This policy gray area reflects that in attempting to consolidate regional influence, the U.S., Australia, and New Zealand must strike a delicate balance between "strategic containment" and "respect for island nation sovereignty."
  
  This adjustment in regional strategy will have a profound impact on the public policy, infrastructure construction, and education industries of Pacific Island nations. As the U.S., Australia, and New Zealand increase their focus on the region, it is expected that more cooperation projects targeting the "Blue Pacific" will emerge, aimed at enhancing the economic resilience of island nations and reducing their reliance on a single external power. For Australia and New Zealand, maintaining stability in the Pacific region is not only a diplomatic priority but also the core of national security, which will directly influence the resource allocation and policy orientation of the region on the international political stage in the coming years.
  
  However, whether this investment of resources can be transformed into substantive regional security guarantees still depends on whether each country can propose specific plans that align with the long-term development interests of the island nations.
  
  Academia and think tanks have also issued warnings against over-interpreting the influence of a single symposium. The diverse voices and complex internal politics within the Pacific Islands Forum are the main variables affecting the promotion of regional policies. While the strategic cooperation between the U.S., Australia, and New Zealand aims to consolidate regional security, if they ignore the actual needs of island nations regarding issues such as climate change and sustainable development, and approach the matter solely from the perspective of great power competition, it may be difficult to gain broad recognition from local countries. Therefore, various sectors suggest that when promoting the Blue Pacific agenda, a more inclusive and long-term dialogue mechanism should be adopted to avoid treating island nations merely as pawns in geopolitics.
  
  If a partnership based on mutual trust cannot be established, unilateral strategic intervention may trigger a backlash from island nations, thereby weakening the long-term influence of the U.S., Australia, and New Zealand in the region.
  
  Subsequent observations will focus on whether the three nations can implement the initiatives from the symposium and translate them into concrete bilateral or multilateral agreements. In particular, economic aid plans for the Solomon Islands and other Pacific Island nations, as well as the interaction patterns of various countries within the Pacific Islands Forum, will be key indicators for evaluating the effectiveness of this strategic adjustment. Furthermore, whether countries can effectively enhance the governance capacity and economic autonomy of local nations without exacerbating regional tensions will be an important aspect of observing the development of Pacific geopolitics in the future.
  
  Against the backdrop of an increasingly complex global geopolitical environment, how the U.S., Australia, and New Zealand can effectively align their strategic intentions with the actual needs of island nations through the "Blue Pacific" agenda will become a litmus test for the success or failure of their regional foreign policies.

# 太平洋地緣政治角力升溫：美紐澳強化藍色太平洋戰略合作

> 太平洋區域的地緣政治重要性近年顯著提升，特別是索羅門群島、澳洲與紐西蘭所構成的戰略三角區域，已成為國際強權競逐的焦點。根據戰略暨國際研究中心（CSIS）與澳洲、紐西蘭駐美國大使館共同舉辦的「建立21世紀藍色太平洋要務」（Building a Blue Pacific Agenda for the Twenty-First Century）研討會內容顯示，太平洋島國論壇（PIF）已成為各國爭取區域影響力的核心場域。此類高層級對話不僅涉及傳統的國防安全，更延伸至經濟架構與外交政策的實質博弈，凸顯出該區域在印太戰略版圖中的關鍵地位。


在本次研討會中，美方代表坎博（Kurt Campbell）明確提出，美國需與澳洲、紐西蘭等傳統盟友緊密合作，共同應對印太經濟架構下的挑戰。媒體報導指出，太平洋島國論壇的掌控權與話語權，被視為反制區域勢力擴張的關鍵。與會學者強調，過去因戰略輕忽而導致索羅門群島外交轉向的教訓，促使美澳紐三國必須重新評估對太平洋島國的投入力度，確保該區域的戰略穩定性。
從地理位置觀察，索羅門群島正好位於澳洲與紐西蘭之間，其戰略價值不言而喻，而巴布亞紐幾內亞作為2018年APEC高峰會主辦國，亦曾成為區域勢力競逐的焦點，顯示此三角地帶的穩定與否，直接牽動印太區域的權力平衡。
儘管各方對於強化太平洋戰略的目標一致，但在具體的執行策略與優先順序上，仍存在不同觀點。部分分析指出，索羅門群島在面對大國博弈時，往往採取多元外交手段，這使得美澳紐在推動「藍色太平洋」議程時，需考量島國自身的自主性與發展需求。此外，針對如何具體化「反制印太經濟架構」的影響，目前各國在經濟援助與安全承諾的具體細節上，仍處於政策討論階段，尚未形成完全一致的行動指南。
這種政策上的模糊地帶，反映出美澳紐在試圖鞏固區域影響力時，必須在「戰略圍堵」與「尊重島國主權」之間取得精細的平衡。
此區域戰略的調整，對於太平洋島國的公共政策、基礎設施建設及教育產業將產生深遠影響。隨著美澳紐加強對該地區的關注，預計未來將有更多針對「藍色太平洋」的合作計畫出爐，旨在提升島國的經濟韌性，並減少其對單一外部勢力的依賴。對於澳洲與紐西蘭而言，維持太平洋區域的穩定不僅是外交優先事項，更是國家安全的核心，這將直接影響未來數年內，該地區在國際政治舞臺上的資源分配與政策導向。
然而，這種資源投入能否轉化為實質的區域安全保障，仍取決於各國能否提出符合島國長期發展利益的具體方案。
學界與智庫亦提出警告，不應過度解讀單一研討會的影響力。太平洋島國論壇內部的多元聲音與複雜的內部政治，是影響區域政策推動的主要變數。美澳紐的戰略合作雖然旨在鞏固區域安全，但若忽視島國在氣候變遷、永續發展等議題上的實際需求，僅從大國競爭的角度出發，恐難以獲得在地國家的廣泛認同。因此，各界建議在推動藍色太平洋議程時，應採取更具包容性與長遠性的對話機制，避免將島國單純視為地緣政治的棋子。
若無法建立基於互信的夥伴關係，單方面的戰略介入恐將引發島國的反彈，進而削弱美澳紐在該區域的長期影響力。
後續觀察重點將聚焦於美澳紐三國是否能落實研討會中的倡議，並將其轉化為具體的雙邊或多邊協議。特別是針對索羅門群島及其他太平洋島國的經濟援助計畫，以及各國在太平洋島國論壇中的互動模式，將是評估此一戰略調整成效的關鍵指標。此外，各國是否能在不激化區域緊張局勢的前提下，有效提升在地國家的治理能力與經濟自主，將是未來觀察太平洋地緣政治發展的重要面向。
在當前全球地緣政治環境日益複雜的背景下，美澳紐如何透過「藍色太平洋」議程，將戰略意圖與島國的實際需求有效對接，將成為檢驗其區域外交政策成敗的試金石。
