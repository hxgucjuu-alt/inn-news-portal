  # Tracing the Origins of the "Spaghetti Western": From European Production to a Global Genre Code
  
  > Summary: The Spaghetti Western originated in low-budget European productions of the 1960s, known for Sergio Leone's style and transnational filming locations. Multiple media outlets cross-reference that the genre name first appeared in writing by a Spanish journalist in 1966, and that it stands in stark contrast to the Hollywood Western tradition in aesthetics, moral narrative, and industrial structure.
  
  The Spaghetti Western, as a film genre, has long been regarded as a creative response by the European film industry to the American Western tradition. According to Wikipedia's definition, the term broadly refers to Western films produced in Europe that became widely known from the mid-1960s onward due to Sergio Leone's directorial style and international box office success.
  
  IMDb's news column notes that the genre emerged in Italy during the 1960s, characterized by an exploration of moral complexity and the extensive use of European directors; the name itself reflects its production context—most of these low-budget, gritty, and highly violent Westerns were made in Italy. Although classified as "Westerns," their production geography, financial structure, and narrative logic differ significantly from American-made Westerns.
  
  A point jointly confirmed by multiple media outlets is that the Spaghetti Western was not the accidental product of a single director or company, but rather a systemic phenomenon arising from the restructuring of the European film industry throughout the 1960s and 1970s. MSN's report states that, simply put, the genre consists of Westerns shot by Italian studios and filmmakers in the 1960s and 1970s, though the actual filming locations were often in Spain or other European settings that could pass for the American West.
  
  An article from No Film School further traces the origin of the term, stating that "Spaghetti Western" was first used by Spanish journalist Alfonso Sánchez Martínez in 1966 to describe the phenomenon of Westerns dominated by Italian production and made by non-Americans. This act of naming itself carries transnational significance: a Spanish journalist gave an English-language name with food associations to a film genre produced in Italy, a label that would later become the globally recognized genre designation.
  
  Regarding differences among sources and information still pending confirmation, the main points concern the boundaries of the genre definition and the timing of the name's first use. Slashfilm's report emphasizes that the aesthetic characteristics of the Spaghetti Western were established by Leone in *A Fistful of Dollars*, describing his style as an "intensified and stylized" version of 1940s Westerns, rather than mere imitation or replication. CBR takes a broader approach, defining Spaghetti Westerns as essentially low-budget films made and shot in Europe—typically Italy—that were not bound by Hollywood's prior moral standards.
  
  Although these two definitions point in the same direction, they differ in emphasis: the former focuses on the decisive influence of the director's aesthetics, while the latter stresses industrial structure and the liberation from moral codes. Additionally, No Film School explicitly states the name first appeared in 1966, but Wikipedia and IMDb do not provide the same precise date, instead using general descriptions such as "mid-1960s" or "1960s." This discrepancy in timing is not a contradiction, but it shows that different sources vary in the depth of their historical verification.
  
  The concrete impact of this genre is reflected in three areas: film aesthetics, global industrial division of labor, and the language of genre criticism. On the aesthetic level, the Spaghetti Western broke with the long-standing Manichaean good-versus-evil dichotomy of American Westerns, instead presenting moral ambiguity, anti-hero protagonists, and more realistic violence. CBR specifically notes that these films were not constrained by Hollywood's "prior moral standards," meaning the European production environment offered greater creative freedom. On the industrial level, the low-cost production environments and diverse landscapes of Italy and Spain allowed Europe to establish a complete Western production chain outside the Hollywood system.
  
  MSN's report reminds readers that although these films were credited as Italian, their actual production often spanned multiple countries, creating a strategy of spatial representation that simulated "fake America." On the level of critical language, the popularity of the term "Spaghetti Western" turned a food name into an official term in film taxonomy, later spawning similar labels such as "Sauerkraut Western," demonstrating that genre labels themselves are products of cultural translation and misreading.
  
  Regarding differing viewpoints and limitations, it must be carefully noted that the Spaghetti Western is not an internally consistent movement but rather a category retrospectively constructed by critics. Slashfilm's report centers on Leone's aesthetic choices, yet not all Italian-produced Westerns followed Leone's style; likewise, the "low-budget" and "moral liberation" characteristics emphasized by CBR do not apply to every work. IMDb's definition focuses on European directors and moral complexity, but this overlooks the fact that some works starred American actors or imitated Hollywood narrative formulas.
  
  Furthermore, while the 1966 origin claim cited by No Film School is specific and traceable, a historical account based on a single source still requires more archival documentation to confirm whether the term circulated earlier within Italian or Spanish industry circles. Therefore, readers should not treat any single media outlet's definition as the final word, but rather understand that the Spaghetti Western as a genre category inherently contains multiple competing definitions and evaluations.

This asymmetry remains an ongoing topic for discussion in postcolonial film studies and global film historiography.
  
  The information currently provided by various media outlets is sufficient to establish the basic contours of the genre, but systematic research is still lacking regarding the actual context of the term's usage, domestic Italian audience reactions, and the reception history of these films in Latin America or Asia—all of which merit further follow-up.

# 「義大利麵西部片」源流考：從歐洲製作到全球型別符碼

> 摘要：義大利麵西部片源自1960年代歐洲低成本製作，以塞吉歐·李昂尼風格與跨國拍攝地點著稱。多家媒體交叉指出，此型別名稱首見於1966年西班牙記者筆下，並在美學、道德敘事與產業結構上與好萊塢傳統西部片形成鮮明對比。

義大利麵西部片（Spaghetti Western）作為一種電影型別，長期以來被視為歐洲電影工業對美國西部片傳統的創造性回應。根據維基百科的定義，此類作品泛指在歐洲製作、並於1960年代中期以後因塞吉歐·李昂尼（Sergio Leone）的導演風格與國際票房成功而廣為人知的西部電影。
IMDb的新聞專欄則指出，該型別於1960年代在義大利興起，特色在於探索道德複雜性，並大量啟用歐洲導演；名稱本身即反映其生產脈絡——多數低成本、風格粗礪且暴力感強烈的西部片均在義大利製作。此類電影雖然被歸類為「西部片」，但其生產地理、資金結構與敘事邏輯，均與美國本土西部片有顯著差異。
多家媒體共同確認的進展在於，義大利麵西部片並非單一導演或單一公司的偶然產物，而是整個1960至1970年代歐洲電影產業結構調整下的系統性現象。MSN的報導指出，此型別簡單而言就是由義大利片廠與電影工作者在1960、1970年代拍攝的西部片迴圈，但實際拍攝地點經常位於西班牙或其他可冒充美國西部地貌的歐洲場景。
No Film School的文章進一步追溯名稱源頭，指稱「Spaghetti Western」一詞最早由西班牙記者阿方索·桑切斯·馬丁內斯（Alfonso Sánchez Martínez）於1966年使用，用以描述當時由義大利製片主導、非美國人拍攝的西部片現象。此一命名行為本身即具有跨國意涵：一個西班牙記者，為義大利製作的電影型別，賦予了一個帶有食物聯想的英語名稱，日後成為全球通用的型別標籤。
關於來源間的差異與尚待確認資訊，主要在於型別定義的邊界與名稱的首次使用時間。Slashfilm的報導強調，義大利麵西部片的美學特徵係由李昂尼在《荒野大鏢客》（A Fistful of Dollars）中所確立，其風格是對1940年代西部片的一種「強化與風格化」版本，而非單純的模仿或複製。CBR則以更寬泛的定義切入，認為義大利麵西部片在本質上是低成本、在歐洲（通常為義大利）製作與拍攝，且不受好萊塢先前道德標準約束的電影。
這兩個定義雖然方向一致，但側重點不同：前者聚焦於導演美學的決定性影響，後者則強調產業結構與道德規範的解放。此外，No Film School明確指出名稱首見於1966年，但維基百科與IMDb並未提供相同精確的時間點，僅以「1960年代中期」或「1960年代」概括描述。此處的時間差異並非矛盾，但顯示不同來源對歷史細節的考證深度不一。
此一型別的具體影響，體現在三個層面：電影美學、全球產業分工與型別批評語言。在美學層面，義大利麵西部片打破了美國西部片長期以來的善惡二元對立，轉而呈現道德模糊、反英雄主角與更為寫實的暴力場面。CBR特別指出，此類電影不受好萊塢「先前道德標準」的約束，這意味著歐洲製片環境提供了更寬鬆的創作空間。在產業層面，義大利、西班牙等地的低成本製作環境與多樣化地貌，使得歐洲能夠在好萊塢體系之外建立一套完整的西部片生產鏈。
MSN的報導提醒讀者，這些電影雖然掛名義大利，但實際拍攝常跨越多國，形成一種「偽美國」的空間再現策略。在批評語言層面，「Spaghetti Western」一詞的流行，使得食物名稱成為電影分類學的正式術語，此後也衍生出「臘腸西部片」（Sauerkraut Western）等類似命名，顯示型別標籤本身即是一種文化翻譯與誤讀的產物。
不同觀點與限制方面，必須謹慎處理的是，義大利麵西部片並非一個內部一致的運動，而是評論者事後歸納的類別。Slashfilm的報導以李昂尼的美學選擇為核心，但並非所有義大利生產的西部片都遵循李昂尼的風格；同樣地，CBR所強調的「低成本」與「道德解放」特徵，也不適用於每一部作品。IMDb的定義側重於歐洲導演與道德複雜性，但這忽略了部分作品其實由美國演員主演、或模仿好萊塢敘事公式的事實。
此外，No Film School所引用的1966年名稱起源說法，雖然具體且可追溯，但單一來源的歷史敘述仍需更多檔案文獻佐證，方能確認該詞是否在更早的義大利或西班牙業界內部流通。因此，讀者不宜將任何單一媒體的定義視為最終定論，而應理解義大利麵西部片作為一個型別範疇，本身即包含多種相互競逐的定義與評價。
後續觀察重點應放在三個方向：第一，義大利麵西部片的檔案修復與數位化進度，是否會改變目前以少數經典作品為主的評價體系；第二，當代串流平臺將此類電影重新上架後，新一代觀眾對其暴力美學與種族再現的接受度，是否會引發新的文化評論；第三，跨國製作與型別命名之間的權力關係——為何義大利製作的西部片必須以食物命名，而美國製作的同類電影則被視為正統，此一不對稱現象在後殖民電影研究與全球影史書寫中，仍有持續討論的空間。
各家媒體目前提供的資訊，足以建立型別的基本輪廓，但對於名稱的實際使用脈絡、義大利國內觀眾的反應，以及此類電影在拉丁美洲或亞洲的接受史，仍缺乏系統性研究，值得後續追蹤。
