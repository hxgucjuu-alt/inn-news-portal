  # Middle East Geopolitics and Tech Strategy: Regional Digital Transformation Amidst Great Power Rivalry
  
  > The Middle East stands at the intersection of great power diplomacy and digital transformation. China is strengthening its influence by mediating between Palestinian factions, while the UAE is actively promoting its AI industry, seeking economic and technological autonomy amidst a shifting international landscape.
  
  The Middle East has long been a critical hub in global geopolitics. In recent years, the region has not only faced traditional security challenges and diplomatic maneuvering but has also become a strategic frontier for global powers competing for influence. According to BBC reports, U.S. policy in the Middle East was long anchored in loyalty to specific allies and the application of "maximum pressure" on Iran, which facilitated the formation of a new axis between Gulf Arab states and Israel. However, as the international situation evolves, Middle Eastern nations are attempting to reposition themselves in a multipolar world, shifting their focus beyond traditional security issues toward future strategic areas such as the digital economy and artificial intelligence (AI), aiming to achieve national development transformation through technological empowerment.
  
  On the diplomatic front, China's recent role in the Middle East has drawn international attention. According to Middle East Eye, Beijing recently hosted reconciliation talks between Hamas and Fatah. Analysts point out that this is the latest example of China's growing influence in the region. By providing a diplomatic platform, China has challenged the traditional discourse of Western nations regarding the Gaza issue.
  
  Meanwhile, Canadian Conservative leader Pierre Poilievre has also addressed related issues. In a speech at a synagogue in Quebec, he described the Middle East conflict as a "difficult issue" and emphasized his stance against antisemitism, reflecting the complex debate within Western nations regarding Middle East policy. These diplomatic developments indicate that the Middle East is no longer just a traditional security hotspot, but a diplomatic battlefield where global powers vie for regional influence.
  
  Despite the volatile diplomatic situation, some Middle Eastern countries have not ceased their investments in the technology sector. According to Arabian Business, the United Arab Emirates (UAE) is actively promoting the "Think AI" initiative. The event brought together over 100 government officials, private sector representatives, and international organization experts to discuss how artificial intelligence will shape the future. This forms an interesting contrast with the 2027 budget proposal recently announced by the South Korean government, which plans to invest 21.3 trillion won to promote three major "super projects"—AI, semiconductors, and data centers—demonstrating that major global economies view AI as the core of national competitiveness.
  
  However, technological development in the Middle East still faces the dual challenges of regional conflict and geopolitical uncertainty, which differs significantly from the policy planning conducted by countries like South Korea in relatively stable environments.
  
  For the Middle East, this phenomenon of parallel "diplomatic mediation" and "technological transformation" has profound implications. On one hand, China's intervention provides a new diplomatic path for the long-stalled internal reconciliation of Palestine, which could alter the political landscape within the region. On the other hand, countries like the UAE are attempting to shift their national economies from traditional energy industries to knowledge-based economies by hosting high-level AI forums. However, the effectiveness of these policies remains highly dependent on regional peace and stability. If geopolitical conflicts continue to escalate, it will directly impact foreign investment confidence in the Middle East's tech industry and may force governments to reallocate budgets toward national defense and security, thereby compressing resources for digital transformation.
  
  Regarding differing perspectives and limitations, analysts warn against over-interpreting the effectiveness of China's mediation in the Middle East. Although Beijing has provided a platform for dialogue, the deep-seated divisions among Palestinian factions are long-standing, and it remains to be seen whether diplomatic mediation alone can bring about substantive political stability. Furthermore, while Middle Eastern countries possess strong capital support in promoting the AI industry, they still face a significant gap compared to top international tech powers in terms of talent reserves and technological autonomy. These structural limitations make the path of digital transformation in the Middle East full of variables. While pursuing technological autonomy, these governments must also maintain strategic balance amidst complex great power games to avoid becoming casualties of geopolitical confrontation.
  
  Future observation will focus on several key aspects: First, whether the China-led Palestinian reconciliation talks can produce concrete implementation plans, and how the U.S. will respond to these regional diplomatic moves. Second, whether investments in the AI sector by Gulf states like the UAE can be successfully transformed into internationally competitive industrial clusters. Finally, as global technological competition intensifies, which camp Middle Eastern countries choose to cooperate with more closely in the technology supply chain will determine the region's position in the future global digital economy. All parties should continue to monitor the implementation progress of these diplomatic and technological policies and assess their long-term impact on regional social stability and economic development.

# 中東地緣政治與科技戰略：大國博弈下的區域數位轉型

> 中東地區正處於大國外交角力與數位轉型交會點。中國透過調停巴勒斯坦派系強化影響力，阿聯酋則積極推動AI產業，試圖在變動的國際局勢中尋求經濟與科技自主。


中東地區長期以來是全球地緣政治的關鍵板塊。近年來，該區域不僅面臨傳統的安全挑戰與外交博弈，更成為全球大國競逐影響力的戰略前線。根據 BBC 的報導，美國過去在中東的政策長期錨定於對特定盟友的忠誠以及對伊朗施加「極限壓力」，這促成了波斯灣阿拉伯國家與以色列之間新軸心的形成。然而，隨著國際局勢的演變，中東各國正試圖在多極化的世界中重新定位，不僅關注傳統的安全議題，更將目光轉向數位經濟與人工智慧（AI）等未來戰略領域，試圖透過科技賦能來實現國家發展轉型。
在外交層面，中國近期在中東扮演的角色引發國際關注。據《Middle East Eye》報導，北京近期主辦了哈馬斯（Hamas）與法塔赫（Fatah）之間的和解談判，分析人士指出，這是中國在中東影響力日益增強的最新例證。中國透過提供外交平臺，挑戰了西方國家在加薩議題上的傳統話語權。
與此同時，加拿大保守黨領袖博勵治（Pierre Poilievre）亦在相關議題上表態，其在魁北克省猶太會堂的演講中，將中東衝突描述為一項「困難的議題」，並強調了對反猶太主義的立場，顯示出西方國家內部對於中東政策的複雜辯論。這些外交動態顯示，中東已不再僅是傳統的安全熱點，而是全球大國爭奪區域影響力的外交戰場。
儘管外交局勢動盪，中東部分國家在科技領域的投入並未停歇。根據《Arabian Business》的報導，阿拉伯聯合大公國（UAE）正積極推動「Think AI」計畫，該活動匯集了超過 100 位政府官員、私營部門代表以及國際組織專家，共同探討人工智慧如何塑造未來。這與韓國政府近期公佈的 2027 年預算案形成有趣的對比；韓國政府規劃投入 21.3 兆韓元推動 AI、半導體及資料中心等三大超級專案，顯示全球主要經濟體皆將 AI 視為國家競爭力的核心。
然而，中東地區的科技發展仍面臨區域衝突與地緣政治不確定性的雙重挑戰，這與韓國等國在相對穩定的環境下進行政策規劃有顯著差異。
對於中東地區而言，這種「外交調停」與「科技轉型」並行的現象帶來了深遠影響。一方面，中國的介入為長期陷入僵局的巴勒斯坦內部和解提供了新的外交路徑，這可能改變區域內的政治版圖；另一方面，阿聯酋等國透過舉辦高規格的 AI 論壇，試圖將國家經濟從傳統能源產業轉向知識型經濟。然而，這些政策的成效仍高度依賴區域內的和平穩定。若地緣政治衝突持續升溫，將直接影響外資對中東科技產業的信心，並可能使政府在預算分配上被迫重新向國防安全傾斜，從而壓縮數位轉型的資源。
不同觀點與限制方面，分析人士提醒，不應過度解讀中國在中東的調停成效。雖然北京提供了對話平臺，但巴勒斯坦內部派系長期以來的分歧極深，單靠外交斡旋是否能帶來實質的政治穩定仍有待觀察。此外，中東各國在推動 AI 產業時，雖然擁有雄厚的資本支援，但在人才儲備與技術自主性上，仍面臨與國際頂尖科技強權的巨大落差。這些結構性的限制，使得中東的數位轉型之路充滿變數，各國政府在追求科技自主的同時，也必須在複雜的大國博弈中維持戰略平衡，避免成為地緣政治對抗的犧牲品。
後續觀察重點將聚焦於幾個關鍵面向：首先，中國主導的巴勒斯坦和解談判是否能產生具體的執行方案，以及美國對此區域外交動向的反應；其次，阿聯酋等波斯灣國家在 AI 領域的投資，能否成功轉化為具備國際競爭力的產業聚落；最後，隨著全球科技競爭加劇，中東國家在技術供應鏈中會選擇與哪一方陣營更緊密合作，這將決定該區域在未來全球數位經濟版圖中的地位。各界應持續關注這些外交與科技政策的落實進度，並評估其對區域社會穩定與經濟發展的長遠影響。
