  # Trade Momentum Slows in Asia-Pacific: Challenges in Services Trade Growth and Sustainable Development Goals
  
  > A recent report by the UNESCAP indicates that commercial services exports in the Asia-Pacific region grew by 5.4% in 2025, outperforming goods trade. However, due to the weakness of major economies such as Japan and China, overall growth remains below the global recovery level, and progress toward Sustainable Development Goals continues to lag.
  
  As the core engine of global economic growth, the Asia-Pacific region is currently facing structural adjustments and challenges. According to the latest data released by the United Nations Economic and Social Commission for Asia and the Pacific (UNESCAP) and the United Nations Public Administration Network (UNPAN), the region is entering a period of slowing momentum following a long-term trade expansion. Background information indicates that while Asia-Pacific nations have invested significant policy resources into the "2030 Agenda for Sustainable Development," the implementation progress of most goals remains significantly behind schedule or stagnant.
  
  This phenomenon not only reflects shifts in the global supply chain landscape but also highlights the vulnerability of major regional economies when faced with weak domestic demand and uncertainty in external demand.
  
  Multiple authoritative sources have confirmed a clear "services replacing goods" trend in the Asia-Pacific trade structure in 2025. Data shows that the growth rate of commercial services exports reached 5.4%, outperforming traditional goods trade in overall trade performance. This growth momentum is primarily driven by the expansion of digital services exports, indicating that digital transformation has become a key strategy for countries in the region to cope with the trade slowdown.
  
  However, this growth is not a comprehensive prosperity. Regional analyses from relevant UN agencies and the *Beijing Times* both point out that despite the relatively strong performance of services trade, its growth rate remains lower than the global average recovery level, indicating that the Asia-Pacific region's post-pandemic economic recovery has yet to fully catch up with the overall pace of the global market rebound.
  
  Regarding information details and specific impacts, there are subtle differences in how various sources attribute the economic weakness. The UN report explicitly points out that the sluggish market sentiment in Japan and China, the two key economies in the region, is the primary factor dragging down overall trade growth. However, regarding the specific mechanisms of "why services trade can maintain growth while goods trade continues to decline," the industry awaits further disaggregated data for verification.
  
  Furthermore, regarding the lag in Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs), the report emphasizes the gap between "execution" and "resource allocation." However, there is currently a lack of unified cross-national survey data on the specific legal or administrative obstacles encountered by countries during policy implementation, and there are significant differences in how each country responds to this issue and in their policy adjustment directions.
  
  This wave of slowing trade momentum has had a direct and profound impact on the industrial layout and policy formulation in the Asia-Pacific region. For manufacturing economies highly dependent on exports, the weakness in traditional goods trade forces companies to accelerate transformation and shift resources toward digital services and high-value-added commercial services. Additionally, as progress toward Sustainable Development Goals lags significantly, governments in the region are being forced to re-evaluate their fiscal priorities. Balancing short-term economic growth with long-term Environmental, Social, and Governance (ESG) goals has become a core challenge for policymakers. Failure to improve the current stagnation may exacerbate wealth gaps and development inequality within the region.
  
  Although digital services trade has shown resilience, experts warn against over-interpreting the economic dividends it brings. First, while digital services are growing quickly, their base is relatively small and insufficient to fully offset the economic impact of declining traditional manufacturing exports. Second, the vast differences in digital infrastructure and talent reserves among countries may lead to an widening "digital divide" in the region, triggering new social inequality issues. Furthermore, excessive reliance on services trade exports may make some economies more vulnerable to changes in global digital regulations. Therefore, viewing digital transformation as a panacea for all economic problems clearly oversimplifies the complexity of regional development.
  
  Subsequent observations will focus on whether Japan and China will introduce more targeted stimulus policies in the coming fiscal year to boost market confidence. At the same time, the follow-up tracking report on Sustainable Development Goals by UNESCAP will be an important indicator for assessing whether governments are truly fulfilling their reform commitments. Observers also suggest closely monitoring the progress of negotiations on cross-border digital trade agreements within the Asia-Pacific region, as this will determine whether services trade can become a stable pillar supporting regional economic growth in the coming years, or if it is merely a short-term structural adjustment.

# 亞太地區貿易動能放緩：服務貿易成長與永續發展目標挑戰

> 聯合國亞太經社會最新報告指出，亞太地區2025年商業服務出口成長5.4%，表現優於商品貿易，但受日中等主要經濟體疲軟影響，整體成長不及全球復甦水準，且永續發展目標進度持續滯後。


亞太地區作為全球經濟增長的核心引擎，近期面臨結構性的調整與挑戰。根據聯合國亞洲及太平洋經濟社會委員會（UNESCAP）與聯合國公共行政網路（UNPAN）發布的最新資料顯示，該區域在經歷長期的貿易擴張後，正進入一段動能放緩的調整期。背景資料顯示，亞太各國在推動「2030年永續發展議程」的程式中，儘管投入了大量政策資源，但多數目標的執行進度仍顯著落後或陷入停滯。
此一現象不僅反映了全球供應鏈格局的轉變，亦凸顯了區域內主要經濟體在面對內需疲軟與外部需求不確定性時的脆弱性。
多個權威來源共同確認，亞太地區在2025年的貿易結構出現了明顯的「服務業取代商品業」趨勢。資料顯示，商業服務出口成長率達到5.4%，在整體貿易表現中優於傳統商品貿易。這一成長動力主要來自於數位服務出口的擴張，顯示數位化轉型已成為區域內各國應對貿易放緩的主要策略。
然而，這種成長並非全面性的繁榮，聯合國相關機構與《北京時報》（Beijing Times）的區域分析均指出，儘管服務貿易表現相對亮眼，但其成長幅度仍低於全球平均復甦水準，顯示亞太地區在後疫情時代的經濟修復速度，尚未能完全追趕上全球市場的整體回升節奏。
在資訊細節與具體影響層面上，各來源對經濟疲軟的歸因存在細微差異。聯合國報告明確指出，日本與中國作為區域內兩大關鍵經濟體，其市場情緒的低迷是拖累整體貿易成長的主因。然而，對於「為何服務貿易能維持成長而商品貿易持續衰退」的具體機制，各界尚待更多產業細分資料的佐證。
此外，關於永續發展目標（SDGs）的滯後，報告中強調了「執行力」與「資源分配」的落差，但對於各國在政策落實過程中遭遇的具體法律或行政障礙，目前仍缺乏跨國性的統一調查資料，各國對此議題的反應與政策調整方向亦存在顯著不同。
這波貿易動能的放緩，對亞太地區的產業佈局與政策制定產生了直接且深遠的影響。對於高度依賴出口的製造業經濟體而言，傳統商品貿易的疲軟迫使企業必須加速轉型，將資源轉向數位服務與高附加價值的商業服務領域。此外，由於永續發展目標進度嚴重落後，區域內各國政府正被迫重新評估其財政優先順序，如何在維持短期經濟成長與達成長期環境、社會治理（ESG）目標之間取得平衡，成為各國決策者當前的核心難題。若無法改善當前的停滯狀況，未來恐將加劇區域內的貧富差距與發展不均。
儘管數位服務貿易展現出韌性，但專家提醒不應過度解讀其帶來的經濟紅利。首先，數位服務的成長雖快，但其基數相對較小，尚不足以完全抵銷傳統製造業出口下滑帶來的經濟衝擊。其次，各國在數位基礎設施與人才儲備上的巨大差異，可能導致區域內「數位鴻溝」進一步擴大，進而引發新的社會不平等問題。此外，過度依賴服務貿易出口可能使部分經濟體在面對全球數位法規變動時，顯得更加脆弱。因此，將數位轉型視為解決所有經濟問題的萬靈丹，顯然過於簡化了區域發展的複雜性。
後續觀察重點將聚焦於日、中兩國在接下來的財政年度中，是否會推出更具針對性的刺激政策以提振市場信心。同時，聯合國亞太經社會針對永續發展目標的後續追蹤報告，將是評估各國政府是否真正落實改革承諾的重要指標。觀察家亦建議，應密切關注亞太區域內各國在跨境數位貿易協定上的談判進展，因為這將決定服務貿易能否在未來幾年內，成為支撐區域經濟成長的穩定支柱，抑或僅是短期內的結構性調整。
