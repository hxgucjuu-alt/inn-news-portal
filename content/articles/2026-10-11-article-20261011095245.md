  # Regional Security and Cooperation in Northeast Asia: Strategic Shifts from Logistical Challenges to Pandemic Collaboration
  
  > The 115th National Day Gala of the Republic of China was held in New Taipei City, highlighting Taiwan's global connections. Meanwhile, the Northeast Asia region faces severe challenges in geopolitics, logistical defense, and transnational pandemic collaboration, with the regional security architecture currently in a critical period of transformation.
  
  On October 10, 115th year of the Republic of China, the National Day Gala was held for the first time at the Banqiao First Stadium in New Taipei City, with the theme "A Stage for the World, Gathering in Taiwan." In his address, President Lai Ching-te emphasized that through the joint efforts of citizens at home and abroad, Taiwan has gradually stepped onto the world stage. The government will continue to implement the spirit of social investment, alleviate the burden on the public regarding childcare, education, and long-term care, and translate international trust in Taiwan into tangible improvements in quality of life. The event brought together approximately 2,500 overseas compatriots from 53 countries, showcasing Taiwan's cohesion in multiculturalism and international connectivity.
  
  However, while Taiwan is committed to demonstrating its soft power and sharing economic achievements, the overall security situation and cooperation mechanisms in the Northeast Asia region present highly complex dynamics due to historical legacies and strategic requirements.
  
  At the regional level in Northeast Asia, security and logistics issues have become the core focus of various nations. According to an analysis by *Asia Times*, the geographical conditions for logistics in Northeast Asia are extremely severe, involving the joint defense operations of the Korean Peninsula, the Japanese archipelago, and their surrounding waters. The operational capability of the coalition relies heavily on key nodes such as deep-water ports, air bases, railway hubs, and fuel supply stations. Maintaining trilateral deterrence in this region is not only a military challenge but also a rigorous test of the resilience of the logistical supply chain. At the same time, historical contradictions within the region remain like heavy anchors, continuing to influence relations between countries.
  
  According to *Yahoo News Canada*, Northeast Asian countries are often tethered by historical memories. This historical baggage not only triggers strong reactions from North Korea and China but also serves as a justification for their military expansion. In particular, historical perception disputes regarding Japan remain one of the key factors leading to rising regional tensions.
  
  Despite the existence of serious geopolitical friction in regional memory, academia and some policymakers are still attempting to promote pragmatic cooperation mechanisms. According to an analysis by *The Hankyoreh*, experts have suggested establishing a regional joint response network for infectious diseases in Northeast Asia, advocating for the participation of South Korea, North Korea, China, and Japan. Such cooperation initiatives aim to establish communication channels between countries through non-traditional security issues. However, these initiatives currently remain largely in the stage of academic discussion and policy analysis, and have not yet formed transnational agreements with substantive binding force.
  
  There are clear differences in perspectives on regional cooperation across various sources: on one hand, the strengthening of military logistics and deterrence capabilities, and on the other, calls for cooperation based on public health and other livelihood issues. Both exist in a parallel and contradictory state within the current strategic environment of Northeast Asia.
  
  These regional issues have had a profound impact on the industries and policies of Northeast Asia. In terms of military logistics, the investment and maintenance of infrastructure by various countries directly affect the stability of supply chains within the region, which is a hidden cost that cannot be ignored for Northeast Asian economies that are highly dependent on maritime transport and international trade. At the social policy level, the investments in education and long-term care emphasized at Taiwan's National Day Gala echo the pressures of aging and social welfare expenditures faced by other countries in Northeast Asia. When countries in the region tilt their resources toward arms races or confrontations over historical issues, how to balance defense spending with social and livelihood needs becomes a common governance challenge that all governments must face.
  
  However, it must be pointed out that the history and security issues of Northeast Asia are extremely sensitive, and the logic of interaction between countries should not be oversimplified. Although experts have called for the establishment of a pandemic cooperation network, due to the deep-seated lack of political trust between countries and the continued interference of historical legacy issues, there are still very high thresholds for achieving substantive transnational cooperation in the short term. Media descriptions of the regional situation reflect different dimensions ranging from military strategy to social welfare. Readers should avoid viewing a single perspective as the entirety of regional interaction, especially regarding debates on historical cognition and security defense, where there are huge gaps in the positions of all parties, making it difficult to reach a consensus in the short term.
  
  Subsequent observations should focus on whether Northeast Asian countries can establish preliminary communication mechanisms through multilateral frameworks when dealing with non-traditional security threats (such as infectious diseases and climate change). At the same time, regarding the military deployment and supply chain resilience of key logistical nodes in the region, it remains to be seen whether countries will adjust their policies in response to geopolitical changes. As a part of the region, how Taiwan responds to the potential economic and geopolitical risks brought about by the changing security landscape in Northeast Asia while continuing to deepen international connections will be an important indicator for observing Taiwan's future foreign and defense policies.

# 東北亞區域安全與合作：從後勤挑戰到防疫協作的戰略變局

> 中華民國115年國慶晚會於新北市舉辦，強調臺灣與全球連結；與此同時，東北亞地區正面臨地緣政治、後勤防衛與跨國防疫協作的嚴峻挑戰，區域安全架構正處於關鍵轉型期。


中華民國115年10月10日，國慶晚會首度移師新北市板橋第一運動場舉行，主題定調為「世界的舞臺．齊聚臺灣」。總統賴清德於致詞時強調，臺灣在海內外國人共同努力下，已逐步站上世界舞臺，政府將持續落實社會投資精神，減輕民眾在育兒、教育及長照方面的負擔，並將國際對臺灣的信任轉化為具體的生活品質提升。此次活動匯聚了來自全球53個國家、約2,500名海外僑胞，展現臺灣在多元文化與國際連結上的凝聚力。
然而，當臺灣致力於展現軟實力與經濟共享成果的同時，東北亞區域整體的安全情勢與合作機制，卻因歷史遺緒與戰略需求，呈現出極為複雜的動態。
在東北亞區域層面，安全與後勤議題成為各國關注的核心。根據《Asia Times》分析，東北亞地區的後勤地理條件極為嚴峻，涉及朝鮮半島、日本列島及其周邊海域的聯防運作。 coalition（聯軍）的作業能力高度依賴深水港口、空軍基地、鐵路樞紐及燃料補給站等關鍵節點。維持這一區域的三邊嚇阻能力，不僅是軍事上的挑戰，更是對後勤供應鏈韌性的嚴格考驗。與此同時，區域內的歷史矛盾仍如沉重的錨，持續影響各國關係。
據《Yahoo News Canada》報導，東北亞國家常受歷史記憶牽絆，這種歷史包袱不僅引發北韓與中國的強烈反應，並成為其擴充軍備的理由，尤其針對日本的歷史認知爭議，仍是導致區域緊張局勢升溫的關鍵因素之一。
儘管區域記憶體在嚴重的地緣政治摩擦，學界與部分政策制定者仍嘗試推動務實的合作機制。據《The Hankyoreh》分析，專家建議建立東北亞區域性的傳染病聯合應對網路，並主張應納入南韓、北韓、中國及日本共同參與。此類合作倡議旨在透過非傳統安全議題，建立各國間的溝通管道。然而，這些倡議目前多停留在學術討論與政策分析階段，尚未形成具備實質約束力的跨國協議。
各來源對於區域合作的看法存在明顯差異：一方面是軍事後勤與嚇阻能力的強化，另一方面則是基於公共衛生等民生議題的合作呼籲，兩者在當前東北亞的戰略環境中呈現並行且矛盾的狀態。
這些區域議題對東北亞的產業與政策產生了深遠影響。在軍事後勤方面，各國對於基礎設施的投資與維護，直接影響了區域內的供應鏈穩定性，這對於高度依賴海運與國際貿易的東北亞經濟體而言，是不可忽視的隱形成本。而在社會政策層面，如臺灣國慶晚會所強調的教育與長照投資，與東北亞其他國家面臨的高齡化、社會福利支出壓力相互呼應。當區域內各國將資源傾斜於軍備競賽或歷史議題的對抗時，如何平衡國防開支與社會民生需求，成為各政府必須面對的共同治理挑戰。
然而，必須指出的是，東北亞的歷史與安全議題極為敏感，不應過度簡化各國間的互動邏輯。雖然有專家呼籲建立防疫合作網路，但受限於各國間根深蒂固的政治互信不足，以及歷史遺留問題的持續幹擾，短期內要達成跨國性的實質合作仍面臨極高門檻。各媒體對於區域局勢的描述，反映了從軍事戰略到社會福利的不同維度，讀者在解讀時應避免將單一觀點視為區域互動的全部，特別是針對歷史認知與安全防衛的辯論，各方立場存在巨大鴻溝，短期內難以達成共識。
後續觀察重點應聚焦於東北亞各國在應對非傳統安全威脅（如傳染病、氣候變遷）時，是否能透過多邊框架建立初步的溝通機制；同時，針對區域內關鍵後勤節點的軍事部署與補給鏈韌性，各國是否會因應地緣政治變化而調整政策。臺灣作為區域的一環，在持續深化國際連結的同時，如何應對東北亞區域安全變局帶來的潛在經濟與地緣風險，將是未來觀察臺灣外交與國防政策的重要指標。
