  # Global Outdoor Digital Billboards Hacked; Hacker Group "LeakersGov" Claims to Have Breached Thousands of Systems in Two Hours
  
  > Recent large-scale cybersecurity incidents in countries including Egypt, Malaysia, and Brazil have seen outdoor large-scale LED billboards remotely hacked to display inappropriate content. The hacker group "LeakersGov" claims it took only two hours to identify thousands of system vulnerabilities, highlighting significant security concerns within the global digital signage supply chain.
  
  Recently, a series of serious cybersecurity scandals have erupted in multiple regions around the world, with digital signage systems becoming targets for hackers. Since late October 2026, large LED billboards located on major traffic arteries and public squares in countries such as Egypt, Malaysia, and Brazil have been remotely compromised by unidentified individuals and forced to display adult, inappropriate content. Such incidents have not only caused chaos in public spaces but have also triggered high levels of concern among governments and the public regarding the security of digital infrastructure. According to technical analysis, these billboards typically update content via networked systems, but due to a lack of sufficient cybersecurity encryption, hackers were able to easily bypass authentication mechanisms and gain direct control over the displays.
  
  This attack is not merely a prank; it is viewed as a public demonstration of the vulnerabilities in global IoT devices, exposing fatal flaws in the software and hardware integration of the digital signage supply chain.
  
  According to cross-reporting by American tech media Gizmodo and the independent Egyptian media outlet "Cairo Report," a hacker group calling itself "LeakersGov" has officially claimed responsibility. The group claimed online that the operation took only about two hours to successfully identify and infiltrate thousands of billboard terminals worldwide that possessed system vulnerabilities. In its declaration, the hacker group ironically pointed out that the technical security threshold for digital signage systems widely used in the market is incredibly low, allowing them to be easily breached without the need for complex penetration techniques.
  
  This statement aligns with reports from Techbang.com, confirming that the attack was not directed at a specific vendor, but was instead an indiscriminate scan and intrusion targeting widespread system vulnerabilities on a global scale.
  
  Although there is a preliminary understanding of the hackers' technical methods, there is still an information gap regarding the specific details of the "thousands of system vulnerabilities" claimed by "LeakersGov." Some cybersecurity experts point out that while the hackers claim it took only two hours, this may only refer to the time spent on scanning and initial intrusion, and does not include the subsequent preparation for large-scale control. Furthermore, there is currently no unified official investigation report regarding the specific models of the compromised billboards, the operating system versions, or whether specific vendor software backdoors were involved.
  
  Police and cybersecurity departments in various countries are still conducting forensics on the affected hardware to determine whether these billboards all used the same brand of controller or cloud management platform. This information remains unconfirmed, and it is inappropriate to directly attribute the responsibility for all victimized billboards to a single supplier.
  
  This incident has had a direct impact on global digital governance and industrial policy in public spaces. As an important component of smart cities, the security of digital signage is directly related to the stability of public communication. If hackers can easily replace content with inappropriate videos, the potential for malicious actors to use the same vulnerabilities to spread misinformation, inflammatory remarks, or even political propaganda poses an immeasurable risk to social stability. This has prompted governments in many countries to begin re-examining procurement standards for public digital infrastructure, requiring suppliers to provide stricter cybersecurity certifications and mandating that all networked devices must possess multi-factor authentication and encrypted transmission capabilities.
  
  For the advertising industry, this crisis also forces operators to re-evaluate their network architecture and incorporate cybersecurity maintenance costs into their operating budgets, rather than merely pursuing display effects and networking convenience.
  
  However, discussions regarding this incident should not be over-interpreted or lead to panic. Cybersecurity experts remind the public that while the actions of "LeakersGov" caused widespread visual impact, this type of attack is essentially a "low-tech, high-exposure" malicious nuisance, rather than an Advanced Persistent Threat (APT) targeting critical national infrastructure (such as power or water systems). The public should distinguish between "cybersecurity vulnerabilities in public display devices" and "cybersecurity protection for core infrastructure" to avoid conflating a single billboard hacking incident with a national-level cybersecurity crisis.
  
  Furthermore, the claims made by hacker groups often contain exaggerations. Whether the "thousands of systems" they claim to control are fully compromised remains to be verified by subsequent cybersecurity audits; the overall defensive capabilities of the global digital signage industry should not be judged solely on the unilateral declaration of the hackers.
  
  Future observations should focus on whether governments will issue mandatory cybersecurity regulations for outdoor digital billboards and whether relevant manufacturers will release large-scale firmware updates to patch vulnerabilities. Currently, international regulatory standards for IoT devices are not unified, which allows transnational hacker groups to exploit regulatory loopholes in different regions. Whether a common security protocol for digital signage emerges in the industry over the coming months will be a key point to watch. At the same time, the progress of legal prosecution by victimized countries regarding these incidents, and whether the hacker group "LeakersGov" will further disclose more technical details or launch subsequent attacks, all require continued attention.
  
  This incident has become a warning for the global digital signage industry, demonstrating that in the process of digital transformation, security protection must evolve in tandem with technological application.

# 全球多國戶外數位看板遭駭入侵 駭客組織「LeakersGov」宣稱兩小時攻破數千系統

> 近期埃及、馬來西亞與巴西等國發生大規模資安事件，戶外大型 LED 看板遭駭客遠端入侵並播放不雅影片。駭客組織「LeakersGov」聲稱僅花費兩小時便發現數千個系統漏洞，此事件凸顯全球數位看板供應鏈的資安防護存在重大隱憂。


近期，全球多個地區接連爆發嚴重的資安醜聞，數位看板系統成為駭客攻擊目標。自 2026 年 10 月底起，位於埃及、馬來西亞與巴西等國的重要交通幹道及公共廣場，其大型 LED 看板遭不明人士遠端入侵，並被強製播放成人不雅影片。此類事件不僅造成公共場所的秩序混亂，更引發各國政府與民眾對數位基礎設施安全性的高度關注。根據相關技術分析，這類看板通常透過聯網系統進行內容更新，卻因缺乏足夠的資安加密防護，導致駭客能輕易繞過驗證機制，直接取得顯示控制權。
此次攻擊事件不僅是一次單純的惡作劇，更被視為針對全球物聯網裝置脆弱性的公開示範，揭露了數位看板供應鏈在軟硬體整合上的致命缺陷。
根據美國科技媒體 Gizmodo 與埃及獨立媒體《開羅報告》（Cairo Report）的交叉報導，自稱「LeakersGov」的駭客組織已正式對外坦承犯案。該組織在網路上聲稱，其行動僅花費約兩小時，便透過隨機掃描技術成功識別並入侵全球數千個存在系統漏洞的看板終端。駭客組織在宣告中極具諷刺意味地指出，目前市面上廣泛使用的數位看板系統，其技術防護門檻低到令人難以置信，甚至無需複雜的滲透手法即可輕易攻破。
這一說法與 Techbang.com 的報導內容相符，證實了該攻擊事件並非針對特定單一廠商，而是針對全球範圍內廣泛存在的系統漏洞進行的無差別掃描與入侵。
儘管各界對於駭客的技術手段已有初步認知，但關於「LeakersGov」聲稱的「數千個系統漏洞」具體細節，目前仍存在資訊落差。部分資安專家指出，雖然駭客宣稱僅花費兩小時，但這可能僅指掃描與初步入侵的時間，並不包含後續大規模控制的準備工作。此外，針對被入侵看板的具體型號、作業系統版本以及是否涉及特定供應商的軟體後門，目前尚無統一的官方調查報告。
各國警方與資安部門仍在針對受影響的硬體進行鑑識，以確認這些看板是否均使用了相同品牌的控制器或雲端管理平臺，這部分資訊目前仍處於待確認狀態，不宜直接將所有受害看板歸咎於單一供應商的責任。
此次事件對全球公共空間的數位治理與產業政策產生了直接影響。數位看板作為智慧城市的重要組成部分，其安全性直接關係到公共傳播的穩定性。若駭客能輕易將內容替換為不雅影片，未來若惡意人士利用相同漏洞散播假訊息、煽動性言論，甚至進行政治宣傳，將對社會穩定造成不可估量的風險。這促使多國政府開始重新審視公共數位基礎設施的採購標準，要求供應商必須提供更嚴格的資安認證，並強制要求所有聯網裝置必須具備多重身份驗證與加密傳輸功能。
對於廣告產業而言，這場危機也迫使營運商必須重新評估其網路架構，將資安維護成本納入營運預算，而非僅僅追求顯示效果與聯網便利性。
然而，針對此事件的討論不應過度解讀或陷入恐慌。資安專家提醒，雖然「LeakersGov」的行動造成了廣泛的視覺衝擊，但這類攻擊本質上屬於「低技術、高曝光」的惡意騷擾，而非針對國家關鍵基礎設施（如電力或水利系統）的高階持續性威脅（APT）。公眾應區分「公共顯示裝置的資安漏洞」與「核心基礎設施的資安防護」，避免將單一廣告看板的入侵事件與國家級資安危機混為一談。
此外，駭客組織的說法往往帶有誇大成分，其聲稱的「數千個系統」是否皆處於完全控制狀態，仍有待後續資安審計的驗證，不應僅憑駭客的單方面宣告判斷全球數位看板產業的整體防護能力。
後續觀察重點應聚焦於各國政府是否會針對戶外數位廣告看板發布強制性的資安規範，以及相關廠商是否會發布大規模的韌體更新以修補漏洞。目前，國際間對於物聯網裝置的監管標準尚不統一，這使得跨國駭客組織得以利用不同地區的法規漏洞進行攻擊。未來幾個月，業界是否會出現針對數位看板的共同安全協定，將是觀察重點。同時，受害國家針對此類事件的法律追訴進度，以及駭客組織「LeakersGov」是否會進一步披露更多技術細節或發動後續攻擊，皆需持續關注。
此事件已成為全球數位看板產業的一個警示，顯示在數位轉型的過程中，安全防護必須與技術應用同步演進。
