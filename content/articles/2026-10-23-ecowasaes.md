  # A New Chapter in West African Security Cooperation: Challenges and Prospects for Re-engaging ECOWAS and the AES
  
  > The regional security landscape in West Africa is at a turning point, as the Economic Community of West African States (ECOWAS) and the Alliance of Sahel States (AES) engage in diplomatic dialogue to explore concrete paths and the feasibility of restarting security cooperation amidst regional instability.
  
  The security architecture in West Africa has recently faced severe challenges, with the security situation in the Sahel becoming a core issue affecting regional stability. The Alliance of Sahel States (AES), primarily composed of Mali, Burkina Faso, and Niger, has seen a significant shift in the regional geopolitical map since announcing its withdrawal from the Economic Community of West African States (ECOWAS). As the primary regional integration organization in West Africa, ECOWAS has long been committed to promoting democratic governance and collective security; however, with the frequent occurrence of military coups and divergent views on regional security governance, cooperation mechanisms between the two sides have stalled.
  
  The backdrop for this diplomatic dialogue stems from the realization by all parties that unilateral security strategies are insufficient to address the increasingly rampant threats of transnational terrorism and extremism. Consequently, the reassessment and negotiation of bilateral relations have become a focal point of attention.
  
  According to comprehensive reports from various media outlets and regional analysis sources, ECOWAS and the AES are currently in a critical stage of negotiations. Facts confirmed by all parties include that they have reached a preliminary consensus on the necessity of regional security cooperation and are attempting to seek solutions to ease tensions through diplomatic mediation. Currently, the core agenda on the negotiating table includes how to rebuild border security monitoring, intelligence-sharing mechanisms, and how to address the sanctions previously imposed due to the coups.
  
  Multiple sources consistently point out that both ECOWAS member states and the leaders of the AES acknowledge that without cross-border security coordination, the unrest in the Sahel will continue to spill over, thereby threatening the stability of all coastal West African nations. This pragmatism, based on shared interests, is the primary driver pushing both sides to engage in dialogue.
  
  However, at the level of concrete implementation, significant differences and unconfirmed information remain among sources. Some analyses indicate that AES member states remain reserved about restoring full cooperation with ECOWAS, emphasizing their sovereign independence and rejection of external interference; meanwhile, there are differing opinions within ECOWAS regarding whether to completely lift economic sanctions against the military juntas. Furthermore, regarding the specific framework for bilateral cooperation, it remains unclear whether a "full return" model will be adopted or if a "flexible security partnership" will be established, as no final agreement has been reached.
  
  Some media reports suggest that the negotiation process is highly susceptible to influence from external forces, and domestic political pressures in each country could lead to the interruption of dialogue at any time. These uncertainties make it difficult for outsiders to predict the final trajectory of the negotiations.
  
  The restart of this security cooperation has a direct and profound impact on the economic development and public welfare of the West African region. If both sides can successfully reach a security consensus, it will help reduce the risks of border trade, improve logistics and supply chain efficiency within the region, and subsequently alleviate food security crises caused by unrest. For industries, a stable security environment is a prerequisite for attracting foreign investment and promoting infrastructure development. Conversely, if the negotiations collapse, continued border closures and security vacuums will further exacerbate the regional refugee crisis and intensify the expansion of extremist organizations in the Sahel, posing an unavoidable threat to the social stability of all West African nations.
  
  Coordination at the policy level will directly determine whether the West African region moves toward regional integration or further descends into geopolitical fragmentation in the coming years.
  
  From different perspectives, analysts caution against over-interpreting the political significance of this dialogue. On one hand, the establishment of the AES itself is a challenge to the existing regional order, and its underlying military-led ideology conflicts fundamentally with the democratic governance principles of ECOWAS; such structural contradictions are difficult to resolve through a single negotiation in the short term. On the other hand, there is room for interpretation regarding the definition of "bilateral relations," with some observers suggesting this may merely be an expedient measure rather than a long-term strategic return. Therefore, all parties must remain cautious when assessing the situation, avoiding the mistake of viewing temporary contact as an absolute guarantee of regional stability.
  
  A limiting factor is that the internal political stability of each government remains a variable, and any fluctuation in domestic politics could become a key factor affecting the progress of negotiations.
  
  Subsequent observations should focus on the concrete outcomes of the ECOWAS-AES negotiations, particularly the progress in signing border security agreements and whether both sides can reach a substantive consensus on the lifting of sanctions and democratic transition timelines. Additionally, the mediating roles played by other countries in the region, such as Nigeria and Ghana, as well as the attitude of the international community toward this process, are key variables affecting the future situation. Observers suggest continuing to monitor whether the frequency of armed conflict in the Sahel decreases as a result of the negotiations, as this will be a core indicator of whether bilateral security cooperation is substantively effective.
  
  In the coming months, as negotiations enter a more complex phase, the security architecture of West Africa will face a severe stress test. Whether all parties can find a balance between sovereignty and collective security will determine the future landscape of regional stability in West Africa.

# 西非安全合作新局：ECOWAS與AES雙邊關係重啟之挑戰與展望

> 西非區域安全域性勢面臨轉折，西非國家經濟共同體（ECOWAS）與薩赫勒國家聯盟（AES）正透過外交對話，探討在區域動盪下重啟安全合作的具體路徑與可行性。


西非地區的安全架構近期面臨嚴峻挑戰，特別是薩赫勒地區（Sahel）的安全域性勢，已成為影響區域穩定的核心議題。薩赫勒國家聯盟（AES），主要由馬利、布吉納法索與尼日等國組成，自宣佈脫離西非國家經濟共同體（ECOWAS）後，區域內部的地緣政治版圖發生顯著變化。ECOWAS 作為西非主要的區域整合組織，長期致力於推動民主治理與集體安全，然而隨著軍事政變頻發及區域安全治理理念的分歧，雙方的合作機制陷入停滯。
此次外交對話的背景，源於各方意識到單邊安全策略難以應對日益猖獗的跨國恐怖主義與極端主義威脅，透過雙邊關係的重新評估與談判，成為各界關注的焦點。
根據多家媒體與區域分析來源的綜合報導，ECOWAS 與 AES 目前正處於關鍵的談判階段。各方共同確認的事實包括：雙方已針對區域安全合作的必要性達成初步共識，並試圖透過外交斡旋尋求緩解緊張局勢的方案。目前，關於如何重建邊境安全監控、情報共享機制，以及如何處理過往因政變引發的制裁措施，已成為談判桌上的核心議程。
多方來源一致指出，無論是 ECOWAS 的成員國還是 AES 的主導者，皆承認若缺乏跨國界的安全協調，薩赫勒地區的動亂將持續外溢，進而威脅到整個西非沿海國家的穩定。這種基於共同利益的務實主義，是推動雙方進行對話的主要動力。
然而，在具體的執行層面上，來源間仍存在顯著的差異與尚待確認的資訊。部分分析指出，AES 成員國對於恢復與 ECOWAS 的全面合作持保留態度，強調其主權獨立性及對外部幹預的排斥；而 ECOWAS 內部對於是否應完全撤銷針對政變軍政府的經濟制裁，亦存在不同意見。此外，關於雙邊合作的具體框架，究竟是採取「全面回歸」模式，還是建立一種「彈性安全夥伴關係」，目前各方尚未達成最終協議。
部分媒體報導暗示，談判過程極易受到外部勢力影響，且各國國內的政治壓力亦可能隨時導致對話中斷，這些不確定性使得外界難以預測談判的最終走向。
此次安全合作的重啟，對西非地區的經濟發展與民生福祉具有直接且深遠的影響。若雙方能成功達成安全共識，將有助於降低邊境貿易的風險，改善區域內的物流與供應鏈效率，進而緩解因動亂導致的糧食安全危機。對於產業而言，安全域性勢的穩定是吸引外資與推動基礎建設的先決條件。反之，若談判破裂，持續的邊境封鎖與安全真空，將進一步惡化區域內的難民問題，並加劇極端組織在薩赫勒地帶的擴張，對西非各國的社會穩定構成不可忽視的威脅。
政策層面上的協調，將直接決定未來數年內西非地區是邁向區域整合，還是進一步陷入地緣政治的碎片化。
從不同觀點來看，分析人士提醒，不應過度解讀此次對話的政治意義。一方面，AES 的成立本身就是對現有區域秩序的挑戰，其背後的軍事主導思維與 ECOWAS 的民主治理原則存在根本性衝突，這種結構性矛盾難以在短期內透過單一談判解決。另一方面，外界對於「雙邊關係」的定義存在解讀空間，部分觀察家認為這可能僅是權宜之計，而非長期的戰略回歸。因此，各方在評估局勢時，必須保持審慎，避免將暫時的接觸視為區域穩定的絕對保證。
限制因素在於，各國政府的內政穩定性仍是變數，任何國內政局的波動都可能成為影響談判進展的關鍵因素。
後續觀察重點應聚焦於 ECOWAS 與 AES 談判的具體成果，特別是針對邊境安全協議的簽署進度，以及雙方是否能就制裁解除與民主過渡期程達成實質共識。此外，區域內其他國家如奈及利亞、迦納等國在談判中扮演的調解角色，以及國際社會對此程式的態度，亦是影響未來局勢的關鍵變數。觀察家建議，應持續關注薩赫勒地區的武裝衝突頻率是否因談判而有所下降，這將是衡量雙邊安全合作是否具備實質效力的核心指標。
未來幾個月內，隨著談判進入深水區，西非的安全架構將面臨嚴峻的壓力測試，各方能否在主權與集體安全之間找到平衡點，將決定西非未來的區域穩定格局。
