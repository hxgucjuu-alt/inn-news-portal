  # Global Public Health Crisis and Geopolitical Tensions: Antimicrobial Resistance Threats and Eurasian Security Analysis
  
  > World Health Organization statistics indicate that over 4.7 million deaths globally each year are linked to antimicrobial resistance, making it the third leading cause of death worldwide. Simultaneously, shifts in Iran's missile policy and the threat of terrorist organizations in Afghanistan are exacerbating geopolitical security uncertainties in the Eurasian region.
  
  Antimicrobial Resistance (AMR) has become one of the most severe challenges in the field of global public health. According to the latest data from the World Health Organization (WHO) and the Global Burden of Disease (GBD) study, more than 4.7 million deaths worldwide are directly or indirectly related to antimicrobial resistance each year. In the ranking of causes of death, the burden of resistance ranks third globally, following ischemic heart disease and stroke.
  
  To address this crisis, the Tri-Service General Hospital in Taiwan, in collaboration with the Taiwan Centers for Disease Control (CDC), the Joint Commission of Taiwan, and the Infectious Diseases Society of Taiwan, launched the "One Health, Immediate Action – One Health Antibiotic Education Bus" exhibition on November 4. The initiative aims to strengthen public awareness of AMR prevention through national-level epidemic prevention integration, focusing on three aspects: infection prevention, precision diagnosis and treatment, and rational drug use.
  
  In the field of public health, multiple sources have consistently confirmed that antimicrobial resistance has evolved into a major global mortality risk. Professional institutions and medical groups are actively promoting the "One Health" concept, which emphasizes the close connection between human, animal, and environmental health. Through the introduction of educational promotion and precision medicine, the medical community hopes to slow down bacterial mutations and the decline in defense capabilities caused by antibiotic abuse. This prevention trend has become a core policy of global medical systems, and Taiwan is also implementing public health policies at the community level through physical activities such as the education bus, attempting to establish a comprehensive line of defense against epidemics.
  
  However, compared to the clear data and prevention strategies in the public health sector, the geopolitical situation in the Eurasian region remains obscure. According to an analysis by *Eurasia Review*, Iran's semi-official ISNA news agency recently released information suggesting that the Tehran authorities may be redefining the strike range of their armed forces. This policy move is seen as a signal of a shift in Iran's missile strategy, sparking widespread discussion in the international community regarding whether Europe is entering the range of Iranian military threats.
  
  Furthermore, the footprint of the Islamic State Khorasan Province (ISKP) in Afghanistan continues to expand. Experts point out that mere condemnation is no longer sufficient to address the threat of terrorism; the international community must seek counter-terrorism cooperation mechanisms that transcend traditional frameworks to maintain regional security in Eurasia.
  
  Although the two aforementioned issues differ in nature, they have collectively shaped the current overall risk environment in the Eurasian region. The impact of antimicrobial resistance on global public health directly affects the resilience of national medical systems and social stability; meanwhile, the shift in Iran's military policy and the entrenchment of terrorist forces pose direct threats to cross-border logistics, energy supply chains, and diplomatic stability. If military conflicts or geopolitical turmoil erupt within the region, it will not only disrupt the scheduling of public health resources and international medical cooperation but may also lead to the interruption of epidemic prevention supply chains, further increasing the complexity of the global public health crisis.
  
  Although there is a certain consensus on AMR data and geopolitical risks, there are limitations that should not be over-interpreted. Regarding AMR, while data shows a very high mortality rate, there are vast differences in medical infrastructure across regions, and there are significant gaps in prevention effectiveness; a single education policy is difficult to solve a global problem. Regarding geopolitics, there is currently no definitive conclusion as to whether the military signals released by Iran represent a substantive strategic shift or are merely bargaining chips for diplomatic negotiations. Analysts warn that military developments in the Eurasian region must be carefully evaluated to avoid misjudging the regional security situation due to one-sided information, which could lead to the compression of diplomatic maneuvering space.
  
  Looking ahead, the focus of observation in the public health field will be on antibiotic research and development programs and the integration of global surveillance systems. Whether the "One Health" strategy promoted by Taiwan can effectively reduce the rate of antimicrobial resistance will become an important reference indicator for other countries in the region. At the same time, the security situation in Eurasia requires close attention to the specific implementation of Iran's missile policy and subsequent coordination by the international community regarding counter-terrorism in Afghanistan. As globalization deepens, the linkage between public health and geopolitical security is becoming increasingly strong, and governments need to adopt more flexible policies to cope with complex crises that may erupt at any time.

# 全球公衛危機與地緣政治緊張：抗藥性威脅與歐亞安全域性勢分析

> 世界衛生組織統計全球每年逾四百七十萬例死亡與細菌抗藥性有關，位居全球死因第三；與此同時，伊朗導彈政策調整與阿富汗恐怖組織威脅，正加劇歐亞地區的地緣安全不確定性。


細菌抗藥性（Antimicrobial Resistance, AMR）已成為全球公共衛生領域最嚴峻的挑戰之一。根據世界衛生組織（WHO）與全球疾病負擔（GBD）研究的最新資料顯示，全球每年有超過四百七十萬例死亡與細菌抗藥性直接或間接相關。在死因排序中，抗藥性負擔僅次於缺血性心臟病與中風，位居全球第三。
為應對此一危機，臺灣三軍總醫院於十一月四日聯合衛生福利部疾病管制署、醫策會及臺灣感染症醫學會，共同啟動「防疫一體，立即行動－OneHealth抗生素衛教巴士」展覽，旨在透過國家級防疫串聯，從感染預防、精準診療及合理用藥三個層面，強化全民抗藥性防治意識。
在公共衛生領域，多方來源一致確認抗藥性已演變為全球性的重大死亡風險。各界專業機構與醫療團體正積極推動「防疫一體」（One Health）的概念，強調人類、動物與環境健康之間的緊密連結。透過教育推廣與精準醫療的匯入，醫療界期望能減緩抗生素濫用所導致的細菌突變與防禦力下降。此一防治趨勢已成為全球醫療體系的核心政策，臺灣亦透過衛教巴士等實體活動，將公共衛生政策落實至社群層面，試圖建立完整的防疫防線。
然而，相較於公共衛生領域的明確資料與防疫策略，歐亞地區的地緣政治局勢則顯得晦暗不明。根據《Eurasia Review》的分析，伊朗半官方的 ISNA 通訊社近期釋出訊息，暗示德黑蘭當局可能正在重新定義其武裝部隊的打擊範圍。這一政策動向被視為伊朗導彈戰略調整的訊號，引發國際社會對於歐洲是否進入伊朗軍事威脅射程的廣泛討論。
此外，伊斯蘭國呼羅珊省（ISKP）在阿富汗的活動足跡亦持續擴大，專家指出，單純的指責已不足以應對恐怖主義威脅，國際社會必須尋求超越傳統框架的反恐合作機制，以維護歐亞大陸的區域安全。
上述兩類議題雖然性質不同，卻共同形塑了當前歐亞地區的整體風險環境。細菌抗藥性對全球公共衛生的衝擊，直接影響各國醫療體系的韌性與社會穩定；而伊朗軍事政策的轉向與恐怖主義勢力的盤據，則對跨國物流、能源供應鏈與外交穩定構成直接威脅。若區域內爆發軍事衝突或地緣政治動盪，不僅會干擾公共衛生資源的排程與國際醫療合作，更可能導致防疫物資供應鏈中斷，進一步加劇全球公衛危機的複雜度。
儘管各界對抗藥性資料與地緣政治風險有一定共識，但仍存在不應過度解讀的限制。關於抗藥性，雖然資料顯示死亡率極高，但不同地區的醫療基礎設施差異巨大，防治成效亦有顯著落差，單一衛教政策難以解決全球性問題。在地緣政治方面，伊朗釋出的軍事訊號是否為實質戰略轉向，抑或僅是外交談判的籌碼，目前尚無確切定論。分析人士提醒，針對歐亞地區的軍事動態，必須謹慎評估各方意圖，避免因片面資訊而誤判區域安全形勢，導致外交斡旋空間遭到壓縮。
展望未來，公共衛生領域的觀察重點將聚焦於抗生素研發程式及全球監測系統的整合。臺灣推動的「防疫一體」策略能否有效降低抗藥性比例，將成為區域內其他國家的重要參考指標。與此同時，歐亞地區的安全域性勢則需密切關注伊朗導彈政策的具體落實，以及國際社會針對阿富汗反恐議題的後續協調。隨著全球化程度加深，公共衛生與地緣安全之間的連動性日益增強，各國政府需採取更具彈性的政策，以應對隨時可能爆發的複合型危機。
