  # Walsin Lihwa Marks 60th Anniversary with Strategic Transformation, Focusing on Nine Frontier Industries Including Nuclear Energy and AI
  
  > On its 60th anniversary, Walsin Lihwa announced a comprehensive strategic transformation, focusing its resources on nine key areas such as AI data centers, nuclear energy, and SMRs. The company also unveiled a new corporate identity, aiming to seize opportunities in the energy transition within high-value and intelligent markets.
  
  Walsin Lihwa Corporation held its "60th Anniversary Corporate Vision and Trend Sharing Session" on November 18, 2026, officially marking the milestone of its six-decade history. Chairman Yu-Lon Chiao announced that the company would initiate a comprehensive strategic transformation. Through five strategic pillars and four driving forces, Walsin will shift its business focus toward nine frontier industries. These sectors include AI Data Centers (AIDC), robotics automation, the aerospace industry, semiconductors, nuclear energy and Small Modular Reactors (SMRs), oil and gas, new energy, the automotive industry, and medical devices.
  
  Founded in 1966, this transformation is viewed as a significant strategic deployment for the company in the era of energy transition and intelligence. It aims to deepen cooperation with global supply chain partners through the vision of "Connecting Peers, Co-creating the Future," while demonstrating the company's determination toward high-value, intelligent, and globalized operations.
  
  According to cross-referenced public information, the core logic behind Walsin's transformation lies in capturing the dividends of shifting global energy demands and the expansion of digital infrastructure. Its focus on nuclear energy and SMRs, in particular, indicates the company's attempt to move beyond traditional wire, cable, and resource industries to enter the high-barrier energy infrastructure market. This transformation plan is not merely a business adjustment but involves a comprehensive update of the Corporate Identity System (CIS), symbolizing Walsin's attempt to shed its traditional manufacturing image and shift toward a positioning as a provider of technology and energy solutions.
  
  Management emphasized during the sharing session that the company will continue to strengthen its global production bases and R&D capabilities to meet the stringent requirements for critical components and energy supply in the aforementioned nine industries.
  
  Although Walsin has revealed a clear transformation path and industrial goals, market analysts point out that there are still variables in the execution of these strategies. For instance, the development of the nuclear and SMR industries is highly dependent on the deregulation and technological maturity of various countries, while competition in the AI data center and semiconductor industries is extremely intense, requiring high supply chain stability and rapid technological iteration. Furthermore, there is currently no detailed financial guidance or specific investment plan disclosed regarding the allocation of resources among the nine industries or how the company will balance the capital needs of its traditional core business with those of its emerging frontier businesses.
  
  Details regarding these technical and capital operations remain to be clarified in subsequent investor conferences or public financial reports. Currently, expectations from various sectors regarding this transformation plan are focused on the validity of the industrial direction rather than immediate profit contributions.
  
  Walsin's strategic transformation holds indicative significance for the energy and industrial supply chain in East Asia. With the expansion of AI data centers in the region, the demand for stable power supply is becoming increasingly urgent. By listing nuclear energy and SMRs as key priorities, Walsin demonstrates its confidence in the rigid demand for low-carbon energy within the region. For related industries, Walsin's entry may drive technological upgrades in cable, power equipment, and material supply chains, thereby influencing the construction efficiency of regional energy infrastructure.
  
  However, this also means that Walsin must face a more complex international trade environment and geopolitical challenges. Especially in sensitive industries such as semiconductors and aerospace, whether its global layout can successfully avoid geopolitical risks will be a key factor affecting the effectiveness of its transformation.
  
  Different perspectives suggest that Walsin may be spreading itself too thin, as entering nine industries simultaneously could lead to a dilution of resources. Some industry insiders warn that corporate transformation should prioritize the continuity of core competitiveness rather than blindly chasing market trends. Investors should not over-interpret the short-term effects of a single strategy announcement and should instead closely monitor whether Walsin can secure key patents, technical certifications, or long-term contracts in each of these frontier industries.
  
  Furthermore, global energy policy is highly volatile. In particular, the social acceptance and political deliberation processes regarding nuclear energy policy in East Asian countries could become the greatest constraints on the execution of this transformation plan. Investors and observers should maintain a cautious attitude and avoid equating vision planning directly with immediate revenue growth.
  
  Looking ahead, the focus for observing Walsin's transformation will center on three aspects: First, whether the company can propose specific technical partners or project progress in the nuclear and SMR fields; second, whether Walsin can successfully enter the supply chain systems of major international technology companies in the AI data center and semiconductor industries; and third, how Walsin will maintain the robustness of its financial structure to support massive capital expenditures for transformation amidst global economic volatility. As Walsin enters its next sixty years, its success in transforming from a traditional industrial manufacturer into a leader in frontier energy and technology solutions will depend on its comprehensive execution capabilities in policy response, R&D, and global market expansion.

# 華新邁入六十週年宣佈轉型 聚焦核能與AI等九大前瞻產業

> 華新麗華於六十週年慶宣佈全面策略轉型，將資源聚焦於AI資料中心、核能與SMR等九大領域，並揭示全新企業識別，力求在高值化與智慧化市場中搶佔能源轉型先機。


華新麗華公司於2026年11月18日舉行「60週年企業願景暨趨勢分享會」，正式邁入創立一甲子的里程碑。董事長焦佑倫在會中宣佈，華新將啟動全面性的策略轉型，透過五大策略主軸與四大驅動力，將業務重心轉向九大前瞻產業。這些領域包括AI資料中心（AIDC）、機器人自動化、航太產業、半導體、核能與小型模組化反應爐（SMR）、油氣、新能源、汽車工業以及醫療裝置。
華新創立於1966年，此次轉型被視為該公司在能源轉型與智慧化時代的重要戰略部署，旨在透過「鏈結同行、共創未來」的願景，深化與全球供應鏈夥伴的合作，並展現其在高值化、智慧化與全球化佈局的決心。
根據多方公開資訊交叉比對，華新此次轉型的核心邏輯在於捕捉全球能源需求變遷與數位基礎設施擴張的紅利。特別是在核能與SMR領域的佈局，顯示該公司試圖在傳統電線電纜與資源產業之外，進一步切入高技術門檻的能源基礎建設市場。這項轉型計畫不僅是單純的業務調整，更涉及企業識別系統（CIS）的全面更新，象徵著華新試圖擺脫傳統製造業形象，轉向更具備科技與能源解決方案提供者特質的企業定位。
管理層在分享會中強調，未來將持續強化全球化生產基地與研發能量，以滿足上述九大產業對關鍵零元件與能源供應的嚴苛要求。
儘管華新對外揭示了明確的轉型路徑與產業目標，但市場分析師指出，相關策略在具體執行層面仍存在變數。例如，核能與SMR產業的發展高度依賴各國政策法規的鬆綁與技術成熟度，而AI資料中心與半導體產業的競爭則極為激烈，對供應鏈的穩定性與技術迭代速度要求極高。此外，華新在九大產業間的資源配置比例，以及如何平衡傳統核心業務與新興前瞻業務的資金需求，目前尚未有詳細的財務指引或具體投資計畫書公開。
這些技術與資本運作的細節，仍有待後續法說會或公開財報中進一步釐清，目前各界對於該轉型計畫的預期，多集中在產業方向的正確性，而非即時的獲利貢獻。
此次華新的策略轉型，對東亞地區的能源與工業供應鏈具有指標性意義。隨著AI資料中心在東亞地區的擴張，對於穩定電力供應的需求日益迫切，華新將核能與SMR列為重點，顯示其看好區域內對低碳能源的剛性需求。對於相關產業而言，華新的加入可能帶動電纜、電力裝置與材料供應鏈的技術升級，進而影響區域內能源基礎設施的建設效率。
然而，這也意味著華新必須面對更複雜的國際貿易環境與地緣政治挑戰，尤其是在半導體與航太等敏感產業，其全球化佈局是否能順利規避地緣政治風險，將是影響其轉型成效的關鍵因素。
不同觀點認為，華新將戰線拉得過長，同時跨足九大產業可能導致資源分散。部分產業界人士提醒，企業轉型應優先考量核心競爭力的延續性，而非盲目追逐市場熱點。對於投資人而言，不應過度解讀單一策略發布會的短期效應，應密切關注華新在各個前瞻產業中，是否能取得關鍵專利、技術認證或長期合約。
此外，全球能源政策的變動性極高，特別是核能政策在東亞各國的社會接受度與政治審議過程，均可能成為該轉型計畫執行時的最大限制，投資者與觀察家應保持審慎態度，避免將願景規劃直接等同於即時營收成長。
展望未來，華新在轉型過程中的後續觀察重點將聚焦於三大面向：第一，該公司是否能針對核能與SMR領域提出具體的技術合作夥伴或專案進度；第二，在AI資料中心與半導體產業的佈局中，華新是否能成功打入國際大型科技公司的供應鏈體系；第三，面對全球經濟波動，華新如何維持其財務結構的穩健性，以支撐龐大的轉型資本支出。隨著華新進入下一個六十年，其能否成功從傳統工業製造轉型為前瞻能源與科技解決方案的領航者，將取決於其在政策應對、技術研發與全球市場開拓上的綜合執行力。
