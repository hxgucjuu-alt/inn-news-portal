  # Rising Global Energy and Inflation Risks: Challenges for African Economies and Climate Change
  
  > The President of the Asian Development Bank has issued a warning regarding global inflation risks, exacerbated by the impacts of energy and climate change. Meanwhile, inflation data in parts of Africa shows signs of slowing, indicating differentiated economic development within the region amidst a volatile environment, where climate resilience and resource management have become key variables for Africa's future development.
  
  In mid-October 2026, Asian Development Bank (ADB) President Masato Kanda issued a warning, noting that the global economy is currently in a state of high uncertainty and that inflationary pressures are likely to worsen. This wave of risk primarily stems from soaring energy prices, rising transportation costs, and the chain reaction caused by geopolitical instability in the Middle East on energy supply chains. Furthermore, the El Niño phenomenon has intensified extreme weather caused by climate change, posing severe challenges to the fiscal sustainability and debt-servicing capacity of developing countries worldwide.
  
  On the African continent, regions such as North Africa, East Africa, and Southern Africa serve as critical observation points for global climate change and energy markets, with their economic performance and environmental resilience receiving significant attention from the international community.
  
  According to cross-referenced media reports, volatility in the global energy market has become a core factor affecting regional economic stability. The Asian Development Bank emphasizes that the Asia-Pacific region's reliance on Middle Eastern crude oil and natural gas makes it highly susceptible to energy price shocks, which in turn trigger long-term inflation. Regarding the African market, TradingView, citing Reuters data, noted that the year-on-year growth rate of the Consumer Price Index (CPI) in the West African nation of Ghana has slowed from 9.4% in September to 8.0% in October, demonstrating that some African economies possess a degree of resilience in price control when facing global inflationary pressures.
  
  However, this economic trend is not consistent across all regions of Africa, and the dynamics of the South African market remain a key focus for investors and policymakers.
  
  Regarding the details and verification of source information, there is a high degree of consensus on the causes of global inflation, namely that energy prices and climate change are the two primary drivers. However, data provided by different media outlets highlight regional developmental differences: the ADB President focuses on the risks of worsening global inflation and the debt burden of developing countries, while TradingView provides specific data on slowing inflation in particular African nations (such as Ghana). What remains to be confirmed is whether this trend of slowing inflation possesses long-term stability, and whether African nations have sufficient fiscal space to support necessary climate adaptation measures in the face of potentially worsening extreme weather events; this part requires further verification with subsequent data.
  
  In terms of industrial and policy impacts, energy and climate risks directly affect Africa's infrastructure and agricultural development. As important economic and tourism regions for Africa, the stability of the ecological environment in North Africa, East Africa, and Southern Africa is directly linked to industrial development. Natural disasters triggered by climate change not only threaten public safety but also impact energy and food security.
  
  Although the New Taipei City Government's policy of promoting tree planting to increase carbon sinks is not directly related to Africa, its concept of emphasizing forests as a "green barrier" to regulate microclimates and conserve water resources holds significant reference value and policy implications for regions in Africa facing the threats of desertification and climate change, demonstrating a global consensus on mitigating the impacts of climate change through tree planting and environmental protection.
  
  Regarding the aforementioned phenomena, there are significant differences in perspectives across various sectors, and these should not be over-interpreted. The warning from the ADB President is a risk alert at the macroeconomic level, intended to remind countries to strengthen fiscal discipline and energy security, rather than a prediction of collapse in specific regions. On the other hand, while the slowing inflation data in Ghana is a positive signal, the economic structures of African countries vary greatly, and data from a single country cannot represent the economic situation of the entire African continent. Furthermore, disasters brought about by climate change are highly unpredictable, and relying too heavily on short-term data to assess long-term economic resilience may lead to inaccurate policy judgments. A limiting factor is the high degree of interconnectedness in global supply chains, which makes it difficult for African economies to completely decouple from the influence of international energy price fluctuations.
  
  Subsequent observations should focus on three directions: first, the continued impact of the situation in the Middle East on the global energy market, which will directly determine the import costs and inflation trends of African countries; second, the specific impact of the El Niño phenomenon on African agricultural production and water resource management, particularly in East Africa and Southern Africa, which are vulnerable to climate change; and finally, the balance between government efforts to address debt pressure and the need for climate adaptation funding. International investment in Africa's climate resilience, as well as the speed at which African nations implement policies for energy transition, will be key indicators in determining the stability of the regional economy in the coming years.

# 全球能源與通膨風險升溫：非洲經濟與氣候變遷的挑戰

> 亞洲開發銀行總裁示警全球通膨風險，能源與氣候變遷衝擊加劇。與此同時，非洲部分地區通膨資料出現放緩跡象，顯示區域經濟在動盪環境中呈現差異化發展，氣候韌性與資源管理成為非洲未來發展的關鍵變數。


亞洲開發銀行（ADB）總裁神田真人（Masato Kanda）於2026年10月中旬發出警告，指出全球經濟正處於高度不確定性之中，通膨壓力恐將持續惡化。此波風險主要源於能源價格高漲、運輸成本攀升，以及中東地緣政治動盪對能源供應鏈造成的連鎖反應。此外，聖嬰現象（El Niño）加劇了氣候變遷帶來的極端天氣，對全球開發中國家的財政永續性與償債能力構成嚴峻挑戰。
在非洲大陸，北非、東非與南非等區域作為全球氣候變遷與能源市場的重要觀察點，其經濟表現與環境韌性正受到國際社會高度關注。
根據各方媒體資訊交叉比對，全球能源市場的波動已成為影響區域經濟穩定的核心因素。亞洲開發銀行強調，亞洲與太平洋地區對中東原油與天然氣的依賴，使得該區域極易受到能源價格衝擊，進而引發長期通膨。在非洲市場方面，TradingView引述路透社資料指出，西非國家迦納的消費者物價指數（CPI）年增率已從9月的9.4%放緩至10月的8.0%，顯示部分非洲經濟體在面對全球性通膨壓力時，展現出一定的物價調控韌性。
然而，這種經濟趨勢在非洲各地區並不一致，南非市場的動態仍是投資人與政策制定者觀察的重點。
在來源資訊的細節與確認度上，目前針對全球通膨的成因存在高度共識，即能源價格與氣候變遷是兩大核心驅動力。然而，不同媒體提供的資料呈現出區域發展的差異：亞銀總裁側重於全球性通膨惡化的風險與開發中國家的債務壓力，而TradingView則提供了非洲特定國家（如迦納）通膨放緩的具體資料。尚待確認之處在於，這種通膨放緩的趨勢是否具備長期穩定性，以及非洲各國在面對未來可能加劇的極端氣候事件時，其財政空間是否足以支撐必要的氣候調適措施，這部分仍需後續資料進一步驗證。
從產業與政策影響層面來看，能源與氣候風險直接衝擊非洲的基礎建設與農業發展。北非、東非與南非作為非洲重要的經濟與旅遊區域，其生態環境的穩定性直接關聯到產業發展。氣候變遷引發的自然災害不僅威脅公共安全，更影響能源與糧食安全。
雖然新北市政府推動植樹以增加碳匯的政策與非洲無直接關聯，但其強調森林作為「綠色屏障」以調節微氣候、涵養水源的概念，對於非洲面臨荒漠化與氣候變遷威脅的區域而言，具有高度的參考價值與政策啟示，顯示全球對於透過植樹與環境保護來緩解氣候變遷衝擊已達成共識。
針對上述現象，各界觀點存在顯著差異，且不應過度解讀。亞銀總裁的警告屬於總體經濟層面的風險預警，旨在提醒各國加強財政紀律與能源安全，而非預測特定地區的崩潰。另一方面，迦納通膨資料的放緩雖是正面訊號，但非洲各國經濟結構差異巨大，單一國家的資料不能代表整個非洲大陸的經濟狀況。此外，氣候變遷帶來的災害具有高度不可預測性，過度依賴短期資料來評估長期的經濟韌性可能導致政策判斷失準。限制因素在於全球供應鏈的高度連結，使得非洲經濟仍難以完全脫離國際能源價格波動的影響。
後續觀察重點應聚焦於三個方向：首先是中東局勢對全球能源市場的持續影響，這將直接決定非洲各國的進口成本與通膨走勢；其次是聖嬰現象對非洲農業生產與水資源管理的具體衝擊，特別是在易受氣候變遷影響的東非與南部非洲地區；最後則是各國政府在應對債務壓力與氣候調適資金需求之間的平衡。國際社會對非洲氣候韌性的投資，以及非洲各國在能源轉型上的政策落實速度，將是決定未來數年區域經濟穩定與否的關鍵指標。
