  # Thai Government Suspends 49 Data Center Projects to Develop New Regulatory Framework for Industry Expansion
  
  > The Thai government recently announced the suspension of 49 data center construction projects. A national data center regulatory committee has intervened, with new regulations expected to be formulated within one month to govern the industry's operations and future development.
  
  The Thai government has recently taken significant regulatory action regarding digital infrastructure. Danucha Pichayanan, Secretary-General of the National Economic and Social Development Council (NESDC), confirmed at a recent press conference that the government has ordered a suspension of construction on a total of 49 data center projects across the country. The primary context for this move is the Thai government's active push for regulatory upgrades in the sector, aiming to establish a more rigorous and comprehensive set of rules and regulations for both existing and upcoming data centers.
  
  The "Data Center Regulatory Committee," chaired by the Minister of Finance of Thailand, held its first meeting recently, officially launching the process of deliberating and formulating relevant regulations. This marks a shift in Thailand's digital economic transformation, as the government begins to strengthen administrative intervention and policy guidance over critical infrastructure.
  
  According to multiple sources, the decision to suspend construction is not an isolated administrative act but is closely related to adjustments in Thailand's overall digital industry policy. The Thai government is currently in a critical period of assessing the impact of digital infrastructure on the national economy. By establishing a dedicated committee, the government hopes to effectively integrate the development pace of the data center industry, ensuring it aligns with national long-term goals regarding digital security, energy consumption, and industrial competitiveness. Relevant authorities have clearly stated that the new regulations are expected to be announced within one month, at which point all affected construction projects will need to undergo compliance reviews under the new rules before they can resume or continue operations.
  
  Although the official policy direction to suspend construction has been confirmed, there is currently no detailed public information regarding the specific geographic distribution of these 49 projects, the list of affected companies, or the financial losses incurred by individual firms due to the work stoppage. Observers are focusing on whether the new regulations will impose stricter requirements on energy efficiency, data storage security standards, or foreign ownership ratios for data centers. Furthermore, as the industry involves high technical intensity and cross-border data flows, there are varying interpretations regarding whether the new norms might lead some international investors to reassess their presence in Thailand.
  
  At present, the industry remains highly concerned about the transparency of the regulatory details and the grace period for implementation, which are communication challenges the government must face during the policy execution process.
  
  From a regional development perspective, Thailand's move reflects a growing trend among Southeast Asian nations to place greater importance on infrastructure management during the digitization process. With the booming digital economy in Southeast Asia, countries have become increasingly reliant on data centers; however, associated concerns regarding data security and crime have also become more prominent. According to relevant United Nations research reports, transnational criminal groups in Southeast Asia are leveraging increasingly integrated network technologies and digital infrastructure to expand the scope of their illicit activities. The Thai government's strict regulation of data centers, beyond considerations of industrial development, may also include strategic intentions to strengthen national digital border security and prevent criminal groups from abusing infrastructure.
  
  This serves as an important reference indicator for other countries in the region that are currently promoting digital transformation.
  
  However, academic and industry analysts point out that the negative impact of this construction suspension on Thailand's ability to attract foreign investment should not be overstated. Economic security analysis of the Southeast Asian region by the Brookings Institution has noted that there is a complex link between trade and economic security in the region. Governments often need to strike a balance between attracting foreign investment and maintaining national security when promoting digital infrastructure. Therefore, the Thai government's regulatory action can, to a certain extent, be seen as a necessary adjustment in the process of industrial maturation, aimed at building a more resilient digital ecosystem rather than a purely exclusionary policy.
  
  Investors and observers should continue to monitor the specific details following the release of the regulations to avoid excessive market panic caused by a single policy adjustment.
  
  Subsequent observations will focus on the specific content of the regulations to be announced in a month, particularly whether there are "sunset clauses" or transition arrangements for existing data centers. Furthermore, how the Thai government balances strict regulatory requirements with maintaining the international competitiveness of its digital industry will be the key to determining whether Thailand can continue to be a data center hub in Southeast Asia. All sectors will closely watch the progress of subsequent meetings of the Thai Data Center Regulatory Committee and whether the relevant norms will trigger imitation by neighboring countries, thereby affecting the digital infrastructure investment environment across the entire Southeast Asian region.

# 泰國政府暫停49項資料中心建設 研擬新監管框架以應對產業擴張

> 泰國政府日前宣佈暫停49項資料中心建設案，由國家資料中心監管委員會介入，預計一個月內制定新法規，以規範該產業的運作與未來發展方向。


泰國政府近期針對數位基礎設施採取重大監管行動，國家經濟和社會發展委員會（NESDC）秘書長達努查（Danucha Pichayanan）於近期召開的新聞發布會上證實，政府已下令暫停國內共計49個資料中心（Data Center）專案的建設工程。此舉的主要背景在於泰國政府正積極推動該行業的監管升級，旨在針對現有營運中以及即將啟動的資料中心，制定一套更為嚴謹且完善的法規與條例。
由泰國財政部長親自擔任主席的「資料中心監管委員會」已於日前召開首次會議，正式啟動相關法規的審議與制訂程式，這標誌著泰國在數位經濟轉型過程中，開始加強對關鍵基礎設施的行政幹預與政策導向。
根據多個資訊來源確認，此次暫停建設的決定並非單一行政作為，而是與泰國整體數位產業政策調整密切相關。泰國政府目前正處於評估數位基礎設施對國家經濟影響的關鍵期，透過設立專責委員會，政府期望能有效整合資料中心產業的發展節奏，確保其與國家長期的數位安全、能源消耗及產業競爭力目標保持一致。相關單位已明確表示，新規章制度預計將在一個月內對外公佈，屆時所有受影響的建設專案將需依循新法規進行合規性審查，方能重啟或持續運作。
儘管官方已確認暫停建設的政策方向，但關於這49個專案具體的分佈區域、受影響的企業名單以及停工對個別廠商造成的財務損失，目前仍未有詳細的公開資訊。各方觀察重點在於，新法規是否會針對資料中心的能源使用效率、資料儲存安全標準或外資持股比例提出更嚴格的要求。此外，由於該產業涉及高度技術密集與跨境資料流動，市場對於新規範是否會導致部分國際投資者重新評估在泰國的佈局，尚存在不同解讀。
目前，業界對於法規細則的透明度與執行寬限期仍抱持高度關注，這是政府在政策執行過程中必須面對的溝通挑戰。
從區域發展的角度來看，泰國此舉反映了東南亞國家在數位化程式中，對於基礎設施管理日益重視的趨勢。隨著東南亞地區數位經濟的蓬勃發展，各國對於資料中心的依賴程度大幅提升，然而隨之而來的資料安全與犯罪隱憂也日益顯著。根據聯合國相關研究報告顯示，東南亞地區的跨國犯罪集團正利用日益整合的網路技術與數位基礎設施，擴張其非法活動範圍。泰國政府此次對資料中心進行嚴格監管，除考量產業發展外，亦可能包含強化國家數位邊界安全、防範犯罪集團濫用基礎設施的戰略意圖。
這對於該地區其他正在推動數位轉型的國家而言，具有重要的參考指標意義。
然而，學界與產業分析觀點指出，不應過度解讀此次暫停建設行為對泰國吸引外資的負面影響。布魯金斯學會（Brookings Institution）針對東南亞地區的經濟安全分析曾指出，該區域在貿易與經濟安全之間存在複雜的連結，各國政府在推動數位基礎建設時，往往需要在吸引外資與維護國家安全之間取得平衡。因此，泰國政府的監管行動，在一定程度上可被視為產業成熟化過程中的必要調整，旨在建立更具韌性的數位生態系統，而非單純的排外政策。
投資者與觀察家應持續關注法規出臺後的具體細節，避免因單一政策調整而產生過度的市場恐慌。
後續觀察重點將聚焦於一個月後公佈的具體法規內容，特別是針對現有資料中心是否設有「落日條款」或過渡期安排。此外，泰國政府如何平衡嚴格的監管要求與維持數位產業的國際競爭力，將是決定泰國能否持續成為東南亞資料中心樞紐的關鍵。各界將密切關注泰國資料中心監管委員會的後續會議進展，以及相關規範是否會引發鄰國效仿，進而影響整個東南亞地區的數位基礎設施投資環境。
