  # COP31 Climate Negotiations Deadlocked: Disputes Over Fossil Fuel Definitions and Energy Transition Pathways
  
  > **Summary:** The United Nations Climate Change Conference (COP31) failed to reach a comprehensive agreement after the European Union and other nations refused to endorse the draft, which lacked a "zero fossil fuel" clause. This event highlights the deep divide in global energy transition policies between oil-producing nations and developing countries, sparking widespread discussion on the effectiveness of the consensus-based mechanism in climate governance.
  
  The United Nations Climate Change Conference (COP31) faced a major negotiating setback in late November 2026. The core focus of the summit was originally to establish a concrete path for the global energy transition; however, the final draft agreement failed to explicitly mention targets for the phase-out or reduction of "fossil fuels," triggering severe opposition among participating nations. The background of this event reflects the structural contradictions in global climate governance when faced with energy security and economic development, particularly regarding the fundamental cognitive gap between oil-producing nations and fossil-fuel-dependent developing countries versus the European Union and other nations actively pushing for energy transition, specifically concerning the definition and implementation timeline of "zero fossil fuels."
  
  According to reports from The News Lens, the consensus-based mechanism of COP31 appeared fragile during this summit. The EU delegation clearly stated that if the draft did not provide substantive commitments regarding the fossil fuel issue, the EU would refuse to endorse it. This move resulted in the conference failing to reach a comprehensive agreement, marking a rare breakdown in recent climate negotiations. All parties confirmed that the issue of exiting fossil fuels has become an insurmountable divide in climate talks, and the current consensus-based system is struggling to produce binding global climate goals when faced with highly conflicting national interests.
  
  However, there remain significant differences in perspectives regarding the specific attribution of responsibility for the breakdown. Some reports indicate that the bloc of oil-producing nations insisted on shifting the focus of the energy transition toward carbon capture technology rather than a direct phase-out of fossil fuels. Conversely, others argue that developing countries, fearing the economic impact and high costs of the energy transition, remain reserved about aggressive emission reduction targets. Furthermore, there is currently no clear international consensus on whether "zero fossil fuels" should include all energy types or only specific high-pollution sources, which remains one of the core technical disputes that led to the deadlock.
  
  This event has had a profound impact on global energy policy and industrial layout. In North America and other parts of the world, the choice of energy transition pathways is no longer just an environmental issue; it is directly linked to the massive electricity demand required for the development of the AI industry. According to an analysis by The News Lens regarding the arguments against nuclear power referendums, nuclear energy is viewed by some as expensive and too slow to meet the immediate electricity needs of the AI industry. Energy experts point out that if the international community cannot reach a consensus on the future of fossil fuels, it will lead to increased uncertainty in the global energy market, thereby affecting the stability of green energy investments.
  
  At the same time, the status of alternative energy sources such as nuclear power in the energy mix has been re-examined due to the failure of these negotiations. Governments may face more severe economic crowding-out effects and challenges to energy supply stability as they push for renewable energy transitions.
  
  There are varying interpretations and limitations regarding the results of COP31. Some viewpoints emphasize that over-interpreting the success or failure of a single summit may overlook the substantive progress made by countries in their domestic policies. Other commentaries argue that blaming the deadlock entirely on oil-producing nations or developing countries is too one-sided, as it ignores the responsibility gap of developed nations regarding technology transfer and climate financing. Experts warn that this breakdown should not be viewed as the end of global climate action, but rather as a warning signal that the current negotiation mechanism requires structural adjustments to avoid repeating the same mistakes in future international meetings.
  
  Subsequent observations will focus on how countries revise their energy transition strategies and whether the United Nations will reform the consensus-based mechanism for climate negotiations. As a major driver of global technology and energy policy, North America's subsequent layout of energy supply chains, and whether it will adopt bilateral or regional climate agreements to bypass the deadlock of multilateral negotiations, will be key indicators for observing the future direction of global climate governance. Additionally, how countries respond to domestic industrial demands regarding energy costs and transition pressures following COP31 will also affect the long-term stability of the global energy market and the progress toward achieving climate goals.
  
  In the current geopolitical landscape, as analyzed by New York Times White House correspondent David E. Sanger regarding the international strategic environment, the intertwining of energy security and technological competition will make future climate negotiations increasingly complex.

# COP31氣候談判陷僵局：化石燃料定義與能源轉型路徑之爭

> **摘要：** 聯合國氣候變遷大會（COP31）因協議草案未納入「零化石燃料」條款，遭歐盟等國拒絕背書，導致大會未能達成總體協議。此事件凸顯全球能源轉型政策在產油國與開發中國家間的巨大分歧，並引發關於氣候治理共識決機制有效性的廣泛討論。


聯合國氣候變遷大會（COP31）於2026年11月下旬面臨重大談判挫敗。本次峰會的核心焦點原在於確立全球能源轉型的具體路徑，然而，最終協議草案因未能明確提及「化石燃料」的淘汰或減量目標，引發與會國間的嚴重對立。此事件背景反映了全球氣候治理在面對能源安全與經濟發展時的結構性矛盾，特別是在產油國與依賴化石燃料的開發中國家，與積極推動能源轉型的歐洲聯盟等國之間，對於「零化石燃料」的定義與執行時程存在根本性的認知落差。
根據《關鍵評論網》（The News Lens）的報導指出，COP31的談判共識決機制在本次峰會中顯得脆弱，歐盟代表團明確表示，若草案內容無法針對化石燃料議題提出實質性的承諾，歐盟將拒絕背書。此舉導致大會最終無法達成總體協議，成為近年來氣候談判中極為罕見的破局案例。各方一致確認的事實為，化石燃料的退出議題已成為氣候談判中不可逾越的鴻溝，且現行的共識決制度在面對各國利益高度衝突時，已難以產生具備約束力的全球性氣候目標。
然而，關於談判破局的具體責任歸屬，各界觀點仍存有顯著差異。部分報導指出，產油國陣營堅持將能源轉型的焦點轉向碳捕捉技術，而非直接淘汰化石燃料；另一方則認為，開發中國家因擔憂能源轉型帶來的經濟衝擊與高昂成本，對激進的減排目標持保留態度。此外，關於「零化石燃料」是否應包含所有能源型別，或是僅針對特定高汙染能源，目前尚無明確的國際共識，這也是導致本次談判陷入僵局的核心技術爭議之一。
此事件對全球能源政策與產業佈局產生深遠影響。在北美與全球其他地區，能源轉型的路徑選擇已不僅是環境議題，更直接關聯到AI產業發展所需的龐大電力需求。根據《關鍵評論網》針對核能公投反方理由書的分析指出，核電被部分觀點認為昂貴且緩不濟急，恐無法滿足AI產業對電力供應的即時需求。能源專家指出，若國際間無法就化石燃料的未來達成共識，將導致全球能源市場的不確定性增加，進而影響綠能投資的穩定性。
同時，核能等替代能源在能源組閤中的地位，亦因本次談判的挫敗而重新受到檢視，各國政府在推動再生能源轉型時，恐將面臨更嚴峻的經濟排擠效應與能源供應穩定性挑戰。
針對COP31的結果，各界存有不同解讀與限制。部分觀點強調，過度解讀單一峰會的成敗可能忽略了各國在國內政策上的實質進展；亦有評論認為，將氣候談判的僵局完全歸咎於產油國或開發中國家過於片面，忽略了已開發國家在技術轉移與氣候融資上的責任缺口。專家提醒，不應將本次談判破局視為全球氣候行動的終結，而應視為現行談判機制需要進行結構性調整的警訊，避免在未來的國際會議中重蹈覆轍。
後續觀察重點將集中於各國如何修正其能源轉型策略，以及聯合國是否會針對氣候談判的共識決機制進行改革。北美地區作為全球科技與能源政策的重要推手，其在後續能源供應鏈的佈局，以及是否會採取雙邊或區域性的氣候協議來繞過多邊談判的僵局，將是未來觀察全球氣候治理走向的關鍵指標。此外，各國在COP31後如何回應國內產業對於能源成本與轉型壓力的訴求，亦將影響全球能源市場的長期穩定與氣候目標的達成進度。
在當前地緣政治局勢下，如紐約時報白宮特派記者桑格所分析的國際戰略環境，能源安全與科技競爭的交織，將使未來的氣候談判更趨複雜。
