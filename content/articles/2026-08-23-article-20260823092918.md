  # US State-by-State Immigrant Employment Map Reveals Shifts in Labor Force Structure, with Middle East and Oceania Data Comparisons Drawing Attention
  
  > US immigration policy research institutions and data media outlets have successively released maps showing the most common occupations among immigrants in each state, revealing that the service industry and healthcare have become the primary employment sectors for immigrants. The related data also provides a cross-national comparative reference for labor policies in Middle Eastern and Oceanian countries.
  
  The employment structure of immigrants within the United States has recently become a focal point for multiple data media outlets and policy research institutions. Based on the latest statistics from the US Census Bureau's American Community Survey and the Bureau of Labor Statistics, several media outlets have respectively produced maps of "the most common occupations of immigrants in each state" and "the most common occupations in every US state," attempting to illustrate the distribution patterns of foreign labor within the US economy. Although these reports were published at different times spanning several years, and some materials lack clear dates, the official data sources they cite are consistent, all pointing to publicly available US federal government statistics, providing a basis for cross-verification in understanding the role of immigrant workers in regional economies.
  
  At the level of commonly confirmed facts, reports from different media outlets show highly overlapping trends. According to Visual Capitalist, citing the latest data from the US Bureau of Labor Statistics, fast food workers, cashiers, and home health aides have become the most common occupational categories in most US states. Meanwhile, Nextshark, citing the US Census Bureau's annual American Community Survey data, points out that the most common occupations among immigrant groups in each state are also highly concentrated in the service industry, food service, and healthcare sectors. The American Immigration Council's interactive map further supplements this by noting that immigrants make significant contributions to state economies through tax payments, consumer spending, and entrepreneurial activities.
  
  Together, these sources paint a picture: immigrant labor has become deeply embedded in America's grassroots service and caregiving systems, rather than being concentrated solely in high-tech or agricultural sectors.
  
  However, there are also notable differences and points requiring further confirmation among the various sources. First, the Matador Network report was published in 2017, and its data foundation is nearly a decade apart from the Visual Capitalist report published in 2026. During this period, the US labor market experienced pandemic shocks and industrial restructuring, so the rankings of "most common occupations" presented by the two inevitably reflect a time gap.
  
  Second, although the Mental Floss report published in June 2026 also focuses on the most common occupations in each state, its analytical framework emphasizes the overall labor force rather than specific immigrant groups, differing from Nextshark's focus on the immigrant population. The two cannot be directly regarded as mutual confirmation of the same indicator. Additionally, while the American Immigration Council's interactive map provides comprehensive state-level data, its statistical caliber is not identical to the Bureau of Labor Statistics' occupational classification methodology, and users should be mindful of definitional differences when making comparisons.
  
  The release of these employment maps holds concrete reference value for industrial policy and labor force planning in the relevant regions. Although the materials in this report focus on the United States, Middle Eastern and Oceanian countries frequently reference US immigrant employment data as a basis for policy design when introducing foreign workers and skilled immigrants. For example, Australia and New Zealand's skilled migration occupation lists have long drawn on the occupational classification framework of the US Bureau of Labor Statistics. Gulf Arab states, when planning policies for foreign domestic workers and healthcare personnel, also pay attention to the growing demand for home health aides in the United States in order to anticipate global trends in care worker mobility.
  
  Although these cross-national comparisons are not the direct subject of this report, the phenomenon revealed by the data maps—that "service and care work has become the primary entry point for immigrants"—does bear similarities to the foreign labor structures of Middle Eastern and Oceanian countries.
  
  Different perspectives and limitations must be carefully considered when interpreting these data. On the one hand, the American Immigration Council emphasizes the positive contributions of immigrants to the economy, including tax revenue and entrepreneurial activity, a view that tends to support the role of immigrants as complementary to the US labor market. On the other hand, using "most common occupation" as the sole observational indicator may oversimplify the diversity of immigrant employment, overlook the dominance of high-skilled immigrants in specific states, and fail to capture deeper structural issues such as immigrant unemployment rates, wage gaps, or occupational mobility.
  
  Furthermore, some media reports do not indicate the data year or clarify whether the sample coverage includes undocumented immigrants. These methodological limitations mean that readers should not treat the maps as precise scientific measurements, but rather as a visual representation of macro-level trends.
  
  Future points of observation should focus on several aspects: first, whether the US Bureau of Labor Statistics and the Census Bureau will update occupational classification standards in the coming years to reflect the impact of remote work and automation on occupational structures after the pandemic; second, whether immigrant employment data will incorporate more granular analysis of median wages, industry turnover rates, and regional differences; and third, whether think tanks in Middle Eastern and Oceanian countries will cite such US data to conduct comparative studies on local foreign worker policies. For readers concerned with international labor mobility, although these maps are US-focused, their methodology and data framework can still serve as a reference starting point for observing global immigrant employment trends.

# 美國各州移民就業地圖揭示勞動力結構變遷，中東與大洋洲資料對比引發關注

> 美國移民政策研究機構與資料媒體陸續發布各州移民常見職業地圖，顯示服務業與醫療照護已成為移民就業主力，相關資料亦為中東與大洋洲國家勞動政策提供跨國比較參考。

美國境內移民就業結構近期成為多個資料媒體與政策研究機構的關注焦點。根據美國人口普查局「美國社群調查」以及勞工統計局的最新統計資料，多家媒體分別繪製了「各州移民最常見職業」與「全美各州最常見職業」地圖，試圖呈現外來勞動力在美國經濟中的分佈樣態。雖然這些報導的發布時間橫跨數年，且部分素材未標註明確日期，但其所引用的官方資料來源一致，均指向美國聯邦政府公開統計，為理解移民勞工在區域經濟中的角色提供了可交叉檢視的基礎。
在共同確認的事實層面，不同媒體的報導呈現出高度重疊的趨勢。根據視覺資本家網站引述美國勞工統計局最新資料，速食業員工、收銀員與居家健康助理已成為美國多數州最常見的職業類別；而Nextshark網站則引用美國人口普查局年度「美國社群調查」資料，指出移民群體在各州最常從事的職業同樣高度集中於服務業、餐飲業與醫療照護領域。美國移民委員會的互動地圖進一步補充，移民在繳納稅款、消費能力與創業活動方面對各州經濟有顯著貢獻。
這些來源共同描繪出一個影象：移民勞動力已深度嵌入美國基層服務業與照護體系，而非僅集中於高科技或農業部門。
然而，各來源之間也存在明顯的差異與尚待確認之處。首先，馬塔多網路（Matador Network）的報導發布於2017年，其資料基礎與2026年發布的視覺資本家報導相隔近十年，期間美國勞動市場歷經疫情衝擊與產業結構調整，因此兩者所呈現的「最常見職業」排序必然存在時間落差。
其次，Mental Floss於2026年6月發布的報導雖然同樣以各州最常見職業為主題，但其分析框架側重於整體勞動力而非特定移民群體，與Nextshark聚焦移民人口的視角不同，兩者不能直接視為同一指標的互證。此外，美國移民委員會的互動地圖雖提供全面性的州級資料，但其統計口徑與勞工統計局的職業分類方式未盡相同，使用者在比較時需留意定義差異。
這些就業地圖的發布對相關地區的產業政策與勞動力規劃具有具體參考價值。雖然本次素材聚焦美國境內，但中東與大洋洲國家在引進外籍勞工與技術移民時，經常參考美國的移民就業資料作為政策設計依據。例如，澳洲與紐西蘭的技術移民職業清單長期參考美國勞工統計局的職業分類架構；波斯灣阿拉伯國家在規劃外籍家務勞工與醫療照護人力政策時，也關注美國居家健康助理需求成長的趨勢，以預判全球照護人力流動方向。
這些跨國比較雖非本次報導的直接主題，但資料地圖所揭示的「服務業與照護工作成為移民主要入口」現象，確實與中東、大洋洲國家的外籍勞動力結構存在相似性。
對於這些資料的解讀，不同觀點與限制必須被審慎考量。一方面，美國移民委員會強調移民對經濟的正面貢獻，包括稅收與創業活動，此觀點傾向支援移民對美國勞動市場的補充作用；另一方面，單純以「最常見職業」作為觀察指標，可能過度簡化移民就業的多樣性，忽略高技能移民在特定州的主導地位，也無法呈現移民失業率、薪資落差或職業流動性等深層結構問題。
此外，部分媒體報導未標註資料年份，或未說明樣本涵蓋範圍是否包含無證移民，這些方法學上的限制意味著讀者不應將地圖視為精確的科學測量，而應理解為一種宏觀趨勢的視覺化呈現。
後續觀察重點應放在幾個面向：首先，美國勞工統計局與人口普查局是否會在未來數年更新職業分類標準，以反映疫情後遠距工作與自動化對職業結構的影響；其次，移民就業資料是否會納入更多關於薪資中位數、行業流動率與區域差異的細部分析；再者，中東與大洋洲國家的智庫是否會引用這類美國資料，進行本地外籍勞工政策的比較研究。對於關心國際勞動力移動的讀者而言，這些地圖雖然以美國為範圍，但其方法論與資料架構，仍可作為觀察全球移民就業趨勢的參考起點。
