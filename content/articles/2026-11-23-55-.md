  # E Ink’s 55-inch Color E-Paper Display Debuts at the National Library of Public Information in Taichung, Integrating AI to Promote Smart Public Space Applications
  
  > E Ink announced that its 55-inch color e-paper signage, "E Ink Marquee," has been officially installed at the National Library of Public Information (NLPI). By combining four-color particle display technology with generative AI, the system offers low power consumption, low glare, and outdoor durability, setting a new benchmark for smart displays in public spaces.
  
  The application of e-paper display technology in public spaces has become increasingly widespread in recent years. E Ink, a global leader in e-paper technology, officially announced on November 21, 2026, that its 55-inch large-format color e-paper signage, "E Ink Marquee," has been successfully implemented at the National Library of Public Information (NLPI) in Taichung, Taiwan. This deployment marks a significant advancement for large-format color e-paper in public infrastructure, aiming to address the common issues of glare and high energy consumption associated with traditional displays in public areas through a low-power, paper-like visual experience.
  
  The display system not only features waterproof and dustproof outdoor protection but also integrates an advanced cloud management platform. This allows the library to update information in real-time and provide interactive art displays and guided services through generative AI technology, further enhancing the quality of the library's digital services and the reading experience for the public.
  
  According to various sources and E Ink’s official release, the 55-inch E Ink Marquee utilizes advanced four-color particle display technology (E Ink Spectra 6), which is capable of rendering richer color saturation and contrast, providing a significant visual advantage for large-format signage. A common feature of this technology is its "bistable" characteristic, meaning it only consumes power when the image is updated and requires virtually no power for static display. For public information signage that requires long-term operation, this drastically reduces energy consumption and maintenance costs.
  
  Furthermore, the system incorporates generative AI interactive functions, allowing the public to generate real-time artistic content through interaction with the signage. This goes beyond simple information delivery, transforming the library into an interactive digital art space and demonstrating a future trend of deep integration between display technology and artificial intelligence.
  
  However, regarding the actual operational details of the technology and the specific scope of the AI interaction, some aspects of the publicly available information remain to be clarified. For instance, while E Ink has emphasized the durability and waterproof/dustproof ratings of the signage for outdoor environments, there are currently no publicly available third-party verification reports regarding its long-term stability under extreme weather conditions (such as intense heat exposure or heavy rainfall).
  
  Additionally, the specific operating mechanisms of the generative AI interaction—including the underlying computing power requirements, privacy protection mechanisms, and the depth of integration with the library's existing management systems—remain limited to official press releases mentioning interactive art experiences. Details such as how the public operates the system, whether the AI model collects data, and the system's response latency still await further technical documentation or user experience sharing for confirmation.
  
  From an industry impact perspective, this deployment by E Ink is significant for the development of global Smart Cities. As countries worldwide place greater emphasis on energy conservation, carbon reduction, and sustainable development, the use of traditional high-energy-consumption LED or LCD displays in public spaces is coming under strict scrutiny. As a low-carbon, low-light-pollution alternative, the market demand for e-paper is expected to continue growing.
  
  For institutions such as libraries, museums, and public transportation hubs, adopting this technology not only aligns with ESG (Environmental, Social, and Governance) criteria but also reduces visual fatigue for the public through its paper-like visual effects. This has a direct positive impact on promoting "reading-friendly" and "digital inclusion" policies in public spaces.
  
  Although e-paper technology performs exceptionally well in energy saving and eye protection, market analysts and industry experts remind that its suitability for all public display scenarios should not be overstated. Compared to traditional displays, color e-paper still has inherent limitations in presenting dynamic images (such as high-frame-rate videos); its refresh rate cannot compete with LCD screens, making it unsuitable for playing high-motion advertisements or live broadcasts. Furthermore, high initial installation costs remain a major barrier to widespread adoption. During the promotion process, it is essential to clearly define its applicable scenarios—namely, information delivery, static display, and low-frequency interaction—rather than replacing all traditional display screens, to avoid user expectation gaps caused by ambiguous technical positioning.
  
  Future observations will focus on the long-term performance data of the system at the NLPI, particularly the specific benefits of energy savings and the public's acceptance and participation rate regarding the AI interactive features. Additionally, whether E Ink replicates this model in public libraries or urban infrastructure in other countries will be a key indicator for assessing the technology's global market potential. In the future, as e-paper technology costs continue to decline and color performance improves, it is expected that more smart display solutions combining AI and the Internet of Things (IoT) will appear in public spaces worldwide, providing more sustainable visual communication media for smart city construction.

# 元太科技55吋彩色電子紙落地臺中國資圖 結合AI技術推動智慧公共空間應用

> 元太科技宣佈其55吋彩色電子紙看板「E Ink Marquee」正式匯入國立公共資訊圖書館。該技術結合四色粒子顯示與生成式AI，展現低耗電、低眩光及戶外防護優勢，為公共場域智慧顯示樹立新指標。


電子紙顯示技術近年來在公共場域的應用日益廣泛，元太科技（E Ink）作為全球電子紙技術的領導廠商，於2026年11月21日正式宣佈，其研發的55吋大型彩色電子紙看板「E Ink Marquee」已成功匯入位於臺灣臺中的國立公共資訊圖書館（國資圖）。此項技術落地標誌著大型彩色電子紙在公共基礎建設中的重要進展，旨在透過低耗電、類紙質感的視覺體驗，改善傳統顯示器在公共空間中常見的眩光與高耗能問題。
該顯示系統不僅具備防水防塵的戶外防護功能，更整合了先進的雲端管理平臺，使館方能即時更新資訊，並透過生成式AI技術提供具備互動性的藝術展示與導覽服務，進一步提升圖書館的數位化服務品質與民眾的閱讀體驗。
根據多方來源與元太科技的官方發布，此次匯入的55吋E Ink Marquee採用了先進的四色粒子顯示技術（E Ink Spectra 6），能夠呈現更為豐富的色彩飽和度與對比度，這在大型顯示看板的應用上具有顯著的視覺優勢。該技術的共同特點在於其「雙穩態」特性，即僅在畫面更新時消耗電力，靜態顯示時幾乎不耗電，這對於需要長時間運作的公共資訊看板而言，極大地降低了能源消耗與維運成本。
此外，該系統結合了生成式AI的互動功能，允許民眾透過與看板的互動產生即時的藝術內容，這不僅是單純的資訊傳遞，更將圖書館轉變為一個互動式的數位藝術空間，展現了顯示技術與人工智慧深度整合的未來趨勢。
然而，針對該技術的實際運作細節與AI互動的具體範疇，目前的公開資訊仍存在部分尚待釐清之處。例如，元太科技雖強調了該看板在戶外環境的耐用性與防水防塵指標，但對於在極端氣候條件下（如高溫曝曬或強降雨）的長期穩定性資料，目前尚未有公開的第三方驗證報告。
此外，生成式AI互動的具體運作機制，包括其背後的算力需求、隱私保護機制以及與館內既有圖書管理系統的整合深度，目前僅能從官方新聞稿中得知其具備互動藝術體驗，但對於民眾如何具體操作、AI模型是否會進行資料蒐集以及系統的反應延遲時間等細節，仍有待後續更多技術檔案或使用者經驗分享來進一步確認。
從產業影響的角度來看，此次元太科技的技術落地，對於全球智慧城市（Smart City）的建設具有指標性意義。隨著全球各國對於節能減碳與永續發展的重視，傳統的高耗能LED或LCD顯示器在公共場域的使用正受到嚴格檢視。電子紙作為一種低碳、低光害的替代方案，其市場需求預計將持續增長。
對於圖書館、博物館及公共交通樞紐等場所而言，採用此類技術不僅能符合ESG（環境、社會與公司治理）指標，還能透過類紙的視覺效果減少民眾的視覺疲勞，這對於推動公共空間的「閱讀友善」與「數位包容」政策具有直接的正面影響。
儘管電子紙技術在節能與護眼方面表現卓越，但市場分析師與產業專家同時提醒，不應過度解讀其在所有公共顯示場景的適用性。相較於傳統顯示器，彩色電子紙在動態影像（如高幀率影片）的呈現上仍存在先天限制，其更新頻率無法與液晶螢幕相比，因此並不適合播放高動態的廣告或即時轉播。此外，高昂的初期建置成本仍是普及化的一大障礙。在推廣過程中，必須明確界定其適用場景，即以資訊傳遞、靜態展示與低頻率互動為主，而非取代所有傳統顯示螢幕，避免因技術定位模糊而導致使用者期待落差。
後續觀察重點將聚焦於該系統在國資圖的長期執行資料，特別是能源節省的具體效益，以及民眾對於AI互動功能的接受度與參與率。此外，元太科技是否會將此模式複製至其他國家的公共圖書館或城市基礎設施，將是評估該技術全球市場潛力的關鍵指標。未來，隨著電子紙技術成本的進一步下降與色彩表現的持續提升，預期將能看到更多結合AI與物聯網（IoT）的智慧顯示解決方案出現在全球公共空間，為智慧城市建設提供更具永續性的視覺溝通媒介。
