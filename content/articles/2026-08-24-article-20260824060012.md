  # Kazakhstan Holds First Single-Chamber Parliamentary Election; Pro-Presidential Party Wins Overwhelming Majority per Exit Polls
  
  > Kazakhstan held its first single-chamber parliamentary election under the new constitution on August 23. Exit polls showed the pro-presidential "Adilet" party securing over 70 percent of the vote, though observers are watching the long-term impact of the electoral reform on the balance of power and energy policy.
  
  Kazakhstan held the first election for the "Kurultai," a single-chamber parliament established under the new constitution, on August 23. This was the first parliamentary election since the country's political system shifted from a bicameral to a unicameral structure. According to multiple foreign media reports, seven parties competed for 145 seats in this election, with a 5 percent electoral threshold. The constitutional reform, promoted by President Kassym-Jomart Tokayev, was approved via referendum in March 2026, streamlining the previous two-chamber structure into a single parliament, a move widely seen as a key step in further consolidating presidential power. The early election was also viewed as an important opportunity for the Tokayev government to reshape the political landscape and strengthen its governing base.
  
  According to multiple media reports, voting in this election ended at 8:00 p.m. local time on August 23, with official preliminary turnout at 74.19 percent. Three different exit polls cited by Russia's TASS news agency showed that five parties crossed the 5 percent threshold and will enter the new parliament. Among them, the newly established pro-presidential "Adilet" party received over 70 percent of the vote, far ahead of other parties. The remaining parties, in order, were the "Oil" party, the "Republic" party, the "Bright Path" party, and the "National Social Democratic Party."
  
  Pakistan's The News also reported that exit polls showed the "Adilet" party receiving between 70 and 72 percent of the vote, securing an overwhelming majority. The party is widely considered a political force created by President Tokayev to consolidate power.
  
  Regarding electoral procedures and basic facts, reports from various media outlets largely align. Turkey's Anadolu Agency noted that this election was Kazakhstan's first single-chamber parliamentary election under the new constitution, with seven parties competing and a 5 percent threshold for entering parliament. Egypt's Ahram Online added that the number of eligible voters exceeded 12.6 million, and the election was held on August 23 to select 145 deputies.
  
  Regarding election results, while all sources agree on the "Adilet" party's leading position, there are slight differences in the specific vote share figures. The polls cited by TASS showed its vote share at "over 70 percent," while The News gave a range of "70 to 72 percent." Additionally, reports from various media outlets were not entirely consistent regarding the ranking and specific vote shares of the other parties, with some details still awaiting confirmation from the official final vote count.
  
  The outcome of this election has far-reaching implications for Kazakhstan's political landscape and energy policy direction. As a major energy-exporting country, Kazakhstan's political stability and policy continuity are closely watched internationally. The new constitution's shift to a unicameral parliament, with a pro-presidential party holding an absolute majority, means the executive branch will face fewer checks and balances in legislation and decision-making. President Tokayev's policy blueprint, including economic reforms, foreign policy direction, and energy export strategy, is expected to proceed more smoothly. However, opposition parties and civil society groups worry that this could weaken parliamentary oversight, intensify power concentration, and pose challenges to the country's democratization process.
  
  The official announcement of the election results will be an important indicator for observing Kazakhstan's future political trajectory.
  
  Despite exit polls showing an overwhelming victory for the "Adilet" party, concerns remain over the fairness and transparency of the election. Some international observer organizations and opposition figures argue that under the new constitutional framework, the electoral system is designed to favor the ruling party, and the lack of an independent election monitoring mechanism may not fully reflect genuine public opinion. Furthermore, although turnout was high, it remains unconfirmed whether the election was conducted in a free and fair environment. Therefore, before the official final results are released, interpretation of the exit polls should remain cautious, and one should avoid overextrapolating their implications for Kazakhstan's democratic development.
  
  It is worth noting that none of the media reports provided detailed information on opposition candidates, the campaign process, or election disputes, which constitutes a limitation of the current information available.
  
  Subsequent observation will focus on the final official vote count announced by Kazakhstan's Central Election Commission, as well as the reactions of various parties to the results. Whether international bodies, particularly organizations such as the Organization for Security and Co-operation in Europe, will send observers and release election assessment reports will also be key to measuring the credibility of this election. Additionally, after the new parliament convenes, President Tokayev's cabinet reshuffle and policy priorities—particularly in international cooperation on energy and economic reform measures—will be important aspects for observing Kazakhstan's future political situation and regional influence. The conclusion of the election is not an endpoint, but rather the starting point of a new round of power alignment and policy maneuvering.

# 哈薩克首屆單院制議會選舉登場 親總統政黨據出口民調獲壓倒性多數

> 哈薩克八月廿三日舉行新憲法下首屆單院制議會選舉，出口民調顯示親總統的「阿迪萊特」黨囊括七成以上選票，惟外界關注選制改革對權力平衡及能源政策的長遠影響。

哈薩克於八月廿三日舉行首屆依新憲法設立的單院制議會「庫魯爾泰」選舉，這是該國政治體制自雙院制改為一院制之後的首次國會改選。根據多家外媒報導，此次選舉共有七個政黨競爭一百四十五個議席，並設有百分之五的政黨門檻。選前由總統卡西姆若馬爾特·託卡葉夫主導推動的憲法改革，已於二〇二六年三月經公投透過，將原本的上下兩院架構精簡為單一議會，被外界視為進一步鞏固總統權力的關鍵步驟。此次提前舉行的選舉，亦被視為託卡葉夫政府重塑政治版圖、強化執政基礎的重要契機。
綜合多家媒體報導，本次選舉投票已於當地時間八月廿三日晚間八時結束，官方初步統計投票率達百分之七十四點一九。據俄羅斯新聞通訊社塔斯社引述的三份不同出口民調顯示，有五個政黨跨越百分之五門檻，將可進入新一屆議會。其中，新成立的親總統政黨「阿迪萊特」黨獲得超過七成選票，遙遙領先其他政黨；其餘依序為「奧伊爾」黨、「共和國」黨、「光明道路」黨以及「全國社會民主黨」。
巴基斯坦媒體《新聞報》亦報導，出口民調顯示「阿迪萊特」黨得票率介於百分之七十至七十二之間，取得壓倒性多數，該黨被認為是託卡葉夫總統為鞏固權力而催生的政治力量。
關於選舉程式與基本事實，各家媒體的報導大致吻合。土耳其安納杜魯通訊社指出，此次選舉是哈薩克在新憲法架構下的首次單院制議會選舉，七個政黨參與角逐，並設有百分之五的進入議會門檻。埃及《金字塔報》英文版則補充，合格選民人數超過一千兩百六十萬人，選舉於八月廿三日舉行，旨在選出一百四十五名議員。
在選舉結果方面，雖然各來源對「阿迪萊特」黨的領先地位並無異議，但在具體得票率數字上略有差異，塔斯社引述的民調顯示其得票率「超過百分之七十」，而《新聞報》則給出「百分之七十至七十二」的區間。此外，對於其餘政黨的排名與具體得票率，各媒體報導亦未完全一致，部分細節仍有待官方最終計票結果確認。
此次選舉結果對哈薩克的政治格局與能源政策走向具有深遠影響。作為主要的能源出口國，哈薩克的政局穩定與政策延續性備受國際關注。新憲法將議會改為一院制，並由親總統政黨掌握絕對多數，意味著行政部門在立法與決策過程中將面臨更少的制衡，託卡葉夫總統的施政藍圖，包括經濟改革、外交路線與能源出口策略，預料將更為順暢地推行。然而，反對黨與公民社會團體擔憂，此舉可能削弱議會監督功能，加劇權力集中，對該國民主化程式構成挑戰。
選舉結果的正式公佈，將是觀察哈薩克未來政治走向的重要指標。
儘管出口民調顯示「阿迪萊特」黨取得壓倒性勝利，但外界對選舉的公正性與透明度仍有疑慮。部分國際觀察組織與反對派人士認為，在新憲法架構下，選舉制度設計對執政黨有利，且缺乏獨立的選舉監督機制，可能無法充分反映真實民意。此外，本次選舉的投票率雖高，但未能確認是否在自由、公平的環境下進行。因此，在官方最終結果公佈前，對於出口民調的解讀應保持審慎，不宜過度推論其對哈薩克民主發展的影響。
值得注意的是，各媒體報導均未提供關於反對黨候選人、競選過程或選舉爭議的詳細資訊，這也構成了現階段資訊的侷限。
後續觀察重點將集中在哈薩克中央選舉委員會公佈的最終官方計票結果，以及各政黨對結果的反應。國際社會，尤其是歐洲安全與合作組織等機構，是否會派員觀察並發布選舉評估報告，亦將是衡量此次選舉公信力的關鍵。此外，新一屆議會成立後，託卡葉夫總統的內閣改組與政策優先序，特別是在能源領域的對外合作與經濟改革措施，將是觀察哈薩克未來政局與區域影響力的重要面向。選舉落幕並非終點，而是新一輪權力佈局與政策博弈的起點。
