  # Regional Security and Technological Challenges in East Asia: From AI Cyber Threats to Cross-Strait Think Tank Competition
  
  > East Asia is currently facing multiple challenges, including Chinese hackers utilizing AI technology to target universities for cyberattacks, as well as structural disparities in policy autonomy and research systems between cross-strait think tanks, both of which impact regional stability and academic development.
  
  Geopolitical and technological security issues in East Asia have recently garnered significant attention. Regarding technological security, the risk of cyberattacks targeting higher education institutions in the region has risen significantly. According to cybersecurity research organizations and media reports, Chinese hacker groups have recently been frequently utilizing artificial intelligence (AI) attack frameworks to launch targeted strikes against various universities in East Asia. Such attacks not only threaten the security of academic research data but also highlight the accelerating evolution of AI technology's application on the cyber battlefield. Meanwhile, the imbalance in research systems and policy influence between cross-strait think tanks has also become a focal point of academic discussion.
  
  Although Taiwan enjoys academic freedom, it faces a talent gap and structural dilemmas in resource allocation and systematic research on China-related issues. In contrast, the mainland Chinese think tank system concerning Taiwan demonstrates highly centralized policy leadership. The differences in autonomy and functionality between the two directly reflect the complexity of current cross-strait relations.
  
  According to information confirmed by multiple sources, cybersecurity threats have become an unavoidable challenge for educational and research institutions in East Asia. The cybersecurity media outlet "iThome" pointed out that Chinese hackers are using an AI attack framework named "Cairn" to conduct targeted attacks against several universities in East Asia, where AI agents can autonomously discover and exploit zero-day vulnerabilities in medical imaging AI software in approximately 11 minutes.
  
  At the same time, a comparative analysis of cross-strait think tanks shows that while the mainland Chinese research system concerning Taiwan is large in scale, its operations are highly subservient to Beijing's policy directives, lacking substantive policy autonomy. Conversely, while Taiwan's China research system possesses the advantage of academic freedom, it has long been plagued by fragmented resources and a talent gap, making it difficult to establish comprehensive and systematic research capacity when facing complex cross-strait situations.
  
  However, different sources provide varying descriptions of the event details and subsequent impacts. Regarding the cybersecurity incident, although media outlets have clearly identified the link between the "Cairn" framework and the medical imaging software vulnerability, there are still different interpretations regarding the specific background and tactical intent of the organization. Some perspectives view this as a precision strike against a specific technical field, while others believe the move aims to test the autonomous penetration capabilities of AI in complex network environments. In terms of think tank research, regarding the discussion on the collapse of the "Northern Hawks and Southern Doves" myth, there are disagreements over how Taiwan should establish a more systematic China research system.
  
  Some scholars advocate for the integration of private and academic resources to cope with the pressure from mainland China's massive system; another group expresses concern that overemphasizing systematic research might impose invisible framework constraints on academic freedom. How to strike a balance between resource integration and academic autonomy remains a point of contention.
  
  These developments have had a profound impact on the industries, policies, and society of the East Asian region. First, in the technology industry, the hacking of medical imaging AI software reveals cybersecurity vulnerabilities in high-tech medical devices during the digital transformation process, which will force related industries to accelerate the deployment of zero-trust architectures. Second, in the education sector, universities, as hubs for AI research and data storage, have become hotspots for cyberattacks; this not only threatens academic freedom but may also lead to the leakage of critical technologies. Furthermore, the imbalance in cross-strait think tank competition directly affects the judgment of policymakers regarding the regional situation.
  
  If Taiwan cannot establish a solid China research system, it will be difficult to present persuasive arguments in information and public opinion warfare, which poses a substantive challenge to Taiwan's international visibility and the quality of its policy decision-making.
  
  Although the aforementioned challenges are imminent, various parties also caution against over-interpreting or blowing single events out of proportion. For example, although Chinese think tanks concerning Taiwan are highly restricted by policy, there is still room for academic discussion at different levels, and they should not be viewed as entirely monolithic propaganda machines. Similarly, while Taiwan's resources are relatively fragmented in the face of cybersecurity threats, the flexibility and responsiveness of the private community and academia in cybersecurity defense remain important assets for Taiwan in regional competition. Experts suggest that all parties should remain objective when evaluating these trends, avoid simplifying complex academic and technological issues into a single narrative of opposition, and place greater emphasis on interdisciplinary cooperation and resource integration to address increasingly severe regional challenges.
  
  Subsequent observations should focus on three aspects: first, the progress of East Asian universities in patching AI vulnerabilities and whether a targeted cybersecurity defense alliance will emerge; second, whether the Taiwan government will propose specific policy support plans for the China research system to address the talent gap; and finally, changes in think tank exchanges and academic interactions across the strait, particularly whether private think tanks can function as buffers and channels for dialogue under the current political climate. The development of these issues will continue to affect the academic environment and social stability in the East Asian region.

# 東亞區域安全與科技挑戰：從AI網攻威脅到兩岸智庫競爭

> 近期東亞區域面臨多重挑戰，包括中國駭客利用AI技術鎖定大學發動網攻，以及兩岸智庫在政策自主性與研究體繫上的結構性差異，影響區域穩定與學術發展。


近期東亞區域的地緣政治與科技安全議題備受關注。在科技安全層面，針對東亞地區高等教育機構的網路攻擊風險顯著提升。根據資安研究機構與媒體報導，中國駭客組織近期頻繁運用人工智慧（AI）攻擊框架，對東亞多所大學發動針對性攻擊。此類攻擊不僅威脅學術研究資料的安全，更凸顯了AI技術在網路戰場中的應用正加速演進。與此同時，兩岸智庫在研究體系與政策影響力上的失衡，也成為學界討論的焦點。
臺灣雖享有學術自由，但在資源配置與系統性研究中國議題上，正面臨人才斷層與結構性困境，而中國大陸的涉臺智庫體系則展現出高度的政策集中領導特性，兩者在智庫自主性與功能上的差異，直接反映了當前兩岸關係的複雜性。
根據多方來源共同確認的資訊，資安威脅已成為東亞教育與研究機構不可忽視的挑戰。資安媒體《iThome》指出，中國駭客利用名為「Cairn」的AI攻擊框架，針對東亞多所大學進行鎖定攻擊，其中AI代理程式僅需約11分鐘，便能自主發現並利用醫療影像AI軟體的零時差漏洞。
與此同時，針對兩岸智庫的比較分析顯示，中國大陸的涉臺研究體系雖規模龐大，但其運作高度服從於北京的政策定調，缺乏實質的政策自主性；相對地，臺灣的中國研究體系雖然擁有學術自由的優勢，卻長期受到資源分散與人才斷層的困擾，導致在面對複雜的兩岸情勢時，難以建立全面且具備系統性的研究能量。
然而，不同來源對於事件的細節描述與後續影響存在差異。關於資安事件，雖然媒體明確指出「Cairn」框架與醫療影像軟體漏洞的關聯，但對於該組織的具體背景與戰術意圖，各界仍有不同解讀；部分觀點認為這是針對特定技術領域的精準打擊，亦有觀點認為此舉旨在測試AI在複雜網路環境下的自主滲透能力。在智庫研究方面，關於「北鷹南鴿」神話破滅的討論，各界對於臺灣應如何建立更系統性的中國研究體系存在分歧。
部分學者主張應整合民間與學界資源，以應對中國大陸龐大的體系壓力；另一派意見則擔憂，過度強調系統性研究可能會對學術自由造成無形的框架限制，如何在資源整合與學術自主間取得平衡，仍是各界爭論的焦點。
這些發展對東亞地區的產業、政策與社會產生了深遠影響。首先，在科技產業方面，醫療影像AI軟體遭駭，顯示出高科技醫療裝置在數位轉型過程中的資安防護漏洞，這將迫使相關產業加速投入零信任架構的部署。其次，在教育領域，大學作為AI研究與資料儲存的核心，已成為網路攻擊的熱點，這不僅威脅學術自由，更可能導致關鍵技術外流。再者，兩岸智庫競爭的失衡，直接影響了政策制定者對於區域局勢的判斷。
臺灣若無法建立穩固的中國研究體系，將難以在資訊戰與輿論戰中提出具備說服力的論述，這對於臺灣在國際社會的能見度與政策決策品質，均構成實質的挑戰。
儘管上述挑戰迫在眉睫，但各界也提醒不應過度解讀或將單一事件無限上綱。例如，雖然中國涉臺智庫受到政策高度限制，但其中仍存在不同層次的學術討論空間，不應將其視為完全單一的宣傳機器。同樣地，臺灣在面對資安威脅時，雖然資源相對分散，但民間社群與學界在資安防護上的靈活度與應變能力，依然是臺灣在區域競爭中的重要資產。專家建議，各界在評估這些趨勢時，應保持客觀，避免將複雜的學術與科技議題簡化為單一的對立敘事，並應更重視跨領域的合作與資源整合，以應對日益嚴峻的區域挑戰。
後續觀察重點應聚焦於三個面向：首先是東亞各大學對於AI漏洞的修補進度，以及是否會出現針對性的資安防禦聯盟；其次是臺灣政府是否會針對中國研究體系提出具體的政策支援方案，以解決人才斷層問題；最後則是兩岸在智庫交流與學術互動上的變化，特別是在當前政治氣氛下，民間智庫是否能發揮緩衝與對話的功能。這些議題的發展，將持續牽動東亞地區的學術環境與社會穩定。
