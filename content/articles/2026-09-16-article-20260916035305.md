  # Thawing China-India Relations and Taiwan’s Economic Outlook: AI-Driven Growth and Geopolitical Shifts
  
  > Oxford Economics is optimistic that improved China-India relations will boost the manufacturing sector, while the Cathay-NTU team forecasts Taiwan's 2027 economic growth rate to reach 5.1%, indicating that AI investment and regional supply chain restructuring are becoming key drivers of East Asian economic growth.
  
  In mid-September 2026, the economic outlook for East Asia and its surrounding regions presented a trend of parallel geopolitical easing and industrial technological upgrading. According to the latest analysis from Oxford Economics, the recent thaw in bilateral relations between China and India has provided an opportunity for the New Delhi authorities to relax restrictions on Chinese investment. This diplomatic progress is seen as a significant turning point for the integration of India's manufacturing sector into the global supply chain.
  
  Meanwhile, the Cathay Financial Holdings and National Taiwan University (NTU) industry-academia cooperation team released their third-quarter "Economic Climate and Financial Situation" report on September 14. The report offered an optimistic forecast for Taiwan's economic growth path over the next two years, specifically highlighting the pull effect of the artificial intelligence (AI) industrial chain on the overall economy.
  
  Multiple sources have confirmed that the leaders of China and India have recently pledged to deepen commercial ties, marking a potential end to the economic policy freeze that has persisted since the 2020 border conflict. Oxford Economics noted that since 2020, India has implemented strict screening mechanisms for investments from neighboring countries, leading to numerous obstacles for Chinese enterprises investing in India; with the improvement in bilateral relations, some of these restrictive measures are now entering the process of being lifted.
  
  Regarding Taiwan, the Cathay-NTU team confirmed that, benefiting from the rising adoption rate of AI by enterprises and the continued increase in capital expenditure by cloud service providers (CSPs), Taiwan's economic growth rate for 2026 has been revised upward to 11.6%. Furthermore, they provided their first forecast for 2027, projecting an economic growth rate of 5.1%, which demonstrates that Taiwan's core position in the East Asian technology supply chain remains solid.
  
  However, there remain differences in how various parties assess geopolitical risks. While Oxford Economics focuses on the specific benefits of thawing China-India economic and trade mechanisms for the manufacturing supply chain, the Cathay-NTU team mentioned in their report that despite Taiwan's strong export momentum, geopolitical tensions in the Middle East, such as the U.S.-Iran conflict, remain uncertain factors affecting the global economic climate. Additionally, Euronews reported that during the BRICS summit, leaders from the UAE and Iran met and expressed support for the "New Delhi Declaration," voicing concerns over the escalation of the situation in the Middle East.
  
  Information from these various sources indicates that although there are signs of a warming in regional economic cooperation, the chain reactions of global geopolitics—particularly the potential impact of the Middle East situation on energy and logistics—remain unknown variables that policymakers in all countries must face together.
  
  For the East Asian region, these developments have profound industrial implications. If the improvement in China-India relations can be implemented, it will facilitate the reallocation of global capital, and India's manufacturing sector is expected to become a new growth point for global manufacturing under deep integration with Chinese supply chains. For East Asian industrial chains that rely on regional division of labor, this is both an opportunity for market expansion and a potential trigger for competition in supply chain restructuring. As for Taiwan, the steady growth of AI investment has become a major pillar in resisting external shocks; the performance of GDP year-on-year growth exceeding 10% for three consecutive quarters proves the resilience of the high-tech industry in the face of global geopolitical turmoil.
  
  On the policy front, the extent to which the Indian government relaxes its screening of Chinese capital, along with Taiwan's continued investment in AI infrastructure, will be key factors in determining regional economic competitiveness over the next two years.
  
  Although the data shows an optimistic outlook, both academia and analytical institutions warn against over-interpreting any single indicator. The Cathay-NTU team emphasized that the "sunny" economic climate forecast for the fourth quarter is primarily based on the assumption of strong AI investment and exports; if Middle East tensions do not ease as expected, it could disrupt the rhythm of global trade. Furthermore, while the perspective from Oxford Economics is optimistic about the thaw in China-India relations, the process of rebuilding bilateral trust is long, and there remains policy execution uncertainty regarding whether past conflict records and investment restrictions can be completely eliminated. Investors and policymakers should be aware that geopolitical easing is often accompanied by complex negotiation processes and does not happen overnight; the transfer and integration of industrial chains also require time to ferment.
  
  Subsequent observations will focus on several core aspects: first, the specific implementation details of the Indian government after relaxing restrictions on Chinese capital, as well as the reactions and investment willingness of Chinese enterprises; second, the evolution of the situation in the Middle East, particularly whether the BRICS nations can achieve substantive results in regional peace mediation to effectively reduce the impact on global supply chains; and finally, whether the capital expenditure of Taiwan's AI industrial chain can maintain the expected high growth, and whether the expansion plans of global cloud service providers (CSPs) will be adjusted due to geopolitical risks. These factors will collectively shape the economic landscape of East Asia and the world in 2027, and all parties need to continue to closely monitor updates on relevant policies and market data.

# 中印關係回暖與臺灣經濟展望：AI驅動成長與地緣政治變局

> 牛津經濟研究院看好中印關係改善對製造業的提振作用，同時國泰臺大團隊預測臺灣2027年經濟成長率達5.1%，顯示AI投資與區域供應鏈重組正成為東亞經濟成長的關鍵驅動力。


2026年9月中旬，東亞及周邊區域的經濟前景呈現出地緣政治緩和與產業技術升級並行的態勢。根據牛津經濟研究院（Oxford Economics）的最新分析，中印雙邊關係的近期回暖，為新德里當局放寬對中國投資的限制提供了契機。此一外交進展被視為印度製造業融入全球供應鏈的重要轉折點。
與此同時，臺灣的經濟研究機構國泰金控與臺灣大學產學合作團隊，於9月14日發布了第三季「經濟氣候暨金融情勢」報告，針對臺灣未來兩年的經濟成長路徑提出了樂觀預測，特別強調人工智慧（AI）產業鏈對整體經濟的拉動效應。
多項來源共同確認，中印兩國領導人近期已承諾深化商業聯絡，這標誌著自2020年邊境衝突以來，兩國在經濟政策上的冰封期有望逐步解除。牛津經濟研究院指出，印度自2020年起對接壤國家的投資採取嚴格審查機制，導致中國企業在印投資面臨諸多阻礙；隨著雙邊關係改善，部分限制措施正進入解除程式。
在臺灣方面，國泰臺大團隊則確認，受惠於企業AI採用率攀升及雲端服務供應商（CSP）資本支出持續調高，臺灣2026年的經濟成長率預估值已上修至11.6%，且首次預測2027年經濟成長率將達5.1%，顯示出臺灣在東亞科技供應鏈中的核心地位持續穩固。
然而，各界對於地緣政治風險的評估仍存在差異。牛津經濟研究院聚焦於中印經貿機制解凍對製造業供應鏈的具體助益，而國泰臺大團隊則在報告中提及，儘管臺灣出口動能強勁，但美伊衝突等中東地緣政治緊張局勢，仍是影響全球經濟氣候的不確定因素。此外，Euronews報導指出，金磚國家（BRICS）峰會期間，阿聯酋與伊朗領導人進行了會晤，並支援「新德里宣言」，表達對中東局勢升級的擔憂。
這些不同來源的資訊顯示，雖然區域經濟合作有回溫跡象，但全球地緣政治的連鎖反應，特別是中東局勢對能源與物流的潛在影響，仍是各國政策制定者必須共同面對的未知變數。
對於東亞地區而言，這些發展具有深遠的產業影響。中印關係的改善若能落實，將有助於全球資本重新配置，印度製造業有望在中國供應鏈的深度整合下，成為全球製造業的新增長點；這對依賴區域分工的東亞產業鏈而言，既是市場擴張的機會，亦可能引發供應鏈重組的競爭。臺灣方面，AI投資的穩健成長已成為抵禦外部衝擊的主要支柱，連續三個季度GDP年增率超過10%的表現，證明瞭高科技產業在面對全球地緣政治動盪時的韌性。
政策面上，印度政府對中資審查的放寬程度，以及臺灣在AI基礎設施上的持續投入，將是決定未來兩年區域經濟競爭力的關鍵。
儘管資料顯示前景樂觀，但學界與分析機構均提醒，不應過度解讀單一指標。國泰臺大團隊強調，第四季經濟氣候預測為「晴」，主要建立在AI投資與出口暢旺的假設上，若中東緊張局勢未能如預期般緩解，將可能幹擾全球貿易節奏。此外，牛津經濟研究院的觀點雖看好中印關係回暖，但雙邊互信的重建過程漫長，過去的衝突紀錄與投資限制是否能徹底消除，仍存在政策執行上的不確定性。投資人與決策者需留意，地緣政治的緩和往往伴隨著複雜的談判過程，並非一蹴可幾，產業鏈的轉移與融合亦需要時間發酵。
後續觀察重點將聚焦於幾個核心面向：首先是印度政府在放寬中資限制後的具體執行細節，以及中國企業對此的反應與投資意願；其次是中東局勢的演變，特別是金磚國家在區域和平斡旋上的實質成效，是否能有效降低對全球供應鏈的衝擊；最後則是臺灣AI產業鏈的資本支出是否能維持預期的高成長，以及全球雲端服務供應商（CSP）的擴產計畫是否會因地緣政治風險而出現調整。這些因素將共同形塑2027年東亞乃至全球的經濟格局，各界需持續密切關注相關政策與市場資料的更新。
