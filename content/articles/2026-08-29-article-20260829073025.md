  # China Officially Becomes an Observer State of the Andean Community, Strengthening Strategic Layout in South America
  
  > China was officially approved as an observer state of the Andean Community at the end of September 2025. This move marks Beijing's deeper integration into South American multilateral mechanisms. Analysts suggest that China intends to strengthen its control over regional strategic resources and deepen political and economic dialogue with Andean nations.
  
  The Andean Community (CAN), as an important organization for regional economic integration in Latin America, has long played a key role in the political and trade integration of South America. According to reports from the Central News Agency and related media, the Andean Community held a regular meeting of the Council of Foreign Ministers at the end of September 2025, during which it formally passed a resolution to accept China as an observer state of the organization. This decision symbolizes a major shift in China's regional diplomatic strategy in Latin America, moving from past bilateral trade cooperation toward actively infiltrating regional institutional frameworks, attempting to influence the policy direction of South America on a more macro level.
  
  The member states of the Andean Community include Bolivia, Colombia, Ecuador, and Peru; these countries hold a central position in the geography and resource distribution of South America.
  
  According to comprehensive media reports, the date China officially obtained observer status was September 30, 2025. Although the "observer" status does not carry voting or decision-making power in the operations of international organizations, this status grants China the legitimate right to attend community meetings and provides a channel to access internal official information and policy trends of the organization. Through this mechanism, China has successfully established closer political and economic dialogue channels with countries in the Andean region. Multiple media outlets agree that this is a key step in Beijing's strategic layout in South America, aimed at expanding its influence in the region through multilateral mechanisms and paving the way for future trade agreements or infrastructure cooperation.
  
  Although there is a high degree of consensus among media regarding the strategic intent behind China joining the Andean Community, there are differing interpretations and details yet to be confirmed regarding its specific impact. Some media emphasize that the core motivation for China's move lies in a long-term layout for South America's abundant mineral resources, particularly the acquisition of lithium and rare earth resources, which highly overlaps with China's development needs in the global electric vehicle supply chain and artificial intelligence industry. However, some analyses point out that the actual authority of observer status is limited, and the extent to which China can influence the decision-making direction of the community in the future still depends on the attitude of the organization's member states toward the intervention of Chinese capital, as well as the balancing power of the United States in the region.
  
  There have been no disclosures regarding specific Chinese investment projects or technology transfers at this time, and the content of such cooperation remains to be observed.
  
  This diplomatic progress has had a direct impact on the industrial and policy environment of South America. Andean countries have long faced the need for economic transformation and infrastructure construction; China's intervention is seen as an important source of capital and technology, but it has also sparked discussions about resource dependence and environmental impact. For South America, establishing a multilateral dialogue mechanism with China means that the region will face more complex choices in international geopolitics. How to strike a balance between Chinese capital injection and traditional Western trade partners has become a major challenge for governments. Furthermore, China's strategic layout in the region may also change the priority of trade agreements within South America, affecting industrial competitiveness within the region.
  
  Regarding China's move to join the Andean Community, academia and public opinion hold different views. Some perspectives suggest that this is part of China's "Global South" diplomatic policy, aimed at enhancing the voice of developing countries in the international system; however, other commentaries warn against over-interpreting the immediate effects of observer status, as the operations of international organizations are often limited by the conflicting interests of member states. Even with observer status, China may not easily sway the collective will of the community.
  
  In addition, whether China's expansion in South America will trigger a direct conflict with the United States is also a focus of attention, but there is currently a lack of concrete evidence of military or geopolitical confrontation, and over-politicizing this event may ignore the essence of regional economic cooperation.
  
  Subsequent observation will focus on whether China and the Andean Community will sign specific trade memoranda or launch large-scale infrastructure cooperation projects within the next year. Furthermore, how observer status is translated into actual economic benefits, and whether China will use this platform to promote RMB settlement mechanisms in South America, will be key indicators for evaluating the effectiveness of this strategic layout. Media and observers will continue to track the reactions of Andean Community member states to China's level of participation, particularly the policy adjustments of countries like Peru and Colombia when facing Chinese capital, and whether the organization will further open up to other major powers for observer status to maintain the balance of power within the region.

# 中國正式成為安地斯共同體觀察員國 強化南美戰略佈局

> 中國於2025年9月底正式獲準成為安地斯共同體觀察員國。此舉標誌北京深入南美多邊機制，各界分析認為中國意在強化對區域戰略資源的掌控，並深化與安地斯國家間的政治經濟對話。


安地斯共同體（Andean Community, CAN）作為拉丁美洲區域經濟一體化的重要組織，長期以來在南美洲的政治與貿易整合中扮演關鍵角色。根據中央社及相關媒體報導，安地斯共同體於2025年9月底召開外長理事會例行會議，會中正式透過決議，接納中國成為該組織的觀察員國。此一決策象徵中國在拉丁美洲的區域外交策略出現重大轉折，從過去的雙邊貿易合作，轉向積極滲透區域性制度架構，試圖在更宏觀的層面上影響南美洲的政策走向。
安地斯共同體成員國包括玻利維亞、哥倫比亞、厄瓜多與秘魯，這些國家在南美洲的地理與資源分佈中具備核心地位。
綜合各方媒體報導，中國正式取得觀察員資格的時間點為2025年9月30日。雖然「觀察員」身份在國際組織運作中並不具備投票權或決策權，但這項身份賦予了中國出席共同體會議的合法權利，並能藉此管道獲取組織內部的官方資訊與政策動向。中國透過此機制，成功建立起與安地斯地區國家間更為緊密的政治經濟對話渠道。多家媒體一致認為，此舉是北京在南美洲戰略佈局的關鍵一步，旨在透過多邊機制擴大其在該地區的影響力，並為未來進一步的貿易協定或基礎設施合作鋪路。
儘管各媒體對於中國加入安地斯共同體的戰略意圖有高度共識，但在具體影響層面上存在不同解讀與尚待確認的細節。部分媒體強調，中國此舉的核心動機在於對南美洲豐富礦產資源的長期佈局，特別是針對鋰礦與稀土資源的取得，這與中國在全球電動車供應鏈及人工智慧產業的發展需求高度重合。然而，也有分析指出，觀察員身份的實際許可權有限，中國未來能多大程度影響共同體的決策方向，仍取決於該組織內部成員國對於中國資本介入的態度，以及美國在該地區的制衡力道。
目前尚未有關於中國具體投資專案或技術轉移的細節披露，相關合作內容仍有待後續觀察。
此項外交進展對南美洲的產業與政策環境產生了直接影響。安地斯地區國家長期面臨經濟轉型與基礎設施建設的需求，中國的介入被視為重要的資金與技術來源，但也引發了關於資源依賴與環境影響的討論。對於南美洲而言，與中國建立多邊對話機制，意味著該地區在國際地緣政治中將面臨更為複雜的選擇，如何在中國的資金挹注與傳統的西方貿易夥伴之間取得平衡，成為各國政府面臨的重大挑戰。此外，中國在該地區的戰略佈局，亦可能改變南美洲內部的貿易協定優先順序，影響區域內的產業競爭力。
針對中國加入安地斯共同體的舉動，學界與輿論持有不同觀點。部分觀點認為，這是中國推動「全球南方」外交政策的一環，旨在提升發展中國家在國際體系中的話語權；然而，也有評論提醒，不應過度解讀觀察員身份的即時效應，因為國際組織的運作往往受到成員國利益衝突的限制，中國即便擁有觀察員身份，也未必能輕易左右共同體的集體意志。
此外，中國在南美洲的擴張是否會引發與美國的直接衝突，亦是各界關注的焦點，但目前缺乏具體的軍事或地緣政治對抗證據，過度將此事件政治化可能忽略了區域經濟合作的本質。
後續觀察重點將聚焦於中國與安地斯共同體在未來一年內是否會簽署具體的貿易備忘錄，或啟動大型基礎設施合作專案。此外，觀察員身份如何轉化為實際的經濟利益，以及中國是否會利用此平臺推動人民幣在南美洲的結算機制，將是評估此戰略佈局成效的關鍵指標。媒體與觀察家將持續追蹤安地斯共同體內部成員國對於中國參與程度的反應，特別是秘魯與哥倫比亞等國在面對中國資本時的政策調整，以及該組織是否會進一步開放其他大國參與觀察，以維持區域內部的權力平衡。
