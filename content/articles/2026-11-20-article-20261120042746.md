  # US Energy Policy Shift Impacts the Globe: Opportunities and Challenges for Southeast Asia's Offshore Wind Industry
  
  > Recent policy shifts by the White House have plunged domestic offshore wind projects in the U.S. into chaos. Meanwhile, Southeast Asia is actively embracing offshore wind development, attempting to fill the global renewable energy investment gap while remaining vigilant against the threat of regional extremism potentially triggered by instability in Syria.
  
  The offshore wind industry is currently facing a global reorganization. According to reports from the Associated Press (AP) and Yahoo News, U.S. President Trump has recently adopted a hardline stance, promoting policies opposed to renewable energy. This move has directly led to a major shift in White House policy, causing severe turmoil for multi-billion-dollar offshore wind projects within the United States. In contrast to the shrinking U.S. market, Southeast Asia is demonstrating a completely different development trend, becoming a bright spot in the global offshore wind industry.
  
  Southeast Asian nations, such as Vietnam, are actively adjusting their energy policies, attempting to fill the global renewable energy investment gap created by U.S. policy changes by attracting international capital and technology. This is not only a critical opportunity for the region's energy transition but also reflects the accelerating shift of the global green energy supply chain toward Southeast Asia.
  
  While embracing green energy development, Southeast Asia also faces complex geopolitical and security challenges. Channel News Asia (CNA) analysis points out that as the political situation in Syria remains volatile, the risk of the spread of extremism cannot be underestimated. Southeast Asia experienced the siege of Marawi City in the Philippines in 2017, when Islamic State (IS)-affiliated militants caused over a thousand deaths, marking the most severe extremist attack in the region in recent years.
  
  Security departments in various countries are currently closely monitoring whether the conflict in Syria will lead to a resurgence of extremist ideology. They have called on ASEAN member states to remain highly vigilant to prevent extremist forces from staging a comeback in the region, which could impact regional economic development and the security of energy infrastructure.
  
  Currently, there are differences in the level of optimism regarding Southeast Asia's offshore wind development and the perception of security risks among various sources. Yahoo News reports focus on the region's industrial potential as a "safe haven" for renewable energy, emphasizing capital flow opportunities following the policy shift. However, Channel News Asia warns that the instability of the regional security environment may pose concerns for long-term, large-scale infrastructure investments. Furthermore, there is currently a lack of quantitative data to support the extent of the impact of U.S. policy shifts on specific Southeast Asian supply chains. It remains to be seen how governments will implement green energy subsidies and supporting regulations to cope with the competitive pressure brought about by the reorganization of global supply chains.
  
  For Southeast Asian countries, the development of offshore wind is not only about energy autonomy but also involves the strategic layout of the regional economy. As U.S. wind power projects stall, technology and talent in the global supply chain may flow to Southeast Asia, which is a major boon for industrial upgrading in countries like Vietnam. However, this also requires Southeast Asian nations to invest more resources in infrastructure construction, grid stability, and regional security cooperation. If the regional power market cannot be effectively integrated, or if the potential threats posed by extremism cannot be properly addressed, the industrial dividends of offshore wind may be difficult to translate into long-term economic growth momentum.
  
  It is worth noting that the shift in U.S. energy policy should not be over-interpreted as the end of the global renewable energy industry. Although domestic wind power projects in the U.S. have suffered setbacks, the proactive attitude of Southeast Asia demonstrates the resilience of market demand. Furthermore, security issues and energy transition are not entirely contradictory, but governments must incorporate geopolitical risk assessments when promoting green energy transitions. For investors and policymakers, how to balance regional security and stability while pursuing energy transition will be a core test for Southeast Asian countries in the coming years. Viewing industrial relocation with excessive optimism while ignoring the risk of the spread of extremism could lead to a deterioration of the subsequent investment environment.
  
  Follow-up observations should focus on two major aspects: First, how Southeast Asian countries can establish a transnational energy internet and security intelligence sharing mechanism through the ASEAN framework to meet the infrastructure protection needs following industrial expansion; second, observing the specific investment scale and technology transfer progress of the global offshore wind supply chain (especially turbine manufacturing and maritime engineering) in Southeast Asia following U.S. policy changes. As the global energy landscape is reshaped, whether Southeast Asia can successfully connect with international green energy capital and maintain stability in a volatile geopolitical environment will be a key indicator of the region's competitiveness over the next decade.

# 美國能源政策轉向衝擊全球，東南亞離岸風電產業迎來機遇與挑戰

> 美國白宮近期政策轉向，導致國內離岸風電計畫陷入混亂。與此同時，東南亞地區正積極擁抱離岸風電發展，試圖填補全球再生能源投資缺口，並警惕敘利亞動盪可能引發的區域極端主義威脅。


離岸風電產業近期正面臨全球性的版圖重組。根據美聯社（AP）與雅虎新聞報導，美國總統川普近期採取強硬立場，推動反對再生能源政策，此舉直接導致白宮政策出現重大轉折，使得美國境內價值數十億美元的離岸風電計畫陷入嚴重動盪。相較於美國市場的萎縮，東南亞地區卻展現出截然不同的發展態勢，成為全球離岸風電產業的亮點。
越南等東南亞國家正積極調整能源政策，試圖透過吸引國際資本與技術，填補因美國政策變化而產生的全球再生能源投資缺口，這不僅是該地區能源轉型的關鍵契機，也反映出全球綠能產業鏈正加速向東南亞轉移。
東南亞地區在擁抱綠能發展的同時，亦面臨複雜的地緣政治與安全挑戰。亞洲新聞臺（CNA）分析指出，隨著敘利亞政治局勢持續動盪，極端主義擴散的風險不容小覷。東南亞曾於2017年經歷菲律賓馬拉威市（Marawi City）圍城戰，當時伊斯蘭國（IS）相關武裝分子造成超過一千人死亡，成為該地區近年來最嚴重的極端主義攻擊事件。
各國安全部門目前正密切關注敘利亞衝突是否會導致極端思想回流，並呼籲東協成員國必須保持高度警惕，防止極端主義勢力在區域內死灰復燃，進而影響區域內的經濟發展與能源基礎設施安全。
目前各來源對於東南亞發展離岸風電的樂觀程度與面臨的安全風險認知存在差異。雅虎新聞報導側重於該地區作為再生能源「避風港」的產業潛力，強調政策轉向後的資本流動機會；然而，亞洲新聞臺則提醒，區域安全環境的不穩定性可能對長期大型基礎設施投資構成隱憂。此外，關於美國政策轉向對東南亞具體供應鏈的影響程度，目前仍缺乏量化資料支援，尚待後續觀察各國政府如何具體落實綠能補貼與配套法規，以應對全球供應鏈重組帶來的競爭壓力。
對於東南亞國家而言，離岸風電的發展不僅關乎能源自主，更涉及區域經濟的戰略佈局。隨著美國風電計畫停滯，全球供應鏈中的技術與人才可能流向東南亞，這對越南等國的產業升級是一大利多。然而，這也要求東南亞各國必須在基礎設施建設、電網穩定性以及區域安全合作上投入更多資源。若無法有效整合區域內的電力市場，或無法妥善處理極端主義帶來的潛在威脅，離岸風電的產業紅利可能難以轉化為長期的經濟成長動能。
值得注意的是，不應將美國的能源政策轉向過度解讀為全球再生能源產業的終結。雖然美國國內風電計畫受挫，但東南亞的積極態度顯示了市場需求的韌性。此外，安全議題與能源轉型並非完全對立，但各國政府在推動綠色能源轉型時，必須納入地緣政治風險評估。對於投資者與政策制定者而言，如何在追求能源轉型的同時，兼顧區域安全穩定，將是未來幾年東南亞國家面臨的核心考驗。過度樂觀地看待產業轉移，而忽視極端主義擴散的風險，恐將導致後續的投資環境惡化。
後續觀察重點應聚焦於兩大面向：首先是東南亞各國如何透過東協（ASEAN）架構，建立跨國能源網際網路與安全情報共享機制，以應對產業擴張後的基礎設施防護需求；其次是觀察美國政策變動後，全球離岸風電供應鏈（特別是渦輪機製造與海事工程）在東南亞的具體投資規模與技術轉移進度。隨著全球能源版圖的重塑，東南亞能否成功接軌國際綠能資本，並在動盪的地緣政治環境中保持穩定，將是決定該地區未來十年競爭力的關鍵指標。
