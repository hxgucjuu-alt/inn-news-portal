  # The Wave of International Event Boycotts and Regional Development Observations Amidst Global Public Health and Geopolitics
  
  > As the humanitarian crisis triggered by the war in Gaza continues, the Eurovision Song Contest faces boycotts from multiple nations. Meanwhile, the tourism industry in Oceania and economic competition in the Asia-Pacific region are becoming key indicators for observation under the lens of global public issues.
  
  The Eurovision Song Contest, as the world's largest televised music event, has recently fallen into a severe public opinion storm involving geopolitical controversy and humanitarian concerns regarding public health, due to questions surrounding Israel's eligibility to participate. According to a public statement by an executive at the Spanish public broadcaster on September 22, 2026, the organization supports the boycott initiated in response to Israel's participation. This move is not merely a cultural statement by a single nation, but reflects the international community's deep concern over the ongoing humanitarian crisis in the Gaza region.
  
  In fact, as early as this year's event held in Austria, Spain had already taken joint withdrawal actions with countries such as the Netherlands, Iceland, Ireland, and Slovenia to protest the severe casualties and humanitarian catastrophe caused by military operations in the area.
  
  Multiple media sources have confirmed that Ireland's Raidió Teilifís Éireann (RTE) and Dutch representatives have officially announced that they will continue to boycott the Eurovision Song Contest scheduled to be held in Bulgaria in 2027. The core demand behind this transnational boycott is to protest the ongoing casualties in the Gaza region. According to data provided by the Gaza Health Ministry, the humanitarian crisis in the area has reached alarming levels since 2025, making cultural exchange activities appear highly challenged under the shadow of war. The collective action of public broadcasting institutions in various countries shows that public health and peace issues have become key variables affecting the willingness to host and participate in large-scale international events.
  
  Although broadcasting institutions in various countries are tending toward a unified stance on the boycott, there are still subtle differences in information regarding the subsequent impact and specific demands of the boycott. Some reports indicate that the boycott could lead to more countries following suit, thereby threatening the representativeness and integrity of the Eurovision Song Contest as a "global music event." However, information regarding how the Bulgarian hosts will respond to such a large-scale boycott, and whether the European Broadcasting Union (EBU) will adjust participation eligibility, remains in the category of unconfirmed information.
  
  Furthermore, while the data provided by the Gaza Health Ministry is widely cited internationally, there is still room for interpretation regarding its statistical methods and data integrity across different geopolitical camps, which is a key point that must be maintained with neutrality when observing this event.
  
  Turning to Oceania and the Asia-Pacific region, the focus of public issues is concentrated on regional industrial development and economic competition. With the redrawing of the global tourism map, the tourism industries of Australia, New Zealand, and Pacific Island nations are actively seeking recovery and transformation, which forms a sharp contrast to the political turmoil triggered by the European event. Regarding economic policy, Hong Kong Financial Secretary Paul Chan recently emphasized that Hong Kong will focus on strengthening its own core advantages rather than engaging in vicious competition with Singapore, reflecting a strategic shift in the Asia-Pacific region's battle for talent and capital.
  
  This policy logic of "strengthening oneself," combined with the development path emphasized by Oceania countries in promoting the tourism industry—which highlights natural geographical wisdom and environmental symbiosis—together constitute the diverse landscape of current global public policy.
  
  From the perspective of public health and social development, one should not over-interpret the long-term destructive nature of a single boycott action on international relations. Although the boycott wave of the Eurovision Song Contest reflects the urgency of humanitarian concerns, cultural exchange and economic cooperation remain the cornerstones of international society. Taking tourism development in Oceania as an example, whether it is the tranquil mountain scenery and glacial environment of New Zealand's South Island or the diverse tourism resources of Australia, the core of their development is built on the foundation of symbiosis with the natural environment.
  
  This governance wisdom of following the natural course coincides with the logic of the Danjiang Bridge's engineering design, which preserves shipping lanes and ecological space, showing that when facing global challenges, adapting to the environment and strengthening core advantages have become common strategies for regions to cope with changing situations.
  
  Subsequent observation should focus on: First, whether the boycott of the Eurovision Song Contest will spread to other international sports or cultural events, thereby triggering a broader global boycott effect; second, whether the resolution process of the humanitarian crisis in the Gaza region will become a key variable affecting international public opinion and the decisions of policymakers; third, how Oceania countries can balance the dual needs of environmental protection and economic growth in the process of promoting regional tourism and industrial upgrading. These issues will continue to influence the direction of global public policy and test the international community's ability to maintain a dynamic balance between conflict and symbiosis.

# 全球公共衛生與地緣政治下的國際賽事抵制浪潮與區域發展觀察

> 隨著加薩戰爭引發的人道危機持續，歐洲歌唱大賽面臨多國抵制。同時，大洋洲旅遊產業與亞太區域經濟競爭，正成為全球公共議題下的重要觀察指標。


歐洲歌唱大賽（Eurovision）作為全球規模最大的電視直播音樂盛事，近期因以色列的參賽資格問題，陷入嚴重的地緣政治爭議與公共衛生人道關懷的輿論風暴。根據西班牙公共廣播公司主管於2026年9月22日的公開表態，該機構支援因以色列參賽而發起的抵制行動。此舉不僅是單一國家的文化表態，更反映了國際社會對於加薩地區持續性人道危機的深切關注。
事實上，早在今年於奧地利舉行的賽事中，西班牙便已與荷蘭、冰島、愛爾蘭及斯洛維尼亞等國採取共同退出行動，以抗議以色列在當地軍事行動所引發的嚴重傷亡與人道災難。
多個媒體來源共同確認，愛爾蘭廣播電視（RTE）與荷蘭方面已正式宣佈，將持續抵制預計於2027年在保加利亞舉行的歐洲歌唱大賽。這一跨國抵制行動的背後，核心訴求在於對加薩地區持續不斷的人員死傷表示抗議。根據加薩衛生部提供的資料顯示，自2025年以來，當地的人道危機已達到駭人聽聞的程度，這使得文化交流活動在戰爭陰影下顯得備受挑戰。各國公廣機構的集體行動，顯示出公共衛生與和平議題已成為影響國際大型活動舉辦與參與意願的關鍵變數。
儘管各國廣播機構在抵制立場上趨於一致，但針對抵制行動的後續影響與具體訴求，各方資訊仍存在細微差異。部分報導指出，抵制行動可能導致更多國家跟進，進而威脅到歐洲歌唱大賽作為「全球性音樂盛事」的代表性與完整性。然而，目前關於保加利亞主辦方如何應對此類大規模抵制，以及歐洲廣播聯盟（EBU）是否會針對參賽資格進行調整，仍屬於尚待確認的資訊範疇。
此外，加薩衛生部所提供的資料在國際間雖被廣泛引用，但其統計方法與資料完整性在不同地緣政治陣營中，仍存在解讀空間，這也是觀察此事件時必須保持中立的關鍵點。
轉向大洋洲與亞太區域，公共議題的焦點則集中在區域產業發展與經濟競爭上。隨著全球旅遊版圖的重新劃分，澳洲、紐西蘭及太平洋島國的旅遊產業正積極尋求復甦與轉型，這與歐洲賽事所引發的政治動盪形成鮮明對比。在經濟政策方面，香港財政司長陳茂波近期強調，香港將專注於強化自身核心優勢，而非與新加坡進行惡性競爭，這反映了亞太區域在人才與資本爭奪戰中的戰略轉向。
這種「強化自身」的政策邏輯，與大洋洲國家在推動觀光產業時，強調自然地理智慧與環境共生的發展路徑，共同構成了當前全球公共政策的多元面貌。
從公共衛生與社會發展的角度觀察，不應過度解讀單一抵制行動對國際關係的長期破壞性。雖然歐洲歌唱大賽的抵制浪潮反映了人道關懷的迫切性，但文化交流與經濟合作仍是國際社會運作的基石。以大洋洲的旅遊發展為例，無論是紐西蘭南島的寧靜山景與冰川環境，還是澳洲多元的旅遊資源，其發展的核心均建立在與自然環境共生的基礎上。
這種順勢而為的治理智慧，與淡江大橋在工程設計上保留航道與生態空間的邏輯不謀而合，顯示出在面臨全球性挑戰時，順應環境與強化核心優勢，已成為各地區應對變局的共同策略。
後續觀察重點應聚焦於：第一，歐洲歌唱大賽的抵制行動是否會擴散至其他國際體育或文化賽事，進而引發更廣泛的全球性抵制效應；第二，加薩地區的人道危機解決程式，是否會成為影響國際公眾輿論與政策制定者決策的關鍵變數；第三，大洋洲國家在推動區域旅遊與產業升級的過程中，如何平衡環境保護與經濟成長的雙重需求。這些議題將持續影響全球公共政策的走向，並考驗國際社會在衝突與共生之間的動態平衡能力。
