  # Global Digital Governance Challenges: AI Security Threats and the Landscape of International Cooperation Amidst Geopolitics
  
  > Global technological development faces severe challenges, with "Godfather of AI" Geoffrey Hinton warning of the risk of human extinction, the EU pushing for transnational cooperation, and Taiwan’s Ministry of Digital Affairs actively strengthening Taiwan-U.S. digital resilience.
  
  Against the backdrop of exponential growth in artificial intelligence (AI) technology, global politics and international governance are undergoing drastic changes. Recently, Europe and Taiwan have taken key actions in the fields of AI safety, transnational foreign policy, and digital resilience. European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen delivered a policy speech outlining a new vision for EU international cooperation, including seeking to make Canada the EU's first "associate member." Meanwhile, Taiwan's Minister of Digital Affairs, Huang Yen-Chih, led a delegation to the United States for in-depth dialogues with the U.S. government and tech giants, aimed at addressing increasingly complex cybersecurity and geopolitical risks.
  
  These events collectively reflect the efforts of the global democratic camp to rebuild lines of defense for digital governance amidst the tech race and geopolitical turmoil.
  
  According to multiple public reports and official information, global technological security has become a core agenda for governments worldwide. Taiwan's Minister of Digital Affairs, Huang Yen-Chih, led a delegation to Washington D.C. to engage with U.S. government departments, think tanks, and tech giants such as Meta, OpenAI, and Amazon. The focus covered AI governance, cybersecurity, and communication resilience, demonstrating that Taiwan is actively aligning its own experience with U.S. policies. In Europe, European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen proposed the concept of incorporating Canada into the EU system; while restricted by EU treaty provisions from becoming a formal member, the move demonstrates the EU's intention to expand transatlantic cooperation.
  
  Furthermore, the incident involving a train attack in Ukraine, which affected European officials including former British Prime Minister Boris Johnson, highlights the direct threat posed by Eastern European geopolitical conflicts to European security officials.
  
  Although governments are actively seeking cooperation, there remains a significant gap between academia and the political sphere regarding the assessment of AI technology risks. Geoffrey Hinton, known as the "Godfather of AI," publicly warned in an interview with the BBC that under a capital-driven arms race, the speed of AI evolution has outpaced the development of safety mechanisms, predicting a greater than 10% chance of AI causing human extinction within the next decade. This view stands in stark contrast to the "digital resilience" and "industrial cooperation" currently promoted by governments.
  
  There is currently no consensus on whether AI should be strictly regulated by the government or driven by market mechanisms, which has become the greatest uncertainty in future international governance.
  
  These technological and political trends have had concrete impacts on the global digital industry and geopolitics. For Taiwan, digital resilience cooperation with the U.S. is not only an enhancement of industrial competitiveness but also a vital line of defense for national security. For Europe, von der Leyen's initiative reflects the EU's attempt to strengthen regional influence by deepening ties with non-EU countries in the face of global competition. However, the threat posed to European officials by the Russia-Ukraine war and the potential risks of out-of-control AI technology demonstrate that international governance must not only face the opportunities of digital transformation but also address the complex challenges where physical conflicts and virtual risks intertwine. Policymakers are currently attempting to strike a balance between promoting innovation and ensuring security.
  
  Regarding the aforementioned issues, the outside world should not over-interpret the effectiveness of any single policy. For example, the cooperation concept between the EU and Canada still faces legal restrictions under EU treaties, and its practical execution details and political feasibility remain subject to subsequent negotiations. While Hinton's warning about an AI apocalypse is based on his professional background, it remains a prediction, and there are differing voices in academia regarding the assessment of AI risks. Furthermore, although digital cooperation between Taiwan and the U.S. has entered a stage of substantive dialogue, how these consensuses will be translated into specific cybersecurity standards and industrial policies remains dependent on the progress of future bilateral agreements.
  
  As countries pursue digital sovereignty, avoiding the trap of protectionism and technological blockades will be a key constraint for future international governance.
  
  Looking ahead, the focus will be on three aspects: First, whether the EU can break through treaty limitations to reach a substantive associate membership model with Canada, thereby changing Europe's international governance structure. Second, whether Taiwan's Ministry of Digital Affairs can implement relevant technical cooperation into domestic cybersecurity infrastructure after its visit to the U.S. Finally, whether governments worldwide will formulate stricter international regulatory treaties in response to the extreme AI risks proposed by Hinton. These developments will determine the direction of global digital governance over the next decade and the collective defense capabilities of nations in responding to the risks of runaway technology.

# 全球數位治理挑戰：AI 安全威脅與地緣政治下的國際合作佈局

> 全球科技發展面臨嚴峻挑戰，AI 教父辛頓示警人類滅絕風險，歐盟推動跨國合作，臺灣數位發展部則積極強化臺美數位韌性。


在人工智慧（AI）技術呈指數級成長的背景下，全球政治與國際治理正經歷劇烈變動。近期，歐洲與臺灣分別在人工智慧安全、跨國外交政策及數位韌性領域採取關鍵行動。歐洲聯盟執行委員會主席馮德萊恩（Ursula von der Leyen）於近期發表政策演說，強調歐盟在國際合作上的新願景，包括尋求加拿大成為歐盟首個「準會員國」；與此同時，臺灣數位發展部部長林宜敬率團訪美，與美國政府及科技巨頭展開深度對話，旨在應對日益複雜的網路安全與地緣政治風險。
這些事件共同反映出全球民主陣營正試圖在科技競賽與地緣政治動盪中，重新建立數位治理的防線。
根據多項公開報導與官方資訊確認，全球科技安全已成為各國政府的核心議程。臺灣數位發展部部長林宜敬率團訪問華盛頓特區，與美國政府部門、智庫及 Meta、OpenAI、Amazon 等科技巨頭進行交流，重點涵蓋 AI 治理、資安防護及通訊韌性，顯示臺灣正積極將自身經驗與美方政策對接。在歐洲方面，歐盟委員會主席馮德萊恩提出將加拿大納入歐盟體系的構想，雖受限於歐盟條約規定無法成為正式成員，但此舉展現了歐盟擴大跨大西洋合作的意圖。
此外，前英國首相強生（Boris Johnson）等歐洲官員在烏克蘭遭遇鐵路攻擊事件，亦凸顯了東歐地緣政治衝突對歐洲安全官員造成的直接威脅。
儘管各國政府積極尋求合作，但在 AI 技術風險的評估上，學界與政界仍存在顯著差異。被譽為「AI 教父」的傑佛瑞·辛頓（Geoffrey Hinton）在接受英國廣播公司（BBC）訪談時公開示警，指出在資本驅動的軍備競賽下，AI 的演進速度已超越安全機制的發展，預測未來十年內 AI 導致人類滅絕的機率超過 10%。此觀點與目前各國政府推動的「數位韌性」與「產業合作」形成強烈對比。
各界對於 AI 是否應由政府嚴格監管，或應由市場機制主導，目前尚未達成共識，這也成為後續國際治理中最大的不確定性因素。
這些科技與政治動向對全球數位產業及地緣政治產生了具體影響。對於臺灣而言，與美方的數位韌性合作不僅是產業競爭力的提升，更是國家安全的重要防線；對於歐洲而言，馮德萊恩的倡議反映了歐盟在面對全球競爭時，試圖透過深化與非歐盟國家的連結來強化區域影響力。然而，俄烏戰爭對歐洲官員的威脅，以及 AI 技術失控的潛在風險，顯示出國際治理不僅要面對數位轉型的機遇，更必須處理實體衝突與虛擬風險交織的複合性挑戰。各國政策制定者目前正試圖在促進創新與保障安全之間取得平衡。
針對上述議題，外界不應過度解讀單一政策的效力。例如，歐盟與加拿大的合作構想仍面臨歐盟條約的法律限制，其實際執行細節與政治可行性尚待後續談判；而辛頓對於 AI 末日的警告，雖基於其專業背景，但仍屬於預測性質，學界對 AI 風險的評估亦有不同聲音。此外，臺灣與美國的數位合作雖已進入實質對話階段，但如何將這些共識轉化為具體的資安防護標準與產業政策，仍需視後續雙邊協議的推動進度而定。
各國在追求數位主權的同時，如何避免陷入保護主義與技術封鎖，將是未來國際治理的關鍵限制。
展望未來，觀察重點將聚焦於以下三個面向：首先，歐盟是否能突破條約限制，與加拿大達成實質性的準會員合作模式，進而改變歐洲的國際治理架構；其次，臺灣數位發展部在訪美後，是否能將相關技術合作落實於國內的資安基礎建設中；最後，全球各國政府是否會針對辛頓所提出的 AI 極端風險，制定更為嚴格的國際監管條約。這些發展將決定未來十年全球數位治理的走向，以及各國在應對科技失控風險時的集體防禦能力。
