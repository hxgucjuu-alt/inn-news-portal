  # Indonesian Forest Fires Trigger Transboundary Haze, El Niño Exacerbates Climate Crisis in Southeast Asia
  
  > Due to prolonged drought caused by the El Niño phenomenon, Indonesia has recently experienced frequent forest and peatland fires. The resulting toxic smoke has led to transboundary haze spreading to neighboring countries, posing severe challenges to public health and environmental governance for ASEAN nations.
  
  Forest and peatland fires in Indonesia have recently shown a worsening trend. According to multiple reports, driven by the abnormal drought caused by the El Niño phenomenon, forest fires within Indonesia have resulted in the destruction of tens of thousands of hectares of land. This climate disaster has not only caused environmental damage within Indonesia but, due to atmospheric circulation, has also carried toxic smoke and haze to other parts of Southeast Asia. As a regional intergovernmental forum, the Association of Southeast Asian Nations (ASEAN) has a core mission that includes regional economic development and peaceful coexistence; however, in the face of such transnational environmental crises, member states are under unprecedented pressure regarding coordinated responses and compliance with international norms.
  
  According to various media reports, the Indonesian government has taken concrete actions to address the fires, including strengthening ground and aerial firefighting operations. Currently, Indonesian authorities are mobilizing significant manpower and equipment in an attempt to control the fires spreading across the Kalimantan and Sumatra regions. This firefighting effort is not only aimed at protecting Indonesia's domestic forest resources but also at alleviating the regional public health crisis triggered by deteriorating air quality. Citizens across Southeast Asia have generally expressed strong dissatisfaction and frustration regarding the spread of the haze, indicating that the issue has shifted from a purely environmental concern to a major social event affecting regional public sentiment and international relations.
  
  Regarding the specific scope and severity of the fires, different sources have provided complementary but detailed information. AP News emphasized that the prolonged dry season caused by El Niño is the primary cause of the fires and noted that the affected area has reached tens of thousands of hectares. The BBC focused on the public's angry reaction to the haze on social media, highlighting that the toxic smog has crossed national borders, affecting the entire Southeast Asian region.
  
  Furthermore, while the World Health Organization (WHO) has not issued emergency medical data specifically regarding these fires, the "Dili Declaration," recently adopted at the WHO South-East Asia Region Health Ministers' Meeting, aims to promote equity in specialized care. This is particularly critical against the backdrop of frequent regional public health emergencies, suggesting a focus on the allocation of medical resources and the response to chronic health threats within the region.
  
  The impact of this transboundary haze on Southeast Asia is multifaceted. First, at the economic level, the destruction of farmland and the disruption of logistics caused by the fires have directly impacted related industrial chains. Second, at the public health level, deteriorating air quality poses a significant threat to patients with respiratory diseases, increasing the burden on medical systems. When facing such transboundary environmental issues, ASEAN countries must strike a balance between economic development and international norms. The efficiency of Indonesia's firefighting efforts concerns not only its internal governance but also directly determines the air quality and public health of neighboring countries (such as Singapore and Malaysia), making environmental governance an indispensable part of ASEAN regional cooperation.
  
  However, there are differing views on the effectiveness of governance. Some observers believe that relying solely on Indonesia's unilateral aerial firefighting operations is insufficient to eradicate the deep-seated structural problems of peatland fires, such as land development policies and illegal clearing activities. In addition, the unpredictability of the El Niño phenomenon limits the response capabilities of various countries, and over-interpreting the effectiveness of single firefighting actions may mask the governance dilemmas under the long-term impacts of climate change. When dealing with haze, countries are often constrained by the principle of sovereignty, making it difficult to conduct efficient cross-border law enforcement, which is a long-standing challenge for ASEAN in handling transboundary environmental issues.
  
  Future observations should focus on whether the Indonesian government can maintain high-intensity firefighting operations and whether ASEAN countries can reach more concrete consensus on haze early warning and the integration of transnational medical resources through regional mechanisms. Furthermore, as the El Niño phenomenon continues, rainfall forecasts for the Southeast Asian region in the coming months will be a key indicator for determining whether fire risks can be effectively reduced. The international community and regional organizations will continue to monitor the transparency of Indonesia's environmental governance and whether ASEAN countries can implement the spirit of medical equity and regional collaboration advocated in the Dili Declaration when facing public health challenges brought about by climate change.

# 印尼森林大火引發跨境霾害，聖嬰現象加劇東南亞氣候危機

> 印尼近期因聖嬰現象導致長期乾旱，森林與泥炭地火災頻傳，濃煙引發的跨境霾害已擴散至周邊國家，東協各國正共同面臨公共衛生與環境治理的嚴峻挑戰。


印尼森林與泥炭地火災近期呈現惡化趨勢，根據多方報導，受聖嬰現象（El Niño）驅動的異常乾旱影響，印尼境內的森林火災已導致數萬公頃土地遭到焚毀。這場氣候災害不僅造成印尼本土的環境破壞，更因大氣環流影響，將含有毒性的濃煙與霾害帶往東南亞其他地區。東南亞國家協會（ASEAN）作為區域性政府間論壇，其核心宗旨包含區域經濟發展與和平共存，然而面對此類跨國環境危機，各國在協調應對與國際規範的遵守上，正承受前所未有的壓力。
綜合各方媒體報導，印尼政府已針對火災採取具體行動，包括加強地面與空中滅火作業。目前，印尼當局正動員大量人力與裝置，試圖控制在加里曼丹（Kalimantan）與蘇門答臘（Sumatra）地區蔓延的火勢。此一滅火行動不僅是為了保護印尼境內的森林資源，更是為了緩解因空氣品質惡化而引發的區域性公共衛生危機。東南亞各國民眾對於霾害擴散普遍表達強烈不滿與挫折，顯示該問題已從單純的環境議題，轉變為影響區域民心與跨國關係的重大社會事件。
針對火災的具體影響範圍與嚴重程度，不同來源提供了互補但細節各異的資訊。美聯社（AP News）強調了聖嬰現象導致的長期旱季是火災主因，並指出受災面積已達數萬公頃；BBC則聚焦於社群媒體上民眾對霾害的憤怒反應，強調毒霾已跨越國界，影響範圍涵蓋整個東南亞。
此外，世界衛生組織（WHO）雖未直接針對本次火災發表緊急醫療資料，但其東南亞區域衛生部長會議近期透過的《帝力宣言》（Dili Declaration），旨在推進專科護理的公平性，這在區域性突發公共衛生事件頻發的背景下，顯得尤為關鍵，暗示了區域內對醫療資源分配與應對慢性健康威脅的重視。
這場跨境霾害對東南亞的影響是多層面的。首先，在經濟層面，火災造成的農地損毀與物流中斷，直接衝擊相關產業鏈；其次，在公共衛生層面，空氣品質惡化對呼吸道疾病患者造成巨大威脅，增加了醫療體系的負擔。東協各國在面對此類跨境環境議題時，必須在經濟發展與國際規範之間取得平衡。印尼的滅火效率不僅關乎其國家內部治理，更直接決定了鄰近國家（如新加坡、馬來西亞等）的空氣品質與國民健康，這使得環境治理成為東協區域合作中不可或缺的一環。
然而，各界對於治理成效的觀點存在差異。部分觀察家認為，僅靠印尼單方面的空中滅火作業難以根除泥炭地火災的深層結構問題，如土地開發政策與非法開墾行為。此外，聖嬰現象的不可控性限制了各國的應對能力，過度解讀單一滅火行動的成效可能掩蓋了氣候變遷長期影響下的治理困境。各國在應對霾害時，往往受限於主權原則，難以進行跨國界的高效執法，這也是東協在處理環境跨境議題時長期面臨的挑戰。
後續觀察重點應聚焦於印尼政府是否能持續維持高強度的滅火作業，以及東協各國是否能透過區域機制，針對霾害預警與跨國醫療資源整合達成更具體的共識。此外，隨著聖嬰現象的持續，未來數月內東南亞地區的降雨量預測，將是判斷火災風險是否能有效降低的關鍵指標。國際社會與區域組織將持續關注印尼在環境治理上的透明度，以及東協各國在面對氣候變遷帶來的公共衛生挑戰時，能否落實《帝力宣言》中所倡導的醫療公平與區域協作精神。
