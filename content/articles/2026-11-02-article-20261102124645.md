  # New Taipei Art Museum International Forum Focuses on Climate Crisis, Explores Sustainable Governance for Cultural Institutions in Australia and the Asia-Pacific
  
  > The New Taipei Art Museum hosted the "Coordinates of Tomorrow" international forum on October 31, inviting experts from Australia and the Asia-Pacific region to discuss sustainable governance and social responsibility for cultural institutions amidst climate change and global political and economic shifts.
  
  The New Taipei Art Museum (NTAM) held the "Coordinates of Tomorrow: Institutional Sustainability and Public Connection" international forum on October 31, 2026. Grounded in the multifaceted challenges of global climate change, resource restructuring, and technological evolution, the forum aimed to explore how cultural institutions can transition from traditional roles of collection and exhibition into a new type of cultural infrastructure with adaptive capacity. The event brought together directors, curators, artists, and community experts from Australia, Japan, South Korea, and Taiwan to outline a blueprint for the future of cultural institutions in terms of sustainable governance and local collaboration.
  
  NTAM Director Lai Hsiang-ling emphasized during the event that the museum has been reflecting on the social role of cultural institutions since 2020, considering how to preserve sustainable cultural and ecological assets for the next generation.
  
  According to the diverse perspectives shared at the forum, a common challenge currently faced by cultural institutions worldwide is how to strengthen their agency and build public trust in the face of the climate crisis. As a major representative of Oceania, Australia's adaptive strategies for its cultural institutions in the face of climate change became a core focus of the forum's exchange. Participants unanimously agreed that the responsibility of cultural institutions is no longer limited to the storage of artworks; they must actively engage in social connections and incorporate climate issues into the core framework of institutional governance. This cross-border exchange of experience demonstrates that the Asia-Pacific region is seeking resilient and sustainable solutions through cultural pathways in the face of global environmental change.
  
  Although representatives from various countries reached a consensus on the goal that "cultural institutions must shoulder sustainable responsibilities," significant differences remain regarding specific implementation strategies and local collaboration models. Australian cultural institutions have adopted different adaptation measures to address the unique island ecology and geography of Oceania; in contrast, Taiwan's cultural institutions focus more on public connection and digital transformation within urban environments. Furthermore, there is currently no unified international standard for the definition of "sustainable governance" under the policy contexts of different countries, particularly regarding whether it includes the same ecological asset assessment indicators. This reflects the challenges countries still face in localized adjustments when promoting cultural sustainability.
  
  The discussions at this forum have a profound impact on cultural industry policies in Oceania and the Asia-Pacific region. As a component of social infrastructure, the transformation strategies of cultural institutions are directly related to future regional cultural resilience. Especially in Oceania, where many nations are island states, the impacts of sea-level rise and ecological disruption caused by climate change are more direct. How cultural institutions can assist local communities in climate adaptation through the governance blueprints proposed at the forum will be key to future policy implementation. Additionally, in terms of industry connection, through cross-border curation and resource sharing, cultural institutions are expected to build more robust international cooperation networks amidst the climate crisis, thereby promoting the practice of green governance.
  
  However, there are still limitations to the view held by academia and the industry regarding "cultural institutions as subjects of climate adaptation," and their actual influence on environmental governance should not be over-interpreted. While cultural institutions can raise public awareness of climate issues through exhibitions and forums, they are not the core decision-makers for environmental policy, nor can they directly solve the substantive physical challenges brought about by climate change. Therefore, it is unrealistic to view the sustainable governance of cultural institutions as a panacea for climate change. The "adaptive capacity" discussed at the forum refers more to the organizational resilience and social influence of the institutions themselves, rather than direct intervention in environmental change. When examining the results of such forums, all parties should distinguish the boundaries between cultural influence and policy execution.
  
  Future observations will focus on whether the NTAM and participating institutions from countries like Australia can translate the blueprints outlined in the forum into concrete institutional indicators or promote substantive cross-border cooperation projects. In particular, regarding the storage of cultural assets in Oceania's island nations under climate change, it remains to be seen whether more international technical and resource support can be obtained through the connections established at this forum. Furthermore, whether "sustainable governance" indicators for cultural institutions in the face of the climate crisis will be incorporated into the evaluation systems of various ministries of culture is also a policy trend worth continuous attention. Whether relevant institutions can maintain dialogue and translate theory into concrete social action will determine the long-term value of this forum.

# 新美館國際論壇聚焦氣候危機，探討澳洲與亞太文化機構永續治理

> 新北市美術館於10月31日舉辦「明日座標」國際論壇，邀請澳洲及亞太地區專家，共同探討文化機構在氣候變遷與全球政經變局下的永續治理與社會責任。


新北市美術館（下稱新美館）於2026年10月31日舉辦「明日座標：機構永續與公共連結」國際論壇。本次論壇背景立基於全球氣候變遷、資源重組及科技演化等多重挑戰，旨在探討文化機構如何從傳統的典藏與展示職能，轉型為具備調適能力的新型態文化基礎設施。活動匯集了來自澳洲、日本、韓國及臺灣本地的館長、策展人、藝術家與社群專家，共同勾勒未來文化機構在永續治理與在地協作上的藍圖。
新美館館長賴香伶於會中強調，該館自2020年起便開始反思文化機構的社會角色，思考如何為下一代留存可持續累積的文化與生態資產。
根據參與論壇的多方觀點，各國文化機構目前面臨的共同挑戰在於如何在氣候危機下強化主體性，並建立公共信任。澳洲作為大洋洲的主要代表，其文化機構在面對氣候變遷的調適策略，成為本次論壇的重要交流核心。論壇參與者一致認同，文化機構的責任已不僅限於藝術品的儲存，更應積極參與社會連結，將氣候議題納入機構治理的核心架構中。這種跨國的經驗交流，顯示出亞太地區在面對全球環境變遷時，正試圖透過文化路徑尋求具備韌性的永續解決方案。
儘管各國代表在「文化機構需肩負永續責任」的目標上達成共識，但在具體的執行策略與在地協作模式上，仍存在顯著的差異。澳洲文化機構在處理大洋洲獨特的島嶼生態與地理環境時，採取了與東亞國家不同的適應措施；相較之下，臺灣的文化機構則更側重於都市環境中的公共連結與數位轉型。此外，論壇中對於「永續治理」的定義，在不同國家的政策語境下，是否包含相同的生態資產評估指標，目前尚無統一的國際標準，這也反映出各國在推動文化永續時，仍需面對在地化調整的挑戰。
本次論壇的討論內容，對大洋洲地區及亞太地區的文化產業政策具有深遠影響。文化機構作為社會基礎設施的一環，其轉型策略直接關係到未來區域性的文化韌性。特別是在大洋洲，由於其多為島嶼國家，氣候變遷帶來的海平面上升與生態衝擊更為直接，文化機構如何透過論壇中提出的治理藍圖，協助在地社群進行氣候調適，將是未來政策落實的關鍵。此外，產業連結方面，透過跨國策展與資源共享，文化機構有望在氣候危機下，建立更穩固的國際合作網路，進而推動綠色治理的實踐。
然而，學界與業界對於「文化機構作為氣候調適主體」的觀點仍存在限制，不應過度解讀其在環境治理上的實際影響力。雖然文化機構能透過展覽與論壇提升公眾對氣候議題的意識，但其並非環境政策的決策核心，亦無法直接解決氣候變遷帶來的實質物理挑戰。因此，將文化機構的永續治理視為氣候變遷的萬靈丹並不切實際。論壇中所探討的「調適能力」，更多是指機構本身的組織韌性與社會影響力，而非對環境變遷的直接幹預。各界在審視此類論壇成果時，應區分文化影響力與政策執行力的界線。
後續觀察重點將聚焦於新美館及參與論壇的澳洲等國機構，是否能將論壇中勾勒的藍圖轉化為具體的館務指標，或是推動跨國間的實質合作計畫。特別是針對大洋洲島嶼國家在氣候變遷下的文化資產儲存，是否能透過本次論壇建立的連結，獲得更多國際技術與資源的支援。此外，文化機構在氣候危機下的「永續治理」指標，未來是否會納入各國文化部的評鑑體系，亦是值得持續關注的政策動向。相關機構是否能持續對話，並將理論轉化為具體的社會行動，將決定此次論壇的長期價值。
