  The United Nations Children's Fund issued a grave warning this Tuesday (exact release date pending confirmation), stating that approximately one million children in Afghanistan are suffering from life-threatening severe acute malnutrition. The figure, drawn from the UN agency's latest monitoring data, highlights how children's nutritional status is rapidly deteriorating in Afghanistan under multiple pressures, including interrupted humanitarian aid, economic collapse, and climate shocks.
  
  UNICEF Representative in Afghanistan Tajudeen Oyewale stated in a public announcement that if these one million children cannot receive timely and adequate treatment, and if the agency fails to act swiftly, all one million of these children will face the risk of death.
  
  According to information cross-checked across multiple media outlets, the core figures and scope of impact highlighted in this warning have been confirmed by several sources. Reports from France 24 and RTL both indicated that UNICEF warned approximately one million Afghan children face the threat of death due to severe acute malnutrition, provided they do not receive immediate treatment. Meanwhile, a report from Anadolu Agency provided broader contextual data, noting that approximately 3.7 million children in Afghanistan are suffering from "wasting," of whom about one million are cases of life-threatening severe acute malnutrition.
  
  A report from Bangladesh Sangbad Sangstha (BSS) also echoed the key figure of one million children, citing Oyewale's remarks and emphasizing that the lack of treatment and slow action would leave these one million children on the brink of death.
  
  Although all sources reached a consensus on the core figure of "one million children," differences remain in the presentation of details and the scope of data. Reports from France 24 and RTL focused solely on "one million children at risk of death," while Anadolu Agency added broader data on 3.7 million children suffering from overall acute malnutrition, and specifically highlighted a concerning phenomenon: nearly 40 percent of children hospitalized with severe wasting were infants under six months old. This detail was not explicitly mentioned in the France 24 and RTL reports, indicating that different news agencies chose different angles and levels of data depth in their coverage.
  
  Furthermore, none of the reports clearly indicated the specific date on which the UN warning was issued, referring only to "Tuesday," which remains ambiguous in terms of time confirmation.

Afghanistan has long relied on international aid to maintain basic medical and nutritional services, but after the Taliban took power, most international aid programs were suspended or significantly reduced, causing the healthcare system to nearly collapse. This UN data implies that if sufficient therapeutic food (such as peanut butter-like RUTF) and medical personnel cannot be mobilized in the short term, Afghanistan's child mortality rate could rise significantly. In particular, infants under six months old account for nearly 40 percent of hospitalized severe wasting cases, indicating that malnutrition has penetrated the most vulnerable age group, with irreversible long-term consequences for infant development and the health of future generations.
  
  For neighboring countries and international donors, this also represents a humanitarian responsibility and an indicator of regional stability.

Therefore, the figure of one million should not be viewed as an absolutely precise population statistic, but rather understood as an indicator reflecting the severity of the crisis.

At the same time, the security situation within Afghanistan, whether humanitarian aid corridors remain open, and whether supplies can be pre-positioned before winter arrives will all be key variables determining the fate of these one million children. Furthermore, observing the progress of political negotiations between the Taliban authorities and international aid agencies, as well as whether more independent survey data will be released to verify or correct the UN's estimates, will also be important bases for assessing the true scale of this crisis and the effectiveness of the response.

# 聯合國警告：百萬阿富汗兒童瀕臨致命營養不良危機

> 聯合國兒童基金會（UNICEF）警告，阿富汗約一百萬名兒童正遭受危及生命的嚴重急性營養不良，若未能即時獲得治療，恐有死亡風險。


聯合國兒童基金會於本週二（實際發布日期待確認）發布一項嚴峻警告，指出阿富汗境內約有一百萬名兒童正遭受危及生命的嚴重急性營養不良（severe acute malnutrition）。此一數字來自聯合國機構的最新監測資料，凸顯阿富汗在人道援助中斷、經濟崩潰與氣候衝擊等多重壓力下，兒童營養狀況正急速惡化。
UNICEF阿富汗代表塔朱德·奧耶瓦萊（Tajudeen Oyewale）在公開宣告中表示，若這一百萬名兒童無法獲得及時且充分的治療，該機構若未能迅速採取行動，這一百萬名兒童都將面臨死亡風險。
根據多家媒體交叉比對的資訊，此次警告的核心數字與受影響範圍已獲得多方確認。法國24電視臺（France 24）與盧森堡廣播電視臺（RTL）的報導均指出，UNICEF警告約一百萬名阿富汗兒童因嚴重急性營養不良而面臨死亡威脅，前提是他們未能立即接受治療。與此同時，安納杜魯新聞社（Anadolu Agency）的報導則提供了更廣泛的資料脈絡，指出阿富汗約有三百七十萬名兒童正遭受「消瘦」（wasting）之苦，其中約一百萬人屬於危及生命的嚴重急性營養不良。
孟加拉通訊社（BSS）的報導亦呼應了百萬兒童的關鍵數字，並引述奧耶瓦萊的談話，強調缺乏治療與行動緩慢將導致這一百萬名兒童處於死亡邊緣。
儘管各來源對「一百萬名兒童」的核心數字達成共識，但在細節呈現與資料範圍上仍存在差異。法國24與RTL的報導聚焦於「一百萬名兒童處於死亡風險」的單一焦點，而安納杜魯新聞社則補充了三百七十萬名兒童整體急性營養不良的更大範圍資料，並特別指出一個令人憂心的現象：在因嚴重消瘦而住院的兒童中，有近百分之四十是未滿六個月的嬰兒。此一細節在法國24與RTL的報導中並未被明確提及，顯示不同通訊社在報導時選擇了不同的切入角度與資料深度。
此外，各報導均未明確標示聯合國警告發布的具體日期，僅以「星期二」概括，這在時間確認上仍屬模糊地帶。
此次警告對阿富汗的公共衛生與人道援助體系構成直接且迫切的壓力。阿富汗長期以來依賴國際援助維持基本醫療與營養服務，但在塔利班接管政權後，多數國際援助計畫暫停或大幅縮減，導致醫療體系幾近崩潰。聯合國此項資料意味著，若無法在短期內動員足夠的治療性營養食品（如花生醬狀的RUTF）與醫療人力，阿富汗的兒童死亡率可能出現顯著上升。特別是未滿六個月的嬰兒佔住院嚴重消瘦病例近四成，這顯示營養不良問題已深入最脆弱的年齡層，對嬰兒發育與未來世代健康將產生不可逆轉的長期影響。
對於鄰近國家與國際捐助者而言，這也是一項人道主義責任與區域穩定指標。
然而，在解讀此項資料時，必須保持審慎態度。首先，聯合國所引用的數字可能來自抽樣調查或模型推估，而非全面性的逐一普查，因此實際受影響人數可能存在誤差範圍。其次，各媒體報導的發布時間與引用來源均為UNICEF的同一場警告或同一份宣告，缺乏獨立第三方機構的即時驗證，這意味著資料的準確性仍依賴聯合國內部監測機制的可靠性。
此外，報導中並未詳細說明「消瘦」與「嚴重急性營養不良」的診斷標準是否與國際通用的中上臂圍（MUAC）測量或身高體重比一致，這可能影響不同地區或不同時間點資料的可比性。因此，不宜將這一百萬數字視為絕對精確的人口統計，而應理解為反映危機嚴重程度的指標。
後續觀察的重點應放在UNICEF及其合作夥伴是否能在短期內啟動大規模的營養治療計畫，以及國際社會是否會針對此次警告作出具體的資金承諾與援助行動。同時，阿富汗境內的安全域性勢、人道援助通道是否暢通，以及冬季來臨前能否完成物資預置，都將是決定這一百萬名兒童命運的關鍵變數。此外，觀察塔利班當局與國際援助機構之間的政治協商進展，以及是否有更多獨立調查資料發布以驗證或修正聯合國的估計，也將是評估此危機真實規模與應對成效的重要依據。
短期內，若UNICEF發布後續的治療覆蓋率或死亡率資料，將能更清楚判斷警報是否已轉化為實際行動。
