  # Obstacles to Transparency Reform in South African Government Procurement and Progress in the African Union’s Climate Early Warning Mechanism
  
  > This article summarizes the difficulties in promoting the South African government procurement transparency platform and the latest policy directions of the African Union regarding climate early warning mechanisms in Central Africa, exploring the challenges faced by regional governance and digital regulation on the African continent.
  
  The issue of transparency in South African government procurement has recently attracted significant social attention, with civil society organizations working to develop digital firewall systems aimed at curbing illegal tendering and corruption in the government procurement process. According to relevant reports, the platform's development team has repeatedly demonstrated the system's efficacy to the South African National Treasury, various government departments, and local municipal governments, hoping to mandate procurement processes through technological means.
  
  However, despite the technology's ability to monitor potential malpractice, the willingness of government agencies at all levels to adopt the system is currently extremely low. There have even been instances where certain units deliberately circumvented update instructions for the official e-tender portal, indicating that digital governance faces severe administrative resistance and institutional obstacles during its implementation.
  
  Under the African Union's (AU) regional governance framework, climate change and public administration reform have become core issues. According to information released by the African Union, the organization is actively promoting the "Climate Services and Related Applications" (ClimSA) program, specifically strengthening climate forecasting capabilities in the Central African region and calling on governments to use precise climate data to drive early warning actions. At the same time, the African Union continues to push for institutional reform, with Kenyan President William Samoei Ruto serving as the champion for institutional reform, attempting to improve the decision-making efficiency and execution of the regional organization in addressing transnational public issues.
  
  In addition, the Electoral Commission of South Africa (IEC) has announced the timetable for the 2026 local government elections, indicating that South Africa maintains its established constitutional operational rhythm in strengthening democratic processes and administrative transparency.
  
  Regarding the current status of the promotion of the South African procurement platform, there is an information gap between various media reports. Duvenage, the head of the private development team, clearly pointed out that government departments have refused to adopt the firewall system, emphasizing that the behavior of some officials deliberately bypassing the official procurement platform has become a major factor hindering transparency. However, official institutions such as the National Treasury have not yet provided a specific response to such allegations, nor have they explained the specific reasons for refusing to adopt the private technology.
  
  Furthermore, while the African Union has clearly set policy goals in promoting climate early warning mechanisms, there is still a significant technical gap in the progress of climate data integration among member states. Some Central African countries still require further international assistance and policy coordination in terms of infrastructure and the configuration of climate monitoring equipment.
  
  These events have a profound impact on public administration and social development in South Africa and Africa as a whole. In South Africa, the lack of procurement transparency not only leads to the loss of public funds but also weakens public trust in the government's governance capabilities. If digital regulatory tools cannot be mandatorily implemented, the malpractice in infrastructure construction and social welfare procurement may be difficult to eradicate. On the other hand, the climate early warning mechanism promoted by the African Union is crucial for the Central African region, where extreme weather occurs frequently. If a transnational data sharing and early warning system can be successfully established, it will effectively reduce the impact of climate disasters on agriculture and public health, and enhance climate resilience within the region.
  
  However, the effectiveness of the policy depends on whether member states are willing to translate regional directives into concrete domestic implementation plans.
  
  There are different interpretations in society regarding the views and limitations of the above issues. Groups supporting the digital firewall believe that technological means are the only way to solve corruption and must be mandatorily included in the procurement process; however, administrative departments may consider system compatibility, data security, or administrative autonomy, leading to a reserved attitude toward private technology. Similarly, although the African Union's reform plan is ambitious, it is limited by differences in national sovereignty and a lack of resources, making it difficult for reform directives to be implemented at the grassroots level. Therefore, analysts warn against being overly optimistic about the influence of a single technology platform or policy initiative, and emphasize that they must be evaluated within the context of complex political games and administrative bureaucratic systems.
  
  Subsequent observations should focus on whether the South African government will face pressure from civil society and audit institutions, thereby adjusting its management policy for the e-tender platform. In addition, the progress of the African Union's implementation of the climate early warning mechanism around 2026, as well as the cooperation of various countries in relevant data integration, will be key indicators for testing whether regional governance reform has substantive significance. Media and the public should continue to pay attention to the government's specific actions in procurement transparency, and whether the African Union can effectively overcome the bottlenecks in administrative execution when promoting the implementation of climate policies by member states.

# 南非政府採購透明化改革受阻與非洲聯盟氣候預警機制進展

> 本文整理南非政府採購透明化平臺的推動困境，以及非洲聯盟針對中非地區氣候預警機制的最新政策方向，探討區域性治理與數位監管在非洲大陸面臨的挑戰。


南非政府採購透明化議題近期引發社會高度關注，民間組織致力於開發數位防火牆系統，旨在遏止政府採購過程中的非法招標與貪腐行為。根據相關報導，該平臺開發團隊已多次向南非國家財政部、各級政府部門及地方市政府展示系統效能，希望透過科技手段強制規範採購流程。
然而，儘管該技術具備監控潛在弊端的功能，目前各級政府機構對採用該系統的意願極低，甚至出現部分單位刻意規避官方電子招標入口網站（e-tender portal）更新指令的現象，顯示出數位治理在推動過程中面臨嚴重的行政阻力與制度性障礙。
在非洲聯盟（African Union）的區域治理架構下，氣候變遷與公共行政改革成為近期核心議題。根據非洲聯盟官方發布的資訊，該組織正積極推動「氣候服務應用計畫」（ClimSA），特別針對中非地區強化氣候預測能力，呼籲各國政府利用精準的氣候資料驅動早期預警行動。與此同時，非洲聯盟內部亦持續推動制度改革，由肯亞總統威廉·魯託（William Samoei Ruto）擔任機構改革倡導者，試圖提升區域組織在應對跨國公共議題時的決策效率與執行力。
此外，南非選舉委員會（IEC）亦已公佈2026年地方政府選舉的時間表，顯示南非在強化民主程式與行政透明度方面，仍維持既定的憲政運作節奏。
針對南非採購平臺的推動現況，各媒體報導間存在資訊落差。民間開發團隊負責人杜維納格（Duvenage）明確指出，政府部門拒絕採用該防火牆系統，並強調部分官員蓄意繞過官方採購平臺的行為，已成為阻礙透明化的主要因素。然而，官方機構如國家財政部對此類指控尚未提出具體回應，亦未說明拒絕採納民間技術的具體原因。
此外，非洲聯盟在推動氣候預警機制時，雖已明確提出政策目標，但各成員國在落實氣候資料整合的進度上，仍存在顯著的技術落差，部分中非國家在基礎建設與氣候監測裝置的配置上，仍有待進一步的國際援助與政策協調。
這些事件對南非及非洲整體的公共行政與社會發展產生深遠影響。在南非，採購透明度的缺失不僅導致公共資金流失，更削弱了民眾對政府治理能力的信任。若無法強制推行數位監管工具，政府在基礎設施建設與社會福利採購上的弊端恐難以根除。另一方面，非洲聯盟推動的氣候預警機制，對於極端氣候頻發的中非地區至關重要。若能成功建立跨國資料共享與早期預警系統，將能有效降低氣候災害對農業與公共衛生的衝擊，並提升區域內的氣候韌性。
然而，政策的成效取決於各成員國是否願意將區域指令轉化為國內的具體執行方案。
關於上述議題的觀點與限制，社會各界存在不同解讀。支援數位防火牆的團體認為，科技手段是解決貪腐的唯一途徑，必須強制納入採購流程；但行政部門可能考量到系統相容性、資料安全或行政自主權，導致對民間技術持保留態度。同樣地，非洲聯盟的改革計畫雖宏大，但受限於各國主權差異與資源匱乏，改革指令往往難以在基層落實。因此，分析人士提醒，不應過度樂觀地看待單一科技平臺或政策倡議的影響力，必須將其置於複雜的政治博弈與行政官僚體系中進行評估。
後續觀察重點應聚焦於南非政府是否會面臨來自民間社會與審計機構的壓力，進而調整其對電子招標平臺的管理政策；此外，非洲聯盟在2026年前後針對氣候預警機制的執行進度，以及各國對相關資料整合的配合度，將是檢驗區域治理改革是否具備實質意義的關鍵指標。媒體與公眾應持續關注政府在採購透明化方面的具體作為，以及非洲聯盟在推動成員國落實氣候政策時，是否能有效克服行政執行力的瓶頸。
