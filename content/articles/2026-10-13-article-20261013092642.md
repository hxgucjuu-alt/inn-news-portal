  # Japan’s LDP Sparks Gender Equality Controversy with Policy on Female Participation in Meetings
  
  > Japan's ruling Liberal Democratic Party (LDP) recently announced that it would invite female party members to attend core meetings, but restricted them to "observing" only, without the right to speak. Following the resignation of the Tokyo Olympics organizing committee chief over sexist remarks, this move has once again triggered intense domestic and international questioning regarding gender equality in Japanese politics.
  
  Japan's ruling Liberal Democratic Party (LDP) recently proposed a new plan for its internal decision-making mechanism, aimed at increasing female participation in party operations. The plan was announced by then-LDP Secretary-General Toshihiro Nikai, stating that female party members would be invited to participate in core party meetings. This occurred against a backdrop of frequent gender controversies in Japanese politics, particularly at a time when the head of the Tokyo Olympics organizing committee was forced to resign after making discriminatory remarks about women, leading to high public concern over the deeply rooted gender inequality in Japan's political system. Although the LDP's move was intended to respond to criticism regarding the lack of female representation, the attached restrictions caused the policy to fall into a public opinion storm at the very beginning of its implementation.
  
  According to reports from the BBC and several other media outlets, the core controversy of the LDP's plan to invite women to core meetings lies in its "observe but do not speak" restriction. The party stipulated that invited female members could only attend as observers and would be unable to participate in discussions or offer opinions during the meetings. Following its announcement, this policy immediately sparked widespread criticism both within Japan and from the international community. Multiple sources have confirmed that the policy does not grant women substantive decision-making power; it is merely a formalistic participation that fails to change the structural status quo of the LDP's decision-making circle being dominated by men for a long time. Critics argue that this move not only fails to alleviate concerns about gender discrimination but further highlights the limitations of the Japanese political establishment's understanding of gender equality.
  
  There is a gap in information among different sources regarding the specific implementation details and internal consensus of this policy. Some reports indicate that this plan was intended to address concerns among senior party members about female voices intervening in decision-making, while other perspectives suggest it was merely an expedient measure aimed at quelling a public relations crisis following the Olympic scandal. What remains to be confirmed is whether other reformist members within the party are attempting to push for more inclusive participation mechanisms, or whether this "observer system" is merely transitional. Furthermore, the level of support for this policy among various factions within the party remains unclear, and there is skepticism regarding whether the restrictions will be relaxed over time.
  
  This incident has had a profound impact on Japanese politics and social policy. First, it has deepened the public's negative impression of the ruling party's lack of determination for reform, which may lead to continued low support for the LDP among female voters. Second, in terms of corporate and social governance, the gender attitudes of Japanese politics are often viewed as a benchmark; this incident may weaken Japan's persuasiveness in promoting workplace gender equality policies. If the ruling party cannot implement true gender inclusivity at the decision-making level, it will be difficult to respond to the international community's expectations for Japan's political transparency and diversity, thereby affecting Japan's influence and image in international governance issues.
  
  From different perspectives, supporters might argue that this is a small step toward female political participation, emphasizing that "participation is better than total exclusion." However, gender equality advocates have strongly criticized this as "tokenism," arguing that such restrictive participation is actually a form of humiliation and a denial of women's professional capabilities. The limitation is that such policies often ignore structural barriers, such as childcare burdens and the glass ceiling in the workplace; focusing only on meeting seats cannot solve the fundamental problems. Therefore, this move should not be over-interpreted as a substantive step forward for gender equality in Japan, but rather as a defensive adjustment made by the party under pressure from public opinion, rather than a structural reform.
  
  The focus for future observation is whether the LDP will revise these meeting participation rules due to public pressure to allow women the right to speak. Additionally, adjustments to the proportion of female candidate nominations in the next election, as well as whether more meaningful gender equality legislation will emerge, will be key indicators in assessing whether Japanese political parties are truly implementing reform. The international community will also continue to monitor how Japan handles the issue of gender imbalance in its politics, which will directly affect Japan's ranking in international gender equality indices and its policy influence.

# 日本自民黨推動女性參與會議政策引發性別平權爭議

> 日本執政黨自民黨日前宣佈，將邀請女性黨員列席核心會議，但限制僅能「旁聽」不得發言，此舉在奧運籌委會主席因性別歧視言論下臺後，再度引發國內外對日本政壇性別平等的強烈質疑。


日本執政黨自由民主黨（LDP）近期針對黨內決策機制提出一項新方案，旨在提升女性在黨務運作中的參與度。該計畫由時任自民黨幹事長二階俊博對外宣佈，內容指出將邀請女性黨員參與黨核心心會議。此背景發生於日本政壇性別爭議頻傳之際，特別是當時日本奧運籌委會主席因發表歧視女性言論而被迫辭職，導致社會大眾對於日本政治體系中根深蒂固的性別不平等問題高度關注。自民黨此舉原意雖為回應外界對於女性代表性不足的批評，卻因附加的限制條件，使其在政策推動初期便陷入輿論風暴。
根據《BBC》等多家媒體報導，自民黨這項邀請女性參與核心會議的計畫，核心爭議點在於其「觀察但不準發言」的限制。該黨規定，受邀出席的女性黨員僅能作為旁聽者，無法在會議中參與討論或提出意見。這一政策在公佈後，隨即引發了日本國內及國際社會的廣泛批評。多方來源共同確認，該政策並未賦予女性實質的決策權，僅是形式上的參與，無法改變自民黨決策圈長期由男性主導的結構性現狀。此舉被批評者認為不僅無法緩解性別歧視的質疑，反而更加凸顯了日本政壇對於性別平權認知的侷限。
關於此政策的具體執行細節與黨內共識，不同來源間存在資訊落差。部分報導指出，此方案是為了應對黨內高層對女性聲音介入決策的擔憂，但也有觀點認為這僅是權宜之計，旨在平息奧運醜聞後的公關危機。目前尚待確認的是，黨內是否有其他改革派成員試圖推動更具包容性的參與機制，或者該「旁聽制」是否僅為過渡性質。此外，該政策在黨內各派系間的支援度亦不明確，外界對於這項措施是否會隨時間推移而放寬限制，仍持保留態度。
此事件對日本政壇及社會政策的影響深遠。首先，它加深了公眾對於執政黨改革決心不足的負面印象，可能導致女性選民對自民黨的支援度持續低迷。其次，在企業與社會治理層面，日本政壇的性別態度往往被視為指標，此次事件可能削弱日本在推動職場性別平等政策上的說服力。若執政黨無法在決策層面落實真正的性別包容，將難以回應國際社會對於日本政治透明度與多元性的期待，進而影響日本在國際治理議題上的話語權與形象。
從不同觀點來看，支持者可能認為這是邁向女性參與政治的一小步，強調「有參與總比完全排除好」；然而，性別平權倡議者則強烈批評此舉為「象徵性政治」，認為這種限制性的參與反而是一種羞辱，是對女性專業能力的否定。限制在於，這類政策往往忽略了結構性障礙，例如育兒負擔與職場玻璃天花板，僅從會議席次著手無法解決根本問題。因此，不應過度解讀此舉為日本性別平權的實質進展，應將其視為政黨在輿論壓力下進行的防禦性調整，而非結構性的改革。
後續觀察重點在於，自民黨是否會因輿論壓力而修正該會議參與規則，允許女性具備發言權。此外，日本政壇在下一次選舉中，針對女性候選人提名比例的調整，以及是否會出現更具實質意義的性別平等法案，將是評估日本政黨是否真正落實改革的關鍵指標。國際社會亦將持續關注日本如何處理其政壇性別失衡問題，這將直接影響日本在國際性別平權指數中的排名與政策影響力。
