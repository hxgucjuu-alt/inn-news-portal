  # Regional Security Situation in Northeast Asia: Nuclear Risks on the Korean Peninsula and Geopolitical Challenges
  
  > As global geopolitical conflicts intensify, security issues in Northeast Asia have garnered significant attention. The Japan Institute of International Affairs and relevant research institutions in Taiwan point out that the nuclear and missile threats from North Korea, along with regional stability amidst great power competition, have become some of the most severe challenges currently facing the Asia-Pacific region.
  
  The security situation in Northeast Asia has long been deeply influenced by the Korean Peninsula issue. This is not merely a regional geopolitical conflict but one that affects multilateral international relations involving the United States, Japan, mainland China, and Russia. According to a report from the Japan Institute of International Affairs (JIIA) regarding its 2025 diplomatic and security survey, North Korea's nuclear and missile development has constituted a substantive regional security risk. This issue is closely linked to South Korea's domestic politics and foreign policy, becoming a focal point of high concern for scholars and policymakers.
  
  Meanwhile, Taiwan's International Council of Cultural Studies (ICCS) has also analyzed that the world is currently facing multiple crises, including the Russia-Ukraine war, the Israel-Hamas conflict, and attacks by Houthi rebels in Yemen. The international order is becoming increasingly complex, and the deepening relationship between Russia and North Korea has further exacerbated tensions in Northeast Asia.
  
  Multiple research sources have confirmed that the nuclear and missile threats on the Korean Peninsula are not a single-country issue but a core variable in the multilateral security architecture of Northeast Asia. Through policy recommendations, the Japan Institute of International Affairs emphasizes the need for in-depth research into North Korea's strategic threats and an assessment of their specific impact on regional stability. At the same time, international observers have pointed out that with the intensification of great power competition, the risk of military confrontation in Northeast Asia is rising. These research institutions unanimously agree that North Korea's military actions and diplomatic strategies directly affect the defense deployments and regional cooperation mechanisms of surrounding countries, making Northeast Asia a highly uncertain zone in the global security landscape.
  
  However, there are still differences among research sources regarding the assessment of specific risks and response strategies. Some sources focus on the concrete threat of North Korea's nuclear technology, while other perspectives emphasize the influence of South Korea's domestic political changes on its foreign policy, as well as the power struggle between the U.S.-Japan-South Korea alliance and the strategic coordination between China and Russia. Furthermore, there are subtle differences in the interpretations by various think tanks and media regarding the evolution of Russia's role in Korean Peninsula affairs, particularly concerning the depth and breadth of Russia-North Korea military cooperation, for which there is currently a lack of public and consistent empirical data. This unconfirmed information makes it difficult to reach a single consensus on regional security assessments in the short term, with all parties remaining in a phase of continuous observation and dynamic adjustment.
  
  This situation has a profound impact on Northeast Asia, first reflected in the increase in national defense budgets and the frequency of military exercises. Faced with North Korean missile threats, Japan and South Korea have been forced to strengthen their missile defense systems and deepen their military alliances with the United States, which has further sparked concerns about an arms race in the region. At the industrial level, geopolitical instability has directly impacted supply chain security, particularly in the semiconductor and high-tech industries, which are extremely sensitive to the risks of logistics and energy disruptions caused by regional conflicts. Additionally, social anxiety regarding the risk of war has influenced the direction of public policy in various countries, forcing governments to seek a more fragile balance between economic development and national security.
  
  Regarding the analysis of the situation in Northeast Asia, experts warn against over-interpreting single events or simplifying complex international relations into binary oppositions. Although the North Korean nuclear threat is clear, the interests of countries in the region are intertwined and cannot be explained by a single security issue alone. The limitation is that reports from international research institutions are mostly based on public information and policy forecasts; for the highly closed North Korean decision-making mechanism, there remains a huge information gap for external observers. Therefore, when interpreting relevant security recommendations, the outside world should consider the flexibility of foreign policy and the unpredictability of geopolitics, and should not view think tank policy suggestions as inevitable precursors to future military conflict.
  
  Subsequent observation will focus on several key indicators: first, the substantive actions of North Korea in missile launches and nuclear tests, and whether it will further engage in military technology transfers with Russia; second, how South Korea and Japan will adjust their autonomous defense capabilities and diplomatic negotiation strategies when facing regional security challenges; and finally, the interaction mode between the U.S. and China in Northeast Asian affairs, particularly whether both sides can maintain minimal communication and control regarding the issue of denuclearization of the peninsula and regional stability. These factors will collectively determine the direction of the security situation in Northeast Asia and are also the focus of continued attention from the international community.

# 東北亞區域安全域性勢：朝鮮半島核武風險與地緣政治挑戰

> 隨著全球地緣政治衝突加劇，東北亞安全議題備受矚目。日本國際問題研究所與臺灣相關研究機構指出，北韓核武與飛彈威脅，以及大國博弈下的區域穩定，已成為當前亞太地區最嚴峻的挑戰之一。


東北亞地區的安全域性勢長期以來深受朝鮮半島問題影響，這不僅是區域內部的地緣政治衝突，更牽動美國、日本、中國大陸及俄羅斯等多邊國際關係。根據日本國際問題研究所（JIIA）針對2025年度外交與安全保障調查研究的報告指出，北韓的核武與飛彈發展已構成實質的區域安全風險。此議題與韓國內政、外交政策緊密相連，成為學界與政策制定者高度關注的焦點。
同時，臺灣的國際事務研究機構（ICCS）亦分析，當前全球面臨俄烏戰爭、以哈衝突及葉門叛軍攻擊等多重危機，國際秩序日益複雜，其中俄羅斯與北韓關係的深化，更進一步加劇了東北亞的緊張情勢。
多個研究來源共同確認，朝鮮半島的核武與飛彈威脅並非單一國家問題，而是東北亞多邊安全架構中的核心變數。日本國際問題研究所透過政策提言，強調需針對北韓的戰略威脅進行深入研究，並評估其對區域穩定造成的具體影響。與此同時，國際觀察家亦指出，隨著大國競爭加劇，東北亞地區的軍事對峙風險正在上升。這些研究機構一致認為，北韓的軍事行動與外交策略，直接影響了周邊國家的防衛部署與區域合作機制，使得東北亞成為全球安全版圖中極具不確定性的地帶。
然而，各研究來源對於具體風險的評估與應對策略仍存在差異。部分來源側重於北韓核武技術的具體威脅，另有觀點則強調韓國內政變動對外交政策的影響力，以及美日韓同盟與中俄戰略協作之間的角力。此外，關於俄羅斯在朝鮮半島事務中的角色演變，不同智庫與媒體的解讀尚有細微差別，特別是針對俄朝軍事合作的深度與廣度，目前仍缺乏公開且一致的實證資料。這些尚待確認的資訊，使得區域安全評估在短期內難以達成單一共識，各方仍處於持續觀測與動態調整的階段。
此局勢對東北亞地區的實質影響深遠，首先體現在各國國防預算的增加與軍事演習的頻率上。日本與韓國在面對北韓飛彈威脅時，被迫強化導彈防禦系統並深化與美國的軍事同盟關係，這進一步引發了區域內的軍備競賽疑慮。在產業層面，地緣政治的不穩定性直接衝擊了供應鏈安全，特別是半導體與高科技產業，對於區域衝突帶來的物流與能源中斷風險極為敏感。此外，社會層面對於戰爭風險的焦慮，也影響了各國的公共政策走向，迫使政府在經濟發展與國家安全之間尋求更為脆弱的平衡。
針對東北亞局勢的分析，專家提醒應避免過度解讀單一事件或將複雜的國際關係簡化為二元對立。雖然北韓的核武威脅是明確的，但區域內各國的利益糾葛盤根錯節，並非僅由單一安全議題所能解釋。限制在於，國際研究機構的報告多基於公開資訊與政策預測，對於極度封閉的北韓決策機制，外部觀察仍存在巨大的資訊落差。因此，外界在解讀相關安全提言時，應考量到外交政策的靈活性與地緣政治的不可預測性，不宜將智庫的政策建議視為未來必然發生的軍事衝突預告。
後續觀察重點將聚焦於幾個關鍵指標：首先是北韓在飛彈試射與核試驗上的實質動作，及其是否會進一步與俄羅斯進行軍事技術轉移；其次是韓國與日本在面對區域安全挑戰時，如何調整其自主防衛能力與外交談判策略；最後則是美中兩國在東北亞事務上的互動模式，特別是針對半島無核化與區域穩定議題，雙方是否能維持最低限度的溝通與管控。這些因素將共同決定未來東北亞安全域性勢的走向，亦是國際社會持續關注的焦點。
