  # Taipei City Promotes Campus Covered Court Project: Integrating Green Energy and Community Shared Spaces
  
  > The Taipei City Department of Education has invested NT$350 million to construct covered sports courts at 12 schools, integrating green energy with shelter from the elements. The initiative aims to resolve weather-related limitations on physical education and enhance the community-sharing benefits of campus facilities.
  
  The Taipei City Department of Education has recently launched a large-scale optimization of campus sports facilities, officially initiating the construction project for covered sports courts. Running from 2023 to 2026, the project has allocated a total budget of NT$350 million to renovate spaces across 12 schools in the city. The policy stems from the frequent extreme weather caused by climate change, which often disrupts outdoor physical education classes due to intense heat or rain, thereby hindering teaching progress.
  
  Zhong Dexin, Chief Secretary of the Department of Education, stated that the construction of these covered courts will not only provide all-weather sports spaces but also incorporate green energy designs and natural ventilation into the planning. This allows the hardware facilities to simultaneously meet multiple needs, including physical education, club activities, school assemblies, and community sharing, effectively alleviating the shortage of indoor activity space in some schools.
  
  According to a cross-comparison of various public information, the Taipei City Government has explicitly listed "multi-use benefits" as a core indicator in its efforts to improve campus infrastructure. The design of the covered courts at the 12 participating schools is not limited to providing shelter from the sun and rain; it emphasizes the integration of green energy technology, which aligns with the policy direction of promoting sustainable campuses in modern cities. The Department of Education emphasizes that the construction of such facilities will significantly increase the flexibility of curriculum scheduling, allowing inter-class competitions, ball practice, and large-scale performances to proceed regardless of weather conditions, thereby increasing the utilization rate of campus space. In essence, this policy transforms campus space from a purely educational venue into a public facility with community-connecting functions, promoting interaction and resource sharing between schools and surrounding communities.
  
  Regarding information verification, the total budget for the project (NT$350 million) and the implementation period (2023–2026) have been officially released by the Department of Education and confirmed by relevant media reports. However, the official press release has not yet detailed which 12 schools were selected, nor has it provided specific construction specifications or energy output benefits for the green energy facilities at each school. Furthermore, although the project emphasizes community sharing, the actual opening hours, management regulations, and safety maintenance mechanisms for community residents to use the campus facilities remain execution details that have yet to be clarified in the current policy description.
  
  Due to differences in geographical location and campus size, different schools may face varying management challenges in the implementation of community access in the future.
  
  The direct impact of this project on Taipei's educational environment is primarily reflected in the improvement of physical education environments and the expansion of campus space functions. For students, the construction of covered courts ensures the continuity of physical education classes, reducing instances where classes are forced to move to indoor classrooms for sedentary lessons due to weather, thereby helping to safeguard students' rights to exercise. For school administration, the presence of covered courts effectively shares the usage pressure on indoor activity centers, providing schools with more diverse venue options when hosting large-scale events. For the community, this policy provides more high-quality sports spaces, especially in Taipei City, where land resources are scarce; opening up campus space will have a positive impact on improving the quality of life for surrounding residents.
  
  Although the project has clear goals for improving the quality of campus facilities, it should be noted that the construction of covered courts is not a panacea for all campus space issues. Over-interpreting its functions may lead to the neglect of school management burdens; for example, after opening to the community, campus security control, cleaning and maintenance costs, and noise coordination with surrounding residents are all realistic challenges that schools must face. In addition, the long-term maintenance and performance degradation of green energy facilities are also hidden costs that cannot be ignored in future policy implementation. When examining this project, all parties should distinguish between "hardware construction" and "software management" to avoid viewing simple facility upgrades as the only path to solving the shortage of campus space.
  
  Subsequent observation should focus on the overall performance evaluation after the project concludes in 2026, including the actual utilization rate of the facilities at these 12 schools, community satisfaction, and the actual contribution of green energy generation to campus energy conservation and carbon reduction. Whether the Department of Education will expand the successful experience of these 12 schools to other schools across the city will be key to observing whether Taipei's campus space policy has continuity. Furthermore, regarding the community-sharing mechanism, whether relevant units will formulate unified charging or management measures to ensure a balance between the campus teaching environment and public community interests is also a policy highlight worth tracking in the future.

# 臺北市推動校園風雨球場計畫：結合綠能與社群共享空間

> 臺北市教育局投入3.5億元經費，於12所學校建置結合綠能與遮陽避雨功能的風雨球場，旨在解決天候對體育教學的限制，並提升校園設施的社群共享效益。


臺北市政府教育局近期針對校園運動設施進行大規模最佳化，正式啟動校園風雨球場建置計畫。該計畫自112年起至115年止，總計編列3.5億元經費，針對市內12所學校進行空間改造。此項政策背景源於氣候變遷導致極端天氣頻繁，學生的戶外體育課程常受烈日或降雨影響，導致教學進度受阻。
教育局主任秘書鍾德馨表示，透過建置風雨球場，不僅能提供全天候的運動空間，更將綠能設計與自然通風納入規劃，使硬體設施能同時滿足體育教學、社團活動、校園集會及社群共享等多重需求，有效緩解部分學校室內活動空間不足的窘境。
根據多項公開資訊交叉比對，臺北市政府在提升校園基礎建設的過程中，明確將「多元使用效益」列為核心指標。參與計畫的12所學校，其風雨球場設計不僅限於遮陽避雨，更強調結合綠能技術，這與現代城市推動永續校園的政策方向一致。教育局強調，此類設施的建置能顯著增加課程安排的彈性，讓班際競賽、球類練習及大型展演活動不再受限於天氣，進而提升校園空間的利用率。此政策實質上是將校園空間從單純的教學場域，轉型為具備社群連結功能的公共設施，促進學校與周邊社群的互動與資源共享。
在資訊確認方面，目前關於該計畫的預算總額（3.5億元）與執行期程（112至115年）已由教育局官方釋出，並獲得相關媒體報導證實。然而，針對具體哪12所學校入選，以及各校在綠能設施的具體建置規格與能源產出效益，目前官方新聞稿中尚未詳盡列出各校細節。此外，雖然該計畫強調社群共享，但社群居民實際使用校園設施的開放時間、管理規範及安全維護機制，在現階段的政策說明中仍屬尚待釐清的執行細節。
不同學校因地理位置與校地規模差異，未來在社群開放的執行面上，可能面臨不同的管理挑戰。
該計畫對於臺北市教育環境的直接影響，主要體現在體育教學環境的改善與校園空間功能的擴充。對於學生而言，風雨球場的建置能確保體育課的連續性，減少因天氣因素被迫移至室內教室進行靜態課程的情況，有助於保障學生的運動權益。對學校行政端而言，風雨球場的出現能有效分擔室內活動中心的使用壓力，讓學校在舉辦大型活動時有更多元的場地選擇。對社群而言，這項政策提供了更多優質的運動空間，尤其在土地資源稀缺的臺北市，校園空間的開放將對提升周邊居民的生活品質產生正面助益。
儘管該計畫在提升校園設施品質上具有明確目標，但外界仍需注意，風雨球場的建置並非解決所有校園空間問題的萬靈丹。過度解讀其功能可能導致對學校管理負擔的忽略，例如開放社群使用後，校園安全控管、清潔維護成本以及與周邊住戶的噪音協調，均是校方必須面對的現實挑戰。此外，綠能設施的長期維護與效能衰退問題，也是未來政策執行中不可忽視的隱形成本。各界在檢視該計畫時，應區分「硬體建設」與「軟體管理」的差異，避免將單純的設施更新視為解決校園空間不足的唯一路徑。
後續觀察重點應聚焦於115年計畫期滿後的整體成效評估，包括這12所學校在設施啟用後的實際使用率、社群滿意度，以及綠能發電對校園節能減碳的實際貢獻。教育局是否會針對這12校的成功經驗，進一步擴大至全市其他學校，將是觀察臺北市校園空間政策是否具備延續性的關鍵。此外，針對社群共享機制，相關單位是否會制定統一的收費或管理辦法，以確保校園教學環境與社群公共利益能取得平衡，亦是未來值得持續追蹤的政策亮點。
