  # Digital Resilience and Global Governance: Progress in Taiwan’s Tech Diplomacy and the UN Disaster Risk Framework
  
  > Minister of Digital Affairs Huang Yen-ching led a delegation to the U.S. to deepen Taiwan-U.S. technological cooperation, while the United Nations officially adopted a global disaster statistics framework. Both initiatives address current challenges and needs in global governance from the perspectives of digital security and risk management.
  
  In mid-September 2026, Minister of Digital Affairs Huang Yen-ching led a delegation to Washington, D.C., for a series of high-level visits and industry dialogues. This visit comes at a critical juncture marked by the rapid evolution of global artificial intelligence technology, escalating cybersecurity threats, and the profound impact of geopolitical risks on global technology supply chains. Digital technology is no longer merely an industrial development issue; it is now directly linked to national security, social resilience, and international competitiveness.
  
  Through direct engagement with U.S. government agencies, top-tier think tanks, and international tech giants such as Meta, OpenAI, and Amazon, Taiwan aims to strengthen strategic cooperation with the U.S. in areas including digital governance, cybersecurity, and communication resilience. This effort is intended to share Taiwan’s experience while simultaneously staying abreast of the latest U.S. policy trends in the field of technological security.
  
  Regarding international governance, the United Nations Sustainable Development Group recently officially adopted the first "Global Disaster-Related Statistics Framework" (G-DRSF). The establishment of this framework aims to provide countries worldwide with a unified set of statistical standards to conduct quantitative analysis on the frequency of disasters, the extent of their impact, economic losses, and potential risk factors. This move will not only effectively improve the precision of disaster risk management for various nations but also strengthen the scientific foundation for policy formulation. The statistical framework will serve as a key monitoring and reporting tool to support the Sendai Framework for Disaster Risk Reduction, the UN Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs), and the Global Goal on Adaptation, providing data support for global responses to climate change and sudden-onset disasters.
  
  Although the two aforementioned events focus on digital technology and disaster governance respectively, both reflect an urgent global need for "standardization" and "cooperative mechanisms" in the face of uncertainty. However, specific information regarding the concrete agreements and follow-up execution details of Minister Huang’s visit to the U.S. remains in the preliminary disclosure stage, and the U.S. side has not yet fully disclosed the details of its responses to specific technological issues. Similarly, while the UN global disaster statistics framework has been officially adopted, how member states will translate this framework into domestic statistical workflows, and the difficulties of implementation across countries with varying levels of economic development, remain key points for future observation.
  
  Furthermore, international media outlets such as the Associated Press (AP News) continue to play their non-partisan role in disseminating information when reporting on such transnational policies and technological developments, ensuring that the global public can access objective policy progress.
  
  The advancement of digital resilience and disaster statistics frameworks has a profound impact on global regional economies and social stability. As a core participant in the global technology supply chain, Taiwan’s deepened cooperation with the U.S. helps establish a more robust digital defense mechanism, reducing the risk of technological decoupling caused by geopolitics. At the same time, the UN-promoted global disaster statistics framework provides a common language for both developing and developed nations, helping the international community allocate resources more precisely and conduct risk early-warning when facing climate disasters. These policy initiatives are expected to drive the standardization and upgrading of related industries and prompt governments to invest more resources into building digital and environmental resilience within public governance.
  
  Perspectives on these developments vary. Some analyses suggest that the deep alignment between Taiwan and the U.S. in AI and cybersecurity will significantly enhance Taiwan’s influence in international digital governance. However, other viewpoints caution that technological cooperation must balance commercial interests with national security, and that over-interpreting the substantive results of a single visit may overlook the complexities of international political reality. Similarly, while the UN statistical framework is symbolic, its effectiveness depends on the willingness of individual countries to implement it and their data collection capabilities; it should not be viewed as a panacea for all disaster risks.
  
  Furthermore, while global information dissemination is highly instantaneous, commercial dynamics in specific industries (such as the global streaming competition between Netflix and YouTube in the sports broadcasting market) are fundamentally different in nature from national policy governance, and the two should not be conflated in terms of their impact.
  
  Future observation will focus on three aspects: First, whether Taiwan and the U.S. will sign specific memorandums of understanding on digital cooperation or launch specific technology research and development projects following Minister Huang’s visit. Second, how UN member states will adjust their domestic disaster statistical databases according to the G-DRSF framework over the next year, and whether this framework can be effectively linked to national climate adaptation budgets. Finally, as global digital and environmental risks intertwine, how international organizations and tech companies reach a new balance between data sharing and privacy protection will be a core variable affecting the future direction of global governance.

# 數位韌性與全球治理：臺灣科技外交與聯合國防災架構進展

> 數位發展部部長林宜敬率團訪美深化臺美科技合作，同時聯合國正式透過全球災害統計框架，兩者分別從數位安全與風險管理層面，回應當前全球治理的挑戰與需求。


數位發展部部長林宜敬於2026年9月中旬率領代表團前往美國華盛頓特區，展開一系列高層訪問與產業對話。此次出訪背景正值全球人工智慧技術快速演進、網路安全威脅升級，以及地緣政治風險對全球科技供應鏈產生深遠影響的關鍵時刻。數位科技已不僅是單純的產業發展議題，更直接牽動國家安全、社會韌性及國際競爭力。
透過與美國政府部門、頂尖智庫以及Meta、OpenAI與Amazon等國際科技巨擘的直接交流，臺灣旨在強化臺美在數位治理、資安防護及通訊韌性等領域的戰略合作，藉此分享臺灣經驗，並同步掌握美方在科技安全領域的最新政策動向。
在國際治理層面，聯合國永續發展集團近期正式透過首個「全球災害相關統計框架」（G-DRSF）。該框架的建立，旨在為全球各國提供一套統一的統計標準，針對災害發生的頻率、影響程度、經濟損失以及潛在風險因素進行量化分析。此舉不僅能有效提升各國在災害風險管理上的精確度，亦能強化政策制定的科學基礎。該統計框架將作為支撐《仙台減少災害風險框架》、聯合國永續發展目標（SDGs）以及全球適應目標的重要監測與報告工具，為全球應對氣候變遷與突發性災害提供資料支援。
儘管上述兩項事件分別聚焦於數位科技與防災治理，但皆反映出全球治理體系在面對不確定性時，對於「標準化」與「合作機制」的迫切需求。然而，目前針對林宜敬部長訪美的具體協議內容與後續執行細節，各界資訊仍處於初步揭露階段，美方對特定科技議題的具體回應細節尚未完全公開。同樣地，聯合國全球災害統計框架雖然已正式透過，但各成員國如何將此框架轉化為國內具體的統計作業流程，以及在不同經濟發展程度國家間的落地執行難度，仍是未來觀察重點。
此外，國際媒體生態如美聯社（AP News）等機構，在報導此類跨國政策與科技動態時，持續發揮其非黨派的資訊傳遞角色，確保全球公眾能獲取客觀的政策進展。
數位韌性與防災統計框架的推進，對全球區域經濟與社會穩定具有深遠影響。臺灣作為全球科技供應鏈的核心參與者，透過與美方的深化合作，有助於建立更為穩固的數位防禦機制，降低地緣政治帶來的科技斷鏈風險。與此同時，聯合國推動的全球災害統計框架，則為開發中國家與已開發國家提供了一套共同語言，有助於國際社會在面對氣候災難時，能更精準地分配資源並進行風險預警。這些政策舉措預期將帶動相關產業的標準化升級，並促使各國政府在公共治理中投入更多資源於數位與環境韌性的建構。
對於上述進展，各界觀點不盡相同。部分分析認為，臺灣與美國在AI與資安領域的深度對接，將能顯著提升臺灣在國際數位治理的話語權；然而，亦有觀點提醒，科技合作需平衡商業利益與國家安全，過度解讀單次訪問的實質成果可能忽略了國際政治現實的複雜性。同樣地，聯合國的統計框架雖具備指標性意義，但其成效取決於各國的執行意願與資料蒐集能力，不應將其視為解決所有災難風險的萬靈丹。
此外，全球資訊傳播的即時性雖高，但針對特定產業（如體育賽事轉播市場中Netflix與YouTube的全球串流競爭）的商業動態，與國家級政策治理的性質截然不同，兩者在影響層面上不可混為一談。
後續觀察重點將聚焦於以下三個面向：首先，林宜敬部長訪美後，臺美雙方是否會簽署具體的數位合作備忘錄或啟動特定技術研發專案；其次，聯合國成員國在未來一年內，如何根據G-DRSF框架調整其國內的災難統計資料庫，以及該框架是否能有效連結至各國的氣候適應預算；最後，隨著全球數位與環境風險的交織，國際組織與科技企業在資料共享與隱私保護之間，將如何達成新的平衡，將是影響未來全球治理走向的核心變數。
