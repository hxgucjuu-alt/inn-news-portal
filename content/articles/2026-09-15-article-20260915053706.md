  # Strategic Analysis of ASEAN’s Maritime Security and Mekong Regional Cooperation
  
  > Facing geopolitical challenges, ASEAN leaders have supported the establishment of a maritime center in the Philippines to strengthen regional coordination. Meanwhile, as the Lancang-Mekong Cooperation mechanism enters its tenth year, regional water diplomacy and cross-border economic integration have become core issues in Southeast Asian governance.
  
  Southeast Asia has recently demonstrated high momentum in the two major areas of maritime security and cross-border watershed governance. As geopolitical tensions rise, maritime security challenges have become a priority for regional governance. According to reports from the Central News Agency (CNA), ASEAN leaders have officially expressed support for the establishment of an "ASEAN Maritime Centre" in the Philippines, aiming to strengthen maritime security cooperation and coordination mechanisms among member states through an institutionalized platform. At the same time, governance of the Mekong River basin has entered a new phase, with the Lancang-Mekong Cooperation (LMC) mechanism marking the tenth anniversary of its leaders' meetings this year.
  
  As a cross-border water system connecting six countries, the LMC involves not only water resource management but also regional economic integration and infrastructure construction, becoming a strategic focus of common concern for both inland and coastal Southeast Asian nations.
  
  Comprehensive public information indicates that the operation of ASEAN and Mekong-related mechanisms has shown a trend toward high institutionalization. In the maritime domain, ASEAN member states are attempting to build a more resilient regional security architecture through the establishment of specialized centers to address increasingly complex maritime disputes and non-traditional security threats. Regarding watershed governance, the Mekong River Commission (MRC), an intergovernmental organization established in 1995 under the "Mekong Agreement," has long played the role of a platform for water diplomacy and knowledge sharing, coordinating the balance between basin development and ecological protection among Cambodia, Laos, Thailand, and Vietnam.
  
  The LMC mechanism has further elevated the economic and infrastructure connectivity of this region to the level of national development strategy, emphasizing the promotion of common regional economic development through cross-border cooperation.
  
  However, there are significant differences in how various sources evaluate the focus and effectiveness of regional cooperation. Perspectives from Guangming Online emphasize the achievements of the LMC in economic development and regional connectivity, viewing it as a model for deepening regional cooperation; meanwhile, the official positioning of the MRC focuses on the sustainability and scientific basis of water resource management, emphasizing the technical and diplomatic functions of multilateral dialogue. As for the establishment of the ASEAN Maritime Centre, it reflects the tendency of regional countries to maintain sovereignty and regional stability by strengthening internal coordination when facing external security challenges.
  
  What remains to be confirmed is how the maritime center will define the scope of its authority in actual operations, and whether ASEAN countries can maintain a consistent strategic stance on maritime security issues in the face of great power competition remains a focus for regional observers.
  
  These developments have profound implications for Southeast Asia's industries, policies, and society. In terms of maritime security, the establishment of the maritime center is expected to promote information sharing and joint training among regional naval and coast guard units, helping to ensure the safety of key shipping lanes and thereby stabilizing the regional trade environment. In the Mekong basin, the dual-track approach of the LMC and the MRC influences the investments of riparian states in hydropower, agricultural irrigation, and shipping infrastructure, but it has also sparked social debates regarding unequal water distribution and environmental changes. For the tens of millions of people who rely on the Mekong for survival, the effectiveness of these transnational cooperation mechanisms is directly related to their food security and economic livelihoods.
  
  Despite the continued promotion of various cooperation mechanisms, regional governance still faces multiple limitations. Reports from Frontier Myanmar point out that although external powers such as the United States attempt to strengthen their engagement with Southeast Asia through diplomatic visits and commitments, the scale of government-backed infrastructure investment often struggles to compare with the resources provided by China. Furthermore, the diverse positions of ASEAN member states on foreign policy often cause the region to be constrained by the "ASEAN Way" consensus-based mechanism when dealing with major geopolitical issues, leading to slow decision-making processes.
  
  Various sectors should not over-interpret the ability of a single cooperation mechanism to completely resolve complex regional contradictions, especially on sensitive issues involving territorial sovereignty and resource competition, where the space for diplomatic dialogue and substantive cooperation remains full of variables.
  
  Subsequent observations will focus on the specific operational structure of the "ASEAN Maritime Centre" and whether it can effectively integrate the maritime security resources of various countries. At the same time, as the LMC enters its next decade, how to balance the development needs of member states with basin ecological protection will be the key to testing the sustainability of the mechanism. Additionally, the competition for influence between the United States and China in Southeast Asia, and how ASEAN maintains strategic autonomy between the two powers, will continue to affect the direction of regional governance. All parties should continue to pay attention to the implementation progress of relevant policies and the actual resilience of these transnational mechanisms in responding to climate change, cross-border crime, and economic fluctuations.

# 東協強化海事安全與湄公河區域合作之戰略佈局分析

> 面對地緣政治挑戰，東協領袖支援於菲律賓設立海事中心以強化區域協調；同時，瀾湄合作機制步入十週年，區域水資源外交與跨境經濟整合成為東南亞治理的核心議題。


東南亞地區近期在海事安全與跨境流域治理兩大領域展現高度動能。隨地緣政治緊張局勢升溫，海上安全挑戰已成為區域治理的優先事項。根據中央社報導，東協（ASEAN）領袖已正式表態支援在菲律賓設立「東協海事中心」（ASEAN Maritime Centre），旨在透過機制化的平臺，強化成員國間的海上安全合作與協調機制。與此同時，湄公河流域的治理亦進入新階段，瀾滄江—湄公河合作（瀾湄合作）機制今年邁入領導人會議召開十週年。
作為連結六國的跨境水系，瀾湄合作不僅涉及水資源管理，亦涵蓋區域經濟整合與基礎設施建設，成為東南亞內陸與沿海國家共同關注的戰略焦點。
綜合多項公開資訊顯示，東協與湄公河相關機制的運作已呈現高度制度化趨勢。在海事領域，東協成員國透過設立專責中心，試圖建立更具韌性的區域安全架構，以應對日益複雜的海域爭端與非傳統安全威脅。而在流域治理方面，湄公河委員會（MRC）作為1995年依據《湄公河協定》成立的政府間組織，長期扮演水資源外交與知識共享平臺的角色，協調柬埔寨、寮國、泰國與越南在流域開發與生態保護間的平衡。
瀾湄合作機制則進一步將此區域的經濟與基礎設施連結提升至國家發展戰略層次，強調透過跨境合作推動區域經濟的共同發展。
然而，各來源對於區域合作的側重點與成效評估存在顯著差異。光明網的觀點側重於瀾湄合作在經濟發展與區域連結上的成就，將其視為區域合作深化的典範；而MRC的官方定位則聚焦於水資源管理的永續性與科學基礎，強調多邊對話的技術性與外交功能。至於東協海事中心的設立，則反映了區域國家在應對外部安全挑戰時，傾向透過強化內部協調來維護主權與區域穩定。
目前尚待確認的是，海事中心在實際運作中如何界定其許可權範疇，以及在面對大國博弈時，東協各國能否在海事安全議題上維持一致的戰略立場，仍是區域觀察家關注的焦點。
上述發展對東南亞的產業、政策與社會影響深遠。在海事安全方面，海事中心的設立預計將促進區域內海軍與海巡單位的資訊共享與聯合訓練，有助於保障關鍵航道的安全，進而穩定區域貿易環境。在湄公河流域，瀾湄合作與MRC的雙軌並行，影響了沿岸國家在水力發電、農業灌溉與航運基礎設施上的投入，但也同時引發了關於水資源分配不均與生態環境變遷的社會辯論。對於依賴湄公河生存的數千萬人口而言，這些跨國合作機制的成效，直接關係到其糧食安全與經濟生計。
儘管各項合作機制持續推動，但區域治理仍面臨多重限制。Frontier Myanmar的報導指出，儘管美國等外部強權試圖透過外交訪問與承諾加強對東南亞的參與，但在政府支援的基礎設施投資規模上，往往難以與中國提供的資源相比擬。此外，東協成員國在對外政策上的多元立場，使得區域在處理重大地緣政治議題時，常受限於「東協方式」（ASEAN Way）的共識決機制，導致決策程式緩慢。
各界不應過度解讀單一合作機制能完全解決複雜的區域矛盾，特別是在涉及領土主權與資源競爭的敏感議題上，外交對話與實質合作的空間仍充滿變數。
後續觀察重點將聚焦於「東協海事中心」的具體運作架構，以及其是否能有效整合各國海上安全資源。同時，瀾湄合作進入下一個十年，如何平衡成員國間的發展需求與流域生態保護，將是檢驗該機制永續性的關鍵。此外，美國與中國在東南亞的影響力競逐，以及東協如何在兩強之間維持戰略自主，將持續牽動區域治理的走向。各方應持續關注相關政策的落實進度，以及這些跨國機制在應對氣候變遷、跨境犯罪與經濟波動時的實際韌性。
