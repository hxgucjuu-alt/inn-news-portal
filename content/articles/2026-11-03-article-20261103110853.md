  # Global Climate Change and Digital Resilience: Sustainable Governance for Cultural Institutions and Challenges to Information Infrastructure
  
  > Facing the global climate crisis and the vulnerability of digital systems, cultural institutions are actively exploring sustainable governance models. Simultaneously, global enterprises are grappling with operational challenges caused by digital disruptions, highlighting the critical importance of infrastructure resilience.
  
  The New Taipei City Art Museum (NTCAM) hosted the international forum "Coordinates of Tomorrow: Institutional Sustainability and Public Connection" on October 31, 2026. The event brought together experts in the cultural sector from Australia, Japan, South Korea, and Taiwan to discuss how cultural institutions should transform amidst the multiple impacts of the climate crisis, technological evolution, and global political and economic shifts. At the same time, the stability of global digital infrastructure has become a focal point; recent large-scale information technology (IT) outages have hindered global business operations, significantly affecting critical services such as air travel and media broadcasting, demonstrating that while global society is highly dependent on digital systems, it faces unprecedented systemic risks.
  
  According to multiple sources, the function of cultural institutions has shifted from traditional collection and exhibition to serving as infrastructure that strengthens social adaptability and accumulates public trust. Lai Hsiang-ling, Director of NTCAM, emphasized at the forum that cultural institutions must reflect on how to preserve sustainable cultural and ecological assets for the next generation.
  
  In the digital realm, media outlets such as Sky News have reported that recent global Windows computer system outages caused widespread operational shutdowns. This not only affected business operations but also led to the temporary interruption of public information delivery channels, such as live television broadcasts, highlighting the vulnerability of global information networks to single points of failure and the importance for all types of institutions to simultaneously strengthen risk management and backup mechanisms during the process of digital transformation.
  
  Regarding the specific details and assessments of these events, different sources provide information from various perspectives. At the level of the cultural forum, participants focused on long-term "sustainable governance" and "local collaboration," emphasizing institutional adaptation strategies in the face of climate change, which is a long-term and ongoing policy discussion. In contrast, the global IT outage represents a sudden technical disaster, with an impact scope covering multiple industries including aviation, media, and finance.
  
  What remains to be confirmed is whether such digital disruptions will directly hinder the digital transformation programs of cultural institutions, and how, against the backdrop of climate change leading to frequent extreme weather, cultural institutions can ensure the continuity of their digital assets and physical collections during power or network instability.
  
  For relevant industries and policies, the transformation strategies of cultural institutions will influence future storage models for cultural assets. In particular, incorporating "ecological assets" into the scope of governance is expected to encourage more cultural institutions to invest in green operations and energy transition. On the other hand, the impact of the global IT outage on enterprises has prompted governments and multinational corporations to re-examine the security of digital supply chains and may drive stricter cybersecurity regulations, requiring critical infrastructure providers to establish more resilient backup systems to reduce excessive reliance on a single operating system or software platform.
  
  However, there are differing views on digital transformation and sustainable development. Some argue that cultural institutions should prioritize resources for climate adaptation in the physical environment to cope with physical damage caused by extreme weather; another perspective emphasizes that digitalization is key to enhancing public connection and educational accessibility and should not be stalled by short-term technical risks. Furthermore, one should not over-interpret the long-term impact of a single IT outage on the global digital architecture, as technological evolution—from the telegraph to the internet—has always been accompanied by an iteration of risks and opportunities.
  
  For global news organizations such as the Associated Press (AP News), digital transformation is not only a technical upgrade but also a core strategy for maintaining the resilience of factual reporting and global information dissemination.
  
  Future observation will focus on how cultural institutions implement the sustainable governance blueprint proposed in "Coordinates of Tomorrow," particularly by integrating climate change adaptation strategies into daily operations. At the same time, the stability of global digital infrastructure will continue to receive attention from all sectors, especially regarding backup mechanisms and risk diversification strategies for critical services. In the future, balancing the digital storage of cultural assets with the sustainable maintenance of the physical environment will become a common challenge for global public policy and institutional governance. All sectors should continue to monitor how technological evolution affects the resilience of social infrastructure and assess whether the measures taken by various types of institutions, amidst the intertwining of climate change and digital risks, are sufficient to support long-term social stability and cultural heritage.

# 全球氣候變遷與數位韌性：文化機構永續治理與資訊基礎設施挑戰

> 面對全球氣候危機與數位系統脆弱性，文化機構正積極探索永續治理模式，同時全球企業亦面臨數位中斷帶來的營運挑戰，突顯基礎設施韌性的重要性。


新北市美術館（新美館）於二〇二六年十月三十一日舉辦「明日座標：機構永續與公共連結」國際論壇，匯集澳洲、日本、韓國及臺灣等多國文化領域專家，共同探討在氣候危機、科技演化及全球政經變局的多重衝擊下，文化機構應如何轉型。與此同時，全球數位基礎設施的穩定性亦成為焦點，近期大規模的資訊科技（IT）中斷事件，導致全球企業營運受阻，包括航空交通與媒體廣播等關鍵服務均受到顯著影響，顯示全球社會在高度依賴數位系統的同時，面臨著前所未有的系統性風險。
根據多方來源確認，文化機構的職能已從傳統的典藏與展示，轉向強化社會調適能力與累積公共信任的基礎設施。新美館館長賴香伶於論壇中強調，文化機構必須反思如何為下一代留存可持續的文化與生態資產。
在數位領域，Sky News 等媒體報導指出，近期全球範圍內的 Windows 電腦系統中斷事件，造成了廣泛的營運停擺，不僅影響企業運作，亦導致電視直播等公共資訊傳遞管道一度中斷，凸顯了全球資訊網路在面臨單點故障時的脆弱性，以及各類機構在推動數位轉型過程中，必須同步強化風險管理與備援機制的重要性。
關於事件的具體細節與評估，不同來源提供了不同面向的資訊。文化論壇層面，與會者聚焦於長期性的「永續治理」與「在地協作」，強調機構在氣候變遷下的調適策略，這是一項長期且持續的政策性探討。相對地，全球 IT 中斷事件則屬於突發性的技術災難，其影響範圍涵蓋航空、媒體與金融等多個產業。
目前尚待確認之處在於，這類數位中斷事件是否會對文化機構的數位轉型程式產生直接阻礙，以及在氣候變遷加劇導致極端天氣頻發的背景下，文化機構如何確保其數位資產與實體館藏在電力或網路不穩定的情況下，仍能維持運作的連續性。
對於相關產業與政策而言，文化機構的轉型策略將影響未來文化資產的儲存模式，特別是將「生態資產」納入治理範疇，預計將促使更多文化機構投入綠色營運與能源轉型。另一方面，全球 IT 中斷事件對企業的影響，則促使各國政府與跨國企業重新審視數位供應鏈的安全性，並可能推動更嚴格的資通安全法規，要求關鍵基礎設施提供者建立更具韌性的備援系統，以降低對單一作業系統或軟體平臺的過度依賴。
然而，各界對於數位轉型與永續發展的觀點存在差異。部分觀點認為，文化機構應優先投入資源於實體環境的氣候調適，以應對極端天氣帶來的物理損害；另一派觀點則強調，數位化是提升公共連結與教育普及的關鍵，不應因短期技術風險而停滯。此外，不應過度解讀單一 IT 中斷事件對全球數位架構的長期影響，因為技術演進如電報至網際網路的發展，始終伴隨著風險與機會的迭代。
對於像美聯社（AP News）這類全球新聞組織而言，數位轉型不僅是技術升級，更是維持事實報導與全球資訊傳遞韌性的核心策略。
後續觀察重點將聚焦於文化機構如何落實「明日座標」中所提出的永續治理藍圖，特別是將氣候變遷的調適策略整合至日常營運中。同時，全球數位基礎設施的穩定性將持續受到各界關注，特別是針對關鍵服務的備援機制與風險分散策略。未來，如何平衡文化資產的數位化儲存與實體環境的永續維護，將成為全球公共政策與機構治理的共同挑戰。各界應持續監測科技演化如何影響社會基礎設施的韌性，並評估在氣候變遷與數位風險交織下，各型別機構所採取的因應措施是否足以支撐長期的社會穩定與文化傳承。
