  # Regional Security Challenges After the Mérida Initiative: Shifts in Central American Gangs and Foreign Policy
  
  > This article examines the regional security impact of the Mérida Initiative on Central America, exploring the complex challenges posed by the expansion of Mexican drug cartels, the rise of local gangs, and the shift in Guatemala's foreign policy on regional stability.
  
  The Mérida Initiative, also known as "Plan Mexico," was a security cooperation agreement signed by the United States, the Mexican government, and various Central American nations, implemented from 2007 to 2021. The initiative aimed to combat drug trafficking, transnational crime, and money laundering in the region through cross-border cooperation, while strengthening the professional capabilities of national law enforcement agencies. However, following the formal conclusion of the initiative in 2021, the security landscape in Central America has not stabilized; instead, it faces even more severe social and public safety challenges due to structural changes in the global drug supply chain.
  
  Multiple media sources have confirmed that the sphere of influence of transnational drug cartels has expanded southward from Mexico into Central American countries. This expansion has forced local governments to adopt unconventional measures, including deploying the military to support local police forces, in order to address the worsening issues of drug smuggling and violent crime. According to observations by The Christian Science Monitor, after entering Central America, Mexican drug cartels have actively established connections with local criminal organizations, particularly the notorious "Maras" gangs. These gangs have formed alliances with local crime bosses, leading to a significant rise in street crime in Central America and directly impacting regional social stability.
  
  Although there is a consensus regarding the expansion routes of drug cartels, there are subtle differences in how various sources describe the specific depth of cooperation between the "Maras" gangs and transnational drug cartels. Some reports emphasize that these gangs have become local operational agents for the cartels, responsible for grassroots logistics and violent intimidation; however, other analyses point out that the organizational structure of these gangs is loose, and their links to drug cartels are based more on short-term cooperation for mutual benefit rather than a rigid hierarchical relationship. Furthermore, assessments vary regarding whether the Mérida Initiative effectively curbed gang expansion in its later stages, a matter that requires cross-verification with more regional security data.
  
  These security issues have had a profound impact on social and public policy in Central America. First, the frequent intervention of the military in domestic security has sparked debates over human rights protections and whether militarized law enforcement might weaken democratic institutions. Second, the sense of social insecurity caused by drug-related violence has further fueled migration waves within the region, becoming a core variable affecting diplomatic relations between Central America and the United States.
  
  Meanwhile, countries like Guatemala have also seen a significant shift in foreign policy. For instance, presidential candidates have openly advocated for promoting close ties with Israel, attempting to position Guatemala as the "Israel of Latin America." This move is viewed as a strategic adjustment in seeking international security assistance and diplomatic support, reflecting the country's attempt to find a new security fulcrum within the complex regional geopolitics.
  
  Regarding the aforementioned situation, experts warn against over-interpreting the immediate benefits of a single foreign policy shift on regional security. While the historical ties between Guatemala and Israel are based on historical connections and the support of a large Christian population within Guatemala, whether such diplomatic alliances can be translated into actual crime-fighting capabilities remains limited by domestic political stability and economic constraints. Furthermore, simplifying Central America's security issues into mere "gang confrontations" or "drug cartel expansion" may overlook structural factors such as poverty, a lack of educational resources, and ineffective government governance. How countries establish autonomous and sustainable security defense mechanisms following the end of the Mérida Initiative remains a focus of concern for all parties.
  
  Future observation should focus on the development of alternative security agreements among Central American nations in the post-Mérida era. In particular, the social backlash following government military intervention in security, as well as the latest assessments by the international community regarding the extent of "Maras" gang infiltration, are key. Additionally, whether the foreign policy adjustments of countries like Guatemala will trigger a chain reaction in other regional nations, or further alter Central America's voting tendencies and diplomatic stances within the Organization of American States (OAS), will be an important indicator for observing the future geopolitical direction of Central America.

# 梅里達倡議後的區域安全挑戰：中美洲幫派與外交政策的變局

> 本文整理梅里達倡議對中美洲的區域安全影響，探討墨西哥販毒集團擴張、當地幫派崛起及瓜地馬拉外交政策轉向對區域穩定帶來的複雜挑戰。


梅里達倡議（Mérida Initiative），又稱「墨西哥計畫」，是一項由美國、墨西哥政府及中美洲各國共同簽署的安全合作協議，執行期間自 2007 年至 2021 年。該倡議旨在透過跨國合作，打擊區域內的毒品販運、跨國犯罪及洗錢活動，並強化各國執法機構的專業能力。然而，隨著該倡議於 2021 年正式結束，中美洲地區的安全域性勢並未因協議的終止而趨於平緩，反而因全球販毒供應鏈的結構性變化，面臨更為嚴峻的社會治安與公共安全挑戰。
多個媒體來源共同確認，跨國販毒集團的勢力範圍已從墨西哥向南擴張至中美洲各國。這種擴張迫使當地政府不得不採取非常規手段，包括調動軍隊支援地方警力，以應對日益嚴重的毒品走私與暴力犯罪。根據《基督教科學箴言報》的觀察，墨西哥販毒集團在進入中美洲後，積極與當地的犯罪組織建立連結，特別是惡名昭彰的「馬拉斯」（Maras）幫派。這些幫派組織與當地犯罪首腦結盟，導致中美洲地區的街頭犯罪率顯著上升，對區域社會穩定造成直接衝擊。
儘管各界對於販毒集團的擴張路徑有共識，但在「馬拉斯」幫派與跨國販毒集團的具體合作深度上，各來源的描述存在細微差異。部分報導強調這些幫派已成為販毒集團的在地執行代理人，負責基層的物流與暴力威懾；然而，亦有分析指出，這些幫派的組織結構鬆散，其與販毒集團的連結更多是基於利益交換的短期合作，而非嚴密的上下游隸屬關係。此外，對於梅里達倡議在執行後期是否有效遏制了幫派擴張，各方評估不一，尚待更多區域安全資料的交叉驗證。
這些安全議題對中美洲的社會與公共政策產生了深遠影響。首先，軍隊頻繁介入國內治安工作，引發了關於人權保障與軍事化執法是否會削弱民主制度的辯論。其次，毒品暴力導致的社會不安全感，進一步推動了區域內的移民潮，成為影響中美洲與美國外交關係的核心變數。
與此同時，瓜地馬拉等國在外交政策上也出現了顯著轉向，例如總統候選人公開主張推動與以色列的緊密關係，試圖將瓜地馬拉定位為「拉丁美洲的以色列」，此舉被視為在尋求國際安全援助與外交支援上的策略性調整，反映出該國試圖在複雜的區域地緣政治中尋求新的安全支點。
針對上述局勢，專家提醒不應過度解讀單一外交政策轉向對區域安全帶來的即時效益。瓜地馬拉與以色列的歷史淵源，雖基於兩國歷史連結與瓜國境內龐大的基督徒人口支援，但這類外交結盟能否轉化為實際的打擊犯罪能力，仍受限於國內政治穩定度與經濟資源限制。此外，將中美洲的安全問題簡化為單純的「幫派對抗」或「販毒集團擴張」可能忽略了貧窮、教育資源匱乏及政府治理效能不彰等結構性因素。梅里達倡議結束後，各國如何建立自主且永續的治安防禦機制，仍是各界關注的焦點。
後續觀察重點應聚焦於中美洲各國在後梅里達時代的替代性安全協議發展。特別是各國政府在軍事介入治安後的社會反彈，以及國際社會對於中美洲「馬拉斯」幫派滲透程度的最新評估。此外，瓜地馬拉等國的外交政策調整是否會引發區域內其他國家的連鎖反應，或進一步改變中美洲在美洲國家組織（OAS）內的投票傾向與外交立場，將是未來觀察中美洲地緣政治走向的重要指標。
