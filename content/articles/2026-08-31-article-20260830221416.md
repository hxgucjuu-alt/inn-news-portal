  # U.S.-ASEAN Engagement: The Evolution of Regional Strategy from the Mekong Initiative to Virtual Summits
  
  > This article examines the history of U.S. engagement with the ASEAN region, from Mike Pompeo’s attendance at the Lower Mekong Initiative meeting in 2019 to Antony Blinken’s recent virtual dialogues with Southeast Asian leaders, analyzing the continuity of U.S. policy in Southeast Asia and its response strategies toward the Myanmar military junta.
  
  The U.S. strategic layout in Southeast Asia has long been conducted through multilateral diplomatic mechanisms, aimed at strengthening ties with ASEAN member states and promoting regional cooperation. Looking back to 2019, then-U.S. Secretary of State Mike Pompeo planned to travel to Bangkok, Thailand, to participate in the ASEAN Ministerial Meetings held from July 29 to August 3.
  
  According to official plans at the time, the focus of Pompeo’s trip was to chair dialogues related to the "Lower Mekong Initiative." This initiative aimed to expand U.S. influence in mainland Southeast Asian countries through investments in infrastructure, environment, and educational resources for the Mekong region, while demonstrating the U.S. commitment to the region's long-term development.
  
  With the evolution of the international landscape and the rise of digital diplomacy, the mode of interaction between the U.S. and Southeast Asia has also adjusted. Recent information indicates that U.S. Secretary of State Antony Blinken will engage in dialogues with Southeast Asian leaders via virtual meetings. The convening of such meetings demonstrates that even when physical attendance is not possible, the U.S. still attempts to maintain high-level communication channels with ASEAN countries. These diplomatic activities are not only for discussing regional economic and security issues but are also an important means for the U.S. to maintain its regional presence in the post-pandemic era or in response to international diplomatic challenges.
  
  Regarding information on U.S.-Southeast Asia interactions, there are subtle differences in how various sources interpret diplomatic strategies. Concerning the virtual meeting between Blinken and Southeast Asian leaders, the focus of external attention is on how to handle the attendance of representatives from the Myanmar military junta. The U.S. government has clearly stated that participating in such virtual meetings does not imply granting legitimacy to the Myanmar military junta, but rather views it as a communication channel to convey specific messages to the junta. This rhetoric differs from the "commitment and cooperation" tone emphasized during the Pompeo era in 2019, reflecting that in the face of internal political turmoil in Myanmar, U.S. diplomatic means have shifted from simple regional development cooperation to more complex political pressure tests and message delivery.
  
  These engagements have had a profound impact on the Southeast Asian region. First, through mechanisms like the "Lower Mekong Initiative," the U.S. has directly influenced the industrial development and public policy directions of countries in the Mekong basin, particularly in water resource management and regional infrastructure construction. Second, the frequency and depth of U.S.-ASEAN interactions directly affect the balancing policies of Southeast Asian nations between the two major powers, the U.S. and China. When the U.S. Secretary of State personally participates in regional meetings or conducts virtual dialogues, it is often seen as a diplomatic endorsement of ASEAN’s collective security and regional stability, serving as an indicator for the regional investment environment and industrial chain layout.

Some argue that U.S. participation in ASEAN meetings can effectively curb the expansion of certain major powers in the region; yet others point out that U.S. policy is often constrained by domestic political shifts and unexpected international events, leading to commitments to Southeast Asia that sometimes appear to be "all talk and no action." Furthermore, regarding the engagement strategy toward the Myanmar military junta, there are concerns that this move could be exploited by the junta as a propaganda tool to claim international recognition. Therefore, one should not over-interpret the substantive influence of a single meeting, but rather view it as part of a long-term diplomatic game, rather than a turning point that will immediately change the regional situation.

# 美國與東協外互動動：從湄公河倡議到虛擬峰會的區域戰略演變

> 本文梳理美國與東協區域的外互動動歷史，從2019年蓬佩奧出席湄公河倡議會議，到近期布林肯透過虛擬會議與東南亞領導人對話，分析美國在東南亞區域政策的連續性與針對緬甸軍政府的應對策略。


美國在東南亞地區的戰略佈局長期以來透過多邊外交機制進行，旨在強化與東協（ASEAN）成員國的連結並推動區域合作。回顧2019年，時任美國國務卿蓬佩奧（Mike Pompeo）計畫前往泰國曼谷，參與7月29日至8月3日舉行的東協部長級會議。
根據當時的官方規劃，蓬佩奧此行的重點在於主持「下湄公河倡議」（Lower Mekong Initiative）相關對話，旨在透過針對湄公河區域的基礎建設、環境與教育資源投入，擴大美國在東南亞內陸國家的影響力，並展現美國對該區域長期承諾的決心。
隨著國際局勢演變與數位外交的興起，美國與東南亞的互動模式亦出現調整。近期資訊顯示，美國國務卿布林肯（Antony Blinken）將透過虛擬會議形式，與東南亞各國領導人進行對話。此類會議的召開，顯示美國即便在無法實體出席的情況下，仍試圖維持與東協各國的高層溝通管道。這類外交活動不僅是為了討論區域經濟與安全議題，更是美國在後疫情時代或因應國際外交挑戰時，維持區域存在感的重要手段。
在關於美國與東南亞互動的資訊中，各來源對於外交策略的解讀存在細微差異。針對布林肯與東南亞領導人的虛擬會議，外界關注焦點在於如何處理緬甸軍政府代表的出席問題。美國官方明確表態，參與此類虛擬會議並不代表給予緬甸軍政府合法性，而是將其視為向軍政府傳遞特定訊息的溝通管道。此說法與2019年蓬佩奧時期強調的「承諾與合作」基調有所不同，反映出美國在面對緬甸內部政局動盪時，外交手段已從單純的區域發展合作，轉向更為複雜的政治壓力測試與訊息傳遞。
這些外互動動對東南亞區域產生了深遠影響。首先，美國透過「下湄公河倡議」等機制，直接影響了湄公河流域國家的產業發展與公共政策走向，特別是在水資源管理與區域基礎設施建設上。其次，美國與東協的互動頻率與深度，直接牽動了東南亞國家在美中兩大強權間的平衡政策。當美國國務卿親自參與區域會議或進行虛擬對話，往往被視為對東協集體安全與區域穩定的一種外交背書，對於區域內的投資環境與產業鏈佈局具有指標性意義。
然而，針對美國的外交策略，各界觀點存在顯著分歧。部分觀點認為，美國透過參與東協會議能有效遏制特定大國在區域內的擴張；但亦有觀點指出，美國的政策常受限於國內政黨輪替與國際突發事件，導致對東南亞的承諾有時顯得「雷聲大雨點小」。此外，針對緬甸軍政府的接觸策略，外界亦擔憂此舉可能被軍政府利用，作為其宣稱獲得國際承認的宣傳工具。因此，外界不應過度解讀單一會議的實質影響力，應將其視為長期外交博弈的一環，而非立即改變區域局勢的轉捩點。
後續觀察重點應聚焦於美國在東協會議中，對於「下湄公河倡議」的後續資源挹注，以及在緬甸問題上，美國是否會採取更為強硬的制裁或外交孤立措施。此外，隨著數位科技在外交領域的應用，未來美國與東南亞的虛擬峰會是否會成為常態，以及這類會議能否在缺乏實體互動的情況下，有效解決跨境議題（如環境保護、公共衛生與區域供應鏈安全），將是未來檢視美國東南亞政策成效的關鍵指標。
