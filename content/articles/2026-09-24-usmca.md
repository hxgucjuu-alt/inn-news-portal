  # Major Changes Ahead for USMCA: U.S. Formally Initiates Ten-Year Countdown Mechanism
  
  > The U.S. government recently announced that it will not renew the United States-Mexico-Canada Agreement (USMCA) under its current terms, formally triggering a countdown mechanism following the expiration of the ten-year period. This move has sparked widespread concerns regarding the restructuring of North American trade rules, leaving supply chains facing high levels of uncertainty.
  
  The United States-Mexico-Canada Agreement (USMCA), serving as the core framework governing trade between the United States, Canada, and Mexico in North America, has recently entered a critical "institutional re-evaluation period." According to relevant information, the U.S. government has clearly indicated that it will not agree to renew the agreement under its current terms and has formally initiated the ten-year countdown mechanism following the agreement's entry into force. This decision is not only a re-examination of past trade rules but also marks a new, highly variable phase for North American regional economic integration.
  
  Looking back at the historical context, the USMCA was designed to replace the old North American Free Trade Agreement (NAFTA), aiming to balance manufacturing competition and economic interests among the three nations through strict rules of origin and labor standards. However, as the 2026 joint review deadline approaches, significant policy divergences have emerged regarding the effectiveness of the agreement's implementation and its future direction.
  
  Currently, progress confirmed by multiple sources indicates that the governments of the U.S., Mexico, and Canada have begun preparatory work for the subsequent arrangements of the USMCA. According to analysis by Vanzbon, the North American economic landscape entered an "institutional re-evaluation period" in late October 2025. The three countries are actively preparing for the 2026 joint review, which implies that regional trade barriers, rules of origin, and key manufacturing chains are highly likely to face large-scale structural adjustments in the coming years.
  
  Meanwhile, recent executive orders issued by the U.S. government, including policies such as designating English as the official language of the United States, reflect a tougher and more unilateral stance taken by Washington in handling domestic cultural identity and international trade negotiations, as it attempts to re-establish American dominance in both economic and social spheres.
  
  However, there are discrepancies in media reports regarding the specific motives behind the U.S. refusal to renew and the details of subsequent negotiations. ETtoday reported that the U.S. explicitly refused to renew the agreement under current terms and has initiated the countdown mechanism, emphasizing that the U.S. will continue to engage in follow-up consultations with its neighbors. Conversely, Vanzbon focuses more on describing this as a "preparatory process," interpreting it within the macroeconomic context of slowing U.S. economic growth and unresolved federal budget issues.
  
  Furthermore, information currently awaiting confirmation includes the specific scope of clauses the U.S. is demanding to amend, the official responses from the Canadian and Mexican governments regarding this "countdown mechanism," and whether the three nations will resort to retaliatory tariff measures should negotiations reach a stalemate. These key details remain inconclusive and will depend on the progress of subsequent diplomatic negotiations.
  
  This policy shift has profound implications for industries and public policy in North America. First, for the automotive and technology industries that rely on cross-border supply chains, changes to rules of origin could lead to a significant rise in production costs, potentially forcing companies to reconfigure their global production bases. Second, regarding public health and cross-border logistics, the instability of trade agreements could affect the efficiency of medical supply distribution. If border clearance procedures become more stringent due to trade disputes, it will directly impact the resilience of the North American public health system.
  
  Additionally, the delay in the U.S. federal budget and the associated risk of government shutdowns may further drag down the progress of trade negotiations, causing regional enterprises to face extremely high policy risk premiums when making long-term capital investments.
  
  Although the market feels anxious about the changes to the USMCA, experts remind stakeholders not to over-interpret the immediate, catastrophic impact of a single policy shift on the regional economy. First, the "ten-year countdown mechanism" itself is part of the agreement's design, intended to provide a legal framework for periodic review rather than serving as a termination order that inevitably leads to the agreement's invalidation. Second, the high degree of complementarity in manufacturing among the three North American nations makes a complete severance of trade ties politically and economically unfeasible.
  
  The limiting factor lies in the U.S. domestic political climate—such as nationalist policies like the push for English as the official language—which may increase communication costs at the negotiating table. This could lead to unnecessary entanglement between trade issues and social issues, thereby blurring the focus of economic negotiations, which observers consider the most unpredictable variable in future talks.
  
  Subsequent observations should focus on the progress of ministerial-level meetings among the three countries before the 2026 joint review, particularly the specific list of amendments proposed by the Office of the United States Trade Representative (USTR). Observers will closely monitor whether Canada and Mexico will form an alliance to counter U.S. unilateral pressure, and how U.S. domestic economic data (such as inflation and employment rates) will influence the toughness of its negotiating stance. Furthermore, if the U.S. federal budget crisis continues to worsen, whether it will lead to a reduction in administrative resources for trade negotiators, thereby affecting negotiation efficiency, will be a key indicator for gauging the stability of the North American trade situation in the coming months.
  
  The public and investors should continue to monitor official announcements released by the three governments to avoid misjudging the regional economic outlook due to fragmented information.

# 北美貿易協定USMCA面臨重大變革：美方正式啟動十年倒數機制

> 美國政府近期宣佈不按現行內容續簽《美墨加協定》（USMCA），正式啟動十年期滿後的倒數機制，此舉引發北美區域貿易規則重組的廣泛擔憂，產業鏈面臨高度不確定性。


《美墨加協定》（USMCA）作為規範北美地區美國、加拿大與墨西哥三國貿易往來的核心框架，近期進入了關鍵的「制度再評估期」。根據相關資訊顯示，美國政府已明確表示不同意依循現行條款直接續簽該協定，並正式啟動了協定生效後的十年倒數機制。這一決策不僅是對過去貿易規則的重新審視，更標誌著北美區域經濟整合進入了一個充滿變數的新階段。
回顧歷史背景，USMCA旨在取代舊有的北美自由貿易協定（NAFTA），透過嚴格的原產地規則與勞工標準，試圖平衡三國間的製造業競爭與經濟利益。然而，隨著2026年聯合評估期限的臨近，各國對於協定內容的執行成效與未來走向，已產生顯著的政策分歧。
目前，多方來源共同確認的進展顯示，美、墨、加三國政府已針對USMCA的後續安排展開籌備工作。根據《萬企幫》的分析，2025年10月下旬，北美經濟格局已進入「制度再評估期」，三國正積極為2026年的聯合評估進行準備，這意味著區域內的貿易壁壘、原產地規則以及關鍵製造鏈條，極有可能在未來幾年面臨大規模的結構性調整。
同時，美國政府近期發布的行政命令，包括將英語正式定為美國官方語言等政策舉措，也反映出華府在處理國內文化認同與國際貿易談判時，正採取更為強硬且單邊的立場，試圖在經濟與社會層面重新確立美國的主導權。
然而，關於美方拒絕續簽的具體動機與後續談判細節，各媒體報導間仍存在差異。ETtoday報導指出，美國明確拒絕按現行內容續簽，並已啟動倒數機制，強調美方將持續與鄰國進行後續協商；但《萬企幫》則更側重於描述這是一個「籌備程式」，並將其置於美國經濟成長放緩、聯邦預算遲遲未決的宏觀背景下進行解讀。
此外，目前尚待確認的資訊包括：美方具體要求修改的條款範圍、加拿大與墨西哥政府對於此項「倒數機制」的官方回應，以及若談判陷入僵局，三國是否會採取報復性關稅措施，這些關鍵細節目前均未有定論，仍需視後續外交談判的進展而定。
此項政策變動對北美地區的產業與公共政策影響深遠。首先，對於依賴跨境供應鏈的汽車製造業與科技產業而言，原產地規則的變更可能導致生產成本大幅上升，甚至迫使企業重新配置全球生產基地。其次，對於公共衛生與跨境物流而言，貿易協定的不穩定性可能影響醫療物資的流通效率，若邊境通關程式因貿易紛爭而趨於嚴格，將對北美公共衛生體系的應變能力造成直接衝擊。
此外，美國聯邦預算的延遲透過與政府部門停擺的風險，亦可能進一步拖累貿易談判的進度，使得區域內企業在進行長期資本投資時，面臨極高的政策風險溢價。
儘管市場對於USMCA的變革感到焦慮，但各界專家提醒，不應過度解讀單一政策變動對區域經濟的立即性毀滅影響。首先，所謂的「十年倒數機制」本身即是協定設計的一部分，旨在提供定期檢視的法律框架，而非必然導致協定作廢的終結令。其次，北美三國在製造業上的高度互補性，使得完全切斷貿易聯絡在政治與經濟上皆不可行。
限制因素在於，美國國內的政治氣候——如推動英語官方化等民族主義政策——可能在談判桌上增加溝通成本，使貿易議題與社會議題產生不必要的糾葛，進而模糊了經濟談判的焦點，這也是各方觀察家認為未來談判最難以預測的變數。
後續觀察重點應聚焦於2026年聯合評估前的三國部長級會議進展，特別是美國貿易代表署（USTR）提出的具體修正清單。觀察家將密切關注加拿大與墨西哥是否會組成聯盟，共同對抗美方的單邊壓力，以及美國國內經濟資料（如通膨率、就業率）對其談判強硬程度的影響。此外，若美國聯邦預算危機持續惡化，是否會導致貿易談判代表的行政資源遭到削減，進而影響談判效率，將是未來數月衡量北美貿易局勢穩定與否的關鍵指標。
公眾與投資人應持續關注三國政府發布的正式公告，避免因片面資訊而對區域經濟前景產生誤判。
