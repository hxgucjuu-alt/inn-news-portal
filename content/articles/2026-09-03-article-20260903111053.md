  # The United States and the Five Central Asian Nations Deepen Cooperation on Critical Minerals to Address Geopolitical Shifts
  
  > The United States recently held high-level dialogues with the leaders of the five Central Asian nations, including Kazakhstan and Uzbekistan, aimed at strengthening cooperation in critical mineral supply chains and balancing the traditional influence of Russia and China in the region, sparking regional geopolitical attention.
  
  The Central Asian region, comprising Kazakhstan, Uzbekistan, Tajikistan, Kyrgyzstan, and Turkmenistan, has long been a core arena for great power competition due to its unique geographical location and abundant energy resources. With the restructuring of global supply chains and the surge in demand for critical minerals, the U.S. government, led by President Trump, held high-level meetings with the heads of the five Central Asian states on November 6, 2025, marking Washington's attempt to initiate a "new era of engagement" in the region. This move is not only intended to ensure a stable supply of strategic resources but is also seen as a significant indicator of the United States reassessing the geostrategic value of Central Asia in the post-Cold War era and following the shifts in the situation in Afghanistan.
  
  In the past, the region maintained close military and security ties with Russia and deep economic cooperation with China. The active intervention of the United States now suggests that the five Central Asian nations will face more complex balancing challenges in their foreign policies.
  
  Synthesizing reports from Reuters and relevant international observation sources, it is confirmed that the United States is actively expanding its cooperation framework with the five Central Asian nations, with core issues focused on the development and trade of "critical minerals." This policy shift contrasts with the backdrop of the past few years, when Russia frequently engaged in high-level calls and security dialogues with Uzbekistan, Tajikistan, and Kazakhstan in response to the political turmoil in Afghanistan. The five Central Asian nations have long adopted multilateral diplomacy to balance regional security and economic development; the U.S. intervention undoubtedly introduces new variables to the region's resource development and international cooperation.
  
  For the heads of state, how to transform abundant mineral resources into national economic advantages while introducing U.S. capital without compromising relationships with traditional neighbors has become a top priority on the diplomatic agenda.
  
  However, media sources differ regarding the expected benefits and specific implementation details of this cooperation. Some reports on tourism and cultural exchange point out that the five Central Asian nations remain highly dependent on existing Silk Road trade routes for logistics and transportation infrastructure, and possess deep historical ties with Russia, which poses technical and logistical challenges to the supply chain decoupling or redirection that the U.S. seeks to promote. Furthermore, analysis from Voice of America emphasizes that the spillover effects of the situation in Afghanistan remain the most significant security concern for Central Asian countries. Compared to the U.S. focus on economic strategies regarding mineral resources, Central Asian nations are more concerned with border security and regional stability.
  
  What remains to be confirmed is the specific scale of U.S. investment in the critical minerals sector, and how the governments of the Central Asian nations will specifically implement various agreements with the U.S. while facing potential political pressure from Russia and China.
  
  This adjustment in geopolitical and economic policy will have a profound impact on the industrial structure of the Central Asian region. For a long time, the economic development of the five Central Asian nations has relied on energy exports and traditional agriculture. If they can successfully introduce critical mineral development technology through cooperation with the U.S., it will help upgrade their industries and enhance their status in the global supply chain. Regarding international tourism and business travel, as the visibility of countries like Kazakhstan and Uzbekistan on the international stage increases, related business inspections and tourism supporting facilities are also developing rapidly.
  
  However, policy shifts may also trigger internal regional competition. How these countries strike a balance between attracting U.S. capital and maintaining regional economic integration will be a key variable affecting Central Asia's economic development in the coming years.
  
  Although the United States has demonstrated an intention to deepen cooperation, analysts caution against over-interpreting this as a sign that the Central Asian region will completely lean toward the U.S. The core of the five Central Asian nations' foreign policy lies in "multi-vector diplomacy," seeking maximum benefits among Russia, China, the U.S., and Europe. Limiting factors include the differences between the political systems and economic structures of Central Asian countries and Western standards, as well as the geographical landlocked nature that makes their economic dependence on neighboring countries difficult to sever in the short term. Additionally, Russia's military presence in the region and China's dominant position in infrastructure investment remain realities that the U.S. cannot ignore. Therefore, this cooperation is more of a strategic hedge rather than a choice of a single camp.
  
  Subsequent observations should focus on 2026 and beyond, specifically whether these countries will sign concrete mineral extraction contracts and whether the U.S. will further expand infrastructure investment in the region. At the same time, it is necessary to closely monitor the official reactions of Russia and China to such high-level interactions, as well as how the five Central Asian nations navigate diplomatic operations between resource sovereignty and great power games. For all parties concerned with Central Asian development, the process of the region transforming from a traditional "Silk Road tourism hotspot" to a "strategic hub for critical minerals" will be an important weather vane for future changes in the global geopolitical and economic landscape.

# 美國與中亞五國深化關鍵礦產合作，應對地緣政治變局

> 美國近期與哈薩克、烏茲別克等中亞五國領導人展開高層對話，旨在強化關鍵礦產供應鏈合作，並平衡俄羅斯與中國在該地區的傳統影響力，引發區域地緣政治關注。


中亞地區包含哈薩克、烏茲別克、塔吉克、吉爾吉斯及土庫曼等五國，長期以來因其獨特的地理位置與豐富的能源資源，成為大國角力的核心地帶。隨著全球供應鏈重組及關鍵礦產需求激增，美國政府於二〇二五年十一月六日，由川普總統與中亞五國元首進行高層會晤，標誌著華盛頓試圖在該地區開創「互動新時代」。此舉不僅是為了確保戰略資源的穩定供應，更被視為美國在後冷戰及阿富汗局勢變動後，重新評估中亞地緣戰略價值的重要指標。
過去，該地區與俄羅斯的軍事安全聯絡及與中國的經濟合作緊密，如今美國的積極介入，預示著中亞五國在外交政策上將面臨更為複雜的平衡挑戰。
綜合路透社與相關國際觀察來源，各方共同確認的事實為美國正積極擴大與中亞五國的合作框架，核心議題聚焦於「關鍵礦產」的開發與貿易。此政策轉向與過去數年該地區面對阿富汗政局動盪時，俄羅斯頻繁與烏茲別克、塔吉克及哈薩克進行高層通話與安全對話的背景形成對比。中亞五國在區域安全與經濟發展之間，長期採取多邊主義外交，而此次美國的介入，無疑為該地區的資源開發與國際合作注入了新的變數。
各國元首對於如何將豐富的礦產資源轉化為國家經濟優勢，並在不損害與傳統鄰國關係的前提下引入美資，已成為外交議程的重中之重。
然而，各媒體來源對於此次合作的預期效益與具體執行細節存在差異。部分旅遊與文化交流報導指出，中亞五國在物流與交通基礎設施上，仍高度依賴現有的絲路貿易路徑，且與俄羅斯的歷史連結深厚，這使得美國欲推動的供應鏈脫鉤或轉向面臨技術與物流挑戰。此外，美國之音的分析強調，阿富汗局勢的外溢效應仍是中亞各國最為擔憂的安全隱憂，相較於美國著重於礦產資源的經濟戰略，中亞國家更為關切邊境安全與區域穩定。
目前尚待確認的是，美國在關鍵礦產領域的具體投資規模，以及中亞各國政府在面對俄中兩國可能施加的政治壓力下，將如何具體落實與美國的各項協議。
此項地緣政治與經濟政策的調整，將對中亞地區的產業結構產生深遠影響。長期以來，中亞五國的經濟發展多依賴能源出口與傳統農業，若能透過與美國的合作成功引進關鍵礦產開發技術，將有助於其產業升級並提升在全球供應鏈中的地位。對於國際旅遊與商務往來而言，隨著哈薩克與烏茲別克等國在國際舞臺上的能見度提升，相關的商務考察與旅遊配套設施亦在快速發展中。
然而，政策的變動亦可能引發區域內部的競爭，各國如何在爭取美資與維持區域經濟整合之間取得平衡，將是未來幾年影響中亞經濟發展的關鍵變數。
儘管美國展現了深化合作的意圖，但分析人士提醒，不應過度解讀此舉為中亞地區將徹底倒向美國。中亞五國的外交政策核心在於「多向量外交」，即在俄、中、美、歐之間尋求最大利益。限制因素在於，中亞各國的政治體制與經濟結構與西方標準存在差異，且地理封閉性使得其對鄰國的經濟依賴難以在短期內切斷。此外，俄羅斯在該地區的軍事存在與中國在基礎設施投資上的主導地位，仍是美國無法忽視的現實。因此，此次合作更傾向於是一種戰略對沖，而非單一陣營的選擇。
後續觀察重點應放在二〇二六年及之後，各國是否會簽署具體的礦產開採合約，以及美國是否會進一步擴大在該地區的基礎設施投資。同時，需密切關注俄羅斯與中國對此類高層互動的官方反應，以及中亞五國如何在資源主權與大國博弈之間進行外交操作。對於關注中亞發展的各界而言，該地區從傳統的「絲路旅遊熱點」轉型為「關鍵礦產戰略要地」的過程，將是未來全球地緣政治與經濟版圖變動的重要風向球。
