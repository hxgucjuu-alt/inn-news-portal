  # U.S. Approval of Nearly 880,000 High-End AI Chip Exports to China Sparks National Security Controversy
  
  > The U.S. House Committee on Foreign Affairs has revealed that the Department of Commerce approved the export of nearly 880,000 high-end AI chips from NVIDIA and AMD to China between December 2025 and May 2026, triggering intense congressional questioning regarding national security policy.
  
  On November 19, 2026, the U.S. House Committee on Foreign Affairs exercised its statutory authority under the Export Control Reform Act (ECRA) to formally release internal licensing data from the U.S. Department of Commerce’s Bureau of Industry and Security (BIS). This data provides a detailed account of the approval status of export applications for high-end AI chips to China submitted by companies such as NVIDIA and AMD between December 2025 and May 2026. According to the disclosed content, U.S. companies submitted export applications totaling over 3.44 million units during this period, with the BIS ultimately issuing 21 export licenses, authorizing the export of 879,868 high-end AI chips to Chinese firms.
  
  According to cross-referenced reports from various media outlets, the chip models disclosed include high-end computing cores currently on the market, such as the NVIDIA H200 and the AMD MI325X. The U.S. Department of Commerce’s Bureau of Industry and Security (BIS) reviewed the applications submitted by relevant companies during this period and ultimately granted export licenses for the aforementioned nearly 880,000 chips. The disclosure of this data directly confirms that, even under a strict export control framework, a significant volume of advanced AI hardware continues to enter the Chinese market.
  
  Currently, the list of specific recipient companies for these chip exports has become a focal point of public attention, sparking strong criticism from bipartisan members of the U.S. Congress, who argue that the standards and transparency of the executive branch in enforcing export controls require review.
  
  There are currently varying interpretations among different sources regarding the "review details of the issued licenses" and "subsequent impact assessments." Although the data itself has been verified by the House Committee on Foreign Affairs, there is no consensus on the specific end-use of these chips within China or whether these licenses violate the original intent of U.S. technology blockades against China. Some members of Congress have questioned whether these chips could be diverted for military or sensitive technology research, but the Department of Commerce has yet to provide further explanation regarding the specific review basis for individual licenses. Furthermore, the industry remains in a stage of speculation and debate over whether these export licenses constitute "exceptions/exemptions" or "routine review results," with specific details awaiting further response from the Department of Commerce.
  
  This incident has a direct impact on the global technology supply chain and geopolitical security. At the industry level, this disclosure exacerbates the policy uncertainty faced by chip manufacturers; the operational strategies of flagship companies like NVIDIA and AMD may face pressure to adjust due to the U.S. government's tightening of export license reviews. At the policy level, this move not only directly challenges current U.S. technology export control policies but may also prompt Congress to further push for more stringent amendments to the Export Control Reform Act, thereby affecting the supply chain layout of the global AI industry. For Chinese companies that rely on high-end chips for AI model training, this data also highlights their strategies and challenges in acquiring critical hardware resources.
  
  Regarding differing perspectives, some industry analysts point out that the issuance of export licenses may involve complex commercial interests and international trade balances, and should not be over-interpreted solely from a national security perspective; rather, the supply chain resilience and corporate competitiveness of the global semiconductor market should be considered. However, members of Congress strongly argue that the inflow of any high-end chips could pose a threat to U.S. military and economic advantages, demanding that the government adopt more transparent and rigorous control measures. The current limitation is that, due to the involvement of trade secrets and sensitive national security information, many details of the review process remain undisclosed, leaving the public without a complete understanding of the decision-making logic behind the license approvals.

Additionally, how relevant manufacturers such as NVIDIA and AMD adjust their export strategies for the Chinese market in the face of congressional pressure, and whether the U.S. government will further revise the AI chip export control list, will be key indicators affecting the trends of the global technology industry in the coming months. The international community will also continue to monitor whether this incident triggers a new round of technology trade friction, thereby affecting the progress of global AI development.

# 美國核准近88萬顆高階AI晶片出口中國引發國安爭議

> 美國眾議院外交委員會揭露，商務部於2025年12月至2026年5月間，核准輝達與超微近88萬顆高階AI晶片出口至中國，引發國會對國家安全政策的強烈質疑。


美國聯邦眾議院外交委員會於2026年11月19日，依據《出口管制改革法案》（ECRA）行使法定許可權，正式公開美國商務部工業與安全域性（BIS）的內部許可資料。該資料詳細揭露了美國政府在2025年12月至2026年5月期間，針對輝達（NVIDIA）及超微（AMD）等企業的高階AI晶片出口中國申請案之核準情形。根據公開內容，美國企業在此期間提出的出口申請總量超過344萬顆，最終由BIS核發21張出口許可，批准總數達879,868顆高階AI晶片出口至中國企業。
根據多方媒體交叉比對，此次揭露的晶片型號涵蓋輝達H200及超微MI325X等目前市場上的高階運算核心。美國商務部工業與安全域性（BIS）在該期間內，針對相關企業提出的申請進行了審查，並最終核准了上述近88萬顆晶片的出口許可。這一資料的披露，直接證實了在嚴格的出口管制架構下，仍有相當數量的先進AI硬體持續進入中國市場。
目前，相關晶片出口的具體受領企業名單已成為各界關注焦點，並引發美國國會跨黨派議員的強烈批評，認為行政部門在執行出口管制時的標準與透明度有待檢討。
目前各來源對於「許可核發的審查細節」與「後續影響評估」存在一定程度的解讀差異。雖然資料本身已由眾議院外交委員會證實，但關於這些晶片在中國境內的具體終端用途，以及這些許可是否違反了美國對華科技封鎖的初衷，目前尚無定論。部分國會議員質疑這些晶片可能被轉用於軍事或敏感科技研發，但商務部尚未針對個別許可的具體審查依據做出進一步說明。此外，業界對於這些出口許可是否屬於「例外豁免」或「常規審查結果」，目前仍處於各方推測與辯論階段，具體細節仍待商務部後續回應。
此事件對全球科技產業鏈及地緣政治安全產生直接影響。在產業層面，這項披露加劇了晶片製造商面臨的政策不確定性，輝達與超微等指標性企業的營運策略可能因美國政府對出口許可審查的收緊而面臨調整壓力。在政策層面，此舉不僅直接挑戰了美國現行的科技出口管制政策，亦可能促使國會進一步推動更嚴苛的《出口管制改革法案》修法，進而影響全球AI產業的供應鏈佈局。對於依賴高階晶片進行AI模型訓練的中國企業而言，這項資料亦顯示出其在獲取關鍵硬體資源上的策略與挑戰。
不同觀點方面，部分產業分析師指出，出口許可的核發可能涉及複雜的商業利益與國際貿易平衡，不應單純以國安角度進行過度解讀，應考量全球半導體市場的供應鏈韌性與企業競爭力。然而，國會議員則強烈主張，任何高階晶片的流入均可能對美國的軍事與經濟優勢構成威脅，要求政府必須採取更為透明且嚴格的管制措施。目前限制在於，由於涉及企業機密與國家安全敏感資訊，許多審查過程的細節仍未完全公開，公眾對於許可核發背後的決策邏輯仍缺乏完整認知。
後續觀察重點將聚焦於美國商務部是否會針對此份報告進行公開說明，以及國會是否會針對BIS的審查機制發起進一步的聽證會或調查。此外，輝達與超微等相關廠商在面臨國會壓力下，如何調整其對中國市場的出口策略，以及美國政府是否會進一步修訂AI晶片的出口管制清單，將是未來數月影響全球科技產業動向的關鍵指標。國際社會亦將持續關注此事件是否會引發新一輪的科技貿易摩擦，進而影響全球AI發展的程式。
