  # Taiwan's 22nd Nuclear Energy Referendum Initiated: A Tug-of-War Between Energy Policy and AI Industry Development
  
  > Taiwan is set to hold a referendum on the 22nd proposal regarding the "abolition of the nuclear-free homeland" policy. Proponents and opponents are engaged in a fierce debate over the impact of nuclear power on the AI industry's electricity demand, electricity price stability, and national security, while the regional nuclear situation in East Asia grows increasingly complex due to North Korea's military expansion.
  
  Taiwan's energy policy is facing a critical turning point. The 22nd referendum aims to decide whether to abolish the current "nuclear-free homeland" policy and utilize nuclear energy as a means to revitalize the economy and stabilize power supply. The background of this issue stems from the power supply pressures Taiwan has faced in recent years, as well as the massive electricity demand brought about by the explosive growth of the global artificial intelligence (AI) industry. Proponents of nuclear power argue that nuclear energy is a stable and low-carbon baseload power source, which is crucial for AI data centers and high-tech industries that require long-term, stable power supply. They contend that restarting or extending the life of nuclear power plants would help alleviate Taipower's long-term losses and provide a stable energy foundation for the industry.
  
  However, the opposition camp emphasizes that nuclear power is not a panacea for energy problems, pointing out that nuclear construction cycles are long and costs are high, making it difficult to meet the urgent electricity needs of the AI industry in a timely manner. They argue that the transition to renewable energy should continue to be promoted.
  
  According to a report by The News Lens, both sides of the 22nd referendum have entered the stage of policy discourse and debate. The pro-nuclear camp focuses primarily on economic development and energy security, believing that nuclear power can effectively reduce electricity price fluctuations and provide a stable power guarantee for Taiwan's high-tech industries. Conversely, the opposition camp, led by the National Nuclear-Free Action Platform, has put forward six major arguments to debunk pro-nuclear myths, asserting that nuclear energy is not only too slow to address immediate needs but also presents the challenge of nuclear waste disposal. They argue that misallocating resources to nuclear power would crowd out space for renewable energy development and drag down the pace of Taiwan's green transition. There is a fundamental cognitive gap between the two sides regarding whether "nuclear power is the best solution for AI industry electricity demand."
  
  Although both sides are locked in a debate over domestic issues, uncertainties remain regarding the relevant information. For instance, the economic benefit estimates cited by the pro-nuclear side are inconsistent with the nuclear power generation cost analysis data cited by the opposition. Furthermore, there is currently no social consensus regarding the site selection and technical feasibility of a final nuclear waste disposal facility, which has become a key variable yet to be clarified in the referendum debate. At the same time, while global tech giants like NVIDIA are driving the development of AI technology, there are no definitive policy assessment reports on whether their global supply chain energy allocation strategies will undergo concrete adjustments based on the results of Taiwan's referendum. Views on whether nuclear power can truly solve the AI electricity gap remain highly divided.
  
  The outcome of this referendum will have a profound impact on Taiwan's energy policy, industrial competitiveness, and social security. If the referendum passes, Taiwan may restart its nuclear energy policy, which would require the government to re-plan the power structure and invest significant resources in nuclear safety upgrades and nuclear waste management; this would bring structural changes to Taipower's financial structure and energy transition path. If the referendum does not pass, the government must invest more resources in the construction speed of renewable energy and energy storage technology to ensure that power supply stability can be maintained amid the surge in AI industry electricity demand. Additionally, this issue also affects the perception of energy security in the East Asian region; against the backdrop of increasingly sensitive regional tensions, energy autonomy has become a vital component of national security.
  
  However, the referendum issue should not be overly simplified into a binary opposition of "nuclear power vs. green energy." Some energy experts remind us that energy transition involves complex grid scheduling, energy storage technologies, and international carbon reduction commitments; a single energy option cannot solve all problems. Furthermore, public concerns regarding nuclear safety and nuclear waste will not automatically disappear due to the referendum results. Policymakers must strike a balance between technical feasibility, economic costs, and social communication. Over-interpreting nuclear power as the only way to revitalize the economy risks ignoring the multiple challenges brought about by global energy market fluctuations, technological innovation, and climate change. Subsequent policy promotion must return to scientific assessment and pragmatic energy planning.
  
  The key point for subsequent observation is whether the post-referendum power supply plan can be precisely aligned with the actual electricity demand of the AI industry. In addition, the nuclear situation in the East Asian region is also worth noting; for example, the International Atomic Energy Agency's (IAEA) recent concerns over North Korea's expansion of the Yongbyon nuclear facility indicate that the nuclear issue in East Asia is not just an energy choice, but also involves regional security and stability. As the core of the East Asian technology supply chain, changes in Taiwan's energy policy will be closely scrutinized by international investors and tech giants. In the coming months, the depth of the referendum debate and the government's subsequent supporting measures will be key indicators for judging the direction of Taiwan's energy policy. All sectors need to continue tracking the implementation progress of relevant policies and social feedback.

# 臺灣第22案核能公投啟動：能源政策與AI產業發展的角力

> 臺灣即將針對「廢除非核家園」進行第22案公投，正反雙方就核電對AI產業用電需求、電價穩定及國家安全影響展開激烈辯論，同時東亞區域核能局勢亦因北韓擴張軍備而更趨複雜。


臺灣能源政策正面臨關鍵轉折點，第22案公投旨在決定是否廢除現行的「非核家園」政策，以核能作為振興經濟與穩定供電的手段。此議題背景源於近年臺灣面臨的供電壓力，以及全球人工智慧（AI）產業爆發式成長帶來的龐大電力需求。擁核方主張，核電是穩定且低碳的基載電力，對於需要長時間穩定供電的AI資料中心與高科技產業至關重要，若能重啟或延役核電，將有助於緩解臺電長期虧損並提供產業穩定的能源基礎。
然而，反對陣營則強調核電並非解決能源問題的萬靈丹，並指出核能建設週期長、成本高昂，難以即時滿足AI產業的迫切用電需求，應持續推動再生能源轉型。
根據《關鍵評論網》報導，第22案公投的正反雙方已進入政策論述攻防階段。擁核陣營主要聚焦於經濟發展與能源安全，認為核電能有效降低電價波動，並為臺灣高科技產業提供穩定的電力保障；反之，由全國廢核行動平臺領軍的反對陣營，則提出六大論點破解擁核迷思，主張核能不僅緩不濟急，且存在核廢料處置難題，若將資源錯置於核電，反而會排擠再生能源的發展空間，拖累臺灣綠色轉型步伐。雙方對於「核電是否為AI產業用電的最佳解方」存在根本性的認知落差。
儘管正反雙方在國內議題上爭論不休，但相關資訊仍存在不確定性。例如，擁核方所引用的經濟效益預估，與反對陣營引用的核能發電成本分析資料並不一致。此外，關於核廢料最終處置場的選址與技術可行性，目前仍缺乏社會共識，這成為公投辯論中尚未釐清的關鍵變數。同時，全球科技大廠如NVIDIA等企業在推動AI技術發展的同時，其全球供應鏈的能源配置策略，是否會因臺灣公投結果而產生具體調整，目前亦無確切的政策評估報告，各界對於核電能否真正解決AI用電缺口的看法仍高度分歧。
此公投結果將對臺灣的能源政策、產業競爭力以及社會安全產生深遠影響。若公投透過，臺灣可能重啟核能政策，這將要求政府重新規劃電力結構，並投入大量資源進行核安升級與核廢料管理，這對臺電的財務結構與能源轉型路徑將帶來結構性改變。若公投未透過，則政府必須在再生能源的建置速度與儲能技術上投入更多資源，以確保在AI產業用電需求激增的情況下，仍能維持供電穩定。此外，此議題亦牽動東亞區域的能源安全觀感，在區域局勢日益敏感的背景下，能源自主性已成為國家安全的重要一環。
然而，公投議題不應被過度簡化為「核電vs.綠能」的二元對立。部分能源專家提醒，能源轉型涉及複雜的電網排程、儲能技術與國際減碳承諾，單一能源選項無法解決所有問題。此外，民眾對於核能安全與核廢料問題的疑慮，並不會因為公投結果而自動消失，政策制定者必須在技術可行性、經濟成本與社會溝通之間取得平衡。過度解讀核電為振興經濟的唯一途徑，恐忽略了全球能源市場波動、技術革新以及氣候變遷帶來的多重挑戰，後續政策推動仍需回歸科學評估與務實的能源規劃。
後續觀察重點在於，公投後的電力供應規劃是否能與AI產業的實際用電需求精確對接。此外，東亞區域的核能局勢亦值得關注，如國際原子能總署（IAEA）近期對北韓擴張寧邊核設施的擔憂，顯示核能議題在東亞不僅是能源選擇，更涉及區域安全穩定。臺灣作為東亞科技供應鏈的核心，其能源政策的變動將受到國際投資人與科技巨頭的密切檢視。未來幾個月，公投辯論的深度與政府後續的配套方案，將是判斷臺灣能源政策走向的關鍵指標，各界需持續追蹤相關政策的執行進度與社會反饋。
