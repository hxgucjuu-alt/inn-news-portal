  # Southeast Asian Nations Restart Nuclear Power Programs: Addressing Energy Demands for AI Data Centers
  
  > Southeast Asian countries are re-evaluating the potential of nuclear power, aiming to address the massive growth in electricity demand driven by artificial intelligence data centers and to resolve the region's increasingly severe energy supply challenges.
  
  As a vital economic bloc in Asia, Southeast Asia is geographically situated east of the Indian subcontinent and south of China, encompassing both mainland and island territories. For a long time, the region's energy policy has relied heavily on fossil fuels and traditional renewable energy. However, with the acceleration of global digital transformation, the proliferation of artificial intelligence (AI) technology has triggered a surge in data center construction, placing unprecedented demands on the stability and scale of power supply. According to international media reports, several Southeast Asian governments have recently begun to re-examine nuclear power programs, attempting to incorporate them into their strategic blueprints for national energy transition and infrastructure expansion to ensure they do not fall behind in the digital economy competition due to power shortages.
  
  Synthesizing information from various sources, the shift in Southeast Asian nations' attitudes toward nuclear energy stems primarily from projections of future energy gaps. Multiple sources confirm that as countries compete to become regional hubs for AI data centers, traditional power grids are struggling to support the high-energy demands of servers. Due to its high energy density and characteristics as a relatively stable baseload power source, nuclear energy has become one of the options considered by policymakers. In major regional centers like Bangkok, Thailand, related discussions have moved beyond simple energy issues to the strategic level of national digital competitiveness and industrial development. Governments are actively evaluating the feasibility of emerging nuclear technologies, such as Small Modular Reactors (SMRs), hoping to balance decarbonization goals with economic development needs.
  
  Despite the clear intention to restart nuclear power, there remains an information gap and details that need to be clarified at the implementation level across various sources. Some reports indicate that countries have yet to reach a unified regional consensus regarding nuclear safety standards, waste disposal mechanisms, and public acceptance. Furthermore, there is a lack of publicly available detailed data to support the specific scale of capital investment expected from each country, environmental impact assessments for nuclear power plant site selection, and the technical feasibility of cross-border power grid interconnection. These uncertainties mean that as nuclear programs move forward, they face multiple challenges stemming from technical barriers and geopolitics.
  
  Currently, most governments are in the feasibility study or preliminary planning stage, and no large-scale nuclear power construction projects have been officially launched.
  
  The shift in nuclear policy has had a profound impact on Southeast Asia's energy industry, policymaking, and social development. In terms of industry, this trend may prompt energy companies to adjust their investment portfolios, increasing their focus on nuclear-related supply chains, while also providing a clearer energy path for tech giants to deploy data centers locally. At the policy level, governments must establish more rigorous regulatory frameworks while promoting nuclear energy to address potential safety risks. This not only tests the administrative execution capabilities of each country but also involves cooperation on energy security within the region. If successfully implemented, it will help alleviate electricity price fluctuations caused by energy shortages, thereby enhancing Southeast Asia's position in the global supply chain.
  
  However, academia and environmental groups hold different views on this, reminding all parties not to over-interpret the role of nuclear energy as a "panacea." Nuclear development not only involves high initial capital expenditure, but its long construction cycle and high technical complexity also represent a significant financial burden for developing countries whose infrastructure is still being perfected. Furthermore, some areas in Southeast Asia are located in seismic zones, making the earthquake resistance and disaster prevention planning of nuclear power plants a subject of high concern. Other limiting factors include the regulations of international nuclear non-proliferation agreements and the sensitivity of countries in the region toward nuclear technology transfer.
  
  Experts point out that while pursuing AI development, the importance of energy diversification and decentralized energy systems must not be ignored, and nuclear energy should not be viewed as the sole solution.
  
  Looking ahead, the progress of Southeast Asian nations on nuclear issues will be a key point of observation. Future focus will be on whether governments will sign regional nuclear safety cooperation agreements and whether specific technical cooperation projects will be implemented. At the same time, how countries balance the electricity demands of the AI industry with environmental protection commitments will determine the success or failure of their energy transitions. The international community will continue to monitor the transparency and safety of Southeast Asia's nuclear technology adoption process; this concerns not only internal regional economic development but also directly impacts Southeast Asia's role in international climate change governance. As global energy transition trends evolve, Southeast Asia's nuclear decisions will become a key indicator of changes in the Asian energy landscape.

# 東南亞國家重啟核能計畫：因應人工智慧資料中心能源需求

> 東南亞各國正重新評估核能發電的可能性，旨在應對人工智慧資料中心帶來的龐大電力需求成長，並解決區域內日益嚴峻的能源供給挑戰。


東南亞地區作為亞洲的重要經濟板塊，地理位置處於印度次大陸以東及中國以南，涵蓋大陸與島嶼兩大部分。長期以來，該區域的能源政策多仰賴化石燃料與傳統再生能源。然而，隨著全球數位轉型加速，人工智慧（AI）技術的普及帶動了資料中心建設潮，對電力供應的穩定性與規模提出了前所未有的要求。根據國際媒體報導，東南亞多國政府近期已開始重新審視核能發電計畫，試圖將其納入國家能源轉型與基礎設施擴張的戰略藍圖中，以確保在數位經濟競爭中不因電力短缺而落後。
綜合各方來源資訊，東南亞國家對核能態度的轉變，主要源於對未來能源缺口的預判。多個來源共同確認，隨著各國競相爭取成為區域性的 AI 資料中心樞紐，傳統電網已難以負荷高耗能的伺服器需求。核能因其具備高能量密度與相對穩定的基載電力特性，成為政策制定者考量的選項之一。在泰國曼谷等重要區域中心，相關討論已從單純的能源議題，上升至國家數位競爭力與產業發展的戰略層面。各國政府正積極評估小型模組化反應爐（SMR）等新興核能技術的可行性，期望能兼顧減碳目標與經濟發展需求。
儘管核能重啟的意向明確，但在具體執行層面，各來源間仍存在資訊落差與尚待釐清的細節。部分報導指出，各國在核能安全標準、廢棄物處理機制以及公眾接受度方面，尚未達成一致的區域性共識。此外，關於各國預計投入的具體資金規模、核電廠選址的環境影響評估，以及跨國電網互連在技術上的可行性，目前仍缺乏公開的詳細資料支援。這些不確定因素使得核能計畫在推進過程中，面臨來自技術門檻與地緣政治的多重挑戰。
目前各國政府多處於可行性研究或初步規劃階段，尚未有大規模的核電建設工程正式啟動。
核能政策的轉向對東南亞的能源產業、政策制定與社會發展產生了深遠影響。在產業方面，這項趨勢可能促使能源企業調整投資組合，增加對核能相關供應鏈的關注，同時也為科技巨頭在當地部署資料中心提供了更明確的能源路徑。在政策層面，各國政府必須在推動核能的同時，建立更為嚴謹的監管架構，以應對潛在的安全風險。這不僅考驗各國的行政執行力，更涉及區域內的能源安全合作。若能成功落實，將有助於緩解因能源短缺導致的電價波動，進而提升東南亞在全球供應鏈中的地位。
然而，學界與環保團體對此持有不同觀點，並提醒各界不應過度解讀核能作為「萬靈丹」的角色。核能開發不僅涉及高昂的初期資本支出，其建設週期長、技術複雜度高，對於基礎設施尚在完善中的開發中國家而言，是一項巨大的財政負擔。此外，東南亞地區部分割槽域處於地震帶，核電廠的抗震能力與防災規劃受到高度關注。限制條件還包括國際核不擴散協議的規範，以及區域內各國對於核能技術轉移的敏感度。
專家指出，在追求 AI 發展的同時，亦不能忽視能源多樣化與分散式能源系統的重要性，不應將核能視為唯一的解決方案。
展望後續，東南亞各國在核能議題上的進展將是觀察重點。未來的關注焦點在於各國政府是否會簽署區域性的核能安全合作協議，以及是否有具體的技術合作案落地。同時，各國如何平衡 AI 產業的電力需求與環境保護承諾，將決定其能源轉型的成敗。國際社會將持續監測東南亞在核能技術引進過程中的透明度與安全性，這不僅關乎區域內部的經濟發展，更直接影響東南亞在國際氣候變遷治理中的角色。隨著全球能源轉型趨勢演變，東南亞的核能決策將成為亞洲能源版圖變化的關鍵指標。
