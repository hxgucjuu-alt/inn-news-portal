  # U.S. Strategic Petroleum Reserve Hits New Low; Trump Administration Turns to Venezuela for Energy Supplies
  
  > The U.S. Strategic Petroleum Reserve has been frequently tapped in response to geopolitical conflicts, causing inventory levels to drop to their lowest point since the 1980s. To stabilize domestic energy supplies, the Trump administration is seeking to restart crude oil cooperation with Venezuela, a move that has sparked widespread discussion regarding energy security and infrastructure stability.
  
  The U.S. Strategic Petroleum Reserve (SPR), the core mechanism for the nation to respond to energy emergencies, is currently at a historic low. Since late February of this year, when U.S. and Israeli military actions against Iran led to disruptions in global oil shipping lanes, the Trump administration has adopted an intensive and sustained oil release strategy to curb surging domestic gasoline prices. According to public data, the reserve has decreased by more than 30% over the past six months, falling rapidly from approximately 415 million barrels in February to below 285 million barrels currently, marking the lowest level since the early 1980s.
  
  Energy security experts and government regulatory agencies have expressed deep concern regarding the rapid depletion of strategic reserves. Multiple sources have confirmed that this high-intensity extraction not only weakens the nation's ability to respond to sudden energy crises but may also cause irreversible physical damage to the underground salt caverns where the oil is stored. Research from the Brookings Institution’s Energy Security and Climate Initiative indicates that excessive extraction could lead to the degradation of storage facilities, thereby affecting their long-term stability and safety. Faced with the pressure of dwindling domestic reserves, the Trump administration has turned its attention to Venezuela, attempting to secure Venezuelan crude oil supplies through diplomatic and trade channels to fill the domestic gap—a move viewed as a strategic adjustment in the government's energy supply chain.
  
  However, there remain significant discrepancies regarding the specific implementation details and long-term effectiveness of this policy shift. Some market analysts argue that it remains to be seen whether the quality of Venezuelan crude and its transportation routes can effectively integrate with existing U.S. refining infrastructure, pending technical assessment. Furthermore, there is no consensus within the U.S. government regarding the political costs of restoring energy cooperation with Venezuela. Currently, there is no unified account from official or private media reports as to whether a formal agreement has been signed or if the plan is merely in the preliminary negotiation stage; further government announcements are required to confirm the details.
  
  This energy policy pivot has profound implications for Central America and the Caribbean. As a key transit point and potential partner for Venezuelan crude oil exports, the role of Caribbean nations in regional energy trade may undergo a qualitative change. If the U.S. purchases Venezuelan crude on a large scale, it could alter tanker routes and price structures within the region, thereby impacting the energy costs of neighboring countries.
  
  At the same time, this highlights the vulnerability of global emerging markets in terms of energy infrastructure; as World Bank President Ajay Banga recently stated, the stability of power supply is a key constraint for the development of large-scale industries. For Central American countries that rely on imported energy, maintaining power stability amidst international energy price fluctuations has become an urgent public policy challenge.
  
  Although the Trump administration emphasizes that this move is intended to stabilize domestic prices and ensure national security, differing perspectives point out that over-reliance on a single or politically sensitive supply source may shift energy security risks rather than eliminate them. Some energy policy analysts warn against over-interpreting the short-term effects of "betting on Venezuela," as the country faces severe challenges regarding its own production capacity recovery and infrastructure maintenance. Additionally, whether this policy will crowd out investments in green energy transitions in other countries in the region is a focus for climate advocacy groups, who fear that short-term fossil fuel subsidies will slow down the progress of renewable energy development in the region.
  
  Future observations will focus on the progress of replenishing the U.S. Strategic Petroleum Reserve and the specific terms of the bilateral agreement between the Trump administration and Venezuela. Furthermore, as global geopolitical tensions persist, fluctuations in international oil prices will directly test the resilience of U.S. energy diplomacy. Observers will also continue to monitor whether the energy supply chains in Central America and the Caribbean will undergo structural adjustments due to U.S.-Venezuela cooperation, and how these countries will balance traditional energy demands with climate resilience construction in the face of global energy transition pressures.

# 美國戰略石油儲備降至新低，川普政府轉向委內瑞拉尋求能源供應

> 美國戰略石油儲備因應地緣政治衝突頻繁動用，庫存量跌至 1980 年代以來新低。為穩定國內能源供給，川普政府正尋求與委內瑞拉重啟原油合作，此舉引發能源安全與基礎設施穩定性的廣泛討論。


美國戰略石油儲備（Strategic Petroleum Reserve, SPR）作為國家應對能源緊急狀況的核心機制，其庫存水位正處於歷史低點。自今年二月下旬美國與以色列針對伊朗採取軍事行動，導致全球石油運輸航道受阻以來，川普政府為抑制國內高漲的汽油價格，採取了密集且持續的釋油策略。根據公開資料顯示，該儲備量在過去六個月內已減少超過三成，從二月份約 4.15 億桶的規模，迅速下滑至目前的 2.85 億桶以下，創下自 1980 年代初期以來的最低紀錄。
針對戰略儲備的快速消耗，能源安全專家與政府監管機構已表達高度關切。多方來源共同確認，這種高強度的抽取方式，不僅削弱了國家應對突發能源危機的能力，更可能對儲存石油的地下鹽穴結構造成不可逆的物理損害。布魯金斯研究院能源安全與氣候倡議相關研究指出，過度抽取可能導致儲存設施退化，進而影響設施的長期穩定性與安全性。面對國內儲備告急的壓力，川普政府已將目光轉向委內瑞拉，試圖透過外交與貿易手段，爭取委國原油供應以填補國內缺口，此舉被視為政府在能源供應鏈上的戰略調整。
然而，關於此項政策調整的具體執行細節與長期成效，目前各界說法仍存在顯著差異。部分市場分析認為，委內瑞拉的原油品質與運輸路徑，是否能有效銜接美國現有的煉油基礎設施，仍有待技術評估。此外，美國政府內部對於與委內瑞拉恢復能源合作的政治成本評估，亦尚未達成一致。目前關於該計畫是否已簽署正式協議，或僅處於初步談判階段，官方與民間媒體報導尚無統一說法，相關細節仍待進一步的政府公告確認。
此一能源政策轉向對中美洲與加勒比海地區具有深遠影響。作為委內瑞拉原油出口的重要中轉與潛在合作夥伴，加勒比海國家在區域能源貿易中的角色可能因此發生質變。若美國大規模採購委國原油，將可能改變區域內的油輪航線與價格結構，進而影響周邊國家的能源成本。
同時，這也凸顯了全球新興市場在能源基礎設施上的脆弱性；正如世界銀行總裁班加近期所言，電力供給的穩定性是發展大型產業的關鍵限制，對於依賴進口能源的中美洲國家而言，如何在國際能源價格波動中維持供電穩定，已成為迫切的公共政策挑戰。
儘管川普政府強調此舉是為了穩定國內物價與國家安全，但不同觀點亦指出，過度依賴單一或政治敏感的供應來源，可能導致能源安全風險轉移而非消除。部分能源政策分析師警告，不應過度解讀「押寶委內瑞拉」的短期效果，因為委國自身的產能恢復與基礎設施維護同樣面臨嚴峻挑戰。此外，這項政策是否會排擠到該區域其他國家對綠色能源轉型的投資，也是氣候倡議團體關注的焦點，擔心短期化石燃料的補貼將拖慢區域內再生能源的發展程式。
後續觀察重點將聚焦於美國戰略石油儲備的補庫存進度，以及川普政府與委內瑞拉雙邊協議的具體條款。此外，隨著全球地緣政治局勢持續緊張，國際油價的波動將直接考驗美國能源外交的韌性。各界也將持續關注中美洲與加勒比海地區的能源供應鏈是否會因美委合作而出現結構性調整，以及這些國家在面對全球能源轉型壓力下，如何平衡傳統能源需求與氣候韌性建設。
