  # US-Mexico-Canada Trade Agreement Renegotiations and North American Industrial Dynamics
  
  > As the North American Free Trade Agreement enters a period of renegotiation, businesses are hoping for market stability; meanwhile, rising trade friction between the U.S. and Canada, coupled with large-scale corporate events held in the U.S., highlights the complex shifts in the North American economic and industrial environment.
  
  The North American region is currently facing multiple economic and industrial challenges, with the core focus centered on the process of the United States, Canada, and Mexico restarting trade agreement negotiations. Since July 2026, the three countries have officially begun consultations regarding trade rules, a move prompted by the tariff policies implemented by the U.S. government over the past year, which have led to a high degree of uncertainty in the regional trade environment. It is widely believed that these negotiations are crucial for stabilizing North American supply chains and the business environment.
  
  At the same time, industrial development across North American countries has not ceased. Taking the beauty and wellness industry as an example, multinational corporations such as RIMAN held a large-scale North American convention in Las Vegas, USA, in early August, demonstrating that despite the volatile policy environment, companies are still actively consolidating market momentum through physical events.
  
  According to confirmation from multiple sources, the current economic and trade situation in North America is at a critical turning point. First, the U.S., Mexico, and Canada have officially launched negotiations to update their trade agreement. The business community holds a positive attitude toward this move, hoping to end the chaotic situation caused by tariff policies over the past year and restore stability to regional trade. Second, the activity level of large corporate events in the North American market remains high; for instance, RIMAN’s 2026 North American convention, held in Nevada from August 7 to 9, attracted thousands of distributors, demonstrating that the industry is still attempting to stabilize market confidence through innovative products and leadership training in the face of policy fluctuations.
  
  Furthermore, the U.S. government's trade policy has had a substantial impact on its neighbors, particularly its stance on tariffs against China, which has become a focal point of regional diplomacy and trade negotiations.
  
  However, there are significant discrepancies and information yet to be clarified among various media reports. According to a report by Deutsche Welle (DW), Canada is currently facing a new wave of U.S. import penalties because Prime Minister Mark Carney has refused to follow the U.S. in implementing tariff policies against China. This information contrasts with the narrative from The Hill, which emphasizes the "renegotiation" between the three countries, indicating that bilateral trade friction continues to escalate outside the negotiating table.
  
  Additionally, reports regarding how Apple CEO John Ternus is addressing the dilemmas in artificial intelligence (AI) development reflect that the North American technology industry is facing internal transformation pressures as severe as those from trade policies. These issues are presented with different priorities in various media reports, and their specific scope of impact remains to be confirmed pending the release of subsequent policy details.
  
  These changes have had a profound impact on industrial policy and socio-economics in the North American region. The uncertainty of trade agreements directly impacts manufacturing and retail industries that rely on cross-border supply chains, forcing companies to seek a balance between tariff barriers and market access. For the federal government, balancing the maintenance of protectionist trade policies with ensuring cooperation from neighboring countries has become a major test of governance. Furthermore, the divergence between the U.S. and Canada on tariffs against China may lead to cracks in North American trade integration, thereby affecting the long-term investment plans of multinational corporations. For workers and consumers dependent on the North American market, this means that future prices and the job market may face more variables.
  
  Although the market has expectations for the negotiations, there are still significant differences in perspectives, and the impact of single events should not be over-interpreted. Some observers believe that the success of corporate events like RIMAN’s in Las Vegas proves that business activities have a certain degree of resilience and can operate decoupled from the government's macro policies; however, another school of thought worries that if trade negotiations do not progress smoothly, even strong industrial performance will be unable to offset the cost increases brought about by tariffs.
  
  Furthermore, regarding the strategic adjustments of Apple CEO Ternus, the outside world should be cautious about its indicative significance for the overall technology industry. One should not conflate the technical challenges of a single company with regional trade conflicts, as the correlation between the two at the policy level still requires more data to be substantiated.
  
  Looking ahead, the focus of observations on North American economic and trade development will be on the specific terms of the trilateral negotiations and the subsequent diplomatic actions following the U.S. imposition of import penalties on Canada. First, it is necessary to closely monitor the extent of concessions made by the U.S., Mexico, and Canada regarding tariff exemptions and market opening, as this will determine the operating costs for North American companies over the next two years. Second, whether the Canadian government will adjust its tariff policy toward China to ease trade tensions with the U.S. will be a key indicator of whether North American cross-border policies are moving toward alignment. Finally, the supply chain adjustment strategies of the technology and traditional manufacturing industries in the face of geopolitical risks will also become a core observation point affecting the resilience of the North American economy.
  
  Market participants should continue to monitor the latest announcements from the federal government to cope with trade rules that may change at any time.

# 美墨加貿易協定重啟談判與北美產業動態觀察

> 隨著北美自由貿易協定進入更新談判期，企業期盼市場穩定；與此同時，美加貿易摩擦升溫，加上企業大型活動在美舉行，凸顯北美經貿與產業環境的複雜變動。


北美地區近期面臨多重經貿與產業挑戰，核心焦點集中於美國、加拿大與墨西哥三國重啟貿易協定談判的程式。自2026年7月起，三國正式針對貿易規則進行協商，此舉背景源於過去一年美國政府實施的關稅政策，導致區域內貿易環境出現高度不確定性。各界普遍認為，此次談判對於穩定北美供應鏈與商業環境至關重要。
與此同時，北美各國的產業發展並未停歇，以美妝與健康產業為例，跨國企業如RIMAN於8月初在美國拉斯維加斯舉辦大規模北美大會，顯示儘管政策環境動盪，企業仍積極透過實體活動凝聚市場動能。
根據多方來源的共同確認，北美當前的經貿局勢正處於關鍵轉折點。首先，美墨加三國已正式啟動貿易協定更新談判，企業界對於此舉持正面態度，期待能結束過去一年由關稅政策帶來的混亂局面，恢復區域貿易的穩定性。其次，大型企業活動在北美市場的活躍度依舊，例如RIMAN於8月7日至9日在內華達州舉辦的2026年北美大會，吸引數千名經銷商參與，展示了產業在面對政策波動時，仍試圖透過創新產品與領導力培訓來穩定市場信心。
此外，美國政府的貿易政策已對鄰國產生實質影響，特別是針對中國的關稅態度，已成為區域外交與經貿談判的焦點。
然而，各媒體報導間亦存在顯著的差異與尚待釐清的資訊。根據《德國之聲》（DW）的報導，加拿大目前正因總理馬克·卡尼（Mark Carney）拒絕跟進美國對中國實施的關稅政策，而面臨美國的新一波進口懲罰。這一資訊與《國會山報》（The Hill）強調三國「重啟談判」的論述形成對比，顯示在協商桌外，雙邊貿易摩擦仍在升溫。
此外，關於蘋果公司執行長約翰·特努斯（John Ternus）如何解決人工智慧（AI）發展困境的報導，則反映了北美科技產業正面臨與貿易政策同樣嚴峻的內部轉型壓力，這些議題在不同媒體的報導中呈現出不同的優先順序，尚待後續政策細節公佈以確認其具體影響範圍。
這些變動對北美地區的產業政策與社會經濟產生了深遠影響。貿易協定的不確定性直接衝擊了依賴跨境供應鏈的製造業與零售業，企業被迫在關稅壁壘與市場准入之間尋求平衡。對於聯邦政府而言，如何在維持貿易保護主義政策與確保鄰國合作之間取得平衡，成為當前施政的巨大考驗。此外，美加兩國在對華關稅立場上的分歧，可能導致北美內部的貿易整合出現裂痕，進而影響跨國企業的長期投資規劃，這對於依賴北美市場的勞工與消費者而言，意味著未來物價與就業市場可能面臨更多變數。
儘管市場對於談判抱有期待，但各界觀點仍存在顯著分歧，且不宜過度解讀單一事件的影響。部分觀察家認為，企業如RIMAN在拉斯維加斯的成功大會，證明瞭商業活動具有一定的韌性，能與政府的宏觀政策脫鉤運作；然而，另一派觀點則擔憂，若貿易談判進展不順，即便產業端表現活躍，也難以抵銷關稅帶來的成本上升。
此外，針對蘋果執行長特努斯的策略調整，外界應謹慎看待其對整體科技產業的指標意義，不應將單一企業的技術挑戰與區域性的貿易衝突混為一談，兩者在政策層面上的關聯性仍需更多資料佐證。
展望後續，北美經貿發展的觀察重點將聚焦於三國談判的具體條文，以及美國對加拿大採取進口懲罰後的後續外交動作。首先，需密切關注美墨加三國在關稅豁免與市場開放上的讓步幅度，這將決定未來兩年北美企業的營運成本。其次，加拿大政府是否會調整對華關稅政策以緩解與美國的貿易緊張，將是觀察北美跨境政策是否走向一致的關鍵指標。最後，科技產業與傳統製造業在面對地緣政治風險時的供應鏈調整策略，亦將成為影響北美經濟韌性的核心觀察點。
市場參與者應持續關注聯邦政府的最新公告，以應對可能隨時變動的貿易規則。
