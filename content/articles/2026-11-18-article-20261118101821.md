  # Trump Implements New AI Chip Ban, Southeast Asian Governments Urgently Assess Impact
  
  > The U.S. government recently issued export control policies targeting AI chips, sparking high concern among Southeast Asian nations. Meanwhile, the region's digital economy, driven by generative AI, is projected to reach a scale of $1 trillion by 2035, creating a tug-of-war between policy shifts and economic growth prospects.
  
  Following the U.S. Department of Commerce's release of a 132-page updated export control policy for artificial intelligence (AI) chips this Monday, governments across Southeast Asia are engaged in an urgent assessment. The timing of this policy release, coinciding with U.S. President Trump’s diplomatic visit to Asia, not only highlights that AI technology has become a core issue in current global diplomacy and geopolitics, but also places severe pressure on Southeast Asian nations that are highly dependent on semiconductor supply chains. According to a report by The New York Times, governments in the region are attempting to clarify the specific impact of this new ban on their domestic tech industries and cross-border supply chains, demonstrating that Washington's technology policy is directly influencing Southeast Asia's economic strategic layout.
  
  Against the backdrop of these policy changes, a recent report released by Google indicates that Southeast Asia's digital economy is in a period of rapid growth, with the market expected to reach $1 trillion by 2035. The report analyzes that the widespread adoption of generative AI is the primary driver of this growth, as consumer acceptance of using AI to handle complex tasks increases, and the market shifts toward an "agentic commerce" model.
  
  This forecast aligns with the current goals of Southeast Asian nations actively seeking technological upgrades; however, U.S. restrictions on chip exports have undoubtedly added uncertainty to this wave of digital transformation, particularly regarding the stability of semiconductor supply chains and the costs of acquiring technology.
  
  Regarding the impact of the new U.S. policy, there are significant differences in perspectives among various sectors, which remain to be clarified. Some market analysts are focused on the risk of supply chain disruptions, particularly for Southeast Asian countries that rely on the Chinese market for chip assembly or re-export, where the new ban could lead to increased production costs or technological decoupling. On the other hand, some believe this may accelerate efforts by Southeast Asian nations to diversify their supply chains to reduce reliance on a single market. However, due to the complexity of the policy details, governments have largely adopted a cautious stance in public statements, with no clear response measures yet released. There remains a high degree of speculation and disagreement regarding whether the ban will lead to a slowdown in regional tech investment.
  
  The specific impact of this policy shift on Southeast Asia has extended to the industrial and public policy levels. On the industrial side, chip manufacturers and AI application developers are facing pressure to re-evaluate procurement sources; on the policy side, Southeast Asian nations must strike a delicate balance between maintaining technological cooperation with the U.S. and preserving commercial ties with China's massive market. Furthermore, the Southeast Asian region is facing economic threats posed by climate change. According to data from Indoneo, the Asia-Pacific region is expected to face up to $450 billion in climate-related losses by 2030, which creates more complex budget and policy crowding-out effects as countries allocate resources to address both digital transformation and climate resilience construction.
  
  Despite the promising outlook for the digital economy, experts warn against over-interpreting the economic dividends brought by AI. The current global economic context is more fragile than in the past, and past BBC analysis regarding economic downturns in Southeast Asia shows that the region's resilience to external economic shocks still needs improvement. Therefore, even if AI technology can bring a trillion-dollar scale of growth, without a stable international trade environment and sound infrastructure, the dividends of digital transformation may be difficult to fully realize. Additionally, the restrictive effects of technology bans, combined with potential threats to infrastructure from natural disasters (such as tsunami warning system tests triggered by earthquakes in the Philippines), are key variables affecting the region's long-term economic stability.
  
  Subsequent observation will focus on three aspects: first, the specific response policies and negotiation progress of Southeast Asian governments regarding the new U.S. chip ban; second, whether the actual penetration rate of generative AI in the Southeast Asian market and the speed of corporate transformation can support the digital economy's growth targets as expected; and finally, the potential damage to Southeast Asian industrial zones from global climate change, as well as adjustments by the international insurance industry to risk exposure in the region. How countries adjust their digital and economic policies while facing the dual pressure of technological blockades and environmental risks will be the key to determining whether the region can achieve its trillion-dollar economic goal in the coming years.

# 川普祭出AI晶片新禁令，東南亞各國政府緊急評估衝擊

> 美國政府近期發布針對AI晶片的出口管制政策，引發東南亞各國高度關注。與此同時，該地區數位經濟在生成式AI推動下，預計至2035年規模將達1兆美元，政策變動與經濟增長前景正產生拉鋸。


隨著美國商務部於本週一發布長達132頁的最新人工智慧（AI）晶片出口管制政策，東南亞各國政府正陷入一場緊急的評估行動。此項政策發布的時間點，適逢美國總統川普展開亞洲外交訪問行程，不僅凸顯AI技術已成為當前全球外交與地緣政治的核心議題，亦讓高度依賴半導體供應鏈的東南亞國家面臨嚴峻的政策調整壓力。根據《紐約時報》的報導，該地區各國政府正試圖釐清此項新禁令對其國內科技產業及跨境供應鏈的具體影響，顯示出華府的科技政策已直接牽動東南亞的經濟戰略佈局。
在政策變動的背景下，Google發布的最新報告指出，東南亞數位經濟正處於高速成長期，預計至2035年將達到1兆美元的市場規模。報告分析認為，生成式AI的普及是推動此一成長的主要動力，消費者對於運用AI處理複雜任務的接受度日益提高，市場正朝向「代理式商務」（agentic commerce）模式轉型。
這項預測與目前東南亞國家積極尋求科技升級的目標相符，然而，美國針對晶片出口的限制措施，無疑為這股數位轉型浪潮增添了不確定性，特別是在半導體供應鏈的穩定性與技術獲取成本方面。
針對美國新政策的影響，目前各界說法存在顯著差異，尚待進一步釐清。部分市場分析關注的是供應鏈中斷的風險，特別是對於那些依賴中國市場進行晶片組裝或轉出口的東南亞國家而言，新禁令可能導致生產成本上升或技術斷鏈。另一方面，也有觀點認為，這可能加速東南亞國家推動供應鏈多元化，以降低對單一市場的依賴。然而，由於政策細節繁雜，各國政府在公開宣告中多採取謹慎態度，尚未有明確的應對措施出爐，外界對於該禁令是否會導致區域內科技投資放緩，仍存在高度的揣測與分歧。
這項政策變動對東南亞的具體影響已延伸至產業與公共政策層面。在產業端，晶片製造與AI應用開發商正面臨重新評估採購來源的壓力；在政策端，東南亞國家必須在維護與美國的科技合作關係，以及保持與中國龐大市場的商業往來之間取得微妙平衡。此外，東南亞地區亦面臨氣候變遷帶來的經濟威脅，根據Indoneo的資料，亞太地區預計至2030年將面臨高達4,500億美元的氣候相關損失，這使得各國在分配資源以應對數位轉型與氣候韌性建設時，面臨更為複雜的預算與政策排擠效應。
儘管數位經濟前景看好，但專家提醒不應過度解讀AI帶來的經濟紅利。當前的全球經濟背景與過去相比更為脆弱，BBC過去針對東南亞經濟衰退的分析顯示，該地區對外部經濟衝擊的抵抗力仍有待提升。因此，即便AI技術能帶來兆美元規模的成長，若缺乏穩定的國際貿易環境與健全的基礎設施，數位轉型的紅利恐難以全面落實。此外，科技禁令的限制效應，加上自然災害（如菲律賓地震引發的海嘯預警系統測試）對基礎設施的潛在威脅，均是影響該地區長期經濟穩定的關鍵變數。
後續觀察重點將聚焦於以下三個面向：首先，東南亞各國政府針對美國新晶片禁令的具體因應政策與談判進度；其次，生成式AI在東南亞市場的實際滲透率與企業轉型速度，是否能如預期般支撐數位經濟的成長目標；最後，全球氣候變遷對東南亞工業區的潛在損害，以及國際保險業對該地區風險暴露的調整。各國在面對科技封鎖與環境風險的雙重夾擊下，如何調整其數位與經濟政策，將是未來幾年決定該地區能否達成兆美元經濟目標的關鍵。
