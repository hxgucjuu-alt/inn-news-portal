  # Social Welfare and Primary Healthcare in Taiwan: From Rural Cancer Patient Care to Local Public Health Candidacy
  
  > Taiwanese society is currently focusing on the distribution of medical resources and public service issues. The Taiwan Cancer Foundation has partnered with Fubon Life to launch transportation subsidies for rural cancer patients, while Tainan City Council candidate Chen Chin-chung has entered the race with a platform centered on public health expertise, both addressing the care of vulnerable groups.
  
  Taiwanese society has recently seen increased attention toward issues of public health and the distribution of medical resources. According to the latest information, the Taiwan Cancer Foundation and Fubon Life jointly announced on September 2, 2026, that they will continue their 10-year partnership to promote a transportation subsidy program for elderly cancer patients in rural areas, aiming to resolve the plight of rural seniors who are forced to interrupt treatment due to the long distances to medical facilities. At the same time, in the realm of local politics, Chen Chin-chung, a candidate for the Tainan City Council in the East District, completed his registration on the same day, emphasizing that he will make public health and social welfare expertise his core platform, pledging to safeguard the health of local seniors and improve deficiencies in the current public health system.
  
  Although these two events are of different natures—one social and one political—they both point to the urgent need for medical accessibility and care for vulnerable groups in Taiwan’s aging society.
  
  Regarding rural medical support, the collaboration between the Taiwan Cancer Foundation and Fubon Life has entered its 10th year. Reports indicate that the popular band "Bestards" served as the ambassadors for the event and participated in the ceremonial first pitch at the Fubon Guardians' home game at the Taipei Dome on September 2, symbolizing the message "If you throw the ball, someone will catch it," thereby calling on the public to pay attention to the medical rights of rural cancer patients. Chang Chia-lun, Deputy Executive Director of the Taiwan Cancer Foundation, and Chen Hsiu-ling, Senior Vice President of Fubon Life, attended the event together, emphasizing the importance of transportation subsidies for rural cancer patients.
  
  This collaborative program has long been committed to reducing the economic burden of transportation costs on cancer patients, ensuring that seniors do not abandon treatment due to the long distances required to reach medical care; this progress in charitable action has been confirmed by multiple sources.
  
  However, there are differing perspectives regarding the specific implementation and political appeals concerning public health issues. Upon registering his candidacy, Chen Chin-chung emphasized that his four years of grassroots service experience have led him to realize that the current public health system still has many shortcomings, particularly in the care of vulnerable groups. Compared to the direct subsidy model of the foundation-corporate partnership, Chen prefers to use the authority of a local representative to advocate for infrastructure and resources from a systemic perspective to improve the quality of life for residents in the East District. Currently, the two approaches differ in their paths to problem-solving: the former focuses on the immediate injection of private charitable resources, while the latter emphasizes political participation and policy advocacy.
  
  Furthermore, regarding the specific details of the "deficiencies in the public health system" pointed out by Chen Chin-chung, as well as the scale of the expansion of the foundation's transportation subsidy program in the coming years, there is currently no further quantitative data or detailed planning information available.
  
  These two events have a profound impact on Taiwanese society. First, the rural transportation subsidy program directly alleviates the burden caused by the unequal distribution of medical resources, which is of substantial significance for improving the survival rate and quality of life of rural seniors, and also reflects the key supplementary role that Corporate Social Responsibility (CSR) plays in Taiwan's medical system. Second, Chen Chin-chung's campaign platform highlights that public health issues have become an important indicator for candidates to gain voter recognition in local elections. This shows that in Taiwan, both private charitable organizations and local politicians have realized the medical care pressures brought about by an aging society and are attempting to respond to social needs through their respective actions.
  
  However, the outside world should not over-interpret the political implications of these activities. Although Chen Chin-chung's candidacy and the charitable event occurred on the same day, his core appeal remains focused on local service and professional governance. At the same time, while the subsidy program for rural cancer patients has received long-term support from corporations and foundations, such resources remain limited and cannot fully replace the government's responsibility for public health infrastructure. When evaluating these developments, all parties should distinguish between the goodwill of charitable actions and the systemic challenges of policy promotion, avoiding the view that a single act of charity is a panacea for all medical inequalities, while also continuing to examine the specific progress of local governments in implementing public health policies.
  
  Subsequent observations will focus on two major directions: First, whether the transportation subsidy program of the Taiwan Cancer Foundation and Fubon Life can expand its service scope or incorporate more digital technology to improve the medical experience for rural seniors; second, whether the public health platforms proposed by candidates like Chen Chin-chung can be transformed into concrete policy white papers during the campaign, and if elected, how they will integrate with the existing social welfare system, which will be a key indicator of whether their professional commitments can be fulfilled. The public can also continue to monitor the cooperation and interaction between relevant charitable organizations and local governments in improving medical accessibility.

# 臺灣社會福利與基層醫療服務：從偏鄉癌友關懷到地方公衛參選

> 臺灣社會近期聚焦醫療資源分配與公共服務議題。臺灣癌症基金會攜手富邦人壽啟動偏鄉癌友交通補助，而臺南市議員參選人陳金鐘則以公衛專業投入選戰，共同關注弱勢族群照護。


臺灣社會對於公共衛生與醫療資源的分配議題，近期在不同領域引發關注。根據最新資訊，臺灣癌症基金會與富邦人壽於2026年9月2日共同宣佈，將延續過去10年的合作模式，持續推動偏鄉銀髮癌友的交通補助計畫，旨在解決偏鄉長者因就醫距離遙遠而被迫中斷治療的困境。與此同時，在地方政治領域，臺南市東區市議員參選人陳金鐘亦於同日完成參選登記，強調將以公共衛生與社會福利專業作為核心政見，承諾守護地方長者健康，並改善現行公衛體系的不足。
兩起事件雖屬不同性質的社會與政治活動，卻共同指向了臺灣高齡化社會下，對於醫療可近性與弱勢照護的迫切需求。
在偏鄉醫療支援方面，臺灣癌症基金會與富邦人壽的合作已邁入第10年。據報導，人氣樂團「理想混蛋」擔任此次活動的守護大使，並於9月2日在臺北大巨蛋的富邦悍將主場賽事中進行開球儀式，象徵「把球投出去，就會有人接住」，藉此呼籲社會大眾關注偏鄉癌友的就醫權益。臺灣癌症基金會醫療副執行長張家崙與富邦人壽資深副總經理陳秀玲共同出席，強調交通補助對於偏鄉癌友的重要性。
此項合作計畫長期以來致力於降低交通成本對癌友造成的經濟負擔，確保長者不會因為漫長的就醫距離而放棄治療，這是多方來源共同確認的公益行動進展。
然而，針對公共衛生議題的具體執行與政治訴求，各界觀點存在差異。陳金鐘在登記參選時強調，其過去4年的基層服務經驗，使其深刻體認到現行公共衛生體系仍有許多缺失，特別是在弱勢群體照護方面。相較於基金會與企業合作的直接補助模式，陳金鐘傾向透過地方民意代表的職權，從制度面爭取建設與資源，以提升東區居民的生活品質。目前兩者在解決問題的路徑上有所不同，前者側重於民間慈善資源的即時挹注，後者則強調政治參與與政策倡議。
此外，關於陳金鐘所指出的「公衛體系不足」具體細節，以及基金會交通補助計畫在未來幾年的擴充套件規模，目前尚未有進一步的量化資料或詳細規劃說明。
這兩起事件對臺灣社會的影響深遠。首先，偏鄉交通補助計畫直接緩解了醫療資源分配不均造成的負擔，對於提升偏鄉長者的存活率與生活品質具有實質意義，也反映出企業社會責任（CSR）在臺灣醫療體系中扮演的關鍵補位角色。其次，陳金鐘的參選政見凸顯了地方選舉中，公共衛生議題已成為候選人爭取選民認同的重要指標。這顯示出在臺灣，無論是民間公益團體還是地方政治人物，皆已意識到高齡化社會帶來的醫療照護壓力，並試圖透過各自的行動來回應社會需求。
然而，外界不應過度解讀這些活動的政治意涵。儘管陳金鐘的參選與公益活動同日發生，但其核心訴求仍聚焦於地方服務與專業問政。同時，對於偏鄉癌友的補助計畫，雖然獲得了企業與基金會的長期支援，但這類資源仍屬有限，無法完全替代政府在公共衛生基礎建設上的責任。各界在評估這些進展時，應區分公益行動的善意與政策推動的系統性挑戰，避免將單一的慈善行為視為解決所有醫療不平等的萬靈丹，也需持續檢視地方政府在公衛政策上的具體落實進度。
後續觀察重點將聚焦於兩大方向：首先，臺灣癌症基金會與富邦人壽的交通補助計畫，在未來是否能擴大服務範圍或結合更多數位科技手段，以改善偏鄉長者的就醫體驗；其次，陳金鐘等參選人所提出的公衛政見，在選戰過程中能否轉化為具體的政策白皮書，並在未來若當選後，如何與現有的社會福利體系進行整合，將是檢視其專業承諾是否能落實的關鍵指標。社會大眾亦可持續關注相關公益團體與地方政府在提升醫療可近性方面的合作與互動。
