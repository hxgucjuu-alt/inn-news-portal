  # Arab Airlines Concerned Over Impact of EU Carbon Regulations on Operations and Profitability
  
  > At its annual general meeting in Doha, the Arab Air Carriers Organization (AACO) indicated that it expects to face significant losses in 2026 due to regional uncertainties. Meanwhile, carriers have expressed deep concern regarding the European Union's increasingly stringent carbon emission regulations, fearing a negative impact on the connectivity of European routes.
  
  The Arab Air Carriers Organization (AACO) recently held its annual general meeting in Doha, Qatar, to discuss the challenges facing the global aviation industry. According to the latest report released by the organization, constrained by geopolitical uncertainties in the Middle East and rising operating costs, airlines in the Arab region are projected to face a collective net loss of up to €3.82 billion in 2026. Against this backdrop, maintaining and expanding route connectivity with the European market has become a strategic objective that airlines urgently need to address. However, various environmental protection and carbon emission regulations recently promoted by the EU have become key variables that airlines must navigate when expanding into the European market.
  
  Based on confirmed information from various sources, the AACO has officially identified EU carbon emission regulations as a key issue affecting future operations. Currently, many airlines are striving to increase flight frequency and connectivity on European routes to alleviate internal regional economic pressures. However, the EU's regulatory oversight of the aviation industry's carbon footprint continues to intensify, involving not only the allocation of carbon emission quotas but also strict standards for aviation fuel and fleet efficiency. Airlines generally believe that while these regulations improve environmental standards, they also significantly increase operating costs. For airlines relying on long-haul routes to connect Europe and the Middle East, the rise in compliance costs has become a significant burden on financial performance.
  
  Although there is a clear consensus among media outlets regarding the industry's loss projections and the organization's stance, there remain differing views on the specific impact of EU policies. Some operators argue that the EU's carbon taxes and regulatory measures lack flexibility, which will lead to a decline in the competitiveness of Middle Eastern airlines and subsequently affect the choices available to European consumers. Conversely, the EU emphasizes that these policies are necessary means to achieve climate goals and are applied equally to all operators entering European airspace.
  
  Furthermore, information currently awaiting confirmation includes whether the EU will provide transitional exemption clauses for non-EU airlines, and whether these carbon regulations will have inconsistent impacts during actual implementation due to policy differences among member states. These details have yet to be officially finalized.
  
  This issue has profound implications for the European aviation industry and relevant policymakers. For Europe, balancing the maintenance of aviation connectivity with the achievement of climate goals is a core policy challenge. If carbon regulations lead Arab airlines to reduce flights to Europe, it could affect the convenience of the European tourism, logistics, and business sectors. At the same time, this forces the aviation industry to accelerate its transformation and invest in more energy-efficient fleets to meet future, more stringent environmental standards. For Middle Eastern airlines, this is not only a financial challenge but also a signal that their business models must shift toward green aviation; otherwise, it will be difficult to maintain long-term competitive advantages in the European market.
  
  In addressing the aforementioned situation, different perspectives and limiting factors must be taken into account, and the impact of any single factor should not be over-interpreted. While carbon regulations have indeed increased cost pressures, the losses of Arab airlines are also influenced by multiple factors, including regional geopolitical turmoil, fuel price fluctuations, and the global economic slowdown; it is not possible to attribute all financial difficulties solely to the EU's environmental policies. Furthermore, the aviation industry's path to decarbonization involves complex technological research and development as well as international regulatory coordination. Unilateral policy restrictions, if lacking international dialogue mechanisms, may struggle to achieve substantive emission reduction effects and could instead trigger trade disputes. Therefore, all parties should remain neutral in their interpretation of the regulations and focus on the progress of subsequent negotiations.
  
  Subsequent observations should focus on the progress of communication between the EU and the AACO, as well as whether specific carbon reduction subsidies or technical cooperation programs emerge. Additionally, close attention should be paid to the gap between actual 2026 operating data and projections, whether geopolitical conditions ease, and whether airlines adjust their fleet configurations or route strategies in response to carbon regulations. Further detailed specifications of carbon emission standards by national governments and international aviation regulatory bodies will be key indicators in determining whether the future aviation connectivity between Europe and the Middle East will undergo structural changes.

# 阿拉伯航空業者憂心歐盟碳排法規衝擊營運與獲利

> 阿拉伯航空業者組織（AACO）於杜哈年會指出，受區域不確定性影響，預計2026年將面臨巨額虧損。同時，業者對歐盟日益嚴格的碳排放監管政策表達高度關切，擔憂其將對歐洲航線的連結性造成負面衝擊。


阿拉伯航空業者組織（AACO）近期於卡達杜哈召開年度股東大會，針對全球航空業面臨的挑戰進行討論。根據該組織發布的最新報告顯示，受限於中東區域地緣政治的不確定性，加上營運成本攀升，阿拉伯地區的航空公司預計在2026年將面臨高達38.2億歐元的集體淨虧損。此背景下，如何維持並擴大與歐洲市場的航線連結，成為各家航空公司亟待解決的戰略目標。然而，歐盟近期推動的各項環境保護與碳排放監管法規，已成為航空業者在拓展歐洲市場時必須應對的關鍵變數。
綜合各方資訊確認，阿拉伯航空業者組織已正式將歐盟的碳排放法規列為影響未來營運的重點議題。目前多家航空公司正致力於提升歐洲航線的航班密度與連結性，以緩解區域內部的經濟壓力。然而，歐盟對於航空業碳足跡的監管力度持續增強，這不僅涉及碳排放額度的分配，還包括針對飛航燃料與機隊效率的嚴格規範。航空業者普遍認為，這些法規在提升環保標準的同時，亦顯著增加了營運成本，特別是對於依賴長程航線連線歐洲與中東的航空公司而言，法規遵循成本的上升已成為財務表現上的顯著負擔。
儘管各媒體對於產業虧損的預測資料與組織立場有明確共識，但關於歐盟政策的具體影響層面仍存在不同說法。部分業者主張歐盟的碳稅與監管措施缺乏彈性，將導致中東航空公司的競爭力下降，進而影響歐洲消費者的選擇權；然而，歐盟方面則強調，相關政策是為了達成氣候目標的必要手段，且對所有進入歐洲領空的業者一視同仁。
此外，目前尚待確認的資訊包括：歐盟是否會針對非歐盟籍航空公司提供過渡期的豁免條款，以及這些碳排法規在實際執行時，是否會因為各成員國的政策差異而產生不一致的影響，這些細節目前仍未有明確的官方定案。
此議題對歐洲航空產業與相關政策制定者具有深遠影響。對於歐洲而言，如何在維持航空連結性與達成氣候目標之間取得平衡，是當前政策的核心難題。若碳排法規導致阿拉伯航空公司削減飛往歐洲的班次，可能影響歐洲旅遊業、物流業及商務往來的便利性。同時，這也迫使航空產業加速轉型，投資於更具能源效率的機隊，以應對未來更為嚴苛的環保標準。對於中東航空業者而言，這不僅是財務上的挑戰，更是營運模式必須轉向綠色航空的訊號，否則將難以在歐洲市場維持長期的競爭優勢。
針對上述情況，不同觀點與限制因素必須納入考量，不應過度解讀單一因素的影響。雖然碳排法規確實增加了成本壓力，但阿拉伯航空業者的虧損亦受到區域地緣政治動盪、燃油價格波動以及全球經濟放緩等多重因素影響，不能將所有財務困境全數歸咎於歐盟的環保政策。此外，航空業的減碳路徑涉及複雜的技術研發與國際法規協調，單方面的政策限制若缺乏國際間的對話機制，恐難以達成實質的減排效果，反而可能引發貿易爭端。因此，各界對於法規的解讀應保持中立，並關注後續的談判進展。
後續觀察重點應聚焦於歐盟與阿拉伯航空業者組織之間的溝通進度，以及是否有具體的減碳補貼或技術合作方案出爐。此外，應密切留意2026年實際營運資料與預測值的落差，觀察地緣政治情勢是否出現緩解，以及航空業者在應對碳排法規時，是否會調整其機隊配置或航線策略。各國政府與國際航空監管機構對於碳排放標準的進一步細化規範，將是決定未來歐洲與中東航空連結性是否會發生結構性改變的關鍵指標。
