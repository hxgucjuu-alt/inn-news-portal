  # World Bank Raises East Asia Economic Forecast While Warning of Risks from Over-reliance on AI Industry
  
  > The World Bank has raised its 2026 GDP growth forecast for the East Asia and Pacific region to 4.5%, driven by AI-related exports. However, ASEAN and AMRO have issued warnings that the region’s high integration into the AI supply chain could lead to severe financial and real-economy shocks should the current boom reverse.
  
  The World Bank released its latest economic report in October 2026, revising the economic outlook for the East Asia and Pacific region. The report indicates that, benefiting from robust exports of artificial intelligence (AI)-related products, the region's 2026 gross domestic product (GDP) growth forecast has been raised from the original 4.2% to 4.5%, an increase of 0.3 percentage points. Regarding the performance of major economies, Vietnam’s growth expectation saw the most significant adjustment, raised by 1.1 percentage points to 7.4%.
  
  However, the World Bank also issued a warning in the report, noting that the region's economic growth momentum is highly tied to the AI manufacturing industry. This structural reliance makes the regional economy extremely susceptible to fluctuations in global technology spending; should demand in the tech market decline in the future, it could pose a threat to overall economic stability.
  
  Meanwhile, the Association of Southeast Asian Nations (ASEAN) and the ASEAN+3 Macroeconomic Research Office (AMRO) further quantified these risks in their "2026 Financial Stability Report," released in early October 2026. The report explicitly states that because East and Southeast Asia are at the core of the global AI supply chain and the correlation between financial markets and the AI industry is deepening, the region would face higher vulnerability than other global economies in the event of a "disorderly correction" of the AI boom. Specific transmission channels include a decline in tech product exports, portfolio losses, capital outflows, and severe refinancing pressures on highly leveraged tech firms and infrastructure projects.
  
  Regarding the details of the data projections, the World Bank’s outlook for the next two years remains conservative. According to the latest report, the region's economic growth rate is expected to slow to 4.4% in 2027 and further decline to 4.3% in 2028. While both the World Bank and AMRO acknowledge the stimulative effect of the AI industry on the current economy, their descriptions of risk transmission paths differ slightly: the World Bank focuses on export reliance in physical manufacturing, while AMRO emphasizes the potential crisis of financial market chain reactions and corporate refinancing capabilities. Currently, there remains uncertainty regarding whether the AI boom will experience a sharp reversal and the timing of such a shift, which remains subject to subsequent changes in global technology spending trends.
  
  From an industrial perspective, this reliance on tech exports is also reflected in the transformation pressures facing related sectors. Taking the textile industry as an example, the 30th Taipei Innovative Textile Application Show (TITAS) opened on October 6, 2026, bringing together 410 exhibitors from 12 countries, including Vietnam, South Korea, Taiwan, and mainland China, showcasing the profound innovation of the global supply chain in the face of net-zero carbon emissions and the wave of green transformation. Although AI technology is the current engine of economic growth, traditional manufacturing industries like textiles are seeking diversified paths for economic growth through sustainable circular technologies to reduce reliance on a single tech industry cycle.
  
  This demonstrates that while regional economies pursue high-tech growth, they are also attempting to diversify macroeconomic risks through industrial upgrading.
  
  However, the assessments of AI industry risks by academia and financial institutions should not be over-interpreted as an indication that the tech industry is about to enter a recession. Analysts point out that the current warnings are primarily based on structural concerns regarding "over-concentration," rather than a denial of AI's long-term contribution to productivity. The limiting factors lie in the sustainability of global tech spending and the resilience of regional enterprises when facing sudden capital outflows. When formulating policies, governments need to strike a balance between enjoying AI dividends and building financial firewalls to avoid betting the economic lifeline entirely on the business cycle of a single industry.
  
  Subsequent observation will focus on the capital expenditure (CapEx) trends of global tech giants and the monetary policy responses of East Asian nations to potential capital outflows. Furthermore, as economic growth expectations for emerging manufacturing hubs like Vietnam rise significantly, their role transition within the AI supply chain—and whether they can successfully convert tech dividends into a diversified industrial base—will be key indicators for assessing regional economic resilience over the next two years. The market will closely monitor subsequent quarterly reports released by the World Bank and AMRO to adjust risk assessments regarding the region's financial stability.

# 世界銀行上調東亞經濟預測，同步示警AI產業過度依賴風險

> 世界銀行上調東亞及太平洋地區今年GDP增長預測至4.5%，歸功於AI出口帶動，惟東協與AMRO示警，該地區高度融入AI供應鏈，一旦熱潮逆轉恐面臨嚴重金融與實體經濟連鎖衝擊。


世界銀行於2026年10月發布最新經濟報告，針對東亞及太平洋地區的經濟前景進行修正。報告指出，受惠於人工智慧（AI）相關產品的出口強勁，該地區2026年的國內生產毛額（GDP）增長預測由原先的4.2%上調至4.5%，增幅達0.3個百分點。在主要經濟體表現方面，越南的增長預期最為顯著，被調升1.1個百分點至7.4%。
然而，世界銀行同時在報告中提出警告，該地區的經濟成長動能與AI製造業高度掛鉤，這種結構性的依賴，使得區域經濟極易受到全球科技支出波動的影響，若未來科技市場需求回落，恐將對整體經濟穩定構成威脅。
與此同時，東協（ASEAN）與中日韓宏觀經濟研究辦公室（AMRO）在2026年10月初發布的《2026年金融穩定報告》中，進一步量化了此類風險。報告明確指出，由於東亞及東南亞地區處於全球AI供應鏈的核心地位，且金融市場與AI產業的關聯度日益加深，一旦AI熱潮出現「失序式修正」，該地區將比全球其他經濟體面臨更高的脆弱性。具體影響管道包括科技產品出口下滑、投資組合損失、資本外流，以及高槓桿科技企業與基礎設施專案面臨嚴重的再融資壓力。
在資料預測的細節上，世界銀行對未來兩年的展望則趨於保守。根據最新報告，該地區的經濟增長率預計將在2027年放緩至4.4%，並於2028年進一步降至4.3%。雖然世界銀行與AMRO皆認同AI產業對當前經濟的拉動效應，但兩者對於風險傳導路徑的描述略有側重：世界銀行聚焦於實體製造業的出口依賴，而AMRO則更強調金融市場連鎖反應與企業再融資能力的潛在危機。目前各界對於AI熱潮是否會出現急劇轉折，以及轉折的時間點仍存在不確定性，尚待觀察全球科技支出趨勢的後續變化。
從產業層面觀察，這種對科技出口的依賴也反映在相關產業的轉型壓力上。以紡織產業為例，第30屆臺北紡織展（TITAS）於2026年10月6日開幕，匯集了包括越南、韓國、臺灣及中國大陸等12個國家、410家廠商，展現了全球供應鏈在面對淨零碳排與綠色轉型浪潮下的深度革新。儘管AI科技是當前經濟成長的引擎，但傳統製造業如紡織業，正透過永續迴圈技術尋求經濟增長的多元路徑，以降低對單一科技產業週期的依賴。
這顯示出區域經濟體在追求高科技成長的同時，亦試圖透過產業升級來分散宏觀經濟風險。
然而，學界與金融機構對於AI產業風險的評估，不應被過度解讀為科技產業即將步入衰退。分析指出，目前的示警主要是基於「過度集中」的結構性隱憂，而非否定AI對生產力的長期貢獻。限制因素在於全球科技支出的可持續性，以及區域內企業在面對突發資本外流時的韌性。各國政府在制定政策時，需在享受AI紅利與建立金融防火牆之間取得平衡，避免將經濟命脈完全押注在單一產業的景氣迴圈之上。
後續觀察重點將聚焦於全球科技巨頭的資本支出（CapEx）動向，以及東亞各國在面對潛在資本外流時的貨幣政策反應。此外，隨著越南等新興製造業基地的經濟成長預期大幅提升，其在AI供應鏈中的角色轉換，以及是否能成功將科技紅利轉化為多元化的工業基礎，將是未來兩年評估區域經濟韌性的關鍵指標。市場將密切關注世界銀行與AMRO後續發布的季度報告，以調整對該地區金融穩定性的風險評估。
