  # UN Launches Global Planetary Defense Exercise with Interstellar Comet 3I/ATLAS as Real-World Target
  
  > The International Asteroid Warning Network, under the United Nations, has announced it will use interstellar comet 3I/ATLAS as a real-world test target for a two-month global planetary defense observation exercise beginning November 27, 2025, to assess Earth's capability to detect and track interstellar objects. The comet poses no threat to Earth.
  
  The United Nations, through the International Asteroid Warning Network (IAWN), has officially launched a global planetary defense exercise, with the core target being an interstellar comet named 3I/ATLAS. According to the framework under the United Nations Office for Outer Space Affairs (UNOOSA), this observation exercise begins on November 27, 2025, and is expected to last for two months, until January 27, 2026.
  
  This comet is the third interstellar object confirmed by humanity to enter the solar system. It was first discovered in July 2025 by the ATLAS telescope in Chile. This exercise will use actual observations to test Earth's level of preparedness in detecting, tracking, and analyzing near-Earth objects and interstellar objects.
  
  Information confirmed by multiple media outlets shows that this exercise is the eighth observation exercise conducted by the International Asteroid Warning Network since 2017. Based on reports from the Cyprus News Agency, London Broadcasting Company in the UK, Gulf News, and The Times of India, this exercise is not a hypothetical scenario but uses the real interstellar comet 3I/ATLAS as a "live test case." Scientists will coordinate a global network of observation stations to continuously track this comet, verifying whether existing observation systems can effectively respond to objects originating from outside the solar system.
  
  All reports consistently emphasize that this comet poses no impact threat to Earth whatsoever, and the purpose of the exercise is to enhance humanity's response capabilities to potential space threats, rather than to address an imminent danger.
  
  Cross-referencing reports from various sources reveals that while the core facts are consistent, there are differences in certain details. The Cyprus News Agency and The Times of India explicitly state that the exercise is led by the United Nations Office for Outer Space Affairs (UNOOSA) and emphasize that this is IAWN's eighth observation exercise; London Broadcasting Company adds details about the comet's discovery in July 2025 and identifies the discovery location as the ATLAS telescope in Chile. Gulf News focuses on highlighting the historical significance of 3I/ATLAS as the "third" confirmed interstellar object to enter the solar system.
  
  Regarding the specific number of telescopes participating in the exercise, the division of labor among observation stations in various countries, and the real-time data sharing mechanisms, current media reports do not provide a consistent and complete list, and these details remain subject to further confirmation.
  
  This exercise holds direct indicative significance for global space science and the field of planetary defense. First, it is one of the few large-scale coordinated observation operations by the international community targeting a real interstellar object, helping to verify the actual effectiveness of a series of processes from discovery, notification, orbit calculation, to spectral analysis. Second, as a rare interstellar visitor, 3I/ATLAS's observation data holds unique scientific value for understanding the composition and origin of objects beyond the solar system, and research teams from various countries can take this opportunity to obtain first-hand data.
  
  Furthermore, this exercise highlights the necessity of international cooperation in space monitoring, particularly the role of Chile as an important astronomical observation base in the Southern Hemisphere. The early detection capability of its ATLAS telescope has been concretely demonstrated in this event, which serves as an encouragement for Global South countries to participate in space science cooperation.
  
  Although this exercise has received extensive coverage in international media, there are still several perspectives and limitations worth noting. Some scientists believe that using a single comet as a test case cannot fully simulate all potential threat scenarios, such as objects that are larger, have more irregular orbits, or approach at higher speeds; additionally, significant differences exist between interstellar comets and near-Earth asteroids in physical characteristics, and whether the results of this exercise can be comprehensively extrapolated to other types of objects requires careful evaluation. Some commentators also point out that the effectiveness of planetary defense exercises ultimately depends on whether governments are willing to invest long-term resources in observation infrastructure, rather than relying solely on short-term exercises to verify capabilities.
  
  Therefore, the results of this exercise should not be overinterpreted as Earth already possessing comprehensive interstellar object defense capabilities, but rather viewed as an important step in continuously strengthening the global space monitoring network.
  
  Key points for subsequent observation will focus on several aspects: first, whether observation stations can successfully obtain and share high-quality observation data of 3I/ATLAS during the exercise, particularly spectral and photometric variation data; second, whether the International Asteroid Warning Network will release a summary report after the exercise concludes, and the specific content of the report regarding gaps in the current observation system and improvement recommendations; third, whether this exercise will prompt more countries or private institutions to invest in planetary defense-related telescope construction and data analysis technology development; and finally, whether 3I/ATLAS will exhibit anomalies in brightness or fragmentation as it departs the solar system, which will also remain a focus of continued attention in the astronomical community.

# 聯合國啟動全球行星防禦演習 星際彗星3I/ATLAS成實測目標

> 聯合國旗下國際小行星警戒網宣佈，將以星際彗星3I/ATLAS為實測物件，於2025年11月27日起展開為期兩個月的全球行星防禦觀測演習，評估地球偵測與追蹤星際天體的能力，此彗星對地球不構成威脅。

聯合國透過國際小行星警戒網（International Asteroid Warning Network, IAWN）正式啟動一項全球性的行星防禦演習，此次演習的核心目標是一顆名為3I/ATLAS的星際彗星。根據聯合國外太空事務廳（UNOOSA）體系的規劃，這項觀測演習自2025年11月27日開始，預計持續至2026年1月27日，為期兩個月。
這顆彗星是人類確認的第三個進入太陽系的星際天體，最早於2025年7月由位於智利的ATLAS望遠鏡所發現，此次演習將以實際觀測方式，檢驗地球對於近地天體與星際天體的偵測、追蹤及分析整備程度。
多家媒體共同確認的資訊顯示，這項演習是國際小行星警戒網自2017年以來的第八次觀測演習。綜合賽普勒斯新聞通訊社、英國倫敦廣播公司、海灣新聞報與印度時報等媒體報導，此次演習並非假想情境，而是以真實存在的星際彗星3I/ATLAS作為「實況測試案例」。科學家將透過協調全球觀測站網路，對這顆彗星進行持續追蹤，以驗證現有觀測系統能否有效應對來自太陽系外的天體。
所有報導均一致強調，這顆彗星對地球並無任何撞擊威脅，演習目的在於提升人類對潛在太空威脅的應變能力，而非因應迫在眉睫的危險。
交叉比對各來源報導後可以發現，雖然核心事實一致，但在若干細節上存在差異。賽普勒斯新聞通訊社與印度時報明確指出此演習由聯合國外太空事務廳（UNOOSA）主導，並強調這是IAWN的第八次觀測演習；而倫敦廣播公司則補充說明瞭彗星發現時間為2025年7月，並點出發現地點為智利的ATLAS望遠鏡。海灣新聞報則側重於強調3I/ATLAS作為「第三個」確認進入太陽系的星際天體之歷史意義。
關於演習具體參與的望遠鏡數量、各國觀測站的分工細節，以及觀測資料的即時分享機制，目前各媒體報導中並未提供一致且完整的清單，屬於尚待進一步確認的資訊範疇。
此次演習對於全球太空科學與行星防禦領域具有直接的指標性意義。首先，這是國際社會少數以真實星際天體為物件的大規模協調觀測行動，有助於驗證從發現、通報、軌道計算到光譜分析等一系列流程的實際效能。其次，3I/ATLAS作為罕見的星際訪客，其觀測資料對於理解太陽系外天體的組成與起源具有獨特的科學價值，各國研究團隊可藉此機會取得第一手資料。
再者，此次演習也凸顯了國際合作在太空監測領域的必要性，尤其是智利作為南半球重要天文觀測基地的角色，其ATLAS望遠鏡的早期發現能力在此次事件中獲得具體展現，對於全球南方國家參與太空科學合作具有鼓舞作用。
儘管此次演習獲得國際媒體廣泛報導，但仍有若干觀點與限制值得注意。部分科學家認為，以單一彗星作為測試案例，並不能完全模擬所有潛在威脅情境，例如體積更大、軌道更不規則或接近速度更快的天體；此外，星際彗星與近地小行星在物理特性上存在顯著差異，此次演習的結果能否全面類比至其他型別天體，尚需審慎評估。也有評論指出，行星防禦演習的成效最終取決於各國政府是否願意投入長期資源於觀測基礎設施，而非僅靠短期演習來驗證能力。
因此，對於此次演習的成果，不應過度解讀為地球已具備完備的星際天體防禦能力，而應視為持續強化全球太空監測網路的重要一步。
後續觀察重點將集中在幾個面向：首先，演習期間各觀測站是否能夠順利取得並分享3I/ATLAS的高品質觀測資料，特別是光譜與光度變化資料；其次，國際小行星警戒網是否會在演習結束後發布總結報告，以及報告中對於現行觀測體系缺口與改進建議的具體內容；再者，此次演習是否會促使更多國家或私人機構投入行星防禦相關的望遠鏡建置與資料分析技術研發；最後，3I/ATLAS在離開太陽系過程中是否會出現亮度異常或分裂等現象，也將是天文學界持續關注的焦點。
