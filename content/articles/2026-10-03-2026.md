  # 2026 Taiwan Science Popularization Train to Set Sail: Integrating Space Technology and Local Culture to Promote Science Education
  
  > The National Science and Technology Council (NSTC) has announced that the "2026 Taiwan Science Popularization Train" will depart from October 19 to 24. Under the theme "When the Doors Open, the Future Unfolds," the train will bring diverse knowledge, including space exploration, marine technology, and smart healthcare, to various locations across Taiwan.
  
  The pre-departure press conference for the "2026 Taiwan Science Popularization Train" was officially held on October 1, 2026, presided over by Wu Tsung-tsong, Minister of the National Science and Technology Council (NSTC). This annual science education event is scheduled to officially depart on October 19 for a six-day journey around the island. The project aims to utilize the convenience of railway transportation to deliver professional scientific research knowledge and hands-on experiences directly to 30 stations across 18 counties and cities in Taiwan.
  
  Amid the current global trend of popularizing technology education, this initiative is not only a major milestone for local science education in Taiwan but also echoes the shared international goal of enhancing public scientific literacy. It transforms cutting-edge fields such as space exploration, marine technology, smart healthcare, green sustainability, and communication technology into practical courses accessible to schoolchildren.
  
  According to confirmation from both the NSTC and media reports, the core objective of this event is to break down the barriers of professional knowledge so that science education is no longer confined to textbooks. More than 450 science popularization activities will be set up inside the train carriages, emphasizing interactive experiences that are "visible, tangible, and playable." NSTC Minister Wu Tsung-tsong emphasized that "space exploration" has been specifically designated as a key exhibition area this year, aiming to help students understand the close connection between science and daily life through hands-on practice. Furthermore, the event incorporates indigenous languages and local culture to demonstrate the inclusivity of science education, and invites several international representative offices in Taiwan to participate, showing that the project promotes science education while also considering cultural diversity and international exchange.
  
  Regarding the details and execution of the event, various reports provide highly consistent information, with slight supplementary differences in the description of the event's scope. Two reports from Yahoo Entertainment detail the event schedule from October 19 to 24, as well as extension plans for remote areas. The reports indicate that for rural areas not directly accessible by train, the organizers have planned outreach activities to ensure that science education resources can reach more students.
  
  However, official information regarding the specific instrument models or technical details of the space exploration exhibits has not yet been disclosed; it is only confirmed as part of the carriage displays. This information remains to be further verified through on-site observations after the event officially begins.
  
  From the perspective of regional development, such science popularization activities have a profound impact on Taiwan's education policy. Currently, the international community—including countries such as the United States, Australia, New Zealand, and Taiwan—is actively promoting infrastructure and educational cooperation in Pacific Island nations, such as building hospitals in Palau, enhancing fuel storage capacity in Tonga, and improving port transportation facilities in the Marshall Islands. Although the nature of these hardware constructions differs from the science education project promoted domestically in Taiwan, both reflect the importance countries place on enhancing regional technological competitiveness and talent cultivation.
  
  By introducing space and marine technology into the education system, Taiwan is attempting to establish a basic education model that aligns with the international scientific research environment. This holds potential significance for talent reserves for future participation in international space science cooperation and marine research projects.
  
  Despite the well-planned nature of the event, all parties should remain mindful of the limitations of promoting science education. First, the physical activities of the science train are limited by railway stations and time; even with extension plans, it is difficult to fully bridge the urban-rural gap. Second, translating high-level scientific research fields like space exploration into hands-on practice that students can understand is a rigorous test of the precision of teaching material design. Over-simplification could lead to deviations in scientific concepts; therefore, one should avoid equating science popularization experiences directly with professional scientific research training when interpreting these activities.
  
  Furthermore, the level of participation and the specific forms of contribution from international partners will depend on the actual execution at the event site. It is not appropriate to over-speculate on their direct impact on foreign policy; the focus should return to the value of science education in inspiring students.
  
  Subsequent observation will focus on the effectiveness of the execution after the event departs, particularly the reactions of students of different age groups to the space exploration and marine technology exhibition areas. Whether the NSTC can transform this island-wide science popularization model into a regular education policy, and how it can further promote Taiwan's science education achievements to the Pacific region through cooperation with international units stationed in Taiwan, will be key points for future observation. Additionally, the execution rate and feedback regarding the outreach activities in remote areas will be key indicators for evaluating whether the project can achieve the goal of "science education equity." All sectors of society should continue to pay attention to public participation data during the event and whether more cross-border science education cooperation projects emerge in the future.

# 2026臺灣科普環島列車將啟航：結合太空科技與在地文化推動科學教育

> 國家科學及技術委員會宣佈「2026臺灣科普環島列車」將於10月19日至24日啟程，以「車門一開，未來展開」為主題，透過環島列車將太空探索、海洋科技及智慧醫療等多元知識帶入全臺各地。


「2026臺灣科普環島列車」行前記者會於2026年10月1日正式召開，由國家科學及技術委員會（國科會）主委吳誠文主持。這項年度科學教育盛事預計於10月19日正式啟程，展開為期6天的環島行程。該計畫旨在透過鐵道運輸的便利性，將專業的科研場域知識與實作體驗，直接送達全臺18個縣市、共計30個站點。
在當前全球推動科技教育普及化的趨勢下，此舉不僅是臺灣在地科學教育的重大里程碑，亦呼應了國際間對於提升公眾科學素養的共同目標，將太空探索、海洋科技、智慧醫療、綠色永續及通訊技術等前瞻領域，轉化為學童可接觸的實作課程。
根據國科會與媒體報導的共同確認，本次活動的核心目標在於打破專業知識的藩籬，讓科學教育不再侷限於課本。列車車廂內將設定超過450項科普活動，強調「看得到、摸得到、玩得到」的互動體驗。國科會主委吳誠文強調，今年特別將「太空探索」列為重點展示領域，旨在讓學童透過實作了解科學與日常生活的緊密連結。此外，活動亦結合了原住民族語及在地文化，展現科學教育的包容性，並邀請多個駐臺國際單位共同參與，顯示該計畫在推動科學教育的同時，亦兼顧了文化多樣性與國際交流的層面。
關於活動細節與執行層面，各來源報導呈現了高度一致的資訊，但在活動廣度的描述上略有補充差異。Yahoo Entertainment的兩篇報導均詳細列出了10月19日至24日的活動期程，以及對偏鄉地區的延伸規劃。報導指出，針對列車無法直接抵達的偏鄉地帶，主辦單位已規劃延伸活動，以確保科學教育資源能觸及更多學童。
然而，目前關於太空探索具體展示的儀器型號或技術細節，官方尚未對外揭露過多資訊，僅確認其為車廂展示內容之一，這部分資訊仍待活動正式啟動後，透過現場實況進一步驗證。
從區域發展的角度觀察，此類科普活動對於臺灣教育政策的影響深遠。當前國際社會如美國、澳洲、紐西蘭及臺灣等國，正積極在太平洋島國推動基礎設施與教育合作，例如在帛琉興建醫院、提升東加燃料儲存能力及改善馬紹爾群島的港口交通設施。這些硬體建設與臺灣在國內推動的科普教育計畫，雖然性質不同，但皆反映出各國對於提升區域科技競爭力與人才培育的重視。
透過將太空與海洋科技引入教育體系，臺灣正試圖建立一套能與國際科研環境接軌的基礎教育模式，這對於未來參與國際太空科學合作與海洋研究計畫，具有潛在的人才儲備意義。
儘管活動規劃完善，但各界仍需留意科學教育推廣的限制。首先，科普列車的實體活動受限於鐵道站點與時間，即便有延伸計畫，仍難以完全覆蓋所有城鄉差距。其次，將太空探索等高階科研領域轉化為學童可理解的實作，極度考驗教材設計的精準度，若過度簡化可能導致科學概念的偏差，因此在解讀時應避免將科普體驗直接等同於專業科研訓練。
此外，國際合作單位的參與程度與具體貢獻形式，亦需視後續活動現場的實際執行情況而定，不宜過度臆測其對外交政策的直接影響，應將焦點回歸至科學教育本身對學童啟發的價值。
後續觀察重點將集中於活動啟程後的執行成效，特別是太空探索與海洋科技展區在各年齡層學童間的反應。國科會是否能將這類環島科普模式轉化為常態性的教育政策，以及如何透過與國際駐臺單位的合作，進一步將臺灣的科學教育成果推向太平洋地區，將是未來觀察的重點。此外，針對偏鄉延伸活動的執行率與反饋，亦將是評估該計畫能否達成「科學教育平權」目標的關鍵指標。社會各界應持續關注活動期間的公眾參與資料，以及後續是否會有更多跨國界的科學教育合作計畫浮出水面。
