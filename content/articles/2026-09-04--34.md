  # Kaohsiung Illegal Dumping Case Concluded: 34 Indicted for Over NT$60 Million in Illicit Gains
  
  > The Kaohsiung District Prosecutors Office has concluded its investigation into an illegal construction waste dumping case. A contractor surnamed Huang, who undertook social housing and airport projects, dumped waste on farmland in Daliao, profiting over NT$60 million. Prosecutors have indicted 34 individuals for violations of the Waste Disposal Act.
  
  The Kaohsiung District Prosecutors Office announced the conclusion of a major environmental crime investigation on September 3, 2026. Prosecutors discovered that a contractor surnamed Huang and his associates had previously been contracted for waste removal services for social housing construction projects in Kaohsiung City under the National Housing and Urban Regeneration Center, as well as for the multi-story parking garage project at Xiaogang Airport under the Civil Aeronautics Administration. To evade high legal disposal costs, the criminal group colluded with intermediaries and soil resource facility operators. Using a "fake unloading" scheme, they instructed dump truck drivers to enter legal soil resource facilities to take photos and lift their truck beds to simulate unloading, while actually transporting large quantities of construction waste to three plots of farmland near the Shanziding drainage channel in Daliao District, Kaohsiung City, for illegal dumping.
  
  This action not only severely damaged the farmland environment but also exposed significant loopholes in the waste disposal procedures for related public works.
  
  According to the indictment from the Kaohsiung District Prosecutors Office, following an in-depth investigation, prosecutors determined that the criminal group accumulated over NT$62.66 million in illicit gains through the aforementioned illegal means. After securing relevant evidence, the prosecution has formally indicted 34 individuals, including the contractor surnamed Huang, the operators of the soil resource facilities, the transport drivers, and the intermediaries. Prosecutors concluded that the defendants' actions severely violated the Waste Disposal Act, endangering national land conservation and causing long-term, irreversible damage to the ecology of the surrounding farmland. The prosecution will continue to track the flow of the waste and hold parties accountable for environmental restoration. The case has now entered the judicial trial stage, and the specific responsibilities and subsequent penalties for those involved will be determined by the court.
  
  While the public monitors this case, there have been varying levels of discussion between prosecutors and environmental authorities regarding regulatory loopholes at the involved soil resource facilities. It is currently known that many of the defendants do not dispute the facts of the illegal dumping; however, there are discrepancies in testimonies regarding the division of labor within the criminal organization, the distribution of profits, and whether the operators of the soil resource facilities were aware of and permitted the "fake unloading" process. Prosecutors have cross-referenced digital evidence with on-site surveillance footage to clarify the specific roles of each party in the criminal chain, which will be subject to further detailed evidence presentation and debate during the court trial.
  
  Furthermore, the estimation of cleanup costs for the contaminated farmland and whether more undisclosed dumping sites are involved remain key focuses of the ongoing investigation.
  
  This incident has had a direct impact on the construction industry and environmental policies in the Kaohsiung area. As the projects involved major public infrastructure under the National Housing and Urban Regeneration Center and the Civil Aeronautics Administration, relevant agencies are facing public pressure to strengthen tracking mechanisms for the removal of construction waste. Industry insiders point out that the fluctuation in waste disposal costs is a primary driver of illegal dumping; if the government cannot effectively control the processing capacity of legal soil resource facilities, similar "fake unloading" abuses will be difficult to eradicate. For local agriculture, the erosion of farmland in Daliao District by waste not only affects the land's cultivation value but may also cause heavy metal soil contamination, bringing long-term negative impacts on local agricultural safety and land-use planning.
  
  Views on this case vary across society. Environmental groups emphasize that a comprehensive review of "real-time GPS monitoring" and "source tracking" for waste removal is necessary, rather than limiting actions to the indictment of individuals. Legal experts warn that the challenge facing the judicial system lies in whether the penalties under the Waste Disposal Act are sufficient to provide a deterrent, and how to effectively recover illicit gains for environmental restoration. Meanwhile, public opinion calls for avoiding the over-interpretation of this single case as representative of all construction projects, while simultaneously acknowledging the severe failure of screening mechanisms for downstream waste removal contractors in the bidding and regulatory phases of public works—an administrative deficiency that government departments must prioritize for improvement.
  
  Future observations will focus on the court's trial progress for the 34 defendants, particularly the sentencing differences between the masterminds and those who assisted in the crimes. Additionally, whether the Kaohsiung City Environmental Protection Bureau will initiate an emergency remediation plan for the contaminated farmland, and how relevant authorities will adjust audit standards for public works waste removal, will be the focus of public attention in the coming months. Whether the prosecution will expand its investigation into other projects involving this group, and whether any public officials were involved in dereliction of duty or collusion, are also critical indicators to watch in the subsequent judicial investigation.

# 高雄廢土傾倒農地案偵結起訴 34人涉案不法獲利逾6千萬

> 高雄地檢署偵結一起營建廢棄物非法傾倒案，黃姓業者承攬社宅及機場工程，卻將廢土棄置於大寮農地，獲利逾6千萬元，檢方依違反《廢棄物清理法》起訴34人。


高雄地檢署於2026年9月3日宣佈偵結一起重大環境犯罪案件。檢方調查發現，黃姓業者等人先前承攬國家住宅及都市更新中心位於高雄市的社會住宅新建工程，以及民航局小港機場立體停車場的廢棄物清運業務。該犯罪集團為規避高昂的合法處置成本，竟與中間人及土資場業者勾結，透過「假卸土」手法，指示砂石車進入合法土資場拍照、抬車鬥偽裝卸土，實則將大量的營建廢棄物運往高雄市大寮區山仔頂大排旁的三處農地進行非法傾倒。
此舉不僅嚴重破壞農地環境，更造成相關公共工程的廢棄物處理程式出現重大漏洞。
根據高雄地檢署的起訴內容，經檢察官深入追查，該犯罪集團透過上述非法手段，累計不法獲利高達6266萬餘元。檢方在掌握相關證據後，已正式對涉案的黃姓業者、土資場負責人、負責運送的司機以及中間人等共計34人提起公訴。檢察官認定，被告等人行為已嚴重違反《廢棄物清理法》，不僅危害國土保育，更對周邊農地生態造成長期且難以挽回的影響，檢方將持續追查廢土流向及環境復原責任。目前，全案已進入司法審判程式，涉案人員的具體責任歸屬與後續罰則，將由法院進一步審理判定。
在各界關注此案的同時，檢方與環保主管機關對於涉案土資場的監管漏洞亦有不同層次的討論。目前已知多名被告對於非法傾倒的行為事實並無異議，然而關於各環節參與者在犯罪組織中的分工比例、獲利分配細節，以及土資場負責人是否知情並默許「假卸土」流程，仍有部分供詞存在差異。檢方目前已透過數位證據與現場監控影像進行交叉比對，試圖釐清各方在犯罪鏈中的具體角色，尚待法院審理時進行更細緻的舉證與辯論。
此外，受汙染農地的後續整治費用估算，以及是否涉及更多未曝光的傾倒點，仍是檢方後續調查的重點。
此次事件對高雄地區的營建產業與環保政策產生直接影響。由於涉案工程涉及國家住宅及都市更新中心與民航局的重大公共建設，相關單位已面臨輿論壓力，要求加強對營建廢棄物清運過程的追蹤機制。產業內人士指出，廢棄物清運成本的浮動是導致非法傾倒的主因之一，若政府無法有效管控合法土資場的處理量能，類似的「假卸土」弊端恐難以根絕。對地方農業而言，大寮區農地遭到廢棄物侵蝕，不僅影響土地耕作價值，更可能造成土壤重金屬汙染，對當地農業安全與土地利用規劃帶來長遠的負面衝擊。
對於此案，社會各界觀點不一。環保團體強調，應針對廢棄物清運的「GPS即時監控」與「產源追蹤」進行全面檢討，不應僅止於對涉案個人的起訴。法律專家則提醒，針對《廢棄物清理法》的罰則是否足以產生足夠的嚇阻力，以及如何有效追回不法所得並用於環境復原，是司法體系面臨的挑戰。同時，輿論也呼籲不應過度解讀此單一案件對所有營建工程的代表性，但也必須正視公共工程在發包與監管環節中，對於下游清運廠商篩選機制的嚴重失靈，這是政府部門必須優先改善的行政缺失。
後續觀察重點將聚焦於法院對34名被告的審理進度，特別是針對主謀與協助犯罪者的量刑差異。此外，高雄市政府環保局是否會針對該處受汙染農地啟動緊急整治計畫，以及相關主管機關如何調整公共工程廢棄物清運的稽核標準，將是未來數月社會大眾關注的焦點。檢方是否會擴大追查其他與該集團合作的工程專案，以及是否涉及公務人員瀆職或包庇，亦是後續司法調查不容忽視的觀察指標。
