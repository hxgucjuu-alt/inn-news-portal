  # Netanyahu’s "New Middle East" Vision: An Analysis of Regional Geopolitics and Policy Objectives
  
  > This article compiles reports from media outlets such as Middle East Eye and Middle East Monitor to explore the "New Middle East" concept proposed by Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu, analyzing its strategic intent, historical context, and the perspectives and skepticism surrounding this vision within the region.
  
  The term "New Middle East" has recently returned to the center of geopolitical discourse in the Middle East, particularly within the regional strategic framework of Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu. This concept is not a brand-new idea; its historical roots can be traced back to a research report published by the Carnegie Endowment for International Peace in March 2004. The discourse at that time aimed to explore how to reshape the order of the Middle East through political and economic reforms and the restructuring of security architectures.
  
  With the current escalation of regional conflicts, the Netanyahu government has repackaged this concept as a guiding principle for Israel to promote diplomatic normalization and security integration in the Levant and the Persian Gulf, attempting to establish new geopolitical connections between traditional Arab states and Israel.
  
  According to Middle East Monitor and related international analytical reports, it is a widely acknowledged fact that the core objective of the "New Middle East" vision promoted by Netanyahu is to establish a security network that excludes the influence of Iran and its proxies by deepening economic and security cooperation with regional states. In practice, this vision is closely linked to the expansion of the "Abraham Accords," intending to transition Israel's relations with certain Persian Gulf states from covert to overt, thereby reshaping the regional balance of power.
  
  Furthermore, media outlets consistently point out that this policy involves not only military defense but also infrastructure connectivity and energy trade, aiming to establish Israel as a regional economic hub, thereby altering the traditional model that has long placed the Israeli-Palestinian conflict at the core of regional diplomacy.
  
  However, there are significant differences and controversies among various sources regarding the substantive content and implementation path of the "New Middle East." Some analyses suggest that Netanyahu's vision is largely viewed as a "top-down" power politics approach that ignores a solution to the Palestinian issue, which has led to regional skepticism regarding the plan's feasibility. In exploring related geopolitical dynamics, Middle East Eye highlighted how the rhetoric of Western political figures, such as Nigel Farage, regarding Middle Eastern issues mirrors internal religious and ethnic conflicts in the region, adding to the complexity of the topic.
  
  Moreover, there is a wide divergence of views on whether the "New Middle East" can truly achieve regional stability or if it is merely intended to consolidate the survival of specific regimes. Information yet to be confirmed includes the specific level of commitment from various countries in the face of this vision, and whether this vision still retains room for diplomatic negotiation amidst the ongoing warfare.
  
  For the society and industries of the Middle East, Netanyahu's "New Middle East" vision has profound potential impacts. At the industrial level, if regional integration is realized, it would promote cross-border cooperation in transportation, water resource management, and digital technology, resulting in structural adjustments to the economic development of the Persian Gulf and the Levant. However, at the social level, this government-led regional reorganization often lacks support from the grassroots. If the policy fails to resolve long-standing ethnic and religious contradictions, it will not only fail to eliminate social opposition but may instead lead to further polarization of regional society due to unequal resource distribution or rigid political confrontations.
  
  Furthermore, exchanges in the field of education could also become tools for political maneuvering; if curricula are constrained by geopolitical frameworks, it will affect the next generation's perception of history and identity within the region.
  
  Regarding differing perspectives and limitations, critics argue that Netanyahu's "New Middle East" vision overemphasizes military and security cooperation while ignoring the aspirations of Middle Eastern societies for democracy, human rights, and Palestinian national self-determination. This "security-first" strategy is criticized by some scholars as an expression of exclusionary regional hegemonic intent rather than a genuine vision for peace. Additionally, it must be noted that one should not over-interpret the immediate effectiveness of this vision, as the political structure of the Middle East is extremely fragile and deeply influenced by the games of major international powers; any single leader's vision could collapse due to sudden conflicts or regime changes.
  
  Therefore, viewing this vision as a definitive conclusion for the region's future appears overly optimistic and lacks a realistic foundation in the current geopolitical environment.
  
  Subsequent observations should focus on the interactions between Israel and Arab states in the region, particularly whether there is a substantive shift in their stance on the Palestinian issue. Furthermore, how Iran and its allies respond to this trend of regional integration will be the key to determining whether the "New Middle East" vision can be implemented or if it will merely devolve into diplomatic rhetoric. Experts suggest continuing to monitor specific agreements between countries regarding energy trade and the integration of defense systems; these data points and public declarations will reflect the true progress of regional integration more accurately than political slogans. At the same time, the reactions of civil society in the Middle East and pressure from international public opinion will continue to influence the pace and direction of this vision's promotion over the coming years.

# 奈坦雅胡倡議「新中東」願景：區域地緣政治與政策目標的分析與爭議

> 本文整理《Middle East Eye》與《Middle East Monitor》等媒體報導，探討以色列總理奈坦雅胡提出的「新中東」概念，分析其戰略意圖、歷史脈絡及區域各界對此願景的觀點與質疑。


「新中東」（New Middle East）一詞近期再度成為中東地緣政治討論的核心，特別是在以色列總理班傑明·奈坦雅胡（Benjamin Netanyahu）的區域戰略框架下。這一概念並非近期才出現的全新構想，其歷史根源可追溯至2004年3月由卡內基國際和平基金會（Carnegie Endowment for International Peace）發表的一份研究報告。當時的論述旨在探討如何透過政治、經濟改革與安全架構的重組，重塑中東地區的秩序。
隨著當前區域衝突加劇，奈坦雅胡政府將此概念重新包裝，作為以色列在黎凡特與波斯灣區域推動外交正常化與安全整合的指導方針，試圖在傳統阿拉伯國家與以色列之間建立新的地緣政治連結。
根據《Middle East Monitor》與相關國際分析報導，各界共同確認的事實在於，奈坦雅胡所推動的「新中東」願景，核心目標在於透過深化與區域國家的經濟與安全合作，建立一個排除伊朗及其代理人影響力的安全網。這一願景在實務上與「亞伯拉罕協議」（Abraham Accords）的擴充套件密切相關，意圖將以色列與部分波斯灣國家的關係從隱蔽轉為公開，並藉此重塑區域權力平衡。
此外，媒體一致指出，此政策不僅涉及軍事防禦，更包含基礎設施連結與能源貿易，旨在將以色列打造為區域經濟樞紐，進而改變長期以來以巴衝突作為區域外交核心的傳統模式。
然而，針對「新中東」的實質內涵與執行路徑，各來源之間存在顯著差異與爭議。部分分析指出，奈坦雅胡的願景在很大程度上被視為一種「由上而下」的強權政治，忽略了巴勒斯坦議題的解決方案，這導致了區域內對於該計畫可行性的質疑。《Middle East Eye》在探討相關地緣政治動態時，強調了西方政治人物如奈傑爾·法拉奇（Nigel Farage）等對中東議題的言論，如何與區域內部的宗教與族群矛盾相互映照，增加了此議題的複雜性。
此外，關於「新中東」是否能真正實現區域穩定，抑或僅是為了鞏固特定政權的生存，各方觀點分歧極大，尚待確認的資訊包括各國在面對此願景時的具體承諾程度，以及在當前戰火持續下，該願景是否仍具備外交談判的空間。
對於中東地區的社會與產業而言，奈坦雅胡的「新中東」願景具有深遠的潛在影響。在產業層面，若區域整合得以落實，將促進交通、水資源管理與數位科技的跨境合作，對波斯灣與黎凡特地區的經濟發展產生結構性調整。然而，在社會層面，這種由政府主導的區域重組，往往缺乏基層民意的支援。若政策未能解決長期存在的民族與宗教矛盾，不僅難以消除社會對立，反而可能因資源分配不均或政治立場的強硬對抗，導致區域社會進一步極化。
此外，教育領域的交流亦可能成為政治角力的工具，若教學內容受制於地緣政治框架，將影響區域內下一代對歷史與認同的認知。
不同觀點與限制方面，批評者認為，奈坦雅胡的「新中東」願景過度強調軍事與安全合作，忽視了中東社會對於民主、人權與巴勒斯坦民族自決的訴求。這種「安全優先」的策略，被部分學者批評為一種排他性的區域霸權意圖，而非真正的和平願景。此外，必須注意不應過度解讀該願景的立即成效，因為中東地區的政治結構極度脆弱，且受到國際大國博弈的深刻影響，任何單一領導人的願景都可能因突發的衝突或政權更迭而崩解。
因此，將此願景視為區域未來的定論，在當前的地緣政治環境下顯得過於樂觀且缺乏現實基礎。
後續觀察重點應聚焦於以色列與區域內阿拉伯國家的外互動動，特別是針對巴勒斯坦問題的立場是否出現實質性轉變。此外，伊朗及其盟友如何應對這一區域整合趨勢，將是決定「新中東」願景能否落地，或僅是淪為外交辭令的關鍵。專家建議，應持續關注各國在能源貿易與防禦系統整合上的具體協議，這些資料與公開宣告將比政治口號更能反映區域整合的真實程式。同時，中東地區的民間社會反應與國際輿論的壓力，亦將在未來數年間持續影響該願景的推動節奏與方向。
