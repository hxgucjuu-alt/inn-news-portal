  # NASA Develops μBites Technology: Transforming Discarded Plastic Bottles into Space Food and Nutritional Cookies
  
  > Researchers at Southern Illinois University Carbondale have developed the μBites technology, which uses genetically modified microorganisms to convert plastic bottles and agricultural waste into protein and fat, providing an innovative solution for space exploration and global food crises.
  
  A research team from Southern Illinois University Carbondale (SIU Carbondale) officially unveiled a biotechnological breakthrough called "μBites" at the recently held American Chemical Society (ACS) fall meeting. The core mechanism of this technology involves using genetically modified microorganisms to decompose discarded polyethylene terephthalate (PET) plastic bottles and various types of agricultural waste, subsequently reassembling the carbon molecules into proteins and fats required by the human body. This technology is not only significant in the field of resource recycling but is also viewed as a key potential solution for addressing long-term food supply issues during future deep space exploration missions.
  
  On a global scale, this technology is also highly anticipated, with the potential to alleviate the increasingly severe pressures of waste management and food shortages.
  
  According to publicly available technical information, the operational process of the μBites technology has been confirmed by multiple reports. The research team used precise bioengineering methods to screen and optimize specific microbial strains, enabling them to decompose plastic polymers. After processing plastic bottles, these microorganisms can convert plastic molecules—which are otherwise difficult to degrade naturally—into bioavailable basic nutrients. The final product, after processing and refinement, can be made into cookies that offer both texture and nutritional value.
  
  The emergence of this technology marks a significant step forward in the circular economy of "converting waste into high-nutrition food," transforming plastic trash, once considered an environmental burden, into high-value space supplies.
  
  Although the μBites technology has demonstrated high feasibility in a laboratory setting, there are differing perspectives and unconfirmed details regarding its practical application. Current reports primarily focus on the conversion efficiency within the lab; however, scaling this technology from laboratory size to industrial production still faces immense technical challenges, including the stability of microorganisms in different environments, the cost-effectiveness of large-scale waste processing, and the performance of the final product in terms of long-term storage and sensory quality.
  
  Furthermore, while the research team claims the output is "edible" and "flavorful," there is currently a lack of public clinical trial data to support long-term safety assessments, nutrient absorption rates, and whether the product meets the stringent standards required for aerospace-grade food.
  
  Regarding its potential impact on South America, this technology holds profound significance for countries surrounding the Andes and nations with massive agricultural output, such as Brazil. As a major global agricultural hub, South America generates vast amounts of agricultural waste annually. If the μBites technology can be successfully commercialized and introduced to the region, it could effectively convert this waste into high-value protein sources. This would not only alleviate food insecurity in parts of South America but also reduce reliance on imported food through localized circular economy models.
  
  Additionally, as South American countries increasingly prioritize environmental protection policies, integrating this technology with local waste management systems could help enhance the competitiveness of the regional environmental industry and provide new opportunities for cross-border green technology cooperation in South America.
  
  However, both academia and industry caution against over-mythologizing the μBites technology. The technology is currently in the research and preliminary validation stage, and there is still a long way to go before it can be applied to actual space missions or large-scale commercial production. Overemphasizing the convenience of "turning trash into cookies" may overlook potential limitations, such as public psychological acceptance, regulatory oversight, and energy consumption during large-scale production. For space exploration in particular, any change to the food supply chain must undergo extremely rigorous certification to ensure that it does not pose a threat to the health of astronauts in extreme environments.
  
  Therefore, until the technology is fully mature, a cautiously optimistic attitude should be maintained, with continued attention paid to subsequent safety assessments and commercialization progress.
  
  Future observations will focus on three dimensions: first, whether the SIU Carbondale team will publish a more detailed technical white paper in the near future to disclose conversion efficiency and product composition analysis under various environmental conditions; second, whether international aerospace agencies such as NASA will include μBites in future space food supply chain assessment programs; and finally, the potential for promoting this technology in major agricultural nations like those in South America, particularly regarding localized applications for agricultural waste treatment.
  
  With the global emphasis on the circular economy and sustainable space development, the progress of μBites technology will continue to serve as an indicator for the fields of space science and environmental engineering, and may influence the direction of future cross-regional resource recycling policies.

# NASA研發μBites技術：將廢棄寶特瓶轉化為太空糧食與營養餅乾

> 美國南伊利諾大學卡本代爾分校研發出μBites技術，透過基因改造微生物將寶特瓶與農業廢棄物轉化為蛋白質與脂肪，為太空探索與全球糧食危機提供創新解決方案。


美國南伊利諾大學卡本代爾分校（SIU Carbondale）研究團隊，於近期舉行的美國化學學會（ACS）秋季會議中，正式發表一項名為「μBites」的生物技術突破。該技術的核心機制在於利用基因改造後的微生物，將廢棄的聚對苯二甲酸乙二酯（PET）寶特瓶以及各類農業廢棄物進行分解，並將其中的碳分子重新組合成人體所需的蛋白質與脂肪。這項技術不僅在資源迴圈利用領域具有指標性意義，更被視為未來深太空探索任務中，解決太空人長期糧食補給問題的關鍵潛在方案。
在地球環境層面，這項技術亦被寄予厚望，有望緩解全球日益嚴峻的廢棄物處理壓力與糧食短缺挑戰。
根據目前公開的技術資訊，μBites技術的運作流程已獲得多方報導證實。該研究團隊透過精密的生物工程手段，篩選並最佳化了特定的微生物菌株，使其具備分解塑膠聚合物的能力。這些微生物在處理寶特瓶後，能將原本難以自然降解的塑膠分子，轉化為生物可利用的基礎營養成分。最終產出的物質經過處理與加工，可製成口感與營養價值兼具的餅乾。
這項技術的出現，標誌著人類在「廢棄物轉化為高營養食品」的迴圈經濟領域邁出了重要的一步，將原本被視為環境負擔的塑膠垃圾，轉化為具備高經濟價值的太空補給品。
儘管μBites技術在實驗室環境下展現了高度可行性，但各界對於該技術的實際應用仍存在不同看法與待確認資訊。目前報導主要聚焦於該技術在實驗室內的轉化效率，然而，將此技術從實驗室規模放大至工業化生產，仍面臨巨大的技術挑戰，包括微生物在不同環境下的穩定性、大規模處理廢棄物的成本效益，以及最終產品在長期儲存與感官品質上的表現。
此外，雖然研究團隊宣稱產出物為「可食用」且「香濃」，但關於該食品的長期安全性評估、營養吸收率，以及是否符合航太級食品的嚴格標準，目前仍缺乏公開的臨床試驗資料支援。
針對南美地區的潛在影響，這項技術對於安地斯山脈周邊國家及巴西等擁有龐大農業產出的國傢俱有深遠意義。南美洲作為全球重要的農業基地，每年產生大量的農業廢棄物，若μBites技術能成功商業化並引進該地區，將能有效將這些廢棄物轉化為高價值的蛋白源，不僅能緩解南美部分地區的糧食不安全問題，亦能透過在地化的迴圈經濟模式，減少對進口糧食的依賴。
此外，南美各國在環境保護政策上日益重視塑膠汙染，該技術若能結合當地的廢棄物管理系統，將有助於提升區域內的環保產業競爭力，並為南美跨國間的綠色科技合作提供新契機。
然而，學界與產業界均提醒，不應將μBites技術過度神話。這項技術目前仍處於研發與初步驗證階段，距離實際應用於太空任務或大規模商業化生產尚有長路。過度強調其「將垃圾變餅乾」的便捷性，可能忽略了生物工程產品在公眾心理接受度、法規監管審核以及大規模生產過程中的能源消耗等潛在限制。特別是對於太空探索而言，任何糧食供應鏈的變動均需經過極其嚴謹的認證，以確保在極端環境下不會對太空人的健康造成威脅。
因此，在技術尚未完全成熟前，應保持審慎樂觀的態度，並持續關注後續的安全性評估與商業化進度。
後續觀察重點將集中在三個維度：首先是SIU Carbondale團隊是否會在近期發表更詳細的技術白皮書，揭露該技術在不同環境條件下的轉化效率與產品成分分析；其次是國際航太機構如NASA是否會將μBites納入未來的太空糧食供應鏈評估計畫；最後則是該技術在南美等農業大國的推廣潛力，特別是針對農業廢棄物處理的在地化應用方案。
隨著全球對於迴圈經濟與太空永續發展的重視，μBites技術的發展動向，將持續作為太空科學與環境工程領域的觀察指標，並可能影響未來跨區域的資源迴圈政策走向。
