  # Central Asia’s Digital Transformation and AI Strategy: Technological and Economic Layout Amidst Geopolitics
  
  > As the geopolitical significance of Central Asia rises, the region is attempting to transform into a global hub for technology and trade through aviation expansion, private sector development, and artificial intelligence strategic planning, while seeking stable growth amidst international crises.
  
  In recent years, the status of Central Asia in the international geopolitical and economic landscape has risen significantly. Sodiq Safoyev, a senator from Uzbekistan, recently pointed out that against the backdrop of frequent global international crises, the Central Asian region is demonstrating unprecedented "predictability" and investment attractiveness. This regional trend is reflected not only in foreign policy but also extends to the construction of the physical economy and digital infrastructure.
  
  Meanwhile, global tech giant Microsoft officially promoted the "Copilot Workspaces" collaboration platform at the Ignite conference held in San Francisco in November 2026, declaring that artificial intelligence (AI) has entered the era of "multi-user" human-machine collaboration. Such technological advancements are creating potential interactions with the needs of Central Asian countries seeking digital transformation.
  
  According to multiple sources, Central Asian countries are actively promoting regional connectivity. In the aviation sector, Euronews reported that the Tashkent conference facilitated agreements to increase flight limits between Central Asian countries and between Central Asia and China, which will directly promote regional trade and the movement of people. World Bank officials also emphasized that the Central Asian region has attracted the attention of global investors; to translate this attention into long-term economic growth, the key lies in creating broader space for the private sector to develop.
  
  Furthermore, Azerbaijan and Central Asian countries have demonstrated an intention to jointly develop into a regional AI hub. Official Norov pointed out that by strengthening cooperation in energy, strategic minerals, transportation, and digital services, the total annual foreign trade of the Central Asian region is expected to increase from the current $216 billion to $500 billion.
  
  However, there are differences among sources regarding the path and risk assessment of Central Asia's digital transformation. While Norov holds an optimistic view of the regional AI hub vision, The Caspian Post has issued a warning regarding the security threats posed by AI, arguing that while Central Asian countries embrace AI technology, they must establish stronger collaborative mechanisms between government agencies, banks, technology companies, media organizations, and research centers to address potential digital security risks.
  
  In addition, the "predictability" emphasized by Safoyev still needs to be built on deeper trust and a shared identity within the region, which means that despite the promising economic outlook, the progress of political integration remains a key variable determining the stability of regional development.
  
  For the Central Asian region, these policy and technological changes will have profound impacts. At the industrial level, the relaxation of aviation agreements will directly reduce cross-border transportation costs and promote supply chain integration; the promotion of digital services is expected to alleviate the over-concentration of traditional manufacturing on energy dependence. At the policy level, governments are being forced to seek a balance between "attracting international investment" and "maintaining national security," especially when introducing advanced AI technology; how to establish a regulatory framework that fits regional characteristics has become a top priority for policymakers in various countries.
  
  If AI collaboration tools promoted by international companies like Microsoft can be imported into the Central Asian market in the future, they may accelerate the digitalization process of enterprises in the region, but they also place higher demands on local digital infrastructure.
  
  Although there is a generally positive attitude toward the development prospects of Central Asia, there are still many limitations and areas that should not be over-interpreted. First, the level of digital infrastructure in countries within the region is uneven, making the implementation of a unified AI strategy extremely difficult. Second, although the trade volume target is set at $500 billion, this is highly dependent on the continued stability of the geopolitical situation; any sudden international conflict could disrupt existing trade routes. Furthermore, the introduction of AI technology is not a panacea; without corresponding education, training, and workforce transformation, the mere import of technology may be difficult to translate into substantive economic productivity.
  
  Therefore, when observing Central Asia's digital transformation, outsiders should distinguish between "policy vision" and "actual execution capability," avoiding the over-interpretation of preliminary intentions for regional cooperation as established economic entities.
  
  Subsequent observation should focus on the specific legislative processes of Central Asian countries regarding AI security regulation, and whether countries can effectively implement the agreements on aviation and trade from the Tashkent conference. In addition, as global AI collaboration tools become more widespread, how Central Asian enterprises integrate these international technological resources to enhance their competitiveness, as well as the actual investment amounts from international investors in the private sectors of these countries, will be core indicators for assessing whether the region can successfully transform into a digital hub. All parties should continue to pay attention to the details of cross-border cooperation mechanisms within the region, especially specific agreements on digital infrastructure sharing and transnational cybersecurity defense, which will determine Central Asia's voice in the future global technological landscape.

# 中亞區域數位轉型與AI戰略：地緣政治下的科技與經濟佈局

> 隨著中亞地區地緣政治重要性提升，該區域正透過航空擴張、私部門發展及人工智慧戰略佈局，試圖轉型為全球科技與貿易樞紐，並在國際危機中尋求穩定發展。


中亞地區近年在國際地緣政治與經濟版圖中的地位顯著提升，烏茲別克參議員薩迪克·薩福耶夫（Sodiq Safoyev）近期公開指出，在當前全球國際危機頻發的背景下，中亞地區正展現出前所未有的「可預測性」與投資吸引力。這一區域趨勢不僅體現在外交政策上，更延伸至實體經濟與數位基礎設施的建設。
與此同時，全球科技巨頭如微軟（Microsoft）於2026年11月在舊金山舉辦的Ignite大會中，正式推動「Copilot Workspaces」協作平臺，宣告人工智慧（AI）進入「多人模式」的人機協作時代，此類技術進步正與中亞國家尋求數位化轉型的需求產生潛在的互動作用。
根據多項來源確認，中亞國家正積極推動區域內的互聯互通。在航空領域，歐洲新聞臺（Euronews）報導指出，塔什干會議促成了中亞國家間以及中亞與中國之間擴大航班上限的協議，這將直接促進區域內的貿易往來與人員流動。世界銀行（World Bank）官員亦強調，中亞地區目前吸引了全球投資者的目光，若要將此關注轉化為長期的經濟成長，關鍵在於必須為私部門創造更寬廣的發展空間。
此外，亞塞拜然與中亞國家已展現出共同發展為區域AI樞紐的意圖，相關官員諾羅夫（Norov）指出，透過加強能源、戰略礦產、運輸及數位服務等領域的合作，中亞地區的年度外貿總額有望從目前的2160億美元提升至5000億美元。
然而，各來源對於中亞數位轉型的路徑與風險評估存在差異。雖然諾羅夫對區域AI樞紐的願景持樂觀態度，但《裡海郵報》（Caspian Post）則針對AI帶來的安全威脅提出警示，認為中亞國家在擁抱AI技術的同時，必須建立政府機構、銀行、科技公司、媒體組織與研究中心之間更強大的協作機制，以應對潛在的數位安全風險。
此外，薩福耶夫強調的「可預測性」仍需建立在區域內更深層的信任與共同身分認同之上，這意味著儘管經濟前景看好，但政治上的整合進度仍是決定區域發展穩定性的關鍵變數。
對於中亞地區而言，這些政策與技術變革將帶來深遠影響。在產業層面，航空協定的放寬將直接降低跨境運輸成本，促進供應鏈整合；而數位服務的推廣則有望緩解傳統製造業對能源依賴的過度集中。在政策層面，各國政府正被迫在「吸引國際投資」與「維護國家安全」之間尋求平衡，特別是在引入先進AI技術時，如何建立符合區域特性的監管架構，已成為各國決策層的當務之急。
微軟等國際企業推動的AI協作工具，未來若能匯入中亞市場，將可能加速該區域企業的數位化程式，但同時也對當地的數位基礎設施提出了更高的要求。
儘管各界對中亞的發展前景多持正面態度，但仍有諸多限制與不應過度解讀之處。首先，區域內各國的數位基礎設施水準參差不齊，統一的AI戰略實施難度極高。其次，儘管貿易額目標設定在5000億美元，但這高度依賴於地緣政治局勢的持續穩定，任何突發的國際衝突都可能打斷現有的貿易路徑。此外，AI技術的引進並非萬靈丹，若缺乏相應的教育培訓與勞動力轉型，單純的技術匯入恐難以轉化為實質的經濟生產力。
因此，外界在觀察中亞數位轉型時，應區分「政策願景」與「實際執行能力」之間的落差，避免將區域合作的初步意向過度解讀為已達成的經濟實體。
後續觀察重點應聚焦於中亞國家在AI安全監管方面的具體立法程式，以及各國是否能有效落實塔什干會議中關於航空與貿易的協議。此外，隨著全球AI協作工具的普及，中亞企業如何整合這些國際技術資源以提升自身競爭力，以及國際投資者在這些國傢俬部門的實際投入金額，將是評估該地區是否能成功轉型為數位樞紐的核心指標。各界應持續關注區域內跨國合作機制的細節，特別是針對數位基礎設施共享與跨國網路安全防禦的具體協定，這將決定中亞在未來全球科技版圖中的話語權。
