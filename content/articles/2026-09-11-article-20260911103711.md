  # Tensions Rise in the Strait of Hormuz: Japanese Shipping Industry Implements Double Pay Allowance to Stabilize Workforce
  
  > Major Japanese shipping companies have reached an agreement with labor unions to provide a special allowance equivalent to the basic wage for crew members operating in high-risk waters such as the Strait of Hormuz, addressing the challenge of talent retention amidst military threats.
  
  The global shipping industry is currently facing severe geopolitical challenges. In critical waters such as the Strait of Hormuz, the Persian Gulf, and the Gulf of Oman, the risk of military conflict continues to escalate, making shipping safety and workforce stability the primary concerns for operators. According to reports from the Japanese media outlet Kyodo News, major shipping companies including Nippon Yusen Kaisha (NYK Line) and Mitsui O.S.K. Lines (MOL) have completed labor negotiations with the International Transport Workers' Federation (ITF) and the International Maritime Employers' Council. They have officially implemented a special allowance system for crew members sailing through these high-risk waters. This move aims to alleviate crew concerns regarding safety through substantial wage increases and to ensure that operational manpower on critical routes is not lost due to panic.
  
  According to various media reports, the core of this agreement is the formal designation of the Strait of Hormuz, the Persian Gulf, and the Gulf of Oman as "military operation areas." Regarding the salary calculation standard, once crew members enter these designated waters, they will receive an initial allowance equivalent to five days of their basic wage. If the stay exceeds five days, a "daily calculation" model will be applied, meaning an additional day's wage will be paid for each extra day spent in the area. Through this cumulative bonus mechanism, the income of crew members during their time in these waters can effectively reach a "double pay" level. Furthermore, the system includes retroactive provisions starting from March 2026, highlighting the urgent need for companies to retain professional maritime talent.
  
  Although both labor and management have reached a consensus on the allowance system, technical differences remain regarding the scope of application for different ship registries and contract details. Reports indicate that the agreement is primarily driven by major Japanese shipping companies and relevant unions. However, in the global shipping industry, wage structures and collective bargaining mechanisms for crew members of different nationalities vary, meaning that not all vessels passing through the region are subject to this "double pay" standard. Additionally, there is currently no clear public information on whether the assessment of military threat levels will lead to adjustments in allowance tiers based on geopolitical changes. Whether shipping companies will further increase subsidies in response to a worsening situation remains a key point of observation for the industry.
  
  This policy has profound implications for the global energy supply chain and the shipping industry. As the Strait of Hormuz is a strategic chokepoint for global oil transport, any labor shortage or shipping disruption will directly impact energy prices and supply stability. By trading higher wage costs for workforce stability, the Japanese shipping industry may temporarily alleviate the crisis of crew attrition; however, this also means that operational costs will rise significantly. These costs are highly likely to be passed on to freight rates, subsequently affecting global logistics prices. For energy-importing countries that rely on these routes, such labor agreements are not merely internal corporate welfare policies, but a direct reflection of the deteriorating international maritime security environment and the heavy price the shipping industry must pay to maintain operations under extreme risk.
  
  However, experts caution against over-interpreting this move as a panacea for geopolitical risks. The allowance system is essentially "risk compensation" rather than "risk elimination." While increased real wages may improve crew members' willingness to stay, if military conflicts escalate further, leading to a significantly higher risk of actual vessel attacks or casualties, simple financial incentives may struggle to offset concerns for personal safety. Furthermore, this policy may trigger competitive pressure among other shipping companies during recruitment, leading to an imbalance in the wage structure of the global maritime labor market, which could pose even more severe operational pressure on smaller shipping firms.
  
  Future observations will focus on the effectiveness of this system and whether other international shipping companies will follow suit. First, it is necessary to closely monitor the dynamics of military threats in the region; if regional conflicts continue to expand, it remains to be seen whether the International Transport Workers' Federation will further expand the scope of the "military operation areas." Second, it is worth noting whether this decision by the Japanese shipping industry will become a wage benchmark for the international shipping community, thereby driving structural adjustments in global maritime labor conditions. Finally, the escort and security measures implemented by various governments and the International Maritime Organization (IMO) for high-risk waters will either complement or conflict with the industry's human resource compensation policies, which will be a key indicator of global shipping resilience in the coming months.

# 荷姆茲海峽局勢升溫：日本海運業祭出雙薪津貼以穩固人力

> 日本海運巨頭與勞方達成協議，針對航行荷姆茲海峽等高風險水域的船員，發放等同基本工資的特別津貼，以應對軍事威脅下的留才挑戰。


近期全球海運產業面臨嚴峻的地緣政治挑戰，特別是在荷姆茲海峽、波斯灣及阿曼灣等關鍵水域，因軍事衝突風險持續攀升，導致航運安全與人力穩定成為業者首要難題。根據日本媒體《共同社》報導，日本郵船、商船三井等大型海運公司，已與國際運輸勞連（ITF）及國際船員勞務協會完成勞資談判，針對航行於上述高風險水域的船員，正式實施特別津貼制度。此舉旨在透過實質的薪資加給，緩解船員對安全環境的疑慮，並確保關鍵航線的營運人力不致因恐慌而流失。
綜合多家媒體報導，此次協議的核心在於將荷姆茲海峽、波斯灣及阿曼灣正式指定為「軍事行動區域」。在薪資計算標準上，船員一旦進入這些指定水域，即先行加發相當於基本工資五日份的津貼；若停留時間超過五日，後續則採取「按日計算」模式，即每多停留一日，便額外加發一日份的薪資。透過此種累積式的加給機制，船員在該水域停留期間的收入實質上可達到「雙薪」水準。此外，該制度更具備追溯效力，自二〇二六年三月起算，顯示業者對於留住專業航運人才的迫切需求。
儘管勞資雙方已對津貼制度達成共識，但針對不同船籍與合約細節的適用範圍，仍存有部分技術性差異。報導指出，該協議主要由日本大型海運公司與相關工會推動，但全球航運業中，不同國籍船員的薪資結構與工會協商機制各異，這意味著並非所有航經該區域的船隻皆適用此項「雙薪」標準。此外，關於軍事威脅程度的評估是否會隨地緣政治變動而調整津貼級距，目前尚無明確的公開資訊，各航運公司是否會因應未來局勢惡化，進一步提高補貼額度，仍是業界觀察重點。
此項政策對全球能源供應鏈與海運產業具有深遠影響。荷姆茲海峽作為全球石油運輸的戰略要衝，任何人力短缺或航運中斷都將直接衝擊能源價格與供應穩定。日本海運業透過提高薪資成本來換取人力穩定，雖能暫時緩解船員流失危機，但這也意味著航運營運成本將顯著上升，相關成本極可能轉嫁至運費，進而影響全球物流價格。對於依賴該航線的能源進口國而言，此類勞資協議不僅是企業內部的福利政策，更直接反映了當前國際海域安全環境的惡化，以及航運業在極端風險下維持運作的沉重代價。
然而，專家提醒，不應過度解讀此舉為解決地緣政治風險的萬靈丹。津貼制度本質上屬於「風險補償」，而非「風險消除」。儘管實質薪資增加能提高船員的留任意願，但若軍事衝突進一步升級，導致船隻遭受實質攻擊或人員傷亡風險大增，單純的薪資激勵恐難以抵銷船員對生命安全的擔憂。此外，此政策亦可能引發其他航運公司在招募人力時的競爭壓力，導致全球航運勞動力市場出現薪資結構的失衡，對於小型航運業者而言，恐將面臨更為嚴峻的經營壓力。
後續觀察重點將聚焦於此制度的實施成效，以及是否有其他國際航運公司跟進。首先，需密切關注該區域軍事威脅的動態，若區域衝突持續擴大，國際運輸勞連是否會進一步擴大「軍事行動區域」的認定範圍。其次，日本海運業的此項決定是否會成為國際航運界的薪資標竿，進而推動全球航運勞動條件的結構性調整。最後，各國政府與國際海事組織（IMO）針對高風險海域的護航與安全保障措施，將與業者的人力補償政策形成互補或衝突，這將是未來數月觀察全球航運韌性的關鍵指標。
