  # The Intertwined Impact of the Global AI Investment Wave and Middle East Geopolitical Risks
  
  > S&P Global has warned that potential AI-related liabilities of major tech giants may exceed expectations. Meanwhile, the United Arab Emirates (UAE) leads the world in AI adoption, yet the Middle East faces a dual challenge of regional stability and technological development due to threats of Red Sea shipping lane blockades and geopolitical turmoil.
  
  The global expansion of artificial intelligence (AI) technology is at a critical turning point. According to the latest report released by S&P Global in October 2026, the cumulative investment in the AI sector by the world's six largest tech giants has surpassed $7 trillion. This capital race has not only driven technological innovation but has also prompted the market to re-examine the financial resilience of major cloud service providers. Simultaneously, Microsoft’s "Global AI Adoption Report" indicates that the global workforce's AI usage rate reached 18.8% in the second quarter of 2026, with the United Arab Emirates (UAE) performing remarkably well; its adoption rate exceeds 60%, making it one of the leaders in the global AI productivity revolution.
  
  However, beneath the veneer of technological progress, geopolitical risks in the Middle East have not abated. Potential actions by Iran and the Houthi militant group in the Red Sea and the Strait of Hormuz are casting uncertainty over global energy supplies and regional stability.
  
  Facts confirmed by multiple sources show that the investment by major tech companies in AI infrastructure has reached an unprecedented scale. Mark Habib, head of digital infrastructure at S&P Global, pointed out that the market should not only focus on bonds issued directly by companies but should also be wary of "interconnected financing structures." Major cloud providers currently tend to provide guarantees for data center construction projects or small AI startups; such implicit liabilities could put pressure on corporate credit ratings if profits fall short of expectations. Regarding regional development, Microsoft's data confirms a significant increase in AI adoption, particularly in countries like the UAE and Singapore, demonstrating these regions' active commitment to digital transformation.
  
  However, at the same time, the complexity of Middle Eastern geopolitics persists. According to Reuters and related media reports, the threat of potential blockades of the Red Sea shipping lanes and the Strait of Hormuz has become a core issue affecting global energy markets and regional security.
  
  Although various data sources present the dual facets of technology and geopolitics, there remain information gaps and areas awaiting confirmation between sources. S&P Global emphasizes that despite the massive scale of potential liabilities, there is no need to adjust the credit ratings of relevant companies at this stage, implying that the market remains cautiously optimistic about the debt-servicing capacity of tech giants. Microsoft's survey reveals concerns about the widening global "digital divide," where the gap between leading countries and those catching up is growing, which contrasts with the conservative trends in social policies within different Middle Eastern nations.
  
  For instance, while the United Arab Emirates is promoting AI technology, its Federal National Council (FNC) has recently issued warnings regarding demographic balance, calling for restrictions on marriages with foreigners. The tension between these social policies and technological modernization reflects the dual pressure Middle Eastern countries face in pursuing global competitiveness while maintaining traditional social structures.
  
  The surge in technology investment has had a profound impact on the global industrial chain. For the Middle East, the increase in AI adoption helps with economic diversification and reduces reliance on traditional energy exports. However, the issue of shipping lane security in the Red Sea and the Strait of Hormuz directly threatens the stability of energy transport and global supply chains. If the Red Sea shipping lanes are blocked, it would create a compounding effect with the current status of the Strait of Hormuz, leading to an intensified global energy crisis. Furthermore, if the massive investments by tech giants in AI infrastructure fail to translate into actual profits, it will not only affect the tech industry itself but could also ripple through financing structures to impact emerging markets in the Middle East that rely on cloud services and digital infrastructure.
  
  Regarding these trends, there are significant differences in perspectives. Some analyses suggest that the adoption of AI technology will be key for Middle Eastern countries to boost productivity and is the only way to break free from resource dependency. However, another school of thought warns that over-reliance on external technology and fragile financing structures could make these countries more vulnerable when facing geopolitical shocks. Additionally, it remains to be seen whether the conservative tendencies in social policies in countries like the UAE will hinder their efforts to attract global AI talent. We should not view the increase in AI adoption as an absolute indicator of regional stability, as the process of digitalization is often accompanied by deeper social and political changes, the long-term impacts of which are difficult to quantify.
  
  Subsequent observations will focus on three dimensions: First, whether S&P Global will adjust the rating outlooks of relevant companies due to the accumulation of debt in the AI sector by tech giants. Second, whether geopolitical tensions in the Middle East, particularly the logistics security of the Red Sea shipping lanes, will cause a substantive disruption to the global technology supply chain. Finally, how countries like the UAE handle the conflict between demographic structures and social values while promoting high-tech development will be a key indicator of whether the region can successfully transition to a digital economy. Market participants should closely monitor the financial performance of major cloud providers and the balancing strategies of Middle Eastern countries between energy exports and technology investments.

# 全球AI投資浪潮與中東地緣政治風險的交織影響

> 標普全球示警大型科技巨頭AI相關潛在負債恐超乎預期，與此同時，阿拉伯聯合大公國（UAE）在AI普及率領跑全球，然中東地區面臨紅海航道封鎖威脅與地緣政治動盪，科技發展與區域穩定正面臨雙重挑戰。


人工智慧（AI）技術的全球擴張正處於關鍵轉折點。根據標普全球（S&P Global）於2026年10月發布的最新報告，全球六大科技巨頭在AI領域的投資規模已累計突破7兆美元。這場資本競賽不僅推動了技術革新，也讓市場開始重新審視大型雲端服務商的財務韌性。與此同時，微軟發布的《全球人工智慧普及報告》指出，2026年第二季全球勞動人口的AI使用率已達18.8%，其中阿拉伯聯合大公國（UAE）表現亮眼，其普及率超過60%，成為全球AI生產力革命的領跑者之一。
然而，在科技進步的表象下，中東地區的地緣政治風險並未減緩，伊朗與胡塞武裝組織在紅海與荷姆茲海峽的潛在行動，正為全球能源供應與區域穩定投下不確定因素。
多方來源共同確認的事實顯示，大型科技企業對AI基礎建設的投入已達到前所未有的規模。標普全球數位基礎設施負責人哈比卜（Mark Habib）指出，市場不應僅關注企業直接發行的債券，更需警惕「相互關聯的融資結構」。大型雲端業者目前傾向於為資料中心建設專案或小型AI新創提供擔保，這種隱性負債可能在獲利不如預期時，對企業信用評級構成壓力。在區域發展方面，微軟的資料證實了AI普及率的顯著增長，特別是阿聯與新加坡等國，顯示出這些地區在數位轉型上的積極投入。
然而，與此同時，中東地緣政治的複雜性亦持續存在，根據路透社與相關媒體報導，紅海航道與荷姆茲海峽的潛在封鎖威脅，已成為影響全球能源市場與區域安全的核心議題。
儘管各方資料呈現了科技與地緣政治的雙重面貌，但來源間仍存在資訊落差與尚待確認之處。標普全球強調，儘管潛在負債規模龐大，但現階段尚無必要調整相關企業的信用評級，這意味著市場對科技巨頭的償債能力仍持審慎樂觀態度。微軟的調查則揭示了全球「數位鴻溝」擴大的隱憂，即領跑國與追趕者之間的差距正在拉大，這與中東內部不同國家在社會政策上的保守趨勢形成對比。
例如，阿拉伯聯合大公國在推動AI科技的同時，其聯邦國民議會（FNC）近期亦針對人口結構平衡提出警告，呼籲限制與外國人通婚。這些社會政策與技術現代化之間的拉扯，反映出中東國家在追求全球化競爭力與維護傳統社會結構之間的雙重壓力。
科技投資的激增對全球產業鏈產生了深遠影響。對於中東地區而言，AI普及率的提升有助於經濟多元化，減少對傳統能源出口的依賴。然而，紅海與荷姆茲海峽的航道安全問題，直接威脅到能源運輸與全球供應鏈的穩定。若紅海航道遭到封鎖，將與荷姆茲海峽的現狀形成疊加效應，導致全球能源危機加劇。此外，科技巨頭在AI基礎設施上的巨額投資，若未能轉化為實際獲利，不僅會影響科技產業本身，亦可能透過融資結構的連鎖反應，波及依賴雲端服務與數位基礎建設的中東新興市場。
對於上述趨勢，各界觀點存在顯著分歧。部分分析認為，AI技術的普及將成為中東國家提升生產力的關鍵，是擺脫資源依賴的唯一途徑；然而，另一派觀點則提醒，過度依賴外部技術與脆弱的融資結構，可能使這些國家在面對地緣政治衝擊時更加脆弱。此外，關於阿聯等國在社會政策上的保守傾向，是否會阻礙其吸引全球AI人才的程式，目前仍有待觀察。我們不應將AI普及率的提升視為區域穩定的絕對指標，因為數位化的程式往往伴隨著更深層的社會與政治變革，這些變革的長期影響尚難以量化。
後續觀察重點將集中於以下三個維度：首先，標普全球是否會因為科技巨頭在AI領域的負債累積，進而調整相關企業的評級展望。其次，中東地區的地緣政治緊張局勢，特別是紅海航道的物流安全，是否會對全球科技供應鏈造成實質性阻斷。最後，阿聯等國在推動高科技發展的同時，如何處理人口結構與社會價值觀的衝突，將成為觀察該地區能否成功轉型數位經濟的關鍵指標。市場參與者應密切關注大型雲端業者的財報表現，以及中東地區在能源出口與科技投資之間的平衡策略。
