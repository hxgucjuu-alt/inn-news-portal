  # Global Climate Change and Energy Supply Challenges: The Reshaping of Emerging Markets and Energy Strategy
  
  > Shifting global climate patterns and geopolitical tensions are prompting financial institutions to adjust investment strategies and triggering a reorganization of energy supply chains. As a critical hub for energy and strategy, Central Asia’s regional stability and international cooperation models are becoming a focal point of global attention.
  
  Extreme weather events caused by global climate change have posed substantial challenges to international financial markets and energy supply chains. The abnormal rise in temperatures caused by El Niño has not only triggered drastic fluctuations in global weather patterns but has also directly impacted crop yields, the stability of energy supplies, and inflationary pressures. Asset management firm T. Rowe Price recently announced the integration of customized climate models, aiming to protect its $165 billion emerging market debt and equity portfolio against potential financial risks posed by climate change.
  
  Meanwhile, the U.S. Strategic Petroleum Reserve (SPR) has fallen to its lowest level since the early 1980s due to a surge in energy demand driven by geopolitical conflicts, forcing the Trump administration to seek new energy supply sources, including Venezuela, signaling that energy security has become a core issue in current national policies.
  
  Regarding regional strategic positioning, the status of Central Asia is becoming increasingly prominent. According to Voice of America, China and the "Five Stans"—Kazakhstan, Kyrgyzstan, Tajikistan, Turkmenistan, and Uzbekistan—are strengthening bilateral cooperation through regular Central Asia summits. This move is viewed as a key strategy to counter the influence of the Group of Seven (G7) amidst the backdrop of global great power competition.
  
  Furthermore, according to POLITICO, the Trump administration has appointed Sergio Gor as the Special Envoy for Central Asia, concurrently serving as the Ambassador to India. The intent is to leverage this dual role to strengthen U.S. influence in Central Asia and promote regional economic and energy deals that align with U.S. interests, indicating that Central Asia has become a strategic frontier for the struggle for influence between the U.S. and China.
  
  Regarding international participation in climate change, Taiwan is also actively seeking breakthroughs. Ambassador Joanne Ou, Representative to New Zealand, recently emphasized in public that Taiwan possesses significant advantages in green technology, renewable energy, and climate resilience, calling on the international community to support Taiwan's meaningful participation in the United Nations Framework Convention on Climate Change (UNFCCC). However, current international political constraints on Taiwan's participation in the UN system present diplomatic challenges as Taiwan seeks to contribute its experience in global climate governance. Information from various parties indicates that climate change is no longer merely an environmental issue, but a complex challenge closely intertwined with regional security, energy supply, and international diplomacy.
  
  The impact of climate change on the global energy market is concrete and profound. T. Rowe Price points out that crop failures and energy supply instability caused by El Niño will directly push up global inflationary pressures, posing a significant threat to capital flows reliant on emerging market investments. To stabilize gasoline prices, the U.S. government drew down more than 30% of its Strategic Petroleum Reserve within six months following the outbreak of the Iran conflict in February this year, causing reserves to fall below 285 million barrels. Energy experts and the Brookings Institution warn that excessive oil extraction could lead to damage to salt cavern structures, subsequently triggering physical degradation of storage facilities, which is undoubtedly another layer of potential structural risk for the long-term U.S. energy security reserve mechanism.
  
  Although there is consensus on the direction of adjustments for climate models and energy policies, expectations regarding policy effectiveness remain divided. Financial institutions hope to avoid risks through big data and climate models, but the unpredictability of climate change still limits the precision of these models. On the geopolitical front, the U.S. attempt to return to Central Asia by appointing a special envoy creates competition with the regional cooperation mechanisms that China has cultivated for years. However, the balancing strategies of Central Asian nations amidst the U.S.-China rivalry, as well as their own trade-offs between energy exports and climate transition, remain unknown variables affecting the regional situation. Observers should not over-interpret the short-term effects of a single diplomatic appointment or investment strategy, but should continue to monitor the actual role of Central Asian countries in the reorganization of energy supply chains.
  
  Subsequent observations should focus on: first, the long-term inflationary impact of El Niño on global food and energy prices, and the actual defensive performance of financial institutions' climate models; second, the progress of rebuilding the U.S. Strategic Petroleum Reserve and the stability of Venezuelan crude oil supply; third, the actual diplomatic choices of Central Asian countries between the two major powers of the U.S. and China, and their concrete actions in regional climate transition cooperation; and fourth, the substantive progress of Taiwan's technology exports in the green energy sector and its participation in international climate governance. These issues will collectively determine the future trajectory of the global energy and geopolitical landscape.

# 全球氣候變遷與能源供應挑戰：新興市場與能源戰略的重塑

> 全球氣候模式變遷與地緣政治緊張，促使金融機構調整投資策略並引發能源供應鏈重組。中亞地區作為能源與戰略要衝，其區域穩定與國際合作模式正成為全球關注焦點。


全球氣候變遷帶來的極端天氣事件，已對國際金融市場與能源供應鏈構成實質挑戰。聖嬰現象（El Niño）導致的氣溫異常升高，不僅引發全球天氣模式劇烈波動，更直接衝擊農作物產量、能源供應穩定性及通貨膨脹壓力。資產管理機構普信集團（T. Rowe Price）近期宣佈匯入客製化氣候模型，旨在保護其規模達 1,650 億美元的新興市場債務與股票投資組合，以應對氣候變遷帶來的潛在金融風險。
與此同時，美國戰略石油儲備（SPR）因地緣政治衝突導致的能源需求激增，已降至 1980 年代初期以來的新低點，迫使川普政府尋求包括委內瑞拉在內的新能源供應來源，顯示能源安全已成為當前各國政策的核心議題。
在區域戰略佈局上，中亞地區的地位日益凸顯。根據美國之音報導，中國與包含哈薩克、吉爾吉斯、塔吉克、土庫曼及烏茲別克在內的「五斯坦」國家，透過定期舉辦中亞峰會強化雙邊合作，此舉被視為在全球大國博弈背景下，對抗七大工業國組織（G7）影響力的重要策略。
此外，根據《POLITICO》報導，川普政府任命塞爾吉奧·戈爾（Sergio Gor）擔任中亞特使，並兼任駐印度大使，意圖透過雙重身分強化美國在中亞的影響力，推動符合美方利益的區域經濟與能源交易，顯示中亞已成為美中兩大強權爭奪影響力的戰略前線。
針對氣候變遷的國際參與，臺灣亦積極尋求突破。駐紐西蘭代表歐江安大使近期於公開場合強調，臺灣在綠色科技、再生能源及氣候韌性方面擁有顯著優勢，呼籲國際社會支援臺灣有意義參與「聯合國氣候變化綱要公約」（UNFCCC）。然而，目前國際政治環境對臺灣參與聯合國體系的限制，使得臺灣在貢獻全球氣候治理經驗時面臨外交挑戰。各方資訊顯示，氣候變遷已非單純的環境議題，而是與區域安全、能源供應及國際外交緊密扣連的複雜挑戰。
氣候變遷對全球能源市場的影響具體且深遠。普信集團指出，聖嬰現象帶來的農作物歉收與能源供應不穩，將直接推升全球通膨壓力，這對依賴新興市場投資的資金流動構成巨大威脅。美國政府為了穩定汽油價格，在今年二月伊朗衝突爆發後，於六個月內抽取超過三成的戰略石油儲備，導致儲備量跌破 2.85 億桶。能源專家與布魯金斯研究院警告，過度抽取石油可能導致鹽穴結構損壞，進而引發儲存設施的物理退化，這對於美國長期的能源安全儲備機制而言，無疑是另一層潛在的結構性風險。
儘管各界對於氣候模型與能源政策的調整方向一致，但對於政策效果的預期仍存在分歧。金融機構寄望透過大資料與氣候模型規避風險，但氣候變遷的不可預測性仍限制了模型的精準度。而在地緣政治方面，美國試圖透過任命特使重返中亞，與中國深耕多年的區域合作機制形成競爭，但中亞國家在美中博弈間的平衡策略，以及其自身在能源出口與氣候轉型間的取捨，仍是影響區域局勢的未知變數。各界不應過度解讀單一外交任命或投資策略的短期效應，應持續觀察中亞國家在能源供應鏈重組中的實際角色。
後續觀察重點應聚焦於：第一，聖嬰現象對全球糧食與能源價格的長期通膨影響，以及金融機構氣候模型的實際防禦績效；第二，美國戰略石油儲備的重建進度與委內瑞拉原油供應的穩定性；第三，中亞國家在美中兩大強權之間的實際外交選擇，以及其在區域氣候轉型合作中的具體行動；第四，臺灣在綠色能源領域的技術輸出與國際氣候治理參與的實質進展。這些議題將共同決定未來全球能源與地緣政治的格局走向。
