  # Deepening Global Space Exploration Cooperation: NASA, JAXA, and ESA Join Forces to Advance Lunar and Planetary Defense Missions
  
  > International space agencies have recently signed a series of cooperation agreements focusing on lunar exploration, planetary defense, and the safety technology of space devices. NASA and JAXA have deepened their intentions for lunar exploration, while JAXA and ESA have officially launched the RAMSES asteroid defense mission, marking a new phase in multinational space research cooperation.
  
  As a core platform for human scientific research in low Earth orbit, the International Space Station (ISS) has long hosted various medical experiments led by institutions such as the National Space Biomedical Research Institute (NSBRI), accumulating critical data for long-term human space habitation. As the demands of space exploration expand from low Earth orbit to deep space, international cooperation models have evolved from singular scientific experiments into deep integration across strategic fields, including lunar resource development, asteroid defense, and the security of space infrastructure.
  
  Recently, several agencies, including the U.S. National Aeronautics and Space Administration (NASA), the Japan Aerospace Exploration Agency (JAXA), and the European Space Agency (ESA), have established a framework for cooperation in deep space over the next decade through a series of signed agreements, aiming to integrate global resources to address the severe challenges of the space environment.
  
  According to confirmed public information from multiple parties, NASA and JAXA have officially reaffirmed their intent to cooperate in the field of lunar exploration. Both sides will further coordinate the technical standards and objectives of lunar missions under the existing space exploration framework. Simultaneously, JAXA and ESA have reached an important consensus, signing a memorandum of cooperation that officially extends their collaboration into the field of planetary defense. The most notable project is their joint participation in the RAMSES mission, which aims to observe the asteroid Apophis at close range and assess its potential impact risk to Earth.
  
  Furthermore, in terms of industrial technology application, Japan’s Yokogawa Electric has launched a research project with JAXA’s Space Exploration Innovation Hub, focusing on the development of fiber-optic sensing technology. This initiative aims to enhance the operational safety of space devices in extreme environments, demonstrating that multinational research cooperation has penetrated into the underlying research and development of aerospace supply chains and sensing technologies.
  
  Although the agencies have reached a consensus on the general direction of cooperation, there remain varying degrees of information gaps regarding specific execution details and resource allocation. For instance, the specific timeline for lunar exploration between NASA and JAXA is currently limited to an expression of intent, with no detailed mission roadmap released. Meanwhile, while JAXA and ESA have signed a memorandum for the RAMSES mission, the priority of data sharing and the specific division of labor for subsequent defense technologies remain subject to further approval by national parliaments or relevant decision-making bodies.
  
  Additionally, although the collaboration project between Yokogawa Electric and JAXA has been announced, there is no clear timetable for when this technology will actually be applied to future lunar bases or deep-space probes. Observers remain cautious regarding the durability of this technology in the high-radiation environment of space.
  
  These multinational collaborations have had a profound impact on the global space industry and technology policy. First, at the policy level, the deepened cooperation between NASA and JAXA will accelerate the "Artemis" program, enhancing the strategic status of the Moon as a springboard for deep space. Second, at the industrial level, the technical alignment between JAXA and private enterprises (such as Yokogawa Electric) provides a model for the transformation of the space industry chain—namely, converting high-precision ground sensing technology into space-grade hardware, which will help reduce the maintenance costs of space devices.
  
  Finally, cooperation on planetary defense is not merely scientific research but also carries significant public safety implications. Through the data integration between ESA and JAXA, global monitoring capabilities for near-Earth asteroids will be significantly enhanced, playing a critical role in protecting Earth from the threat of celestial impacts.
  
  However, academia and the industry also caution that these cooperative projects still face multiple limitations and should not be over-interpreted in terms of immediate results. First, the extreme nature of the space environment means that the validation cycle for any new technology is extremely long; even with excellent laboratory data, moving to field testing can take years or even decades. Second, multinational cooperation is often influenced by geopolitics and national budget allocations; once the domestic policies of participating countries change, the continuity of large-scale space missions may be threatened.
  
  Furthermore, planetary defense missions like RAMSES are inherently high-risk scientific explorations. The success of the mission depends on the precise integration of technologies from multiple countries, and any delay in any link could lead to a stagnation of the entire project. Therefore, expectations regarding the mission should remain rational, avoiding the conflation of scientific exploration with commercial development.
  
  Subsequent observation will focus on three aspects: First, whether NASA and JAXA will release more specific lunar mission execution contracts in the near future, which will serve as a litmus test for the depth of their cooperation. Second, whether ESA and JAXA will invite other international space agencies to join the RAMSES mission after its launch to expand the global planetary defense monitoring network. Third, the performance of Yokogawa Electric’s fiber-optic sensing technology in JAXA’s test environment; if successfully validated, it could become standard equipment for the future International Space Station or the Lunar Gateway.
  
  By continuously tracking these key nodes, it will be possible to more clearly map out the development path of human deep-space exploration over the next decade.

# 全球太空探索合作深化：NASA、JAXA與ESA攜手推進月球與行星防禦任務

> 國際太空機構近期密集簽署合作協定，聚焦於月球探測、行星防禦及太空裝置安全技術。NASA與JAXA深化月球探索意向，JAXA與ESA則正式啟動RAMSES小行星防禦任務，標誌著跨國太空科研合作進入新階段。


國際太空站（ISS）作為人類在近地軌道進行科學研究的核心平臺，長期以來透過國家航太生物醫學研究所（NSBRI）等機構主持各類醫學實驗，為人類長期太空駐留積累關鍵資料。隨著太空探索需求從近地軌道向深空拓展，國際間的合作模式已從單一的科學實驗，演變為針對月球資源開發、小行星防禦及太空基礎設施安全等戰略領域的深度整合。
近期，美國國家航空暨太空總署（NASA）、日本宇宙航空研究開發機構（JAXA）以及歐洲太空總署（ESA）等多個機構，透過一系列協議的簽署，確立了未來十年在深空領域的合作框架，旨在整合全球資源以應對太空環境的嚴峻挑戰。
根據多方公開資訊確認，NASA與JAXA已正式重申在月球探測領域的合作意向，雙方將在既有的太空探索架構下，進一步協調探月任務的技術標準與目標。與此同時，JAXA與ESA亦達成重要共識，雙方簽署了一份合作備忘錄，正式將合作觸角延伸至行星防禦領域。其中，最受矚目的專案為共同參與RAMSES任務，該任務旨在近距離觀測小行星阿波菲斯（Apophis），並評估其對地球潛在的撞擊風險。
此外，在產業技術應用方面，日本橫河電機已與JAXA旗下的太空探索創新中心啟動研究專案，專注於開發光纖感測技術，旨在提升太空裝置在極端環境下的執行安全性，此舉顯示出跨國科研合作已深入至航太供應鏈與感測技術的底層研發。
儘管各機構對於合作的大方向已達成共識，但在具體執行細節與資源分配上，仍存在不同程度的資訊差異。例如，NASA與JAXA在月球探索的具體時程表上，目前僅止於意向確認，尚未公佈詳細的任務執行路線圖；而JAXA與ESA針對RAMSES任務的合作，雖已簽署備忘錄，但關於各方在觀測資料共享的優先權，以及後續防禦技術的具體分工，目前仍有待各國議會或相關決策機構進一步核准。
此外，橫河電機與JAXA的合作專案雖已對外公佈，但該技術何時能實際應用於未來的月球基地或深空探測器，目前尚無明確的時間表，各界對於該技術在太空高輻射環境下的耐久性仍持有審慎態度。
這些跨國合作對全球太空產業與科技政策產生了深遠影響。首先，在政策層面，NASA與JAXA的深化合作將加速「阿提米絲」（Artemis）計畫的程式，提升月球作為深空跳板的戰略地位。其次，在產業層面，JAXA與民間企業（如橫河電機）的技術對接，為太空產業鏈的轉型提供了範本，即將高精密的地面感測技術轉化為太空級硬體，這將有助於降低太空裝置的維護成本。
最後，針對行星防禦的合作，不僅是單純的科學研究，更具備公共安全意義，透過ESA與JAXA的資料整合，全球對於近地小行星的監控能力將顯著提升，這對於保護地球免受天體撞擊威脅具有關鍵作用。
然而，學界與產業界亦提醒，這些合作計畫仍面臨多重限制，不應過度解讀其立即性的成效。首先，太空環境的極端性使得任何新技術的驗證週期極長，即便實驗室資料優異，進入實戰測試仍需耗費數年甚至數十年。其次，跨國合作往往受到各國地緣政治與預算編列的影響，一旦參與國家的國內政策發生變動，大型太空任務的連續性便可能受到威脅。
此外，行星防禦任務如RAMSES，其本質上屬於極高風險的科學探索，任務成功與否取決於多國技術的精確銜接，任何一個環節的延遲都可能導致整個計畫的進度停滯，因此各界對於任務的預期應保持理性，避免將科學探索與商業開發混為一談。
後續觀察重點將聚焦於以下三個面向：第一，NASA與JAXA是否會於近期發布更具體的月球任務執行合約，這將是檢驗雙方合作深度的試金石；第二，RAMSES任務在啟動後，ESA與JAXA是否會邀請其他國際太空機構加入，以擴大行星防禦的全球監測網；第三，橫河電機的光纖感測技術在JAXA測試環境中的表現，若能成功透過初步驗證，將可能成為未來國際太空站或月球門戶（Lunar Gateway）的標準配備。
透過持續追蹤這些關鍵節點，將能更清晰地描繪出未來十年人類深空探索的發展路徑。
