  # China Revises "National Defense Mobilization Law" for the First Time in 16 Years, Sparking Heightened Concern Over Regional Security
  
  > The Standing Committee of the National People's Congress of China recently revised the "National Defense Mobilization Law," completing the legislative process after 16 years. This move has raised concerns regarding peace-to-war transition mechanisms and geopolitical tensions in Northeast Asia, with Taiwan and neighboring countries closely monitoring the subsequent impact.
  
  The 14th Standing Committee of the National People's Congress of China completed the revision process of the "National Defense Mobilization Law of the People's Republic of China" on August 28, 2026. This marks a significant amendment to the law, which has been in effect since 2010, after a 16-year interval. According to relevant official explanations, the core objective of this revision is to establish a more comprehensive peace-to-war transition mechanism, aimed at effectively converting social resources and economic productivity into combat power during national defense mobilization. This revision comes at a time when the geopolitical environment in Northeast Asia is complex and volatile, including the ongoing issues surrounding nuclear submarines on the Korean Peninsula and the strategic rivalry between the U.S.-Japan-South Korea bloc and the China-Russia bloc, making the revision not only an adjustment to China's internal administrative system but also an important signal of shifts in the regional security architecture.
  
  According to cross-referenced sources, this revision has been confirmed by official Chinese procedures. Democratic Progressive Party (DPP) Legislator Wang Ting-yu pointed out that this revision can be observed from three levels: first, the high importance countries have placed on peace-to-war economic transition following the Russia-Ukraine war; second, China's attempt to exert strategic pressure on the First Island Chain and Western countries through legal means; and finally, the focus on China's internal social stability needs. Meanwhile, military tensions in Northeast Asia have not eased; South Korea has recently seen public protests triggered by the visit of the U.S. Secretary of Defense and issues regarding nuclear submarine deployment, indicating that the region's security situation is extremely sensitive under the dual pressures of historical grievances and modern arms races.
  
  Regarding the specific details and execution of this revision, there are significant discrepancies among current media reports and public information. Although Wang Ting-yu emphasized that Taiwan is on the front line and must adopt a "cautious approach to the enemy" to remain vigilant, the Chinese authorities have not yet provided further detailed explanations regarding the specific scale of mobilization, criteria for resource requisition, or the strategic intent declared to the outside world following the revision. Furthermore, details concerning whether South Korean nuclear submarines will be launched in the mid-to-late 2030s and the specifics of related military technology transfers remain in the stage of speculation and media reporting, without formal conclusions from U.S. or South Korean officials; such information awaits further verification by military observers.
  
  The specific impact of this revision on industries and policies in the Northeast Asian region is gradually emerging. At the policy level, governments are reassessing the resilience of defense supply chains and regulations for the requisition of civilian facilities during wartime, which means that multinational corporations in the region will face higher geopolitical risks in their future operating environments. At the social level, China's strengthening of its national defense mobilization mechanism may lead to increased internal social control, while neighboring countries like South Korea are seeing polarized domestic public opinion on U.S. defense policies due to the intensification of military issues.
  
  Such changes not only affect regional security and stability but may also indirectly impact economic cooperation and technological exchanges in Northeast Asia, forcing countries in the region to face more severe policy choices between seeking economic prosperity and national security.
  
  Although external anxiety regarding the revision of the "National Defense Mobilization Law" has risen, experts caution against over-interpreting the changes to a single piece of legislation. First, there remains a gap between legal revisions and the actual possession of mobilization capabilities; the complexity of peace-to-war transition is extremely high and cannot be completed through legislation alone. Second, the tensions in Northeast Asia largely stem from historical legacies and great power rivalry; it is not objective to simplify all regional conflicts as the product of a single law. Furthermore, countries often employ multiple diplomatic and military means when addressing security challenges; over-hyping a single event may lead to misjudgments within the region, thereby exacerbating unnecessary confrontation. All parties should remain calm during analysis and distinguish between legal declarations and actual military deployments.
  
  Subsequent observation will focus on the implementation of related supporting administrative regulations following China's revision, and whether large-scale mobilization exercises or resource allocation adjustments will occur. At the same time, it is necessary to continue monitoring the specific actions of the U.S., Japan, and South Korea regarding nuclear submarine deployment and defense coordination in Northeast Asia, particularly whether the situation on the Korean Peninsula will lead to sudden conflicts due to an escalating arms race. For Taiwan, in addition to continuing to monitor the evolution of China's mobilization mechanism, it is also necessary to strengthen internal defense resilience and social psychological preparation, and to ensure through international cooperation that national interests and regional peace and stability can be effectively maintained during the process of changes in the regional security architecture.

# 中國修訂《國防動員法》時隔16年再啟動，區域安全域性勢引發高度關注

> 中國全國人大常委會近期修訂《國防動員法》，時隔16年完成立法程式。此舉引發外界對於平戰轉換機制及東北亞地緣政治緊張的擔憂，臺灣與鄰近國家正密切監控後續影響。


中國第14屆全國人大常委會於2026年8月28日完成《中華人民共和國國防動員法》的修訂程式。這是該法自2010年實施以來，時隔16年的重大修正。根據相關官方說明，此次修法的核心目標在於建立更為完善的平戰轉換機制，旨在將社會資源、經濟生產力有效轉化為國防動員時的戰力。此次修法正值東北亞地緣政治環境複雜多變之際，包括朝鮮半島核潛艇議題持續發酵，以及美日韓與中俄陣營間的戰略博弈，使得該法的修訂不僅涉及中國內部行政體系的調整，更被視為區域安全架構變動的重要訊號。
根據多方來源交叉比對，此次修法已獲得中國官方程式的確認。民進黨立委王定宇指出，此次修法可從三個層次進行觀察：首先是因應俄烏戰爭後，各國對於平戰經濟轉換的高度重視；其次是中國試圖透過法律手段，對第一島鏈及西方國家施加戰略壓力；最後則是著眼於中國內部的社會維穩需求。與此同時，東北亞地區的軍事緊張態勢並未緩解，韓國近期亦因美國國防部長到訪及核潛艇部署議題，引發國內民眾集會抗議，顯示該區域在歷史糾葛與現代軍備競賽的雙重壓力下，安全情勢極為敏感。
關於此次修法的具體細節與執行層面，目前各媒體報導與公開資訊存在顯著差異。雖然王定宇強調臺灣處於最前線，必須採取「料敵從寬」的態度提高警覺，但中國官方對於修法後的具體動員規模、資源徵用標準及對外宣示的戰略意圖，尚未有更進一步的詳細說明。此外，關於韓國核潛艇是否將於2030年代中後期下水，以及相關軍事技術轉移的細節，目前仍屬於各界揣測與媒體報導階段，尚未獲得美韓官方的正式定論，相關資訊仍有待後續軍事觀察家進一步查證。
此次修法對東北亞區域產業與政策的具體影響已逐漸浮現。在政策層面，各國政府正重新評估國防供應鏈的韌性與戰時民用設施的徵用規範，這對區域內的跨國企業而言，意味著未來營運環境將面臨更高的地緣政治風險。在社會層面，中國強化國防動員機制可能導致內部社會管控力度的提升，而鄰近國家如韓國，則因軍事議題的激化，導致國內輿論對美國防務政策出現兩極化反應。
此類變動不僅影響區域安全穩定，更可能間接衝擊東北亞的經濟合作與科技交流，使得區域內各國在尋求經濟繁榮與國家安全之間，面臨更為嚴峻的政策選擇。
儘管外界對《國防動員法》修訂後的威脅感上升，但專家提醒，不應過度解讀單一法律條文的變動。首先，法律的修訂與實際動員能力的具備仍有落差，平戰轉換的複雜度極高，並非僅靠立法即可完成。其次，東北亞地區的緊張局勢多源於歷史遺留問題與大國博弈，將所有區域衝突簡化為單一法律的產物並不客觀。此外，各國在應對安全挑戰時，往往採取多重外交與軍事手段，過度渲染單一事件可能導致區域內的誤判，進而加劇不必要的對立，各界在分析時應保持冷靜，並區分法律宣示與實際軍事部署的差異。
後續觀察重點將聚焦於中國修法後，相關配套行政法規的落實情形，以及是否會出現大規模的動員演習或資源配置調整。同時，需持續關注美、日、韓在東北亞針對核潛艇部署及防衛協調的具體行動，特別是朝鮮半島局勢是否會因軍備競賽升級而出現突發性衝突。臺灣方面，除了持續監控中國動員機制的演變外，亦需強化內部的國防韌性與社會心理準備，並透過國際合作，確保在區域安全架構變動過程中，能有效維護國家利益與區域和平穩定。
