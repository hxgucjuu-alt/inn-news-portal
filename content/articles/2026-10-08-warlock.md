  # Ransomware Threat Escalates: Warlock Group Targets Critical Infrastructure
  
  > The ransomware group Storm-2603 has been continuously exploiting SharePoint vulnerabilities to launch attacks, shifting its focus toward critical infrastructure such as water resources and telecommunications, as well as educational institutions, sparking high global cybersecurity concern.
  
  A significant threat has recently emerged in the global cybersecurity landscape. According to reports from cybersecurity research institutions and media, the hacker group identified by Microsoft as "Storm-2603" (also known as Longlegs) remains active in launching "Warlock" ransomware attacks. The group primarily infiltrates systems by abusing the "ToolShell" zero-day vulnerabilities in SharePoint (including CVE-2025-49704, CVE-2025-49706, CVE-2025-53770, and CVE-2025-53771).
  
  This attack methodology has drawn widespread attention from the international cybersecurity community, particularly regarding the threat posture toward critical infrastructure, which has become a focal point for defense by governments and enterprises worldwide.
  
  According to joint monitoring by Symantec and the Carbon Black threat hunting team, the activity trajectory of the Storm-2603 hacker group indicates a clear shift in its attack targets. While the group previously gained attention for the aforementioned SharePoint vulnerability attacks, recent investigations reveal that the hackers have not ceased their operations; instead, they have intensified their infiltration of critical infrastructure in specific linguistic regions. The affected scope covers water resource management systems, the telecommunications industry, and educational institutions. These confirmed findings indicate that the hacker group is utilizing a sophisticated exploit chain to conduct systematic attacks against core sectors of societal operation.
  
  Although multiple cybersecurity sources have confirmed the group's attack methods and shift in targets, further public information is still needed to verify the specific geographic distribution of the group, a detailed list of victimized entities, and the specific defensive effectiveness of various governments in responding to this wave of threats. Furthermore, while Microsoft has previously flagged and identified the relevant vulnerabilities, there remain discrepancies in the details provided by various cybersecurity firms regarding the patching progress of these vulnerabilities across different industrial environments, and whether the hackers have developed new variant attack methods, necessitating further cross-referencing of threat intelligence.
  
  Such ransomware attacks targeting critical infrastructure pose a direct threat to public safety and industrial stability in North America and globally. The water and telecommunications industries are lifelines of a nation; once encrypted or paralyzed by ransomware, they severely impact public livelihood and social order. Additionally, as hubs for research and data storage, educational institutions, if targeted, risk the leakage of academic resources or the disruption of administrative systems. This wave of attacks demonstrates that hackers are leveraging corporate reliance on digital transformation and cloud collaboration software (such as SharePoint) to expand the impact of ransomware attacks from simple commercial extortion to the level of national infrastructure security.
  
  However, experts caution that one should not overreact to ransomware threats, nor should all attack incidents be attributed to a single hacker group. While the threat from Storm-2603 is real, enterprises and government agencies should return to fundamental cybersecurity defense architectures when facing ransomware, including regular system patching, the implementation of Identity and Access Management (IAM), and the establishment of robust offline backup mechanisms. Over-interpreting the scale of a single hacker group's threat may lead to an imbalance in the allocation of defensive resources, causing organizations to overlook other potential supply chain attacks or social engineering risks.
  
  Subsequent observation will focus on whether the group further expands its attack scope and whether the global cybersecurity defense system's patching rate for the "ToolShell" vulnerability improves. Furthermore, the progress of cooperation between international law enforcement agencies and private sector cybersecurity teams, as well as whether cybersecurity resilience regulations for critical infrastructure will be adjusted in response to this wave of threats, will be key indicators for assessing global cybersecurity risks in the coming months.

# 勒索軟體威脅升級：Warlock組織鎖定關鍵基礎設施

> 勒索軟體組織Storm-2603近期持續利用SharePoint漏洞發動攻擊，目標轉向水資源、電信等關鍵基礎設施與教育機構，引發全球資安高度關注。


近期全球資安情勢出現顯著威脅，根據資安研究機構與媒體報導，被微軟點名為「Storm-2603」（又稱Longlegs）的駭客組織，其發動的「Warlock」勒索軟體攻擊活動依然活躍。該組織主要透過濫用SharePoint系統的零時差漏洞「ToolShell」（包含CVE-2025-49704、CVE-2025-49706、CVE-2025-53770、CVE-2025-53771等編號）進行入侵。
這類攻擊手法已引起國際資安界的廣泛注意，特別是針對關鍵基礎設施的威脅態勢，已成為各國政府與企業防禦的重點。
根據賽門鐵克（Symantec）與Carbon Black威脅獵捕團隊的共同監測，Storm-2603駭客組織的活動軌跡顯示，其攻擊目標已出現明顯轉移。過去該組織即因上述SharePoint漏洞攻擊而受到關注，近期調查發現，駭客不僅未停止行動，反而加強了對特定語系國家關鍵基礎設施的滲透，受影響範圍涵蓋水資源管理系統、電信產業，以及教育機構。這些共同確認的資訊顯示，駭客組織正利用成熟的漏洞利用鏈，針對社會運作的核心部門進行系統性攻擊。
儘管多方資安來源已確認該組織的攻擊手法與目標轉移，但目前針對該組織的具體地理位置分佈、受害單位的詳細清單，以及各國政府應對此波威脅的具體防禦成效，仍有待進一步的公開資訊證實。此外，雖然微軟先前已針對相關漏洞進行標記與點名，但對於這些漏洞在不同產業環境下的修補進度，以及駭客是否已開發出新的變體攻擊手法，目前各資安公司提供的報告細節仍存在一定程度的差異，尚待更多威脅情報進行交叉比對。
此類針對關鍵基礎設施的勒索軟體攻擊，對北美及全球的公共安全與產業穩定構成直接威脅。水資源與電信產業屬於國家命脈，一旦遭到勒索軟體加密或癱瘓，將嚴重影響民生運作與社會秩序。此外，教育機構作為研究與資料儲存的重鎮，若遭鎖定，恐導致學術資源外洩或校務系統中斷。這波攻擊顯示，駭客正利用企業對數位化轉型及雲端協作軟體（如SharePoint）的依賴，將勒索軟體攻擊的影響力從單純的商業勒索，擴大至國家級的基礎設施安全層面。
然而，專家提醒，針對勒索軟體威脅不應過度恐慌，亦不應將所有攻擊事件歸咎於單一駭客組織。儘管Storm-2603的威脅確實存在，但企業與政府機關在面對勒索軟體時，仍應回歸基礎的資安防禦架構，包括定期修補系統漏洞、落實許可權管理（IAM）以及建立完善的離線備份機制。過度解讀單一駭客組織的威脅規模，可能導致防禦資源配置失衡，忽略了其他潛在的供應鏈攻擊或社交工程風險。
後續觀察重點將聚焦於該組織是否會進一步擴大其攻擊範圍，以及全球資安防禦體系針對「ToolShell」漏洞的修補率是否提升。此外，國際執法機構與私部門資安團隊的合作進展，以及針對關鍵基礎設施的資安韌性法規是否會因應此波威脅而有所調整，將是未來數月內評估全球網路安全風險的重要指標。
