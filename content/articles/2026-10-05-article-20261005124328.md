  # The AI Safety Regulation Debate: Zuckerberg and Li Fei-Fei on External Evaluation Mechanisms
  
  > Regarding safety regulations for frontier artificial intelligence research, Meta CEO Mark Zuckerberg and AI pioneer Li Fei-Fei have recently presented their respective perspectives. Zuckerberg advocates for building trust through market mechanisms and external evaluations, while Li Fei-Fei emphasizes the need to break the current status quo of tech giants acting as "both player and referee," calling for intervention by independent institutions to prevent the risk of systems spiraling out of control.
  
  The development of artificial intelligence has entered a critical phase, with the global tech community engaged in a fierce debate over "whether frontier AI research should be slowed down" and "how to ensure AI safety." Recently, Meta CEO Mark Zuckerberg and Stanford University professor and World Labs co-founder Li Fei-Fei have expressed their views on AI safety evaluation mechanisms. The core of this issue lies in the increasing power of current AI systems; establishing a credible set of safety evaluation standards has become a focal point in global technology policy and humanitarian safety fields.
  
  This concerns not only commercial competition in technological applications but also the potential risks and human welfare implications that AI systems may bring to societal operations.
  
  Progress confirmed by multiple sources indicates that the tech industry has reached a high level of consensus on the importance of "AI safety evaluation." On the social platform X, Zuckerberg explicitly opposed a "passive braking" strategy for slowing down research, arguing that the core of safety assurance should lie in building a diverse and professional ecosystem of independent external evaluations. Meanwhile, in an interview with Bloomberg Television, Li Fei-Fei also emphasized the necessity of external evaluation, pointing out that the current model where developers conduct their own safety assessments has serious flaws. Although both heavyweights agree on the importance of external evaluation, they hold different perspectives on how to implement regulation and the role of market mechanisms therein.
  
  However, there are clear differences in their paths of execution. Zuckerberg advocates viewing "model alignment" as a survival threshold for commercial competition, believing that if a product is unsafe, it will naturally be eliminated by market mechanisms; therefore, trust should be built by introducing independent external evaluations rather than restricting research processes. Li Fei-Fei takes a more cautious approach, harshly criticizing the status quo of "grading one's own homework." She argues that the structure of tech giants acting as "both player and referee" must be broken, calling for academia, the public sector, and independent institutions to intervene forcefully to establish a set of standards with public credibility, rather than relying solely on market competition to filter for safety.
  
  Furthermore, there is no consensus yet on "whether developers possess sufficient self-discipline," and various sectors continue to debate the intensity and scope of regulatory intervention.
  
  This issue has profound implications for the global technology industry and regional security policies. If Zuckerberg’s market-oriented model is adopted, industry research and development speeds may maintain high growth, but the risk of relying on market self-discipline is that once a major security vulnerability occurs, the catastrophic consequences may be difficult to repair in time through market mechanisms. Conversely, if the strict external regulation advocated by Li Fei-Fei is adopted, while it could improve system transparency and safety, it might also lead to a surge in innovation costs due to overly cumbersome evaluation processes, potentially affecting the strategic advantages of nations or companies in the AI race.
  
  For regions such as South America that are actively investing in digital transformation and technological infrastructure, balancing the dividends of AI development with the red lines of safety regulation will be an important reference for future policy formulation.
  
  It is worth noting that the debate between the two should not be over-interpreted as a simple opposition. Neither has denied the immense potential brought by AI technology, nor have they ignored the necessity of safety protections. The limitation and unknown factor lie in the fact that there is currently no globally unified standard for "independent external evaluation," and the speed of AI technology iteration far exceeds the speed at which existing regulations are formulated. Furthermore, the monopoly position of tech giants in AI infrastructure means that independent institutions may face difficulties in obtaining technical data or resource inequality when conducting evaluations; these are the realistic dilemmas that policymakers must face when promoting regulation.
  
  The focus for subsequent observation will be on whether major global economies will establish cross-border cooperation frameworks for AI safety evaluation, and whether academia and the industry can reach a concrete consensus on "independent evaluation standards." Additionally, whether more stringent mandatory external review mechanisms will emerge for the application of AI systems in sensitive fields (such as finance, national defense, and public safety) will be a key indicator for observing the direction of AI governance in the coming months and years. All sectors should continue to monitor how the initiatives of these tech leaders translate into concrete policy drafts and assess their long-term impact on the global technology ecosystem.

# 科技巨頭AI安全監管爭議：祖克柏與李飛飛對於外部評估機制之論辯

> 針對人工智慧前沿研發的安全監管議題，Meta執行長祖克柏與AI先驅李飛飛近日分別提出觀點。祖克柏主張透過市場機制與外部評估建立信任，李飛飛則強調必須打破科技巨頭「球員兼裁判」的現狀，呼籲由獨立機構介入評測，以防範系統失控風險。


人工智慧發展進入關鍵期，全球科技圈針對「是否應放緩前沿AI研發」以及「如何確保AI安全性」展開激烈辯論。近期，Meta執行長馬克·祖克柏（Mark Zuckerberg）與史丹佛大學教授、World Labs共同創辦人李飛飛，分別針對AI安全評估機制發表看法。此議題的核心在於當前AI系統能力日益強大，如何建立一套具備公信力的安全評測標準，已成為全球科技政策與人道安全領域的焦點。
這不僅關乎技術應用的商業競爭，更涉及AI系統在社會運作中可能帶來的潛在風險與人類福祉。
目前多方來源共同確認的進展顯示，科技界對於「AI安全評估」的重要性已達成高度共識。祖克柏在社群平臺X上明確反對採取「消極踩煞車」的研發放緩策略，他認為安全保障的核心應在於建立多元且專業的獨立外部評估生態系。與此同時，李飛飛在接受彭博電視專訪時，同樣強調了外部評估的必要性，並直指當前由開發商自行進行安全評估的模式存在嚴重缺陷。兩位重量級人物雖皆認同外部評估的重要性，但對於如何落實監管與市場機制在其中的角色，則展現出不同的側重觀點。
然而，兩者在執行路徑上存在明顯差異。祖克柏主張將「模型對齊（Model Alignment）」視為商業競爭的生存死線，認為若產品不安全，自然會被市場機制淘汰，因此應透過引進獨立外部評估來建立信任，而非限制研發程式。李飛飛則採取更為審慎的態度，她嚴厲批評「自己替自己打分數」的現狀，主張必須打破科技巨頭「球員兼裁判」的結構，呼籲學術界、公共部門及獨立機構必須強勢介入，建立一套具備公共公信力的標準，而非單純依賴市場競爭來篩選安全性。
此外，對於「開發商是否具備足夠自律能力」一事，目前尚無定論，各界仍持續爭辯監管介入的強度與範疇。
此議題對全球科技產業與區域安全政策具有深遠影響。若採納祖克柏的市場導向模式，產業研發速度可能維持高速增長，但依賴市場自律的風險在於，一旦出現重大安全漏洞，其災難性後果可能難以透過市場機制及時修復。反之，若採取李飛飛主張的嚴格外部監管，雖能提升系統的透明度與安全性，但也可能因過度繁瑣的評測流程，導致創新研發成本激增，甚至影響國家或企業在AI競賽中的戰略優勢。
對於南美等正積極投入數位轉型與科技基礎建設的區域而言，如何平衡AI發展紅利與安全監管紅線，將是未來政策制定的重要參考。
值得注意的是，不應過度解讀兩人的爭辯為單純的對立。兩者皆未否認AI技術帶來的巨大潛力，也未忽視安全防護的必要性。限制與未知資訊在於，目前尚無全球統一的「獨立外部評估」執行標準，且AI技術迭代速度遠超現有法規的制定速度。此外，科技巨頭在AI基礎設施上的壟斷地位，使得獨立機構在進行評估時，可能面臨技術資料取得困難或資源不對等的問題，這些都是政策制定者在推動監管時必須面對的現實困境。
後續觀察重點在於，全球主要經濟體是否會針對AI安全評估建立跨國合作框架，以及學術界與產業間能否就「獨立評測標準」達成具體共識。此外，針對AI系統在敏感領域（如金融、國防、公共安全）的應用，是否會出現更為嚴格的強制性外部審查機制，將是未來數月至數年間觀察AI治理走向的關鍵指標。各界應持續關注這些科技領袖的倡議如何轉化為具體的政策草案，並評估其對全球技術生態系的長遠影響。
