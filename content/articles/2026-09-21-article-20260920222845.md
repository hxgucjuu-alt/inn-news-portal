  # Raymond Greene: Taiwan is the Core of the Global Supply Chain; Without Taiwan, the Global Economy Might Struggle to Function
  
  > At the "International Forum on Whole-of-Society Defense Resilience," AIT Director Raymond Greene pointed out that Taiwan holds a critical position in the global supply chain, urging the international community to consider the potential crisis the global economy would face if trade and commerce with Taiwan were to cease.
  
  On September 19, 2026, American Institute in Taiwan (AIT) Director Raymond Greene attended the "International Forum on Whole-of-Society Defense Resilience" hosted by the Office of the President of Taiwan. Addressing the long-standing international concern regarding Taiwan’s energy security—specifically the debate over the number of days of liquefied natural gas (LNG) reserves—Greene offered a different perspective. He emphasized that the external focus on how many days Taiwan can sustain energy supplies during a crisis overlooks Taiwan's irreplaceable strategic role in the global economic system. Greene argued that the truly critical question is: "How long could the global economy continue to run at full speed if trade and commerce from Taiwan were absent?"
  
  This argument elevates Taiwan’s strategic value from mere energy reserves to the core level of global supply chain resilience, creating a profound link to issues of stability in Southeast Asia and global trade.
  
  According to reports from various media outlets, the core of Greene’s remarks lies in redefining Taiwan’s defense resilience within international geopolitics and economics. Multiple sources confirmed that Greene explicitly pointed out that international discourse regarding Taiwan’s energy reserves (such as 11 or 14 days) fails to fully define Taiwan’s survival capability in a potential crisis. Representatives from the UK and Europe at the forum offered corresponding views; while acknowledging Taiwan’s core position in the global supply chain, they also highlighted the structural vulnerability of Taiwan’s heavy reliance on energy imports and called on the Taiwan government to accelerate low-carbon transitions and deregulate relevant policies to strengthen overall economic resilience.
  
  These perspectives collectively outline the complex challenges and international expectations Taiwan faces in its pursuit of economic stability and energy autonomy.
  
  However, there are subtle differences in the focus of the information provided by various parties. Greene’s remarks focused on the "indispensability" of Taiwan in the global economic supply chain to counter external doubts about Taiwan’s defense capabilities, while the European and British representatives shifted the focus back to Taiwan’s internal "energy transition" and "regulatory environment." Furthermore, the question of "how long the global economy could function" remains a strategic hypothesis, with no specific economic models or quantitative data to support a concrete timeline. This indicates a gap in risk assessment standards among nations when discussing global supply chain resilience.
  
  Additionally, details regarding how to specifically implement supply chain resilience to address regional conflicts remain subject to further policy discussions.
  
  These remarks have profound implications for the Asia-Pacific region and global industrial chains. As an important link in the global supply chain, Southeast Asian nations have been actively seeking U.S. commitments and regional economic cooperation in recent years due to geopolitical risks and the expansion of China’s economic and military influence. Greene’s perspective reinforces Taiwan’s status as a supply chain hub, which is not only directly related to the stability of the semiconductor and high-tech industries but also affects the regional trade policy layouts of ASEAN countries. For Southeast Asian economies highly dependent on global trade, the stability of the Taiwanese supply chain directly impacts their manufacturing output and export momentum.
  
  If the Taiwanese supply chain were to be interrupted, the global economy would face severe chain reactions, prompting Southeast Asian nations to re-examine their positioning in regional defense resilience.
  
  Although Greene’s views have received support from some international observers, there are limitations to these perspectives. Some analyses point out that overemphasizing the criticality of the Taiwanese supply chain, while highlighting Taiwan’s strategic value, may also exacerbate regional tensions. Furthermore, viewing energy transition and deregulation as means to enhance resilience faces tug-of-wars among different interest groups in Taiwan regarding environmental protection, land use, and industrial policy; a single international forum statement should not be oversimplified as a panacea for all structural problems.
  
  While pursuing supply chain resilience, countries must still consider the practical limitations of their own economic development to avoid the rise of trade protectionism caused by over-interpreting geopolitical risks, which could in turn affect overall regional economic integration.
  
  Future observations will focus on how the Taiwan government responds to the suggestions from international representatives regarding energy transition and deregulation, and how the U.S. and ASEAN countries translate "supply chain resilience" into concrete policy collaboration in subsequent bilateral or multilateral trade negotiations. Furthermore, whether the international community’s assessment of Taiwan’s economic continuity in crisis scenarios will further evolve into substantive economic defense cooperation mechanisms will be a key indicator for the future direction of the global economy and trade. Observers will closely monitor how Taiwan balances the dual challenges of energy security and industrial development while maintaining its core position in the global supply chain.

# 谷立言：全球供應鏈核心在臺，世界經濟若無臺灣恐難運轉

> 美國在臺協會處長谷立言於「全社會防衛韌性國際論壇」指出，臺灣在全球供應鏈中具關鍵地位，國際社會應關注若缺乏臺灣商務貿易，全球經濟將面臨停擺危機。


美國在臺協會（AIT）處長谷立言（Raymond Greene）於2026年9月19日出席由臺灣總統府主辦的「全社會防衛韌性國際論壇」。針對國際間長期關注的臺灣能源安全議題，特別是液化天然氣（LNG）的存量天數爭論，谷立言提出了不同的視角。他強調，外界過度聚焦於臺灣在危機發生時能堅持多少天的能源供應，這種討論方式忽略了臺灣在全球經濟體系中不可替代的戰略角色。谷立言認為，真正關鍵的問題在於「若缺乏來自臺灣的貿易與商務往來，全球經濟究竟能維持全速運轉多久？」。
此一論述將臺灣的戰略價值從單純的能源存量，提升至全球供應鏈韌性的核心層次，並與東南亞及全球貿易穩定議題產生深刻連結。
綜合多家媒體報導，谷立言的發言核心在於重新定義臺灣在國際地緣政治與經濟中的防衛韌性。多個來源共同確認，谷立言明確指出國際輿論對於臺灣能源存量（如11天或14天）的爭論，並不能完整定義臺灣在潛在危機中的生存能力。與會的英、歐代表則在論壇中提出相對應的觀點，雖然認同臺灣在全球供應鏈的核心地位，但也同時點出臺灣能源高度仰賴進口的結構性脆弱，並呼籲臺灣政府應加速推動低碳轉型，以及進行相關法規的鬆綁，以強化整體經濟韌性。
這些觀點共同勾勒出臺灣在追求經濟穩定與能源自主之間，所面臨的複雜挑戰與國際期待。
然而，各方資訊在論述重點上存在細微差異。谷立言的發言側重於臺灣在全球經濟供應鏈中的「不可或缺性」，以此反轉外界對臺灣防衛能力的質疑；而歐英代表的發言則將焦點拉回臺灣內部的「能源轉型」與「法規環境」。此外，關於「全球經濟能運轉多久」的提問，目前仍屬於戰略性假設議題，尚無具體的經濟模型或量化資料支援該論述的具體時程，這也顯示出在討論全球供應鏈韌性時，各國對於風險評估的標準仍存在落差。
此外，論壇中對於如何具體落實供應鏈韌性以應對區域衝突的細節，仍有待後續政策討論。
此番言論對亞太地區及全球產業鏈具有深遠影響。東南亞國家作為全球供應鏈的重要環節，近年來因地緣政治風險與中國經濟軍事影響力擴張，正積極尋求美國的承諾與區域經濟合作。谷立言的觀點強化了臺灣作為供應鏈樞紐的地位，這不僅直接關係到半導體及高科技產業的穩定，也牽動東協各國在區域貿易政策上的佈局。對於高度依賴全球貿易的東南亞經濟體而言，臺灣供應鏈的穩定性直接影響其製造業產出與出口動能。
若臺灣供應鏈出現中斷，全球經濟將面臨嚴重的連鎖反應，這也促使東南亞國家必須重新審視其在區域防衛韌性中的定位。
儘管谷立言的觀點獲得部分國際輿論的支援，但各界觀點仍存在限制。部分分析指出，過度強調臺灣供應鏈的關鍵性，雖能凸顯臺灣的戰略價值，但也可能加劇區域內的緊張局勢。此外，將能源轉型與法規鬆綁視為提升韌性的手段，在臺灣國內亦面臨環保、土地使用及產業政策等不同利益團體的拉扯，不應將單一的國際論壇發言過度簡化為解決所有結構性問題的藥方。
各國在追求供應鏈韌性的同時，仍需考量自身經濟發展的現實限制，避免因過度解讀地緣政治風險而導致貿易保護主義抬頭，進而影響區域經濟的整體整合。
後續觀察重點將聚焦於臺灣政府如何回應國際代表對於能源轉型與法規鬆綁的建議，以及美國與東協國家在後續雙邊或多邊貿易談判中，如何將「供應鏈韌性」轉化為具體的政策協作。此外，國際社會對於臺灣在危機情境下的經濟持續運作能力，是否會進一步轉化為實質的經濟防衛合作機制，將是未來觀察全球經濟與貿易走向的重要指標。各界將密切關注臺灣如何在維持全球供應鏈核心地位的同時，平衡能源安全與產業發展的雙重挑戰。
