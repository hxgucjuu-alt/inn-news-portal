  # Geopolitical Shifts in Eurasia: Regional Reorganization and Risk Forecasts Following the Russia-Ukraine War
  
  > The Eurasia Group has released its 2026 global risk report, noting that the Russia-Ukraine war has reshaped the regional diplomatic landscape. Armenia is seeking to move away from Russian influence, while digital sovereignty and democratic frameworks have become core policy issues in Europe and the Eurasian region.
  
  Since the outbreak of the Russia-Ukraine war, the geopolitical structure of the Eurasian region has undergone dramatic turbulence. According to an analysis by the Atlantic Council, Russian President Vladimir Putin’s overextension of military resources on the Ukrainian battlefield has led to a loosening of his influence over neighboring countries. Armenia, a traditional strategic ally of Russia, has recently shown a clear tendency toward "de-Russification," attempting to pivot its foreign policy and seek more diverse international cooperation. At the same time, European nations are actively promoting the strengthening of digital sovereignty and democratic frameworks, attempting to establish a resilient regional governance system amidst the complex international situation to address external political and security challenges.
  
  In its annual "Top Risks" report released on January 5, 2026, the Eurasia Group clearly pointed out that changes in the U.S. political system will become a core variable in the future global landscape. The report emphasizes that the concentration and weaponization of government power will not only affect U.S. domestic politics but will also have profound spillover effects on global geopolitics. Combining the perspectives of the Atlantic Council and the Eurasia Group, the world is currently in a critical period of "strategic reorganization." Russia's traditional hegemonic status in the Eurasian region is being challenged by multiple factors, and transnational digital governance and foreign policy adjustments have become the primary means for countries to cope with regional instability.
  
  However, there are significant differences in the focus of various sources regarding the specific trajectory of the regional situation. The Atlantic Council's analysis focuses on the specific shift in Armenia-Russia relations, emphasizing this as a concrete case of the reshaping of the regional diplomatic landscape; meanwhile, the Eurasia Group's report elevates the perspective to the level of global risk, particularly the impact of U.S. political changes on the global order. Regarding the extent of Armenia's pivot and whether Russia's "overextension" on the Ukrainian battlefield will lead to a comprehensive contraction in other parts of Eurasia, there is currently no consensus. Furthermore, there is a lack of transparency and policy gaps regarding the implementation details and effectiveness of digital sovereignty policies among different Eurasian countries, which remains to be observed.
  
  This wave of geopolitical change has profound implications for the industries and societies of the relevant regions. First, in terms of energy and logistics, as Russian influence wanes, energy transport pipelines and trade routes in the Eurasian region are facing pressure for replanning, which will directly affect Europe's energy security and supply chain resilience. Second, the promotion of digital sovereignty will prompt relevant countries to establish new standards in information security, internet governance, and artificial intelligence applications. This involves not only policy formulation but will also affect the market access and operating models of multinational technology companies in the region.
  
  On a social level, the pivot in foreign policy may trigger divisions in domestic public opinion, especially in countries that have long relied on Russian security guarantees, where the risks of internal identity crises and political instability cannot be ignored.
  
  Although various sectors have put forward many predictions about future risks, analysts warn that one should not overinterpret the policy shift of a single country as a total collapse of the regional order. For example, Armenia's diplomatic adjustments are still constrained by its geographical location and existing economic dependencies, and its transition process is full of variables; similarly, the Eurasia Group's forecast regarding U.S. political change reflects a structural risk assessment rather than an inevitable historical conclusion. When formulating foreign and security policies, countries are often constrained by multiple factors such as domestic economic needs, military capabilities, and international alliance structures. Therefore, viewing these policy adjustments as a dynamic game process is a more accurate way to understand the current Eurasian situation.
  
  Looking ahead, the focus will be on several key indicators: first, Russia's subsequent movements on the Ukrainian battlefield, which will directly determine its strategic leeway in the Eurasian region; second, the specific implementation of foreign policies by Armenia and other Central Asian and Caucasian countries, particularly whether they can successfully establish alternative security and economic partnerships; and finally, the competition and cooperation between major global powers in the Eurasian region, especially the adjustments to infrastructure investment and diplomatic intervention in Eurasia following U.S. political changes. The interaction of these factors will determine whether the Eurasian region moves toward regional integration or falls into deeper fragmentation and conflict in the coming years, which deserves close and continuous attention from the international community.

# 歐亞地緣政治變局：俄烏戰爭後的區域重組與風險預測

> 歐亞集團發布2026年全球風險報告，指出俄烏戰爭重塑區域外交版圖。亞美尼亞正尋求脫離俄羅斯影響，而數位主權與民主架構亦成為歐洲與歐亞地區政策核心議題。


俄烏戰爭爆發以來，歐亞地區的地緣政治結構發生劇烈動盪。根據大西洋理事會（Atlantic Council）的分析，俄羅斯總統普丁因在烏克蘭戰場上的軍事投入過度擴張，導致其對周邊國家的影響力出現鬆動。亞美尼亞作為傳統俄羅斯的戰略盟友，近期展現出明顯的「去俄羅斯化」傾向，試圖在外交政策上轉向，尋求更多元的國際合作空間。與此同時，歐洲各國正積極推動數位主權與民主架構的強化，試圖在複雜的國際局勢中，建立具備韌性的區域治理體系，以應對來自外部的政治與安全挑戰。
歐亞集團（Eurasia Group）於2026年1月5日發布的年度「全球十大風險」報告中，明確指出美國政治體系的變革將成為未來全球局勢的核心變數。報告強調，政府權力的集中與武器化運用，不僅影響美國國內政局，更將對全球地緣政治產生深遠的外溢效應。綜合大西洋理事會與歐亞集團的觀點，當前全球正處於一個「戰略重組」的關鍵期，俄羅斯在歐亞地區的傳統霸權地位正受到多重因素的挑戰，而跨國的數位治理與外交政策調整，已成為各國應對區域不穩定的主要手段。
然而，針對區域局勢的具體走向，不同來源的側重點存在顯著差異。大西洋理事會的分析聚焦於亞美尼亞與俄羅斯關係的具體轉向，強調這是區域外交版圖重塑的具體案例；而歐亞集團的報告則將視角拉高至全球風險層面，特別是美國政治變革對全球秩序的衝擊。關於亞美尼亞轉向的程度，以及俄羅斯在烏克蘭戰場的「過度擴張」是否將導致其在歐亞其他地區的全面收縮，目前尚缺乏一致的評估。此外，數位主權政策在不同歐亞國家間的執行細節與成效，亦存在資訊不透明與政策落差，仍有待後續觀察。
此波地緣政治變動對相關地區的產業與社會影響深遠。首先，在能源與物流方面，隨著俄羅斯影響力的減弱，歐亞地區的能源運輸管道與貿易路線正面臨重新規劃的壓力，這將直接影響歐洲的能源安全與供應鏈韌性。其次，數位主權的推動將促使相關國家在資訊安全、網路治理與人工智慧應用上建立新的標準，這不僅涉及政策制定，亦將影響跨國科技企業在該地區的市場准入與營運模式。
社會層面而言，外交政策的轉向可能引發國內輿論的分歧，特別是在長期依賴俄羅斯安全保障的國家，社會內部的認同危機與政治動盪風險不容忽視。
儘管各界對未來風險提出諸多預測，但分析人士提醒，不應將單一國家的政策轉向過度解讀為區域秩序的全面崩解。例如，亞美尼亞的外交調整仍受限於地理位置與既有的經濟依賴，其轉向過程充滿變數；同樣地，歐亞集團針對美國政治變革的預測，反映的是一種結構性的風險評估，而非必然發生的歷史定論。各國在制定外交與安全政策時，往往受到國內經濟需求、軍事能力與國際聯盟架構的多重製約，因此，將這些政策調整視為動態的博弈過程，較能準確理解當前的歐亞局勢。
展望未來，觀察重點將聚焦於幾個關鍵指標：首先是俄羅斯在烏克蘭戰場的後續動向，這將直接決定其在歐亞地區的戰略餘裕；其次是亞美尼亞及其他中亞、高加索國家在外交政策上的具體實踐，特別是其是否能成功建立替代性的安全與經濟夥伴關係；最後則是全球主要大國在歐亞地區的競合關係，特別是美國政治變革後，對歐亞地區基礎設施投資與外交介入的調整。這些因素的互動作用，將決定未來數年歐亞地區是走向區域整合，抑或是陷入更深層的碎片化與衝突之中，值得國際社會持續密切關注。
