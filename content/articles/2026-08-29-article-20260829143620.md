  > As the U.S. federal government faces a budget deliberation impasse and ongoing partisan gridlock in Congress, federal agencies are simultaneously advancing open-source software security management standards to address increasingly severe cyber threats and administrative operational challenges.
  
  The U.S. federal government is currently facing multiple governance challenges, with the budget deliberation impasse at the core, severely affecting the operational stability of government departments. According to reports from the Federal News Network, negotiations between the two parties in Congress regarding the government budget have entered an intensive phase; however, disagreements on key issues remain difficult to bridge, leading to a persistent risk of a government shutdown. At the same time, the U.S. National Security Agency (NSA) and the Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency (CISA) have released important guidance on digital infrastructure security, requiring software developers and supply chain operators to strengthen management mechanisms for open-source software.
  
  This move reflects that while maintaining administrative operations, the U.S. government must still address systemic risks from the global cyberspace. In the current U.S. political environment, these two issues have become key indicators for evaluating government governance effectiveness and interagency coordination capabilities.
  
  Synthesizing information from reports by the Associated Press (AP News), NBC News, and CNBC, the federal government is currently committed to addressing the two major agendas of budget and national security. Regarding the budget, although bipartisan negotiators have held multiple closed-door meetings, no consensus has been reached on spending caps and priority appropriation projects, which directly affects the salary adjustment calculations for federal civil servants and the expected returns of the Thrift Savings Plan (TSP). Regarding cybersecurity policy, the initiative jointly launched by the NSA and CISA aims to establish more rigorous security standards for the open-source software supply chain, requiring developers to adopt more proactive vulnerability management and risk prevention measures.
  
  These policy developments indicate that despite the tense political atmosphere within Washington, federal agencies continue to maintain their established operational rhythm in safeguarding national digital security and administrative stability, attempting to mitigate external threats through policy guidance.
  
  However, there are differences in the focus of reports from various media sources on the aforementioned issues, and some information is still in a state of dynamic adjustment. The Federal News Network focuses on the details of budget negotiations and the impact on civil servant rights, emphasizing the status quo of "difficulty in reaching an agreement" during the negotiation process; in contrast, CNBC and NBC News are more concerned with the political maneuvering and public announcements by the White House during the promotion of these policies.   These information gaps reflect the complexity of the policy-making process in Washington and the varying expectations from all sectors regarding the consequences of policy implementation.
  
  These policy developments have had a profound impact on American society and the industrial environment. First, the threat of a government shutdown directly affects the livelihoods of millions of federal employees, including the stability of salary payments and benefit planning, thereby sparking social concerns about government administrative efficiency. Second, the shift in cybersecurity policy poses new compliance requirements for the software industry chain, especially for technology companies that rely on open-source technology, which must invest more resources to meet the new standards of the NSA and CISA. This will increase the operating costs of the industry, but at the same time, it will also enhance the resilience of the overall digital infrastructure.
  
  The promotion of these policies shows that the U.S. government is attempting to extend national security standards to the private sector supply chain. This is not only an upgrade at the technical level but also a concrete strategic manifestation of the U.S. strengthening its defense capabilities for critical infrastructure against a geopolitical background.
  
  Although the relevant policy intentions are clear, various sectors still hold different views on the effectiveness of implementation. Some analysts believe that under the condition that the congressional budget impasse remains unresolved, pushing for cybersecurity management standards may face challenges of insufficient resource allocation, leading to a discount in policy execution. In addition, over-interpreting the government's regulatory requirements for open-source software may be seen as a restriction on the freedom of technological innovation. Therefore, the industry calls on the government to provide more specific technical support and a transition period while promoting security standards to avoid supply chain disruptions caused by sudden policy shifts.
  
  These views remind policymakers that when pursuing digital security and administrative goals, they must balance the actual needs of industrial development and avoid unnecessary shocks to the overall economic environment due to the mandatory nature of a single policy.
  
  Subsequent observation will focus on the final progress of congressional budget negotiations and how federal agencies will translate cybersecurity guidance into specific procurement contract terms. The market and the public should closely monitor whether the White House will issue further executive orders to resolve the interference of the budget impasse on the operations of federal agencies. At the same time, the speed of software suppliers' response to new security standards, and whether the NSA and CISA will adjust implementation details based on industry feedback, will be key to evaluating whether the U.S. government's cybersecurity strategy is successful. As time goes on, how these policies will affect the competitiveness and security of the U.S. in the global digital economy will continue to be a focus of attention for society and the industry.

# 美國聯邦政府預算僵局與網路安全政策之跨部門協調挑戰

> 隨著美國聯邦政府面臨預算審議僵局，國會兩黨協商持續膠著，同時聯邦機構正同步推動開源軟體安全管理規範，以因應日益嚴峻的網路威脅與行政運作挑戰。


美國聯邦政府近期面臨多重治理挑戰，其中以預算審議僵局為核心，嚴重影響政府部門的運作穩定性。根據聯邦新聞網（Federal News Network）的報導，國會兩黨針對政府預算案的談判已進入密集階段，然而雙方在關鍵議題上的歧見依然難以彌合，導致政府停擺的風險持續存在。與此同時，美國國家安全域性（NSA）與網路安全暨基礎設施安全域性（CISA）針對數位基礎設施安全發布了重要指引，要求軟體開發商及供應鏈業者強化開源軟體的管理機制。
此舉反映出美國政府在維持行政運作之餘，仍須應對來自全球網路空間的系統性風險，這兩項議題在當前美國政治環境下，已成為檢視政府治理效能與跨部門協調能力的關鍵指標。
綜合美聯社（AP News）、美國國家廣播公司（NBC News）及CNBC的報導資訊，聯邦政府目前正致力於處理預算與國家安全兩大議程。在預算方面，兩黨談判代表雖多次進行閉門會談，但針對支出上限與優先撥款專案仍未達成共識，這直接影響了聯邦公務員的薪資調整計算及退休儲蓄計畫（TSP）的預期收益。在網路安全政策方面，由NSA與CISA共同發起的倡議，旨在建立更為嚴謹的開源軟體供應鏈安全標準，要求開發商必須採取更主動的漏洞管理與風險防範措施。
這些政策進展均顯示，儘管華府內部政治氣氛緊張，聯邦機構在維護國家數位安全與行政穩定方面，仍維持著既定的運作節奏，並試圖透過政策指引來降低外部威脅。
然而，各媒體來源對於上述議題的報導重點存在差異，且部分資訊仍處於動態調整中。聯邦新聞網側重於預算協商的細節與公務員權益的影響，強調兩黨在談判過程中「協議難以達成」的現狀；相較之下，CNBC與NBC News則更關注白宮在這些政策推動過程中的政治角力與公開宣告。此外，關於開源軟體管理實踐的具體執行細節，目前仍有許多技術性細節待相關部門進一步釐清，特別是在供應商如何落實這些強制性建議，以及若預算案無法透過，是否會延宕網路安全政策的執行進度，目前尚無確切的定論。
這些資訊落差反映出華府政策制定過程中的複雜性，以及各界對於政策執行後果的預期不一。
這些政策發展對美國社會與產業環境產生了深遠影響。首先，政府停擺的威脅直接影響了數百萬聯邦僱員的生計，包括薪資發放的穩定性與福利規劃，進而引發社會對政府行政效率的擔憂。其次，網路安全政策的轉向，對軟體產業鏈構成了新的合規要求，特別是對於依賴開源技術的科技企業而言，必須投入更多資源以符合NSA與CISA的新標準，這將增加產業的營運成本，但也同時提升了整體數位基礎設施的韌性。
政策的推動顯示出美國政府正試圖將國家安全標準延伸至私部門供應鏈，這不僅是技術層面的升級，更是地緣政治背景下，美國強化關鍵基礎設施防禦能力的具體戰略體現。
儘管相關政策意圖明確，但各界對於執行成效仍持有不同觀點。部分分析人士認為，在國會預算僵局未解的情況下，強推網路安全管理規範可能會面臨資源分配不足的挑戰，導致政策執行力打折扣。此外，過度解讀政府對開源軟體的監管要求，可能會被視為對技術創新自由的限制。因此，產業界呼籲政府在推動安全規範的同時，應提供更具體的技術支援與過渡期，避免因政策急轉彎而造成供應鏈中斷。
這些觀點提醒決策者，在追求數位安全與行政目標時，必須平衡產業發展的實際需求，避免因單一政策的強制性而對整體經濟環境造成不必要的衝擊。
後續觀察重點將聚焦於國會預算談判的最終進展，以及聯邦機構如何將網路安全指引轉化為具體的採購合約條款。市場與公眾應密切關注白宮是否會發布進一步的行政命令，以化解預算僵局對聯邦機構運作的幹擾。同時，軟體供應商對於新安全規範的反應速度，以及NSA與CISA是否會針對產業回饋調整執行細節，將是評估美國政府網路安全戰略是否成功的關鍵。隨著時間推移，這些政策將如何影響美國在全球數位經濟中的競爭力與安全性，將持續成為社會與產業各界關注的焦點。
