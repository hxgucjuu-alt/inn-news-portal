  # COP31 Advocates for Global Electrification Transition; Southeast Asia Faces New Challenges in Energy Security and Regional Cooperation
  
  > The COP31 conference in 2026 introduced the "35-by-35 Global Electrification Initiative," aiming to increase the share of electricity in final energy consumption to 35% by 2035. This move has sparked widespread global discussion regarding energy transition pathways, while Southeast Asia has become a key indicator for regional energy and economic security due to water resource governance and foreign investment in infrastructure.
  
  In November 2026, the 31st Conference of the Parties to the United Nations Framework Convention on Climate Change (COP31) was held in Antalya, Turkey, with the core focus on the deep transformation of the global power structure. The conference presidency officially proposed the "35-by-35 Global Electrification Initiative," aiming to significantly increase the share of electricity in global final energy consumption from the current level of approximately 23% to 35% by 2035. The initiative emphasizes that comprehensive electrification in the transportation, industrial, and building sectors, combined with the decarbonization of power systems, will be the key pathway to achieving global net-zero emission targets.
  
  Industry representatives, including Chenya Energy, have also expressed that merely increasing the electrification ratio is insufficient to achieve carbon reduction; it is essential to simultaneously ensure the scale of clean power supply to effectively support the massive electricity demand required for the transition.
  
  As the COP31 initiative is being promoted, energy and infrastructure development in Southeast Asia is showing a trend of diversification. According to recent information, Southeast Asian countries have adopted different strategies in response to the pressure of the global energy transition. On one hand, infrastructure investment in the region continues to increase; for example, Delta Electronics decided to increase its capital investment by $36 million to expand production capacity in response to the needs of its Indian subsidiary, and GIS-KY’s Vietnamese subsidiary has also secured relevant mechanical and electrical installation projects, indicating that as Southeast Asia becomes the center for the relocation of the global manufacturing supply chain, its industrial electricity demand is rising accordingly.
  
  On the other hand, countries such as Japan have long provided economic aid to assist ASEAN nations in infrastructure construction, and such transnational cooperation has a profound impact on the energy stability and economic development of the region.
  
  However, there are significant differences in viewpoints and debates regarding the path of energy transition. Regarding whether nuclear energy should be used as a means of carbon reduction, the National廢核 (Anti-Nuclear) Action Platform pointed out that nuclear power suffers from high costs and long construction periods that fail to meet urgent needs, and it is difficult to satisfy the requirements for power stability and flexibility in high-tech fields such as the AI industry. The platform argues that the global energy transition trend clearly points toward renewable energy, and if national resources are excessively invested in nuclear power, it may crowd out the development of green energy, thereby dragging down the efficiency of economic transformation.
  
  Although this view and the "power decarbonization" goal emphasized in the COP31 initiative both point toward carbon reduction, there remain irreconcilable differences between pro-nuclear and anti-nuclear camps regarding specific technical pathways, which await decisions by each country based on their own energy endowments and economic conditions.
  
  The intertwining of energy transition and geopolitics is particularly evident in the Mekong River basin. Scholars have analyzed that China is expanding its political and economic influence in the Indochina Peninsula by "securitizing" the governance of Mekong water resources. Water resources are not only the foundation of agriculture and livelihoods in the region but are also closely related to energy supplies such as hydropower. When the energy transition requires an increase in power supply, the cross-border governance and development rights of water resources have become complex political challenges that ASEAN countries must face in the pursuit of energy security.
  
  This means that energy policy in Southeast Asia is not just a matter of technology and industry, but is deeply mired in regional geopolitical competition, and any single energy development project could affect water rights and environmental security within the region.
  
  Currently, regarding the "35-by-35" initiative proposed by COP31, countries still face many limitations at the implementation level. First, whether the increase in the electrification ratio can directly correspond to a proportional carbon reduction effect depends highly on the penetration rate of renewable energy in each country's power grid. If the power source remains dominated by fossil fuels, electrification may instead increase grid load and carbon emissions. In addition, when Southeast Asian countries accept foreign aid for construction, how to balance energy sovereignty with the technical path requirements of foreign investors is also a point that policymakers need to evaluate carefully.
  
  One should not over-interpret a single technology (such as nuclear power or a single hydropower project) as the only solution to the energy crisis, because the energy transition is essentially a systematic project that requires the coordination of policy, technology, and regional geopolitics.
  
  Subsequent observations should focus on the implementation details of the post-COP31 communiqué, particularly the specific mechanisms for providing technology transfer and financial support to developing countries. In the Southeast Asian region, it is necessary to closely monitor how countries handle the contradictions between Mekong water resource governance and cross-border energy cooperation while increasing power supply. Furthermore, as the scale of AI and high-tech industry plants in Southeast Asia expands, industrial electricity demand will become a key indicator for testing the success or failure of each country's energy transition policy. Whether countries can implement decarbonization goals while ensuring the competitiveness of energy costs will be an important reference for evaluating the effectiveness of the global electrification initiative.

# COP31倡議全球電氣化轉型，東南亞能源安全與區域合作面臨新挑戰

> 2026年COP31大會提出「35-by-35全球電氣化倡議」，目標於2035年將電力佔終端能源消費比提升至35%。此舉引發全球對於能源轉型路徑的廣泛討論，東南亞地區則因水資源治理與外資基礎建設投入，成為區域能源與經濟安全的重要觀察指標。


2026年11月，聯合國氣候變化綱要公約第31次締約方大會（COP31）於土耳其安塔利亞召開，會議核心聚焦於全球電力結構的深度轉型。大會主席國正式提出「35-by-35全球電氣化倡議」，旨在將電力佔全球終端能源消費的比例，從目前的約23%大幅提升至2035年的35%。該倡議強調，透過交通、工業及建築領域的全面電氣化，配合電力系統的低碳化程式，將是達成全球淨零排放目標的關鍵路徑。
辰亞能源等產業代表亦對此表示，單純提升電氣化比例不足以達成減碳，必須同步確保潔淨電力的供給規模，才能有效支撐轉型所需的龐大電力需求。
在COP31倡議推動的同時，東南亞地區的能源與基礎建設發展正呈現多元化趨勢。根據近期資訊，東南亞各國在面對全球能源轉型壓力時，採取了不同的策略應對。一方面，區域內的基礎建設投資持續增加，例如臺達電因應印度子公司建廠需求，決議增資3,600萬美元以擴大產能；GIS-KY越南子公司亦取得相關機電安裝工程，顯示東南亞作為全球製造業供應鏈轉移的重心，其工業用電需求正隨之攀升。
另一方面，日本等國長期透過提供經濟援助，協助東協國家進行基礎設施建設，這類跨國合作對於該地區的能源穩定性與經濟發展具有深遠影響。
然而，關於能源轉型的路徑選擇，各界存在顯著的觀點差異與辯論。針對核能是否應作為減碳手段，全國廢核行動平臺指出，核電存在造價昂貴、建設期程緩不濟急等問題，且難以滿足AI產業等高科技領域對電力穩定性與靈活性的需求。該平臺主張，全球能源轉型趨勢已明確指向再生能源，若將國家資源過度投入核電，恐將排擠綠能發展，進而拖累經濟轉型效率。
此觀點與COP31倡議中強調的「電力低碳化」目標雖皆指向減碳，但在具體技術路徑上，擁核與反核陣營仍存在難以調和的歧見，尚待各國依據自身能源稟賦與經濟條件做出決策。
能源轉型與地緣政治的交織，在湄公河流域尤為明顯。學者分析指出，中國正透過將湄公河水資源治理「安全化」的手段，擴大其在中南半島的政治與經濟影響力。水資源不僅是區域內的農業與民生基礎，更與水力發電等能源供給息息相關。當能源轉型要求提升電力供給時，水資源的跨境治理與開發權益，已成為東協國家在追求能源安全過程中，必須面對的複雜政治挑戰。
這意味著東南亞的能源政策不僅是技術與產業問題，更深陷於區域地緣政治的角力之中，任何單一的能源開發計畫都可能牽動區域內的水權與環境安全。
目前，針對COP31所提出的「35-by-35」倡議，各國在執行層面上仍面臨諸多限制。首先，電氣化比例的提升是否能直接對應等比例的減碳效果，高度依賴各國電網中再生能源的滲透率。若電力來源仍以化石燃料為主，電氣化反而可能增加電網負荷與碳排放。此外，東南亞國家在接受外資援助建設時，如何平衡能源自主權與外資的技術路徑要求，亦是政策制定者需謹慎評估之處。
不應過度解讀單一技術（如核能或單一水電專案）為解決能源危機的唯一解方，因為能源轉型本質上是一個系統性的工程，需要政策、技術與區域地緣政治的協調。
後續觀察重點應聚焦於COP31後續公報的執行細節，特別是針對開發中國家提供技術轉移與資金支援的具體機制。在東南亞地區，需密切關注各國在提升電力供給的同時，如何處理湄公河水資源治理與跨境能源合作的矛盾。此外，隨著AI與高科技產業在東南亞設廠規模擴大，工業用電需求將成為檢驗各國能源轉型政策成敗的關鍵指標。未來各國是否能在確保能源成本競爭力的前提下，落實低碳化目標，將是評估全球電氣化倡議成效的重要參考依據。
