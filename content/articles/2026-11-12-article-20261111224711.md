  # The 31st UN Climate Change Conference Opens: Taiwanese Enterprises Focus on Sustainability and Energy Transition
  
  > The 31st UN Climate Change Conference (COP31) has officially commenced in Antalya, Turkey. Taiwanese enterprises Cathay Financial Holdings and Delta Electronics are actively participating, focusing on climate finance, smart energy, and electrification transitions to address the challenges of global warming.
  
  The 31st UN Climate Change Conference (COP31) officially kicked off on November 9, 2026, in Antalya, Turkey. This two-week international event brings together representatives from countries around the world to discuss climate action, sustainable transition, and specific strategies to address global warming. As the trend of global warming intensifies, climate change is no longer a public issue for individual nations, but a core challenge affecting the global economy and industrial development. Against this backdrop, iconic Taiwanese enterprises Cathay Financial Holdings and Delta Electronics have both sent delegations to attend, demonstrating Taiwan's international influence in the fields of green finance and energy technology.
  
  Cathay Financial Holdings is led by President Chang-Ken Lee, accompanied by Senior Advisor Sophia Cheng and Cathay United Bank Executive Vice President Chen-Han Wen. Delta Electronics is represented by corporate delegates and continues to promote substantive cooperation with international organizations.
  
  According to information confirmed by multiple sources, Cathay Financial Holdings has participated in the COP series of meetings for six consecutive years and is hosting a side event in the official Blue Zone for the fourth time at this conference. The focus is on climate resilience, nature-based actions, and innovative financial cooperation, aiming to deepen the impact of climate finance in the Asia-Pacific region. Meanwhile, Delta Electronics also announced its participation in COP31, marking the company's 19th consecutive year attending the UN climate conference. It has explicitly responded to the "35×35" initiative proposed by the host country, with a goal to increase the electrification ratio to 35% by 2035.
  
  At the conference, Delta Electronics is focusing on issues such as microgrids, smart energy management, and biodiversity conservation, while strengthening its position in the international energy transition by sharing technical practices.
  
  Although the companies have reached a consensus on their participation goals, their specific business priorities differ. Cathay Financial Holdings' strategic focus is on "climate finance," using financial instruments to guide capital toward sustainable transition projects. Delta Electronics focuses on "technology application and revenue growth"; in addition to sharing smart energy solutions at the conference, it also announced strong financial performance, with its October consolidated revenue reaching NT$74.666 billion, a record high for a single month. This indicates that its business expansion in power supplies, components, and infrastructure is complementary to its climate transition strategy.
  
  Furthermore, although COP31 aims to address global climate issues, the current focus of discussions at the venue is largely on technology transfer and financial mechanisms. There remains room for negotiation among countries regarding mandatory commitments to specific decarbonization pathways, which will be revealed as the conference proceedings continue.
  
  For North America and the global industry, the discussions at COP31 have a direct impact. The North American region has recently been frequently hit by extreme heat, with temperatures in cities like New York approaching historical records. Such climate events highlight the urgency of upgrading energy infrastructure. The smart energy management and microgrid technologies advocated by Delta Electronics are key technical pathways to solving the rising power demand and insufficient grid resilience in North America. Additionally, the climate finance standards promoted internationally by the financial industry, such as Cathay Financial Holdings, will indirectly affect the ESG ratings and costs of North American multinational corporations when raising capital, prompting companies to accelerate their transitions to comply with global low-carbon financial regulations.
  
  However, all parties should maintain a cautious perspective on the effectiveness of climate action. Although companies are actively investing in international initiatives, the mitigation of climate change depends not only on a single technology or financial means but also on the deep cooperation of government policies worldwide. Limiting factors include global geopolitical uncertainty, differences in energy transition costs across countries, and the complexity of climate data monitoring. For example, the EU's Copernicus Climate Change Service (C3S) predicts that 2026 will be the hottest year on record, indicating that the speed of global warming may exceed expectations.
  
  Therefore, while the initiatives of enterprises at COP31 are significant, they still face challenges in making a substantive contribution to slowing global warming if global policy consistency and execution are lacking. The immediate cooling effect that can be brought about by the actions of a single company should not be over-interpreted.
  
  Subsequent observation will focus on the specific implementation mechanisms for the "35×35" initiative during COP31, and how financial institutions incorporate climate risk assessment into broader investment portfolios. Furthermore, Delta Electronics' technology exports in the smart energy sector and Cathay Financial Holdings' progress in Asia-Pacific climate finance cooperation will be key indicators for assessing the status of Taiwanese enterprises in the international sustainable supply chain. As the conference enters its second week, the negotiation results on various issues will have a profound impact on the investment trends of the global green finance and energy industries over the coming year. All parties should continue to pay attention to the joint declaration and specific action plans following the conclusion of the conference.

# 第31屆聯合國氣候變遷大會揭幕：臺灣企業聚焦永續與能源轉型

> 第31屆聯合國氣候變遷大會（COP31）於土耳其安塔利亞正式展開，臺灣企業國泰金控與臺達電積極參與，聚焦氣候金融、智慧能源與電氣化轉型，以應對全球暖化挑戰。


第31屆聯合國氣候變遷大會（COP31）於2026年11月9日在土耳其安塔利亞正式拉開帷幕，這場為期兩週的國際盛會匯聚全球各國代表，共同商討氣候行動、永續轉型及應對全球升溫的具體策略。隨著全球暖化趨勢加劇，氣候變遷已非單一國家的公共議題，而是影響全球經濟與產業發展的核心挑戰。在此背景下，臺灣指標性企業國泰金控與臺達電均派員出席，展現臺灣在綠色金融與能源技術領域的國際影響力。
國泰金控由總經理李長庚率隊，偕同資深顧問程淑芬與國泰世華銀行副總經理溫珍瀚與會；臺達電則由企業代表參與，並持續推動與國際組織的實質合作。
根據多方來源共同確認的資訊，國泰金控已連續六年參與COP系列會議，並於本次大會中第四度進入官方藍區（Blue Zone）主辦座談，重點探討氣候韌性、自然行動及創新金融合作，旨在深化亞太地區的氣候融資影響力。與此同時，臺達電亦宣佈參與COP31，這不僅是該企業連續第19年參與聯合國氣候會議，更明確響應了主辦國提出的「35×35」倡議，目標在2035年前將電氣化比例提升至35%。
臺達電在會中聚焦於微電網、智慧能源管理及生物多樣性保育等議題，並透過分享技術實務，強化在國際能源轉型中的定位。
儘管各企業在參與目標上達成共識，但針對具體業務的推動重點仍有所差異。國泰金控的策略重心在於「氣候金融」，透過金融工具引導資金流向永續轉型專案；臺達電則側重「技術應用與營收驅動」，除了在會議中分享智慧能源解決方案，亦公佈了亮眼的財務表現，其10月合併營收達746.66億元，創下單月歷史新高，顯示其在電源、零元件及基礎設施領域的業務擴張與氣候轉型策略相輔相成。
此外，雖然COP31旨在解決全球氣候問題，但目前會場討論的焦點多集中在技術轉移與金融機制，各國對於具體減碳路徑的強制性承諾仍存在不同程度的協商空間，尚待後續會議程式揭曉。
對於北美及全球產業而言，COP31的討論內容具有直接影響。北美地區近期頻繁遭受極端高溫侵襲，部分城市如紐約的氣溫紀錄甚至逼近歷史極值，這類氣候事件凸顯了能源基礎設施升級的迫切性。臺達電所倡導的智慧能源管理與微電網技術，正是解決北美電力需求攀升與電網韌性不足的關鍵技術路徑。此外，金融業如國泰金控在國際間推動的氣候融資標準，亦將間接影響北美跨國企業在籌資時的ESG評級與成本，促使企業加速轉型以符合全球低碳金融規範。
然而，各界對氣候行動的成效仍應保持審慎觀點。儘管企業積極投入國際倡議，但氣候變遷的緩解不僅依賴單一技術或金融手段，更需各國政府政策的深度配合。限制性因素包括全球地緣政治的不確定性、各國能源轉型成本的差異，以及氣候資料監測的複雜性。例如，歐盟哥白尼氣候變遷服務（C3S）預測2026年將成為有紀錄以來最熱的一年，顯示氣候暖化的速度可能超乎預期。
因此，企業在COP31的倡議雖具指標意義，但若缺乏全球政策的一致性與執行力，其對減緩全球升溫的實質貢獻仍面臨挑戰，不應過度解讀單一企業行動所能帶來的即時性降溫效果。
後續觀察重點將聚焦於COP31大會期間，各國針對「35×35」倡議的具體落實機制，以及金融機構如何將氣候風險評估納入更廣泛的投資組合。此外，臺達電在智慧能源領域的技術輸出，以及國泰金控在亞太氣候融資的合作進展，將是評估臺灣企業在國際永續供應鏈中地位的關鍵指標。隨著會議進入第二週，各項議題的談判結果將對未來一年全球綠色金融與能源產業的投資風向產生深遠影響，各界應持續關注大會閉幕後的聯合宣言與具體行動計畫。
