  # U.S. Mandated Rebranding to "Superintelligence" Sparks Tech Industry Transformation and Market Volatility
  
  > The U.S. government recently ordered federal agencies to replace the term "Artificial Intelligence (AI)" with "Superintelligence (SI)." This move has prompted industry-wide adoption, while SpaceX’s successful Starship test flight has been met with a stock price pullback, leaving global technology and energy markets in a period of high volatility.
  
  There has been a significant shift in U.S. technology policy recently. President Trump signed an executive order mandating that all federal agencies across the United States replace the term "Artificial Intelligence (AI)," which has been in use for decades, with "Superintelligence (SI)" in all official documents, policy reports, and public communications. In the executive order, Trump stated bluntly that the technology should not be viewed as "artificial." This move is seen not merely as a change in terminology, but as a strategic gamble by the U.S. government to maintain economic growth while circumventing existing regulatory frameworks.
  
  This executive order immediately triggered a domino effect within the industry. According to relevant reports, multiple tech leaders, including NVIDIA CEO Jensen Huang and Tesla CEO Elon Musk, have quickly adjusted their rhetoric in public settings, opting to refer to the technology as "Superintelligence." Market analysts believe this battle over defining power reflects the U.S. government's attempt to reconfigure regulatory authority over future computing capabilities by redefining the scope of technology, while simultaneously seeking a new balance between technological development and ethical constraints.
  
  However, this policy change has sparked varying levels of concern in both academia and the industry. Some experts point out that the mandatory renaming may merely simplify complex technological ethics issues, fearing that so-called "ethical constraints" could become a "fig leaf" for tech giants to evade responsibility. Meanwhile, the tech market remains skeptical about the profitability of high capital expenditures. Taking SpaceX as an example, although its 14th Starship test flight successfully reached orbit and deployed 26 Starlink satellites—marking a significant milestone for the manned lunar landing program—its stock price fell by over 2%.
  
  Beyond concerns regarding Starship recovery technology and manned safety testing, market investors are also focusing on whether the company's high capital expenditures in "Superintelligence"-related businesses can be converted into tangible profits.
  
  From the perspective of global trade and security, technological transformation is closely linked to the stability of energy supply chains. According to the latest estimates from J.P. Morgan and Goldman Sachs, although the U.S.-Iran conflict has persisted for eight months and shipping risks remain, Middle Eastern crude oil transport volumes have rebounded to 17.5 million barrels per day, approximately 98% of pre-war levels. Analysts believe that given the ongoing conflict, the recovery of export channels to this level is "astonishing," though the recovery is uneven, with refined petroleum product (such as diesel and gasoline) transport volumes having only recovered to 58% of pre-war levels.
  
  This data indicates that although the global tech industry is undergoing a definitional transition to "Superintelligence," the resilience of traditional energy supply chains and geopolitical risks remain key variables affecting the stability of the Asia-Pacific and global economies.
  
  For relevant industries, policy changes and market volatility present dual challenges. On one hand, tech companies must adapt to the U.S. government's new regulatory and narrative framework for "Superintelligence," which may affect future R&D investment directions and compliance costs. On the other hand, the cautious attitude of investors toward high-valuation tech companies shows that the market is no longer satisfied with mere declarations of technological breakthroughs and is demanding clear paths to profitability. Furthermore, as global supply chains gradually adapt to energy transport fluctuations caused by geopolitics, companies must incorporate both technological policy shifts and energy supply imbalances into their assessments of cross-border operational risks.
  
  Currently, various sectors remain reserved regarding the long-term impact of the "Superintelligence" renaming policy. Experts warn against over-interpreting the impact of a single name change on the substantive development of technology. The focus should be on whether the U.S. government will subsequently introduce specific regulatory details for "SI" and whether these policies will further spread to technology regulations in the Asia-Pacific region. Subsequent observation will focus on: first, the specific regulatory actions taken by U.S. federal agencies after implementing the SI policy; second, the strategic adjustments made by tech giants like SpaceX in balancing capital expenditure and profit returns; and third, the stability of Middle Eastern energy transport amid the ongoing conflict and its ripple effects on global inflation and industrial costs.

# 美國強制更名「超級智慧」引發科技界變革與市場震盪

> 美國政府近期下令聯邦機構將「人工智慧（AI）」全面更名為「超級智慧（SI）」，此舉引發產業界跟進，同時 SpaceX 星艦試射成功卻面臨股價回撥，全球科技與能源市場正處於高度變動期。


近期美國科技政策出現重大轉向。美國總統川普簽署行政命令，強制要求全美聯邦機構在所有公文、政策報告及公開宣傳中，全面以「超級智慧（Superintelligence，簡稱 SI）」取代沿用數十年的「人工智慧（AI）」一詞。川普在行政命令中直言，該技術不應被視為「人工」，此舉不僅是名詞上的更迭，更被視為美國政府在維繫經濟成長與規避既有監管框架間的策略性博弈。
此項行政命令隨即在產業界引發骨牌效應。根據相關報導，包括輝達（NVIDIA）執行長黃仁勳與特斯拉（Tesla）執行長馬斯克在內的多位科技領袖，已迅速在公開場合修正措辭，改以「超級智慧」稱呼相關技術。市場分析認為，這場定義權的爭奪戰，反映出美國政府試圖透過重新定義科技範疇，來重新配置對未來運算能力的監管許可權，並試圖在技術發展與道德約束間取得新的平衡。
然而，此項政策變動在學界與業界引發了不同層次的擔憂。部分專家指出，強制更名可能僅是將複雜的技術倫理問題簡化，擔憂所謂的「道德約束」恐淪為科技巨頭規避責任的「遮羞布」。與此同時，科技市場對於高額資本支出的獲利能力仍存疑慮。以 SpaceX 為例，儘管其星艦（Starship）第 14 次試射成功進入軌道並釋放 26 枚 Starlink 衛星，標誌著載人登月計畫的重要里程碑，但其股價卻反向下跌逾 2%。
市場投資人除了關注星艦回收技術與載人安全測試的挑戰外，亦將目光聚焦於該公司在「超級智慧」相關事業上的高額資本支出是否能轉化為實質獲利。
從全球經貿與安全形度觀察，科技變革與能源供應鏈的穩定性密切相關。根據摩根大通與高盛的最新估算，儘管美伊衝突已持續八個月，且航運風險依舊存在，但中東原油運輸量已回升至每日 1,750 萬桶，約為戰前水準的 98%。分析師認為，在戰事持續背景下，出口通道能恢復至此水準相當「驚人」，但恢復程度並不均衡，成品油（如柴油、汽油）運輸量僅恢復至戰前水準的 58%。
此一資料顯示，儘管全球科技產業正經歷「超級智慧」的定義轉型，但傳統能源供應鏈的韌性與地緣政治風險，仍是影響亞太及全球經濟穩定性的關鍵變數。
對於相關產業而言，政策變動與市場波動帶來了雙重挑戰。一方面，科技公司需適應美國政府對「超級智慧」的新監管與敘事框架，這可能影響未來的研發投入方向與合規成本；另一方面，投資人對於高估值科技公司的審慎態度，顯示市場已不再僅滿足於技術突破的宣示，更要求明確的獲利路徑。此外，隨著全球供應鏈逐漸適應地緣政治帶來的能源運輸波動，企業在評估跨國營運風險時，必須同時納入技術政策變動與能源供應不均衡的雙重考量。
目前各界對於「超級智慧」更名政策的長期影響仍持保留態度。專家提醒，不應過度解讀單一名稱變更對技術實質發展的影響，重點應在於美國政府後續是否會針對「SI」推出具體的監管細則，以及這些政策是否會進一步擴散至亞太地區的科技法規中。後續觀察重點將聚焦於：第一，美國聯邦機構在執行 SI 政策後的具體監管作為；第二，SpaceX 等科技巨頭在處理資本支出與獲利回報間的策略調整；第三，中東能源運輸在戰事持續下的穩定性，以及其對全球通膨與產業成本的連動效應。
