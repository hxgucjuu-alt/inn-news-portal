  # Japanese Firms Show Weakness in Geopolitical Risk Response: Global Survey Reveals Gap in International Competitiveness
  
  > A survey by PwC Japan Group indicates that Japanese companies significantly lag behind their international peers in geopolitical risk management. Although global enterprises generally perceive heightened risks, Japanese firms still need to strengthen their intelligence management and response mechanisms as they face potential impacts from Trump's tariff policies.
  
  PwC Japan Group held a seminar on November 11, 2026, to officially release the results of the "2026 Corporate Geopolitical Risk Response Survey (Global Edition)." This large-scale survey conducted in-depth interviews and comparative analyses with 2,100 managers across nine countries and regions worldwide for the first time. The background indicates that as the global geopolitical situation becomes increasingly complex, the economic security challenges faced by companies have become a core issue at the board level.
  
  According to the survey report, global companies generally view geopolitics as a key variable affecting operational stability. In this wave of global risk assessment, Japanese companies were identified as having a significant execution gap compared to major overseas competitors in terms of intelligence gathering, risk assessment, and the establishment of response mechanisms.
  
  Based on cross-referenced sources, the survey data shows that 77% of Japanese companies explicitly stated that the level of geopolitical risk they have faced over the past five years has shown an upward trend, with 35% of respondents considering the risk level to have "increased significantly." Regarding specific risk items, the issues Japanese firms are most concerned about include trade barriers and supply chain restructuring pressures brought about by the "full implementation of Trump's tariffs." In contrast, the survey results show that more than half of the companies among peers in major overseas countries have already established standardized response processes and intelligence monitoring systems for geopolitical risks.
  
  This confirmed data indicates that Japanese companies show a clear lag in organizational resilience when responding to geopolitical changes, which may directly affect their supply chain layout and cost control capabilities in the global market.
  
  However, there are still information asymmetries and differences in interpretation among companies in various countries regarding the definition of "risk preparedness" and actual implementation effectiveness. Although the survey points out that Japanese companies are lagging, the report does not detail whether the actual effectiveness of overseas companies in dealing with specific geopolitical events (such as tariff barriers) can fully offset the negative impacts caused by the risks. Furthermore, there is currently no internationally unified assessment framework for specific standards of "economic security intelligence management," which leaves room for different interpretations by some respondents regarding the definition and measurement indicators of the term "lagging" in the survey results.
  
  The survey also did not detail the specific strategies of these overseas companies, such as whether they are avoiding risks through supply chain diversification or localized production; these details remain to be supplemented by subsequent data.
  
  The results of this survey may have profound indirect effects on the global industrial chain, especially on the South American market, which is highly connected to the Japanese manufacturing industry. As an important global base for mineral and agricultural product supply, if Japanese companies become more conservative in cross-border investment decisions or engage in divestment due to insufficient geopolitical risk response capabilities, it will directly impact the industrial development and foreign investment inflows of South American countries. At the same time, if Japanese companies are forced to adjust their global supply chain strategies, export-oriented industries in South America need to more actively improve their sensitivity to international political changes to cope with potential risks arising from changes in Japanese corporate procurement policies.
  
  This survey is not only a wake-up call for Japanese companies but also provides a reference for relevant countries in the global supply chain to re-examine their economic security strategies.
  
  Although the survey results show that Japanese companies face challenges, all sectors should not over-interpret this data as a comprehensive decline in the global market competitiveness of Japanese companies. The nature of geopolitical risk is highly uncertain, and a single response survey cannot fully reflect a company's comprehensive strength in technological innovation, brand value, and localized operations. In addition, the survey sample covers nine countries, and the industrial structure and market dependence of companies in each country are different; generalizing them may ignore the flexibility of specific industries in responding to risks.
  
  Therefore, when analyzing such surveys, it is necessary to consider the differences in the definition and willingness to disclose "risk" that may exist under different cultural backgrounds, and avoid viewing a single survey result as an absolute standard for judging corporate quality.
  
  Subsequent observation points will focus on whether Japanese companies will adjust their overseas investment and supply chain management strategies in response to this survey report, especially regarding specific defensive measures against Trump's tariff policies. In addition, whether PwC will subsequently release more detailed risk analyses for different industries (such as automotive, electronics, and energy) will be key to observing how Japanese firms enhance their geopolitical resilience. At the same time, how global companies move from simple "risk avoidance" to "strategic layout" when facing geopolitical risks will become a core issue of future international business governance. The market should continue to pay attention to follow-up tracking reports issued by international accounting firms and think tanks on this issue to obtain more comprehensive global economic security dynamics.

# 日企地緣風險應對顯現弱點：全球調查揭示國際競爭力落差

> 資誠日本分公司調查顯示，日本企業在地緣政治風險管理上顯著落後於國際同業。儘管全球企業普遍感受到風險加劇，日企在情報管理與應對機制上的準備程度仍待加強，面臨川普關稅政策帶來的潛在衝擊。


資誠聯合會計師事務所日本分公司（PwC Japan Group）於 2026 年 11 月 11 日舉辦研討會，正式公佈「2026 年企業地緣政治風險應對調查（全球版）」結果。此項調查規模宏大，首次針對全球 9 個國家與地區的 2,100 名經理人進行深度訪談與比較分析。背景顯示，隨著全球地緣政治局勢日益複雜，企業面臨的經濟安全挑戰已成為董事會層級的核心議題。
根據調查報告，全球企業普遍將地緣政治視為影響營運穩定性的關鍵變數，而日本企業在這一波全球性風險評估中，被點名在情報收集、風險評估與應對機制建立方面，與海外主要競爭對手存在顯著的執行落差。
根據多項來源交叉比對，調查資料顯示高達 77% 的日本企業明確指出，過去五年內所面臨的地緣政治風險水準呈現上升趨勢，其中更有 35% 的受訪者認為風險程度為「顯著增加」。在具體的風險專案中，日企最為擔憂的議題包括「川普關稅全面實施」所帶來的貿易壁壘與供應鏈重組壓力。相較之下，調查結果顯示海外主要國家的同業，已有超過半數的企業針對地緣政治風險建立了一套標準化的應對流程與情報監控系統。
這項共同確認的資料顯示，日本企業在應對地緣政治變局的組織韌性上，呈現出明顯的滯後狀態，這可能直接影響其在全球市場的供應鏈佈局與成本控制能力。
然而，關於「風險準備」的定義與實際執行成效，各國企業間仍存在資訊不對稱與詮釋上的差異。雖然調查指出日本企業落後，但報告中並未詳盡說明海外企業在面對具體地緣政治事件（如關稅壁壘）時，其實際成效是否能完全抵銷風險帶來的負面衝擊。此外，針對「經濟安全情報管理」的具體標準，目前尚無國際統一的評估框架，這使得部分受訪者對於調查結果中「落後」一詞的定義與衡量指標，仍保有不同的解讀空間。
調查中亦未詳細說明這些海外企業在應對策略上的具體細節，例如是透過分散供應鏈還是透過在地化生產來規避風險，這些細節仍有待後續資料補充。
此項調查結果對全球產業鏈，尤其是與日本製造業高度連結的南美地區市場，可能產生深遠的間接影響。南美地區作為全球重要的礦產與農產品供應基地，若日本企業因地緣政治風險應對能力不足，導致其在跨國投資決策上更趨保守或出現撤資行為，將直接衝擊南美各國的產業發展與外資流入。同時，若日本企業被迫調整全球供應鏈策略，南美地區的出口導向型產業需更積極地提升自身對國際政治變動的敏感度，以應對來自日資企業採購政策變更的潛在風險。
這項調查不僅是日本企業的警鐘，也為全球供應鏈中的相關國家提供了重新審視經濟安全策略的參考。
儘管調查結果顯示日本企業面臨挑戰，但各界不應過度解讀此資料為日本企業在全球市場競爭力的全面衰退。地緣政治風險的本質具有高度不確定性，單一的應對調查無法完全反映企業在技術創新、品牌價值與在地化營運上的綜合實力。此外，調查樣本涵蓋 9 個國家，各國企業的產業結構與市場依賴度不同，將其一概而論可能忽略了特定產業在應對風險時的靈活性。
因此，在分析此類調查時，必須考量到不同文化背景下，企業對於「風險」的定義與揭露意願可能存在的差異，避免將單一調查結果視為企業體質的絕對評判標準。
後續觀察重點將聚焦於日本企業是否會針對此調查報告，調整其海外投資與供應鏈管理策略，特別是針對川普關稅政策的具體防禦性措施。此外，資誠後續是否會針對不同產業別（如汽車、電子、能源）發布更細緻的風險分析，將是觀察日企如何提升地緣政治韌性的關鍵。同時，全球企業在面對地緣政治風險時，如何從單純的「風險規避」轉向「戰略性佈局」，將成為未來國際商業治理的核心議題。市場應持續關注國際會計師事務所與智庫針對此議題發布的後續追蹤報告，以獲取更全面的全球經濟安全動態。
