  # Political Shift in Saxony-Anhalt, Germany: Historic Victory for Far-Right AfD
  
  > The recent state election in Saxony-Anhalt, Germany, saw the far-right party "Alternative for Germany" (AfD) win by an overwhelming margin, breaking post-war German political traditions and sparking widespread concern over the expansion of far-right forces in Europe.
  
  The results of the recent state election in Saxony-Anhalt, Germany, have shocked the European political landscape. According to exit polls, the far-right party "Alternative for Germany" (AfD) achieved an overwhelming victory in this vote, significantly outperforming traditional mainstream parties. This event is of landmark significance in German political history, as since the end of World War II, all levels of government in Germany (including federal, state, and major cities) have long been held by center-left or center-right traditional parties, with far-right or right-wing populist parties never having secured governing dominance.
  
  The election results not only mark a dramatic reorganization of the state's political landscape but are also viewed as a critical test of public opinion regarding current immigration policies, the economic status quo, and European Union integration, drawing widespread attention from media and political observers across Europe.
  
  According to reports from media outlets including Euronews, the Financial Times, and BBC News, multiple sources have confirmed that the AfD achieved historic growth in votes in Saxony-Anhalt. The party has long advocated against immigration, questioned current EU policies, and maintained a strongly critical stance on Germany's current energy transition and economic policies. In this election, the AfD successfully mobilized voters dissatisfied with the status quo, with its vote share not only exceeding pre-election polling expectations but also showing an "overwhelming" lead in multiple districts.
  
  Media reports collectively point out that this victory is not an isolated incident but rather a reflection of the long-standing sense of political alienation in eastern Germany and a response to the public's feeling of powerlessness regarding the governance of mainstream parties. All media outlets have framed this event as a major challenge to Germany's post-war political system, symbolizing that far-right forces have moved from the fringes into the core of mainstream politics.
  
  However, there remain subtle differences and unresolved aspects among sources regarding the specific scope of impact and subsequent political effects of the AfD's victory. Some media emphasize that although the AfD won the majority of votes in Saxony-Anhalt, whether it can successfully form a coalition government still depends on whether other parties are willing to break the "Cordon Sanitaire" to engage in alliance negotiations. Currently, Germany's mainstream parties remain absolutely opposed to cooperating with the AfD, which may lead to a stalemate in the state's future cabinet formation process.
  
  Furthermore, regarding the specific reasons for the surge in the AfD's vote share, analyses vary in focus: the Financial Times emphasizes the impact of structural economic issues on voter behavior, while the BBC News focuses more on the catalytic role of immigration issues in local elections. These differing perspectives reflect that there is no consensus within German society on the core factors leading to this political shift.
  
  The election results have had a direct impact on the political stability of Germany and the EU. At the policy level, the rise of the AfD may force the German federal government to face greater domestic pressure when dealing with issues such as immigration, climate change, and EU budgets, and could even lead to a more conservative decision-making model for Germany within the EU. For industry, the far-right party's questioning of the green transition may affect the long-term planning and investment confidence of Germany's energy sector. At the social level, this election has exacerbated the political rift between eastern and western Germany, and the trend toward the legitimization of far-right ideology has raised high concerns among German civil society groups and minority communities, who believe it poses a threat to social inclusion and diverse values.
  
  Although the AfD achieved a significant victory, analysts warn against over-interpreting the results of a single state election as a comprehensive shift in national German politics. First, the political uniqueness of Saxony-Anhalt is very high; its voter structure differs significantly from that of the industrial states in western Germany and cannot be directly extrapolated to federal-level elections. Second, while the AfD demonstrated strong mobilization capabilities during the election, its lack of governing experience and concrete policy implementation plans remain key points for future voter scrutiny. Furthermore, all parties should be cautious in distinguishing between "protest votes" and "policy support," as many voters may have voted for the AfD out of dissatisfaction with the incumbent government rather than full agreement with all of the party's extreme positions.
  
  Therefore, the outside world should not simplify this result into a conclusion that German society is shifting entirely to the right; it is still necessary to observe the party's performance after it actually participates in local governance.
  
  The focus for subsequent observation lies in the state's cabinet formation process and how Germany's major parties respond to the political shock brought by the AfD. If other parties continue to refuse to cooperate with the AfD, the state may face long-term political instability or even require new elections. In addition, all sectors in Germany will closely watch whether the AfD's success model will spread to other states, thereby affecting the future distribution of seats in the federal parliament. Observers point out that in the coming months, Germany's mainstream parties will inevitably adjust their narrative strategies to respond to voter anxiety over security, the economy, and immigration in an attempt to win back lost votes. The subsequent effects of this election will become a key indicator for examining the resilience of Germany's democratic system and the direction of European politics.

# 德國薩克森-安哈特邦選舉變天：極右翼政黨AfD取得歷史性勝利

> 德國薩克森-安哈特邦近期舉行地方選舉，極右翼政黨「德國另類選擇黨」（AfD）以壓倒性優勢勝出，打破德國戰後政壇傳統，引發各界對歐洲極右翼勢力擴張的關注。


德國薩克森-安哈特邦（Saxony-Anhalt）近期舉行的地方選舉結果震撼歐洲政壇。根據出口民調顯示，極右翼政黨「德國另類選擇黨」（AfD）在此次投票中獲得壓倒性勝利，得票率大幅領先傳統主流政黨。此事件在德國政治史上具有指標性意義，因為自第二次世界大戰結束以來，德國各級政府（包含聯邦、各邦及主要城市）長期由中間偏左或中間偏右的傳統政黨把持，極右翼或右翼民粹主義政黨始終未能取得執政主導權。
此次選舉結果不僅標誌著該邦政治版圖的劇烈重組，更被視為德國社會對於當前移民政策、經濟現狀及歐盟整合議題的一場重要民意檢驗，引起歐洲各國媒體與政治觀察家的廣泛重視。
綜合《Euronews》、《Financial Times》及《BBC News》等媒體的報導，多方來源一致確認AfD在薩克森-安哈特邦取得了歷史性的選票增長。該黨長期以來主張反移民、質疑歐盟現行政策，並對德國當前的能源轉型與經濟方針持強烈批判態度。此次選舉中，AfD成功動員了對現狀不滿的選民，其得票率不僅超出了選前民調的預期，更在多個選區呈現「壓倒性」的領先優勢。
媒體報導共同指出，這次勝利並非單一事件，而是反映了德國東部地區長期存在的政治疏離感，以及民眾對於主流政黨施政無力感的回應。各家媒體皆將此事件定調為德國戰後政治體系面臨的重大挑戰，象徵極右翼勢力已從邊緣進入主流政治核心。
然而，針對AfD此次勝利的具體影響範圍與後續政治效應，各來源間仍存在細微差異與尚待釐清之處。部分媒體強調，儘管AfD在薩克森-安哈特邦獲得多數選票，但其是否能順利組成聯合政府，仍取決於其他政黨是否願意打破「政治防火牆」（Cordon Sanitaire），與其進行結盟談判。目前，德國主流政黨對於是否與AfD合作仍持絕對否定的態度，這導致該邦未來的組閣過程可能陷入僵局。
此外，關於AfD得票率飆升的具體原因，各方分析各有側重，《Financial Times》側重於經濟結構性問題對選民行為的影響，而《BBC News》則更關注移民議題在地方選舉中的催化作用。這些不同觀點反映了德國社會內部對於導致此次政治變動的核心因素，尚未達成共識。
此次選舉結果對德國及歐盟的政治穩定產生了直接影響。在政策層面，AfD的崛起可能迫使德國聯邦政府在處理移民、氣候變遷及歐盟預算等議題時，面臨更大的國內壓力，甚至可能導致德國在歐盟內部的決策模式趨於保守。對於產業而言，極右翼政黨對綠色轉型的質疑，可能影響德國能源產業的長期規劃與投資信心。社會層面上，此次選舉加劇了德國東西部之間的政治裂痕，極右翼意識形態的合法化趨勢，已引起德國公民社會團體與少數族群的高度擔憂，認為這將對社會包容性與多元價值構成威脅。
儘管AfD取得了顯著勝利，但分析人士提醒，不應過度解讀單一邦的選舉結果為德國全國政治的全面轉向。首先，薩克森-安哈特邦的政治特殊性極高，其選民結構與德國西部工業大邦存在顯著差異，不能直接類推至聯邦層級的選舉。其次，AfD在選舉中展現的動員能力雖強，但其缺乏執政經驗與具體政策執行方案的短板，仍是未來選民檢視的重點。此外，各界應謹慎區分「抗議性投票」與「政策性支援」，許多選民投給AfD或許是出於對現任政府的不滿，而非完全認同該黨的所有極端主張。
因此，外界不應將此結果簡化為德國社會全面右傾的定論，仍需觀察該黨在實際參與地方治理後的表現。
後續觀察重點在於該邦的組閣程式，以及德國主要政黨如何應對AfD帶來的政治衝擊。若其他政黨持續拒絕與AfD合作，該邦恐將面臨長期的政治不穩定，甚至可能需要重新選舉。此外，德國各界將密切關注AfD此次的成功模式是否會擴散至其他邦，進而影響未來聯邦議會的席次分配。觀察家指出，未來數月內，德國主流政黨勢必會調整其論述策略，以回應選民對於安全、經濟與移民議題的焦慮，試圖挽回流失的選票。這場選舉的後續效應，將成為檢視德國民主體制韌性與歐洲政治走向的關鍵指標。
