  # Global Tech Capital Rotation: Rising AI Investment Concerns and U.S.-EU Supply Chain Restructuring
  
  > As markets remain volatile ahead of Nvidia’s earnings report, global fund managers are offloading tech stocks at the fastest pace in 20 years, shifting capital toward European equities. Meanwhile, the U.S. defense industry is accelerating the localization of chip production, while Southeast Asia faces the dual challenges of supply chain transformation and geopolitical alignment under U.S. chip export controls.
  
  The global technology industry is currently facing significant pressure from capital outflows and supply chain restructuring. According to the Bank of America’s monthly global fund manager survey released in November 2026, institutional investors are pulling out of tech stocks at the fastest rate since 2006, as concerns regarding potential overinvestment in the artificial intelligence (AI) sector have climbed to a historical peak.
  
  This trend mirrors recent performance in the U.S. stock market. Influenced by a wait-and-see atmosphere ahead of Nvidia’s earnings release, oil price fluctuations caused by geopolitical tensions in the Middle East, and rising interest rate expectations, the three major U.S. stock indices closed lower for two consecutive days, with tech stocks facing heavy selling pressure. Simultaneously, there has been a clear cross-market rotation of global capital, with fund manager optimism toward European stock markets reaching its highest level since February of this year.
  
  Regarding supply chain restructuring, U.S. defense technology unicorn Anduril announced a strategic partnership with Qualcomm to officially launch "BlueChip," a ruggedized laptop product line designed for military, law enforcement, and public safety sectors. This product line is explicitly targeted at meeting the 2027 Buy American Act threshold, with a commitment to increase the proportion of domestic components to 65% and utilize U.S.-made chips. This move is seen as a significant step in the U.S. effort to reorganize its domestic computer supply chain and reduce defense procurement reliance on overseas sources.
  
  Anduril’s Vice President of Communications, Shannon Prior, has publicly addressed concerns regarding the level of component localization, committing that the first BlueChip model will achieve its supply chain localization goals and emphasizing the core role of TSMC’s Arizona facility in the manufacturing process.
  
  However, the adjustment of the global supply chain is not proceeding smoothly, as different regions face varying geopolitical challenges. On the occasion of the 80th anniversary of U.S.-Philippines diplomatic relations, the Philippines had intended to promote a "Silicon Prosperity" AI park agreement during the ASEAN Summit, aiming to secure a position in the chip supply chain and reduce reliance on nickel exports to China. However, the initiative was unexpectedly met with a cold response from the U.S., which stated that such an agreement was "never planned," forcing the signing to be postponed until the end of the year. This incident highlights the increasingly heavy geopolitical costs ASEAN nations face while attempting to strike a balance between the U.S. and China.
  
  Meanwhile, the U.S. policy of tightening chip export controls toward Southeast Asia has forced ASEAN nations to face more complex economic and security considerations as they navigate the process of choosing sides in the supply chain.
  
  In response to these developments, Bank of America’s survey data indicates that global capital allocation is shifting from tech-oriented to defensive or regionally balanced strategies, presenting a potential capital injection opportunity for the European market. While European companies are not directly on the front lines of U.S.-China chip controls as they face global tech supply chain restructuring, the shift in capital by global fund managers reflects market expectations for a correction of overvalued tech stocks. For related industries, the Anduril case demonstrates that defense technology and chip localization have become the core of U.S. industrial policy, which will force global supply chain participants to re-evaluate the compliance of their production bases and component sources to adapt to increasingly stringent U.S. "Buy American" procurement requirements.
  
  Although there is divergence in the market regarding the prospects of the AI industry, analysts caution against over-interpreting selling behavior ahead of a single earnings report. The Bank of America survey covered 169 respondents worldwide with $461 billion in assets under management; it reflects the risk-averse mindset of institutional investors toward short-term risks rather than a total rejection of long-term AI technological development. Furthermore, while Anduril’s BlueChip laptop promises a high proportion of domestic components, whether the project can successfully achieve full supply chain localization by 2027 remains dependent on the capacity expansion progress of TSMC’s Arizona plant and the stability of Qualcomm’s chip supply.
  
  For Southeast Asian nations like the Philippines, there remains a high degree of uncertainty regarding how to secure critical technological cooperation while caught in the middle of geopolitical tensions.
  
  Future observations will focus on whether there is a substantive shift or reduction in AI capital expenditure following Nvidia’s earnings release. At the same time, whether European stock markets can continue to attract long-term investment after gaining favor will depend on their industrial performance in AI applications and the green energy transition. Additionally, the execution details of U.S. Department of Defense procurement contracts for companies like Anduril, as well as the specific policy responses of Southeast Asian countries to U.S. chip export controls, will be key indicators for observing whether the global tech supply chain landscape will further fragment. All parties should continue to monitor the long-term structural impact of these policy changes on the global semiconductor supply chain.

# 全球科技資本輪動：AI 投資疑慮升溫與美歐供應鏈重組

> 隨輝達財報發布前夕市場動盪，全球基金經理人正以 20 年來最快速度減持科技股並轉向歐股。同時，美國國防產業加速推動晶片本土化，而東南亞在美方晶片出口管制下，面臨供應鏈轉型與地緣政治選邊的雙重挑戰。


全球科技產業近期面臨顯著的資本流動與供應鏈重組壓力。根據美國銀行（Bank of America）於 2026 年 11 月發布的月度全球基金經理人調查顯示，機構投資人正以 2006 年以來最快的速度撤出科技類股，市場對於人工智慧（AI）領域是否存在過度投資的擔憂已攀升至歷史峰值。
此一趨勢與美股近期的表現相互呼應，受輝達（Nvidia）財報發布前夕的觀望氛圍、中東地緣政治緊張導致油價波動，以及利率預期升溫影響，美股三大指數連續兩日收黑，科技類股面臨沉重的拋售賣壓。與此同時，全球資金出現明顯的跨市場輪動，基金經理人對歐洲股市的樂觀情緒已升至今年 2 月以來最高水位。
在供應鏈重組方面，美國國防科技獨角獸 Anduril 宣佈與高通（Qualcomm）展開戰略合作，正式發表專為軍方、執法與公共安全領域設計的強固型筆電產品線「BlueChip」。該產品線明確鎖定 2027 年的美國貨法案（Buy American Act）門檻，並承諾將本土零件佔比提升至 65%，且搭載美製晶片。此舉被視為美國試圖重組本土電腦供應鏈、降低國防採購對海外依賴的重要舉措。
Anduril 通訊副總裁普賴爾（Shannon Prior）已針對零件本土化程度的質疑，公開定調並承諾首款 BlueChip 將落實相關供應鏈在地化目標，強調臺積電亞利桑那州廠區在其中的代工核心地位。
然而，全球供應鏈的調整並非同步順暢，不同區域面臨的地緣政治挑戰各異。在美菲建交 80 週年之際，菲律賓原擬於東協峰會期間推動「矽盛世」AI 園區協議，意圖在晶片供應鏈中尋求定位並擺脫對中國鎳礦出口的依賴，卻意外遭遇美方冷處理。美方對此協議表示「從未規劃」，導致簽約被迫延至年底。這一事件凸顯了東協國家在試圖於美中之間尋求平衡時，面臨日益沉重的地緣政治成本。
與此同時，美國對東南亞收緊晶片出口管制的政策，亦使得東協國家在供應鏈選邊站的過程中，面臨更為複雜的經濟與安全考量。
針對上述發展，美銀的調查資料顯示，全球資金配置正從科技導向轉向防禦性或區域性平衡，這對歐洲市場而言是一項潛在的資本挹注機會。歐洲企業在面對全球科技供應鏈重組時，雖未直接捲入美中晶片管制的最前線，但全球基金經理人的資金轉向，反映了市場對於科技股估值過高的修正預期。對於相關產業而言，Anduril 的案例顯示，國防科技與晶片本土化已成為美國產業政策的核心，這將迫使全球供應鏈參與者必須重新評估其生產基地與零件來源的合規性，以適應美國日益嚴格的「美國貨」採購要求。
儘管市場對 AI 產業前景出現分歧，但分析師提醒，不應過度解讀單一財報前的拋售行為。美銀調查涵蓋了全球 169 名受訪者，管理資產規模達 4,610 億美元，其反映的是機構投資人對於短期風險的避險心態，而非對 AI 長期技術發展的全面否定。此外，Anduril 的 BlueChip 筆電雖承諾了高比例的本土零件，但該計畫能否在 2027 年前順利達成供應鏈完全在地化，仍需視臺積電亞利桑那廠的產能擴充進度以及高通晶片供應的穩定性而定。
對於菲律賓等東南亞國家而言，如何在地緣政治夾縫中確保關鍵技術合作，亦存在高度的不確定性。
後續觀察重點將聚焦於輝達財報發布後，市場對 AI 資本支出是否出現實質性的轉向或縮減；同時，歐洲股市在獲得資金青睞後，能否持續吸引長期投資，將取決於其在 AI 應用與綠色能源轉型中的產業表現。此外，美國國防部針對 Anduril 等企業的採購合約執行細節，以及東南亞各國在面對美國晶片出口管制時的具體應對政策，將是觀察全球科技供應鏈版圖是否進一步碎片化的關鍵指標。各界應持續關注這些政策變動對全球半導體產業鏈的長期結構性影響。
